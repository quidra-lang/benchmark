## Minimization and alternatives

A static inbound translation plus a different outbound PAT candidate was compared with a full TCP handshake, inbound port forwarding, and creation/expiry of a prior PAT mapping. This two-UDP-packet case meets the shared question with fewer state-transition and configuration paths: UDP needs no SYN/FIN state machine; NAT PAT supplies the outside role; direct DEV routing needs no next-hop resolution. The selected post-ACL allocation/reuse and timestamp write sites prove both no-create and no-refresh even though the concrete table is empty. A replying packet subject to reverse static NAT would not itself exercise the PAT candidate distinction. SHOW COUNTERS exposes packet/interface totals. Extra SHOW ROUTES/ACL/NAT/CONNS commands were rejected because the selected state updates already establish those table/hit facts; their formatting adds no requested behavioral evidence. Omit other commands, TCP state transitions, RPF checks beyond its inactive guard/default, expiry, successful PAT packet bookkeeping beyond the allocation/refresh facts, VIA resolution, and error-only diagnostics where they are on separate lines; preserve the validation predicates and successful path. Blank lines and delimiters that do not delimit a required control-flow step are omitted. Internal scope boundaries needed to establish validation, the OUT-ACL rejection barrier and subsequent shared updates are retained; formatting indentation alone is not treated as a scope guarantee in languages whose scope is delimited by tokens.
