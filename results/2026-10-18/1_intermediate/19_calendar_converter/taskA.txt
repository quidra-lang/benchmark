[Overview]
Write a program that converts dates between various calendar systems and computes weekdays, the numbers of days in years and months, date addition and subtraction, holidays such as Easter, monthly calendars and more. The program reads a sequence of commands from standard input, executes them one at a time from the first, and writes the results to standard output.
The calendars handled are ten: the Gregorian calendar, the Julian calendar, the ISO week calendar, the Islamic calendar (a tabular calendar with a 30-year cycle), the Hebrew calendar (an arithmetic calendar based on the molad and postponement rules), the Persian calendar (an arithmetic calendar with a 2820-year cycle), the Coptic calendar, the Ethiopic calendar, the French Republican calendar (an arithmetic calendar) and the Mayan calendar (long count, tzolkin and haab), plus two representations that express a day as a day count: the Julian Day Number and the fixed day number. Every calendar is computed using only the integer formulas defined in this statement (even where details differ from real-world calendar practice or astronomical computation, follow this statement).
The program has no state, and commands do not affect each other.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N, and each of the following N lines contains one command. The input consists of exactly these N + 1 lines.
A command line consists of a command name followed by zero or more arguments, with the command name and the arguments separated by single spaces. All valid command names and keywords consist of uppercase English letters. Weekday names and Mayan names must match the exact sequence of uppercase and lowercase letters shown in the tables of this statement.
Below, "a digit string of k digits" means a string of length k consisting only of ASCII digits (0 to 9). A digit string is read as a decimal number; leading zeros are allowed. Each argument is interpreted, according to its position as defined for each command, as one of the following kinds.
- Calendar name: a string exactly equal to one of GREGORIAN, JULIAN, ISO, ISLAMIC, HEBREW, PERSIAN, COPTIC, ETHIOPIC, FRENCH, MAYAN, JD, FIXED. Which calendar names may be used depends on the command.
- Date: a date in the calendar named by the calendar name written immediately before it. Its notation is defined per calendar in "Date validation".
- Year: a digit string of 1 to 5 digits whose value is at least 1 (0 and 00 are invalid).
- Month: a digit string of 1 to 2 digits. The range of values is defined per command.
- Integer: a digit string of 1 to 7 digits, optionally preceded by a single "-" (-0 means 0).
- Weekday name: one of Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday.
- Others: keywords (TO, DAYS, etc.) and names defined per command.

[Processing]
* Numbers
floor(x) is the largest integer not exceeding x (for example floor(7 / 2) = 3 and floor(-7 / 2) = -4). a mod n (n a positive integer) means a - n * floor(a / n), which is at least 0 and less than n even when a is negative (for example -1 mod 7 = 6). All computations are done in integers.

* Fixed day numbers and the supported range
Dates are handled as integers called "fixed day numbers". The fixed day number of Gregorian year 1, January 1 is 1, and it increases by 1 for each day forward (the fixed day number of the day before it is 0). The program handles only days whose fixed day number is at least 1 and at most 3652059 (from Gregorian year 1, January 1 to year 9999, December 31); this range is called the "supported range".
The weekday of the day with fixed day number f is Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday when f mod 7 is 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 respectively (the day with fixed day number 1 is a Monday). Below, the weekdays are numbered 0 to 6 in this order; this number is called the weekday number.

* Year-month-day calendars
The eight calendars GREGORIAN, JULIAN, ISLAMIC, HEBREW, PERSIAN, COPTIC, ETHIOPIC, FRENCH are called "year-month-day calendars". In a year-month-day calendar, for each integer year y, the number of months in that year, the number of days in each month, and the fixed day number S(y) of the first day of the year (month 1, day 1) are determined by the formulas below. Months are numbered 1, 2, ... from the beginning of the year.
The fixed day number of year y, month m, day d is S(y) plus the total number of days of months 1 through m - 1 of that year plus d - 1. In every calendar, S(y + 1) - S(y) equals the total number of days of all months of year y. Conversely, the day with fixed day number f is represented as the (f - S(y) + 1)-th day, counted from the beginning of the year, of the year y with S(y) <= f < S(y + 1). However, a day for which this y is less than 1 (a day with f < S(1)) is "not representable" in that calendar.
The formulas below are evaluated with the same formula for every integer y, including when y is less than 1 or greater than 99999.

(1) GREGORIAN (Gregorian calendar)
- Leap year: a year with y mod 4 = 0 and "y mod 100 != 0 or y mod 400 = 0".
- Months: 12 months. The numbers of days, from month 1, are 31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31; in a leap year month 2 has 29 days.
- S(y) = 365 * (y - 1) + floor((y - 1) / 4) - floor((y - 1) / 100) + floor((y - 1) / 400) + 1
(2) JULIAN (Julian calendar)
- Leap year: a year with y mod 4 = 0.
- Months: same as GREGORIAN (in a leap year month 2 has 29 days).
- S(y) = 365 * (y - 1) + floor((y - 1) / 4) - 1
  For example, the fixed day number of Julian year 1, January 1 is -1, which is outside the supported range.
(3) ISLAMIC (Islamic calendar)
- Leap year: a year for which y mod 30 is one of 2, 5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 24, 26, 29.
- Months: 12 months. Odd-numbered months have 30 days and even-numbered months have 29 days; in a leap year month 12 has 30 days.
- S(y) = 227015 + 354 * (y - 1) + floor((3 + 11 * y) / 30)
(4) HEBREW (Hebrew calendar)
- The first month of the year is Tishri. Leap years, month lengths and S(y) are defined in "Hebrew calendar computation".
(5) PERSIAN (Persian calendar)
- Let a = y - 474 and b = (a mod 2820) + 474.
- Leap year: a year with ((b + 38) * 682) mod 2816 < 682.
- Months: 12 months. Months 1 to 6 have 31 days, months 7 to 11 have 30 days, and month 12 has 29 days; in a leap year month 12 has 30 days.
- S(y) = 226896 + 1029983 * floor(a / 2820) + 365 * (b - 1) + floor((682 * b - 110) / 2816)
(6) COPTIC (Coptic calendar)
- Leap year: a year with y mod 4 = 3.
- Months: 13 months. Months 1 to 12 have 30 days and month 13 has 5 days; in a leap year month 13 has 6 days.
- S(y) = 103605 + 365 * (y - 1) + floor(y / 4)
(7) ETHIOPIC (Ethiopic calendar)
- Leap years and months: same as COPTIC.
- S(y) = 2796 + 365 * (y - 1) + floor(y / 4)
(8) FRENCH (French Republican calendar)
- Leap year: a year with y mod 4 = 0, y mod 400 equal to none of 100, 200, 300, and y mod 4000 != 0.
- Months: 13 months. Months 1 to 12 have 30 days and month 13 has 5 days; in a leap year month 13 has 6 days.
- S(y) = 654415 + 365 * (y - 1) + floor((y - 1) / 4) - floor((y - 1) / 100) + floor((y - 1) / 400) - floor((y - 1) / 4000)

* Hebrew calendar computation
- Leap year: a year with (7 * y + 1) mod 19 < 7. A common year has 12 months and a leap year has 13 months.
- Time units: 1 hour is 1080 parts, and 1 day = 24 hours = 25920 parts. A Hebrew day begins at 6 p.m. of the previous day.
- Day index: days are counted with integer day indices. The day with day index D corresponds to the day with fixed day number D - 1373427, and its weekday is the weekday of that fixed day number (the day with day index D begins at 6 p.m. of the day before the day with that fixed day number).
- Molad (time of the mean new moon): the molad of Tishri of year y is expressed by the following B(y), Q, D and t.
  B(y) = floor((235 * y - 234) / 19) (the number of months from the beginning of year 1 to the beginning of year y)
  Q = 5604 + 765433 * B(y)
  D = floor(Q / 25920), t = Q mod 25920
  The molad is the moment t parts after the start of the day with day index D (6 p.m. of the previous day) (5604 parts are 5 hours 204 parts, and 765433 parts are 29 days 12 hours 793 parts).
- Postponement rules: compute the day index R by the following steps.
  1. If t >= 19440, let R = D + 1 (rule ZAKEN).
  2. If 1 does not apply, year y is a common year, the weekday of the day with day index D is Tuesday, and t >= 9924, let R = D + 2 (rule GATARAD).
  3. If neither 1 nor 2 applies, year y - 1 is a leap year, the weekday of the day with day index D is Monday, and t >= 16789, let R = D + 1 (rule BETUTAKPAT).
  4. If none of 1 to 3 applies, let R = D.
  5. Then, if the weekday of the day with day index R is Sunday, Wednesday or Friday, add 1 to R (rule ADU).
- S(y) = R - 1373427.
- Year types: the number of days in year y, L = S(y + 1) - S(y), is one of 353, 354, 355, 383, 384, 385. A year with L equal to 353 or 383 is called DEFICIENT, one with 354 or 384 REGULAR, and one with 355 or 385 COMPLETE.
- Month lengths: in order of month number, a common year has 30, h, k, 29, 30, 29, 30, 29, 30, 29, 30, 29 and a leap year has 30, h, k, 29, 30, 30, 29, 30, 29, 30, 29, 30, 29. Here h is 30 in a COMPLETE year and 29 otherwise, and k is 29 in a DEFICIENT year and 30 otherwise.

* Month names
The month names of the year-month-day calendars, in order of month number, are as follows.
- GREGORIAN, JULIAN: January February March April May June July August September October November December
- ISLAMIC: Muharram Safar RabiI RabiII JumadaI JumadaII Rajab Shaban Ramadan Shawwal DhuAlQidah DhuAlHijjah
- HEBREW, common year: Tishri Heshvan Kislev Tevet Shevat Adar Nisan Iyyar Sivan Tammuz Av Elul
- HEBREW, leap year: Tishri Heshvan Kislev Tevet Shevat AdarI AdarII Nisan Iyyar Sivan Tammuz Av Elul
- PERSIAN: Farvardin Ordibehesht Khordad Tir Mordad Shahrivar Mehr Aban Azar Dey Bahman Esfand
- COPTIC: Thout Paopi Hathor Koiak Tobi Meshir Paremhat Parmouti Pashons Paoni Epip Mesori Nasie
- ETHIOPIC: Meskerem Tekemt Hedar Tahsas Ter Yekatit Megabit Miazia Genbot Sene Hamle Nehase Pagume
- FRENCH: Vendemiaire Brumaire Frimaire Nivose Pluviose Ventose Germinal Floreal Prairial Messidor Thermidor Fructidor Sansculottides

* ISO (ISO week calendar)
A day is represented by the triple of year y, week w and weekday number d. The ISO weekday number is 1 for Monday, 2 for Tuesday, ..., 7 for Sunday.
- Let T(y) = s - ((s - 1) mod 7), where s = S_G(y) + 3 and S_G is the S of GREGORIAN. s is the fixed day number of Gregorian year y, January 4, and T(y) is the fixed day number of the nearest Monday on or before that day (the fixed day number of the Monday of week 1 of year y).
- The number of weeks in year y, W(y) = (T(y + 1) - T(y)) / 7, is 52 or 53.
- The fixed day number of the day with weekday number d of week w of year y is T(y) + 7 * (w - 1) + (d - 1). Conversely, the day with fixed day number f belongs to the year y with T(y) <= f < T(y + 1).
- The first day of ISO year y is T(y), the number of days in year y is T(y + 1) - T(y), and a year with W(y) = 53 is a leap year.

* MAYAN (Mayan calendar)
A day is represented by the long count b.k.t.u.n (five integers, each at least 0). k, t and n are at most 19, u is at most 17, and b has no upper limit.
- The fixed day number of the day with long count b.k.t.u.n is c - 1137142, where c = (((b * 20 + k) * 20 + t) * 18 + u) * 20 + n.
- Conversely, for the day with fixed day number f, let c = f + 1137142; then b = floor(c / 144000), k = floor(c / 7200) mod 20, t = floor(c / 360) mod 20, u = floor(c / 20) mod 18, n = c mod 20.
- Tzolkin: the pair of the number (c + 3) mod 13 + 1 (1 to 13) and the ((c + 19) mod 20)-th name of table TZ.
- Haab: with h = (c + 348) mod 365, the pair of the day h mod 20 (0 to 19) and the floor(h / 20)-th name (month) of table HB. The month Uayeb has only days 0 to 4.
The tables are as follows, counted from 0.
- TZ: Imix Ik Akbal Kan Chicchan Cimi Manik Lamat Muluc Oc Chuen Eb Ben Ix Men Cib Caban Etznab Cauac Ahau
- HB: Pop Uo Zip Zotz Tzec Xul Yaxkin Mol Chen Yax Zac Ceh Mac Kankin Muan Pax Kayab Cumku Uayeb

* JD and FIXED
- JD (Julian Day Number): a day is represented by an integer J. The fixed day number of that day is J - 1721425.
- FIXED: a day is represented by its fixed day number itself.

* Date validation
A date argument is validated by the following steps. If a step fails, validation ends with the reason stated in that step (one of FORMAT, YEAR, MONTH, WEEK, DAY, FIELD).
- Year-month-day calendars: a date has the form "year-month-day".
  1. If splitting at "-" does not give exactly 3 parts with the first a digit string of 1 to 5 digits and the second and third digit strings of 1 to 2 digits, FORMAT.
  2. If the year is 0, YEAR.
  3. If the month is not at least 1 and at most the number of months of that year, MONTH.
  4. If the day is not at least 1 and at most the number of days of that month, DAY.
- ISO: a date has the form "year-Wweek-weekday number".
  1. If splitting at "-" does not give exactly 3 parts with the first a digit string of 1 to 5 digits, the second an uppercase letter W followed by a digit string of 1 to 2 digits, and the third a digit string of 1 digit, FORMAT.
  2. If the year is 0, YEAR.
  3. If the week is not at least 1 and at most W(year), WEEK.
  4. If the weekday number is not at least 1 and at most 7, DAY.
- MAYAN: a date has the form "b.k.t.u.n".
  1. If splitting at "." does not give exactly 5 parts, each a digit string of 1 to 2 digits, FORMAT.
  2. If any of k, t, n exceeds 19, or u exceeds 17, FIELD.
- JD, FIXED: a date is an integer.
  1. If it is not a digit string of 1 to 8 digits, optionally preceded by a single "-", FORMAT (-0 means 0).
Finally, compute the fixed day number of the date; if it is outside the supported range, validation fails with reason RANGE. If none of these applies, the date passes validation.
For example, GREGORIAN 2024-2-30 is DAY, 2024-02-29x is FORMAT, JULIAN 0001-01-02 is RANGE (fixed day number 0), HEBREW 5784-13-29 passes, and HEBREW 5785-13-01 is MONTH (5785 is a common year).

* Date format
To "write the day with fixed day number f in the date format of calendar C" means to build the following string.
- Year-month-day calendars: the year in 4 digits (padded with leading zeros if it has fewer than 4 digits, as is if it has 5 digits), "-", the month in 2 digits, "-", and the day in 2 digits, concatenated (example: 0233-13-05). However, if the day f is not representable in that calendar, it cannot be written.
- ISO: the year in at least 4 digits in the same way, "-W", the week in 2 digits, "-", and the weekday number in 1 digit, concatenated (example: 2021-W05-7).
- MAYAN: b, k, t, u, n joined with ".", the tzolkin number, the tzolkin name, the haab day and the haab month, in this order, separated by single spaces (example: 12.19.19.17.19 3 Cauac 2 Kankin). Numbers have no extra leading zeros.
- JD: J, and FIXED: f, each written in decimal.
A day in the supported range can be written in every calendar, except when it is not representable in a year-month-day calendar.

* Command processing steps
Each command is processed in the following order.
1. If the command name is none of those defined below, or the number of arguments differs from what that command defines, output INVALID COMMAND (this includes command names containing lowercase letters).
2. Check the arguments other than dates (calendar names, years, months, integers, weekday names, keywords, names); if any of them does not satisfy the condition defined for its position, output INVALID COMMAND. This includes a calendar name of a calendar that cannot be used with that command. However, for month arguments, only check here that the argument is a digit string of 1 to 2 digits (its value is checked in step 4).
3. Validate the date arguments from left to right as in "Date validation"; on failure, output OUT OF RANGE if the reason is RANGE and INVALID DATE otherwise (only the VALID command is defined differently).
4. Perform the command's own processing. If a condition defined in the command's description applies, output INVALID DATE, OUT OF RANGE or NONE as defined there.
In the descriptions below, cal, cal1, cal2 are calendar name arguments; date, date1, date2 are date arguments, each in the calendar of the calendar name immediately before it; y is a year argument, m a month argument, n an integer argument, and wd a weekday name argument. f, f1, f2 are the fixed day numbers of the days of date, date1, date2. "(all)" after a calendar name means that all 12 calendars can be used, and "(year-month-day)" means that only the year-month-day calendars can be used. When something cannot be written in a date format, output OUT OF RANGE unless stated otherwise.

* Conversion commands
- CONVERT cal date TO cal2 (cal and cal2: all): 4 arguments. The third argument must be TO. Output f written in the date format of cal2.
- ALL cal date (all): output the day f written in the 12 calendars, one per line, in the order GREGORIAN, JULIAN, ISO, ISLAMIC, HEBREW, PERSIAN, COPTIC, ETHIOPIC, FRENCH, MAYAN, JD, FIXED, 12 lines in total. Each line consists of the calendar name, a space, and the date in that calendar's date format, in this order; for a year-month-day calendar it is further followed by a space and the month name of that day. If the day cannot be written in that calendar, the line is "calendar-name OUT OF RANGE".
- VALID cal date (all): instead of step 3, validate date; if it passes, output YES and f written in the date format of cal, separated by a space. If it fails, output NO and the reason, separated by a space (examples: NO DAY, NO RANGE).
- WEEKDAY cal date (all): output the weekday name of the day f.
- INFO cal date (year-month-day): when the day f is year Y, month M, day d in the calendar cal, output it in the form "weekday-name d month-name Y day=x/L". Here x = f - S(Y) + 1 (the day of the year, counted from its beginning) and L = S(Y + 1) - S(Y). Numbers have no extra leading zeros. However, for FRENCH, instead of the weekday name, use the ((d - 1) mod 10)-th name of table DC if M is 1 to 12, and the (d - 1)-th name of table FS if M is 13 (counted from 0).
  DC: Primidi Duodi Tridi Quartidi Quintidi Sextidi Septidi Octidi Nonidi Decadi
  FS: Vertu Genie Travail Opinion Recompenses Revolution

* Year and month commands
- LEAP cal y (year-month-day and ISO): output YES if year y is a leap year and NO otherwise, and the number of days in year y, separated by a space. For HEBREW, further append a space and the year type (one of DEFICIENT, REGULAR, COMPLETE). No supported-range check is performed.
- MONTHS cal y (year-month-day): for each month of year y, build "month-name=number of days of that month", and output these in order of month number, separated by spaces. No supported-range check is performed.
- MONTHCAL cal y m (year-month-day): output the calendar of month m of year y. If the value of m is not at least 1 and at most the number of months of year y, INVALID DATE. Let F be the fixed day number of the first day of that month and E the fixed day number of its last day; if F < 1 or E > 3652059, OUT OF RANGE. Otherwise output the following lines.
  Line 1: the month name, a space, the value of y, a space, "(", F written in the GREGORIAN date format, " to " (with one space before and after), E written in the GREGORIAN date format, and ")", concatenated in this order.
  Line 2: Mo Tu We Th Fr Sa Su
  Line 3 onward: one line per week. Each line consists of 7 fields (2 characters each), from Monday to Sunday, separated by single spaces. For each day of the month, write its day number (1, 2, ...) right-aligned in the field of that day's weekday (a 1-digit number is preceded by one space). The first line is the week containing day 1, and the last line is the week containing the last day of the month. A field for a day not belonging to the month is two spaces, and trailing spaces are removed from each line (leading spaces are kept).
- NTH cal y m k wd (year-month-day): k must be a string exactly equal to one of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, LAST. The check of the value of m and F and E are the same as for MONTHCAL (but no supported-range check of F and E is performed). Let w be the weekday number of wd; if k is a digit, let x = F + ((w - F) mod 7) + 7 * (k - 1) (the day of the k-th wd of the month), and if it is LAST, let x = E - ((E - w) mod 7) (the day of the last wd of the month). If x > E, output NONE; otherwise, if x is outside the supported range, output OUT OF RANGE; otherwise output x written in the date format of cal.
- NEWYEAR cal y (year-month-day and ISO): if y > 9999, OUT OF RANGE. Find all the first days of the years Y (Y >= 1) of that calendar (S(Y) for a year-month-day calendar, T(Y) for ISO) that fall in Gregorian year y (fixed day number at least S_G(y) and less than S_G(y + 1)), and output "Y:date" for each, in increasing order of fixed day number, separated by spaces. Y is written without extra leading zeros, and date is that day written in the GREGORIAN date format. If there are none, output NONE.

* Day count commands
- DIFF cal1 date1 cal2 date2 (cal1 and cal2: all): output f2 - f1.
- COUNT cal date1 date2 wd (all): output the number of days whose weekday is wd among the days from the smaller of f1 and f2 to the larger (both ends inclusive).
- ADD cal date n unit (all): unit must be one of DAYS, WEEKS, MONTHS, YEARS. However, MONTHS can be used only with the year-month-day calendars, and YEARS only with the year-month-day calendars and ISO (a combination with any other calendar is INVALID COMMAND in step 2). Compute the resulting fixed day number g as follows.
  DAYS: g = f + n. WEEKS: g = f + 7 * n.
  MONTHS: g is the fixed day number of the day f "shifted by 0 years and n months". YEARS: g is the fixed day number of the day f "shifted by n years and 0 months" (for ISO, "shifted by n years"). Shifting is defined in "Year and month shifting".
  If the shift is out of bounds, if g is outside the supported range, or if g cannot be written in the date format of cal, output OUT OF RANGE. Otherwise output g written in the date format of cal.
- SPAN cal date1 date2 (year-month-day): compute the period between the two dates in years, months and days. If f1 <= f2, let a be the day of date1 and b = f2; otherwise let a be the day of date2 and b = f1. Let Y be the largest integer >= 0 such that the fixed day number of a shifted by Y years and 0 months is <= b, and let M be the largest integer >= 0 such that the fixed day number of a shifted by Y years and M months is <= b. D is b minus the fixed day number of a shifted by Y years and M months. Output in the form "Yy Mm Dd" (example: 1y 2m 3d), prefixed with "-" if f1 > f2 (example: -1y 2m 3d). The shifts in this computation are never out of bounds.

* Year and month shifting
The day obtained by "shifting by p years and q months" (p, q integers) the date year Y, month M, day d of a year-month-day calendar is found by the following steps.
1. Let Y' = Y + p. For calendars other than HEBREW, let M' = M. For HEBREW, if year Y' has a month with the same name as month M of year Y, let M' be that month. Otherwise, if that name is Adar (year Y' is then a leap year), let M' be the month AdarII, and if it is AdarI or AdarII (year Y' is then a common year), let M' be the month Adar.
2. Let A = K(Y') + M' - 1 + q. K(x) is the formula for the number of months before year x: for HEBREW, K(x) = B(x) = floor((235 * x - 234) / 19); for the other calendars, K(x) = c * (x - 1) (c is the number of months in a year of that calendar, 12 or 13).
3. Find the integer Y'' with K(Y'') <= A < K(Y'' + 1), and let M'' = A - K(Y'') + 1.
4. If Y'' is not at least 1 and at most 99999, the shift is "out of bounds". Otherwise the result is year Y'', month M'', day min(d, number of days of month M'' of year Y'').
The day obtained by "shifting by p years" the ISO day with year Y, week w, weekday number d is found with Y'' = Y + p: if Y'' is not at least 1 and at most 99999, the shift is out of bounds; otherwise it is the day with weekday number d of week min(w, W(Y'')) of year Y''.
For example, in GREGORIAN, 2024-01-31 shifted by 0 years and 1 month is 2024-02-29, and 2024-02-29 shifted by 1 year and 0 months is 2025-02-28. In HEBREW, AdarI 30 of year 5784 (a leap year) shifted by 1 year and 0 months is Adar 29 of year 5785 (a common year) (5785-06-29).

* Holiday commands
- EASTER y kind: kind must be GREGORIAN or JULIAN. If y > 9999, OUT OF RANGE. Find the Easter day of kind for year y as in "Easter computation", and output it written in the GREGORIAN date format (even when kind is JULIAN, output it as a Gregorian date).
- COMPUTUS y kind: the argument conditions and the OUT OF RANGE condition are the same as for EASTER. Output in the form "golden=G epact=e letters=L moon=P easter=E". G = (y mod 19) + 1, e and L are the e and the dominical letters of "Easter computation", and P and E are the full moon day and the Easter day written in the date format of the calendar kind.
- PASSOVER y: if y > 9999, OUT OF RANGE. Output Nisan 15 of HEBREW year y + 3760 (it lies within Gregorian year y) written in the GREGORIAN date format, and its weekday name, separated by a space.
- HOLIDAYS y: if y > 9999, OUT OF RANGE. Find all the days of the holidays in the table below that fall in Gregorian year y (fixed day number at least S_G(y) and less than S_G(y + 1)), and output one line per holiday in the form "date weekday-name holiday-name calendar-name calendar-date". date is that day written in the GREGORIAN date format, calendar-name is the calendar of the table, and calendar-date is that day written in the date format of the table's calendar. Lines are ordered by increasing fixed day number; for equal fixed day numbers, the one higher in the table comes first.
  Holidays 1 to 6 are days computed for year y and always fall in Gregorian year y. Holidays 7 to 31 are the table's day of the month with the table's name in each year Y (Y >= 1) of the table's calendar. All of them that fall in Gregorian year y are output (0, 1 or 2 occurrences in one year are all possible). In a year in which that day exceeds the number of days of that month (for example Sansculottides 6 in a common year), the holiday does not occur. Also, since a HEBREW leap year has no month named Adar, Purim in a leap year is AdarII 14.
  No. Holiday Calendar Day
  1 AshWednesday GREGORIAN 46 days before the GREGORIAN Easter of year y
  2 GoodFriday GREGORIAN 2 days before the GREGORIAN Easter of year y

* Easter computation
The Easter day of kind (GREGORIAN or JULIAN) for year y is found as follows. Let g = y mod 19.
- If kind is GREGORIAN: let c = floor(y / 100) + 1 and e0 = (14 + 11 * g - floor(3 * c / 4) + floor((5 + 8 * c) / 25)) mod 30. If e0 = 0, or if e0 = 1 and g > 10, let e = e0 + 1; otherwise let e = e0.
- If kind is JULIAN: let e = (14 + 11 * g) mod 30.
The fixed day number P of the full moon day is the fixed day number of April 19 of year y in the calendar kind, minus e. The fixed day number E of Easter day is the fixed day number of the first Sunday after P (P not included), that is, E = P + 7 - (P mod 7).
Dominical letters: let s = (-S_k(y)) mod 7 (S_k is the S of the calendar kind). The dominical letter is the s-th character (counted from 0) of the string ABCDEFG. If year y is a leap year in the calendar kind, it is further followed by the ((s - 1) mod 7)-th character (when the letters A, B, ..., G, A, ... are assigned to the days in order from January 1, this is the letter of the Sundays; in a leap year the letters shift by one after February 29, so there are two letters).

* Hebrew and Mayan commands
- MOLAD y: as in "Hebrew calendar computation", find D, t and S(y) for year y, and the rules applied in the postponement steps. If S(y) is outside the supported range, OUT OF RANGE. Otherwise output in the form "weekday-name-1 Hh Pp rules weekday-name-2 date". weekday-name-1 is the weekday name of the day with day index D, H = floor(t / 1080), and P = t mod 1080. rules is the names of the rules applied in steps 1 to 5, joined with "+" in the order they were applied (NONE if no rule was applied). weekday-name-2 is the weekday name of the day S(y), and date is S(y) written in the GREGORIAN date format.
- ROUND cal date num name day month (all): num must be a digit string of 1 to 2 digits with a value at least 1 and at most 13, name one of the names in table TZ, day a digit string of 1 to 2 digits, and month one of the names in table HB. Moreover, the value of day must be at most 4 if month is Uayeb, and at most 19 otherwise. Let g be the smallest fixed day number at least f and at most f + 18979 whose tzolkin is (the value of num, name) and whose haab is (the value of day, month). If no such fixed day number exists, output NONE (this combination never occurs on any day); if g > 3652059, output OUT OF RANGE; otherwise output g written in the date format of cal.

[Output format]
For each command, ALL outputs 12 lines, MONTHCAL 3 or more lines, HOLIDAYS 1 or more lines, and every other command 1 line. However, when outputting INVALID COMMAND, INVALID DATE or OUT OF RANGE, any command outputs only that single line.
Every line ends with a newline and has no trailing spaces. When a line contains several items, they are separated by single spaces. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, with "-" for negative numbers.
Example lines for each command:
- CONVERT: 5785-01-01, 2024-W40-4, 13.0.0.0.0 4 Ahau 3 Kankin, 2460587, etc.
- ALL: 12 lines such as GREGORIAN 2024-10-03 October, ISO 2024-W40-4, ISLAMIC OUT OF RANGE
- VALID: YES 2024-02-29, NO MONTH, etc.
- WEEKDAY: Thursday
- INFO: Thursday 1 Tishri 5785 day=1/355, Primidi 11 Vendemiaire 233 day=11/365, etc.
- LEAP: YES 366, NO 364, YES 385 COMPLETE, etc.
- MONTHS: one line such as Thout=30 Paopi=30 ... (13 items) ... Nasie=6
- MONTHCAL: the line with the month name and the period, the weekday header line, and the week lines
- NTH, ADD: a date written in a date format (NTH can also output NONE)
- NEWYEAR: 1429:2008-01-10 1430:2008-12-29, or NONE, etc.
- DIFF, COUNT: 13, -5, 0, etc.
- SPAN: 23y 11m 30d, -0y 0m 7d, etc.
- EASTER: 2025-04-20
- COMPUTUS: golden=12 epact=6 letters=E moon=2025-04-13 easter=2025-04-20, etc.
- PASSOVER: 2025-04-13 Sunday
- HOLIDAYS: lines such as 2025-03-31 Monday EidAlFitr ISLAMIC 1446-10-01
- MOLAD: Thursday 9h 391p NONE Thursday 2024-10-03, etc.
- ROUND: a date written in a date format, or NONE

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500. The first line is a decimal integer without extra leading zeros.
- Each input line consists only of printable ASCII characters and spaces, is at most 100 characters long, and ends with a newline (LF).
- A command line contains at least one word, and words are separated by single spaces. There are no leading or trailing spaces, no consecutive spaces, and no empty lines.
- Command names and arguments are not guaranteed to be valid. Invalid commands are processed according to the rules above.
- The absolute value of every integer that appears during the computation is less than 2^53 when computed exactly as in the formulas above. However, for example, the Hebrew Q can exceed 9 * 10^11 (LEAP HEBREW 99999 computes S(100000)), so 32-bit integers are not enough.
