[Overview]
Write a program that computes a company's biweekly payroll. It reads commands one line at a time from standard input and, for each command, writes a result in a specified format to standard output.
All monetary amounts are handled as integers counted in cents (units of 1/100), and no floating-point numbers are used. There are three employee pay types: hourly (HOURLY), salaried (SALARIED) and commission (COMMISSION). For hourly employees, the program computes from the shift records the overtime beyond 8 hours a day, double pay on Sundays and holidays, overtime beyond 40 hours a week, the night premium and paid time off; for salaried employees it deducts unpaid leave; for commission employees it applies tiered commission rates to the period's sales.
In a payroll run (RUN), the program subtracts from gross pay, in order, the retirement contribution, the health premium, income tax (a progressive tax table given in the input), a flat-rate income tax on bonuses, state tax, social security tax (with an annual wage cap), Medicare tax (with an additional rate) and garnishments (with a cap) to obtain net pay, prints a pay stub for each employee, and updates the year-to-date totals. In addition, it handles employer contribution calculation, stub previews and reprints, hours breakdowns, year-to-date totals, journal entries, per-department summaries, rankings by pay, and so on.

[Input format]
The first line contains N, the number of command lines that follow. The N lines starting from line 2 each contain one command. Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by single spaces, with no whitespace at the start or end of the line. The program ends after processing all N command lines.

The token formats are as follows.
- Employee id: an integer from 1 to 9999, written without extra leading zeros.
- Department code: 1 to 8 characters consisting only of uppercase English letters (A-Z) (e.g. ENG, SALES).
- Pay type: one of HOURLY, SALARIED, COMMISSION.
- Amount: an integer part and a fractional part joined by ".". The integer part is a decimal number from 0 to 999999 without extra leading zeros. The fractional part has exactly 2 digits (e.g. 0.07, 32.50, 91000.00). An amount "a.bc" represents the integer a*100+bc counted in cents.
- Rate: the same format as an amount, except that the integer part is from 0 to 999. A rate "a.bc" means a.bc percent, and below it is treated as the integer a*100+bc in units of 1/10000 (e.g. 6.20 is 620, 100.00 is 10000).
- Clock time: 5 characters consisting of 2 digits, ":", and 2 digits, in this order (e.g. 08:00, 23:59). Let the number represented by the first 2 digits be the hour h and the number represented by the last 2 digits be the minute m. It is a "valid clock time" only when h is at most 23 and m is at most 59, and it then represents the number of minutes elapsed since 0:00, h*60+m (for example, 24:00 and 12:60 are invalid clock times).
- Duration: the same 5-character format as a clock time, representing a length of h hours m minutes (which durations are accepted is described under PTO).
- Setting name: 1 to 12 characters consisting only of uppercase English letters.
- Other numbers (day, period number, allowance count, k): non-negative decimal integers without extra leading zeros.

The commands are the following 27 kinds. Each part in < > is a token of the format above. The value of SET is in rate format if the setting name is a rate setting (described later), and in amount format otherwise.

[Processing]
Below, floor(x) denotes the largest integer not exceeding x. Every value whose floor is taken in this problem is at least 0.

== Calendar and pay periods ==
- A day is a serial number within the year; days from 1 to 364 are valid. Day 1 is a Monday. The weekday of day d is Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun when (d-1) mod 7 is 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 respectively. That is, a day with d mod 7 = 0 is a Sunday, and a day with d mod 7 = 6 is a Saturday.
- Week w (1 <= w <= 52) is the 7 days from day 7w-6 to day 7w (Monday through Sunday).
- Pay period p (1 <= p <= 26, below "period p") is the 14 days from day 14p-13 to day 14p, consisting of week 2p-1 and week 2p. The number of the period that day d belongs to is floor((d-1)/14)+1.
- The "number of closed periods" is initially 0 and increases by 1 each time RUN succeeds. A day whose period number is at most the number of closed periods is called a "closed day".

== State and initial state ==
- Settings: there are the following 15 settings. Each line is "setting name, kind, initial value, meaning". A setting of kind "rate" is called a "rate setting", and a setting of kind "amount" is called an "amount setting".
  STATE, rate, 5.00, state tax rate
  SUPP, rate, 22.00, supplemental (bonus) income tax rate
  SSRATE, rate, 6.20, social security tax rate
  MEDRATE, rate, 1.45, Medicare tax rate
  ADDMED, rate, 0.90, additional Medicare tax rate
  UIRATE, rate, 2.70, unemployment insurance tax rate
  MATCH, rate, 50.00, retirement matching rate
  MATCHMAX, rate, 6.00, upper limit on the share of gross pay that is eligible for matching
  GARNCAP, rate, 25.00, garnishment cap rate
  SSBASE, amount, 168600.00, annual wage cap for social security tax
  ADDBASE, amount, 200000.00, annual amount at which additional Medicare tax begins to apply
  UIBASE, amount, 7000.00, annual wage cap for unemployment insurance tax
  RETIRECAP, amount, 23000.00, annual limit on retirement contributions
  ALLOWANCE, amount, 150.00, income tax deduction per allowance
  NIGHT, amount, 1.00, night premium (per hour)
- Tax table and commission table: both are collections of (lower bound, rate) pairs and are initially empty. Within one table there is at most one pair with a given lower bound. The tax table is changed by BRACKET, and the commission table by TIER.
- Holidays: a set of days, initially empty. Days are added by HOLIDAY.
- Employees: initially there are none. Employees are registered by EMP. Each employee has the following information (the values at registration are in parentheses).
  Employee id, department (the department code of EMP), pay type, base amount (the amount of EMP; it is the hourly rate for hourly employees, the annual salary for salaried employees, and the fixed pay per period for commission employees), employment status (active; TERM makes it terminated), retirement rate (0), health premium (0), allowance count (0), extra withholding (0), list of garnishment orders (empty), pending bonus (0), PTO balance (0 minutes).
  Shift records (empty): a collection of shifts per day. One shift is a pair of a start time s and an end time t (s < t) and represents the time span of elapsed minutes that are at least s and less than t (t-s minutes). The time spans of shifts on the same day do not overlap.
  PTO records (empty): the length (in minutes) of paid time off per day. There is at most one per day.
  Leave records (empty): a set of days of unpaid leave.
  Sales records (empty): the total sales per period. The total sales of a period with no record is 0.
  Stub records (empty): for each period, the pay stub (described later) for that period and the department at the time that pay stub was created.
  Year-to-date totals (all 0): the sums of the values of the pay stubs created so far, and the number of pay stubs.
- Garnishment order: a pair of a rate and a balance. Orders are created per employee by GARNISH and are given order numbers 1, 2, 3, ... per employee in the order of creation.
A command that results in an error does not change the state at all; it only prints one error line.

== Progressive calculation ==
The value of the "progressive calculation" of a table (the tax table or the commission table) and an amount x is obtained as follows. Let the pairs of the table in increasing order of lower bound be (b1, r1), (b2, r2), ..., (bk, rk), and let b(k+1) be infinity. Let T be the sum of ri*(min(x, b(i+1))-bi) over all i with bi < x; the value is floor(T/10000). If the table is empty, the value is 0. In other words, the part of x above bi and at most b(i+1) is multiplied by the rate ri, these are summed, and the total is rounded down only once at the end. No rate applies to the part of x at or below b1.

== Classification of hours (hourly) ==
For an hourly employee and a day d, the following values (all in minutes) are called the "daily breakdown".
- Worked time m: the sum of (t-s) over the shifts on day d.
- Night time n: for each shift on day d, count the elapsed minutes u contained in its time span, one per minute (s <= u < t), that satisfy 0 <= u < 360 (00:00 to 06:00) or 1320 <= u < 1440 (22:00 to 24:00), and sum these counts. For example, a shift from 21:00 to 23:59 has 119 minutes of night time, and a shift from 04:00 to 12:00 has 120 minutes of night time.
- PTO time f: the length of the PTO record of day d (0 if there is no record).
- When day d is a Sunday or a holiday, regular time r = 0, overtime o = 0 and double time x = m.
- Otherwise, r = min(m, 480), o = min(max(m-480, 0), 240), x = max(m-720, 0). That is, of a day's work, up to 8 hours is regular, the part beyond 8 hours up to 12 hours is overtime, and the part beyond 12 hours is double time.
The "weekly breakdown" of week w is as follows. Let rw be the sum of r over the 7 days of that week, and let the weekly overtime z = max(0, rw-2400). The weekly regular time is rw-z, the weekly overtime is (the sum of o)+z, and the weekly double time, night time and PTO time are the sums of x, n and f respectively. That is, only regular time beyond 40 hours a week turns into overtime. Time that already carries a premium (overtime and double time) and PTO time are not counted toward the 40 hours a week.
The "period breakdown" of period p is the sum of the weekly breakdowns of week 2p-1 and week 2p, written in order as regular time R, overtime O, double time D, night time N and PTO time P.

== Pay stub calculation ==
For an employee and a period p, the "pay stub" (below "stub") is obtained by the following steps. RUN and PREVIEW use these steps. The settings, the tables, the holidays, all records and the employee's information are all taken as of the time of the calculation. "YTD X" denotes the X value of the year-to-date totals as of the time of the calculation.

(1) Base pay E
- Hourly: from the period breakdown R, O, D, N, P of period p, E = floor(((2*(R+P)+3*O+4*D)*hourly rate+2*N*NIGHT)/120). That is, regular time and PTO time are paid 1x the hourly rate, overtime 1.5x and double time 2x; night time additionally earns NIGHT per hour; and the total is rounded down only once at the end.
- Salaried: if active, let the base salary S = floor(annual salary/26), the leave days k = the number of days in the leave records that belong to period p and are not holidays, and the leave deduction K = k*floor(annual salary/260); then E = S-K. If terminated, S, k, K and E are all 0.
- Commission: let the sales X = the total sales of period p in the sales records, and the commission C = the value of the progressive calculation of the commission table and X; then E = C+(the fixed pay if active, 0 if terminated).
(2) Bonus and gross pay
Let the bonus B = the pending bonus, and the gross pay G = E+B. If G = 0, this employee has "no pay" for period p, and no stub is created.
(3) Employee deductions and net pay
Starting with the remaining amount L = G, determine the deductions in the order (a) to (h) below. For (a) to (g), first compute the value of the formula; if it is greater than L, replace it with L; the result is the deduction, and the deduction is subtracted from L. The names such as retirement, health and federal that appear in the formulas below and in the stub denote the deductions after being limited by L (for example, when the health premium is greater than L, health is not the health premium but the value of L at that time).
  (a) Retirement contribution retirement: min(floor(G*retirement rate/10000), max(0, RETIRECAP-YTD retirement)).
  (b) Health premium health: the employee's health premium.
  Here, let the taxable wages W be the L after (b). Also let Q = max(0, E-retirement-health) and A = max(0, Q-allowance count*ALLOWANCE). Q is the taxable wages of the base pay part, and W-Q is the taxable wages of the bonus part.
  (c) Income tax federal: (the value of the progressive calculation of the tax table and A)+extra withholding.
  (d) Supplemental income tax supplemental: floor((W-Q)*SUPP/10000).
  (e) State tax state: floor(W*STATE/10000).
  Here, let the Medicare wages F = G-health, the social security wages Y = min(F, max(0, SSBASE-YTD Y)), and the additional amount H = max(0, min(F, YTD F+F-ADDBASE)).
  (f) Social security tax social-security: floor(Y*SSRATE/10000).
  (g) Medicare tax medicare: floor(F*MEDRATE/10000)+floor(H*ADDMED/10000).
  (h) Garnishment garnishment: let the disposable amount V be the L at this point, and let the cap U = floor(V*GARNCAP/10000). Examine the garnishment orders one at a time in increasing order of order number; the garnished amount of each order is min(floor(V*that order's rate/10000), that order's balance, U-the sum of the garnished amounts determined so far), and that order's balance minus its garnished amount is that order's "post-stub balance". garnishment is the sum of the garnished amounts of all orders (0 if there are no garnishment orders).
Let the net pay net = L-garnishment.
(4) Employer contributions
Employer contributions are not subtracted from the payment to the employee; they are computed by the following formulas (they are not limited by the remaining amount L either).
- Employer social security tax: floor(Y*SSRATE/10000).
- Employer Medicare tax: floor(F*MEDRATE/10000) (the additional Medicare tax is not included).
- Unemployment insurance tax unemployment: with the unemployment wages J = min(F, max(0, UIBASE-YTD J)), floor(J*UIRATE/10000).
- Matching match: floor(min(retirement, floor(G*MATCHMAX/10000))*MATCH/10000).
The sum of these four is called the "total employer contribution".
(5) PTO accrual (hourly only)
Let the accrued time a = min(960-PTO balance, floor((R+O+D)/20)), and call the PTO balance+a the "post-stub PTO balance". That is, 1 minute of paid time off is granted for every 20 minutes worked, and the PTO balance does not exceed 960 minutes (16 hours). For pay types other than hourly, a is 0.

A stub consists of the following values computed above: R, O, D, N, P, a for hourly; S, k, K for salaried; X, C for commission (these are 0 for the other pay types). For all pay types: E, B, G, retirement, health, federal, supplemental, state, social-security, medicare, garnishment, net, employer social security tax, employer Medicare tax, unemployment, match, total employer contribution, W, Y, F, J. In addition, the stub also includes the sum of the post-stub balances of all garnishment orders (the "post-stub garnishment balance") and the post-stub PTO balance.
The year-to-date totals consist of the sums of each value of the stubs, except the post-stub garnishment balance and the post-stub PTO balance, and the number of stubs.

== Processing of each command ==
Errors are checked in the order written, and only the first error that applies is printed. The text of the error lines is given in [Output format]. Frequently used checks are named as follows.
- Active check: (1) no employee with that employee id is registered: unknown employee, (2) that employee is terminated: terminated employee.
- Registration check: performs only (1) of the active check.
- Day check: (1) the day is not between 1 and 364 inclusive: invalid day, (2) the day is a closed day: period closed.
- Rate check: the rate is greater than 10000 (100.00): invalid rate.
- Amount check: the amount is 0 (0.00): invalid amount.

1. SET n v: (a) n is none of the 15 setting names: unknown setting, (b) n is a rate setting and v fails the rate check: invalid rate. If there is no error, set the value of setting n to v.
2. BRACKET b r: perform the rate check on r. If there is no error, then if the tax table has a pair with lower bound b, replace its rate with r; otherwise add the pair (b, r).
3. TIER b r: perform the same processing as BRACKET on the commission table.
4. EMP i g c v: (a) employee i is already registered (including terminated): duplicate employee, (b) the amount check on v. If there is no error, register the employee.
5. RATE i v: perform the active check and the amount check on v. If there is no error, set the base amount to v. The new base amount is used for stubs computed from then on.
6. DEPT i g: perform the active check; then, if g is the same as the current department, it is a same department error. If there is no error, set the department to g.
7. BENEFIT i r h: perform the active check and the rate check on r. If there is no error, set the retirement rate to r and the health premium to h.
8. W4 i c x: perform the active check. If there is no error, set the allowance count to c and the extra withholding to x.
9. GARNISH i r v: perform the active check, the rate check on r and the amount check on v. If there is no error, create a garnishment order with rate r and balance v and append it to the end of that employee's list of garnishment orders. Orders whose balance has become 0 also remain in the list.
10. TERM i: perform the active check. If there is no error, make the employee terminated. Recorded shifts, PTO, sales and the pending bonus are still paid in RUNs after termination, according to the rules of the stub calculation.
11. HOLIDAY d: perform the day check; then, if d is already a holiday, it is a duplicate holiday error. If there is no error, add d to the holidays.
12. TIME i d s t: check in the following order. (a)(b) the active check, (c) the employee is not hourly: not hourly, (d)(e) the day check on d, (f) s is an invalid clock time: invalid time, (g) t is an invalid clock time: invalid time, (h) s >= t: invalid shift, (i) there is an existing shift (s', t') on day d with s < t' and s' < t (one whose time span overlaps): overlapping shift, (j) the sum of (t'-s') over the existing shifts on day d plus t-s is greater than 960 (16 hours): daily limit. For (i), the error line shows the overlapping shift with the earliest start time. A shift whose end time merely equals the start time of another shift does not overlap it. If there is no error, add the shift (s, t) to the shift records of day d.
13. UNTIME i d s: check in the following order. (a)(b) the active check, (c) the employee is not hourly: not hourly, (d)(e) the day check on d, (f) s is an invalid clock time: invalid time, (g) day d has no shift with start time s: no shift. If there is no error, remove that shift from the shift records.
14. PTO i d h: let hh be the number represented by the first 2 digits of the duration h and mm the number represented by the last 2 digits, and let the length len = hh*60+mm (minutes). Check in the following order. (a)(b) the active check, (c) the employee is not hourly: not hourly, (d)(e) the day check on d, (f) mm is greater than 59, or len is not between 1 and 480 inclusive: invalid duration, (g) day d already has a PTO record: duplicate pto, (h) len is greater than the PTO balance: insufficient pto. If there is no error, set the PTO record of day d to len and subtract len from the PTO balance. PTO time is counted separately from worked time and is not included in the daily limit calculation either.
15. LEAVE i d: check in the following order. (a)(b) the active check, (c) the employee is not salaried: not salaried, (d)(e) the day check on d, (f) d is a Saturday, a Sunday or a holiday: not workday, (g) d is already in the leave records: duplicate leave. If there is no error, add d to the leave records. If d later becomes a holiday, d is not counted in the leave days (as stated in step (1) of the stub calculation).
16. SALES i d v: check in the following order. (a)(b) the active check, (c) the employee is not commission: not commission, (d)(e) the day check on d, (f) the amount check on v. If there is no error, add v to the total sales of the period that d belongs to.
17. BONUS i v: perform the active check and the amount check on v. If there is no error, add v to the pending bonus.
18. HOURS i p: (a) the registration check, (b) the employee is not hourly: not hourly, (c) p is not between 1 and 26 inclusive: invalid period. The state is not changed (terminated employees may also be specified).
19. RUN p: if p is not equal to (the number of closed periods+1), or p is greater than 26, it is an invalid period error. If there is no error, set the number of closed periods to p, and compute the stub of period p for every registered employee (including terminated ones) in increasing order of employee id. For an employee with no pay, do nothing. Otherwise, print the stub and make the following updates: set the pending bonus to 0, set the PTO balance to the post-stub PTO balance, set the balance of each garnishment order to that order's post-stub balance, add each value of the stub to the year-to-date totals and increase the number of stubs by 1, and record the stub and the current department under period p in the stub records.
20. PREVIEW i: perform the registration check. The state is not changed. With period p = the number of closed periods+1, compute the stub of period p from the current state, using the same steps as RUN.
21. REPRINT i p: (a) the registration check, (b) p is not between 1 and 26 inclusive: invalid period, (c) the stub records have no stub for period p: no stub. The state is not changed.
22. YTD i: perform the registration check. The state is not changed.
23. JOURNAL p: if p is not at least 1 and at most the number of closed periods, it is a no run error. The state is not changed.
24. REPORT, TOP k, EMPLOYEES, CONFIG: there are no errors. The state is not changed.

[Output format]
For each command, print one or more lines in the following formats. Text inside double quotes is written literally as given, in ASCII characters, and each part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value (the "->" arrow is literal text, not part of a placeholder). Except for indentation, every space between tokens is a single space. "(indent 2)" means placing 2 spaces at the start of the line, and "(indent 4)" means placing 4 spaces at the start of the line. The output consists only of ASCII characters, no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No blank lines are printed.

== Number notation ==
- Amount notation: for a number of cents c (at least 0), the integer part of c/100 (a decimal number without extra leading zeros), ".", and c mod 100 as 2 digits (with a leading 0 when it is a single digit), in this order (e.g. 0.07, 32.50, 16860.00).
- Rate notation: for a rate r (an integer in units of 1/10000), the integer part of r/100 (a decimal number without extra leading zeros), ".", r mod 100 as 2 digits, and "%", in this order (e.g. 6.20%, 0.00%, 100.00%).
- Duration notation: for a number of minutes m, the integer part of m/60 (a decimal number without extra leading zeros), ":", and m mod 60 as 2 digits, in this order (e.g. 0:00, 8:30, 310:00).
- Clock time notation: for a number of elapsed minutes u, the integer part of u/60 as 2 digits (with a leading 0 when it is a single digit), ":", and u mod 60 as 2 digits, in this order (e.g. 08:00, 23:59).
- Employment status: "active" if active, "terminated" if terminated.
Below, unless stated otherwise, amounts are written in amount notation, rates in rate notation, durations (numbers of minutes) in duration notation, and clock times in clock time notation. Integers such as days, period numbers, week numbers, numbers of people, counts and allowance counts are written as decimal numbers without extra leading zeros.

== Stub block ==
The following lines, in this order, representing the contents of a stub or of the year-to-date totals, are called a "stub block". The indentation of each line is specified for each place where it is used.
- Only for hourly: "hours reg <R> ot <O> dbl <D> night <N> pto <P>"
- Only for hourly: "pto accrued <a> balance <PTO balance>"
- Only for salaried: "salary <S> leave <k> deduction <K>"
- Only for commission: "sales <X> commission <C>"
- "earnings <E>"
- "bonus <B>"
- "gross <G>"
- "retirement <retirement>"
- "health <health>"
- "federal <federal>"
- "supplemental <supplemental>"
- "state <state>"
- "social-security <social-security>"
- "medicare <medicare>"
- "garnishment <garnishment> balance <garnishment balance>"
- "net <net>"
- "employer social-security <employer social security tax> medicare <employer Medicare tax> unemployment <unemployment> match <match>"
In the stub block of a stub, each value is the stub's value, the PTO balance is the post-stub PTO balance, and the garnishment balance is the post-stub garnishment balance. In the stub block of the year-to-date totals, each value is the year-to-date total's value (k is the total number of leave days), the PTO balance is the current PTO balance, and the garnishment balance is the sum of the current balances of all garnishment orders.
"Printing a stub" means printing the line (indent 2) "STUB <employee id> <department> <pay type> period <period number>" followed by that stub's stub block with (indent 4). The department is the current department for RUN and PREVIEW, and the department in the stub records for REPRINT.

== Output of each command (on success) ==
- SET: "SET <setting name> <old value> -> <new value>". The values are written in rate notation for a rate setting and in amount notation for an amount setting.
- BRACKET: "BRACKET <lower bound> <rate> added" if a new pair was added, and "BRACKET <lower bound> <rate> replaced" if the rate was replaced.
- TIER: the same format as BRACKET, with "TIER" as the first word.
- EMP: "EMP <employee id> <department code> <pay type> <base amount>"
- RATE: "RATE <employee id> <old base amount> -> <new base amount>"
- DEPT: "DEPT <employee id> <old department> -> <new department>"
- BENEFIT: "BENEFIT <employee id> retirement <retirement rate> health <health premium>"
- W4: "W4 <employee id> allowances <allowance count> extra <extra withholding>"
- GARNISH: "GARNISH <employee id> order <order number> <rate> balance <total>"
- TERM: "TERM <employee id>"
- HOLIDAY: "HOLIDAY <day> <weekday>"
- TIME: "TIME <employee id> day <day> <start time>-<end time> <shift length t-s>"
- UNTIME: "UNTIME <employee id> day <day> <start time of the removed shift>-<end time of the removed shift>"
- PTO: "PTO <employee id> day <day> <len> balance <PTO balance after subtracting len>"
- LEAVE: "LEAVE <employee id> day <day> <weekday>"
- SALES: "SALES <employee id> day <day> <amount> period <number of the period the day belongs to> total <total sales of that period after adding>"
- BONUS: "BONUS <employee id> <amount> pending <pending bonus after adding>"
- HOURS: The first line is "HOURS <employee id> period <p>". Then, for each day of period p that has at least one shift or a PTO record, one at a time in increasing order of day, print (indent 2) "day <day> <weekday> <shift list> total <m> reg <r> ot <o> dbl <x> night <n> pto <f>" (m, r, o, x, n, f are that day's daily breakdown). If the day is a holiday, add the single word "holiday" right after the weekday. The shift list is that day's shifts in increasing order of start time, each in the form "<start time>-<end time>", separated by single spaces (for a day with no shifts and only a PTO record, the shift list part is not written, and "total" follows right after the weekday (or holiday)). If there is no such day, print instead one line (indent 2) "(none)". Then, for week 2p-1 and then week 2p, print (indent 2) "week <week number> reg <weekly regular time> ot <weekly overtime> dbl <weekly double time> night <weekly night time> pto <weekly PTO time> weekly-ot <z>". Finally, print (indent 2) "total reg <R> ot <O> dbl <D> night <N> pto <P>".
- RUN: The first line is "RUN <p> days <14p-13>-<14p>". Then, for each employee for whom a stub was created, in increasing order of employee id, print the stub. Finally, print (indent 2) "TOTAL paid <number of employees for whom a stub was created> skipped <number of active employees with no pay> gross <sum of G> deductions <sum of G-net> net <sum of net> employer <sum of total employer contribution>". Terminated employees with no pay are not counted in skipped.
- PREVIEW: The first line is "PREVIEW <employee id> period <p>". Then, if there is no pay, print (indent 2) "no pay"; otherwise, print the computed stub.
- REPRINT: The first line is "REPRINT <employee id> <p>". Then print the stub of period p in the stub records.
- YTD: The first line is "YTD <employee id> <current department> <pay type> <employment status> periods <number of stubs>". Then print the stub block of the year-to-date totals with (indent 2), and finally print (indent 2) "wages federal <YTD W> social-security <YTD Y> medicare <YTD F> unemployment <YTD J>" and (indent 2) "retirement-room <max(0, RETIRECAP-YTD retirement)> pending-bonus <pending bonus>", in this order.
- JOURNAL: For all employees for whom a stub of period p was created, sum the values of the stubs of period p in the stub records, and print the following lines using those sums. The first line is "JOURNAL <p> stubs <number of those stubs>". Then print the following 11 lines with (indent 2).
  "debit wages <G>"
  "debit employer-tax <employer social security tax+employer Medicare tax+unemployment>"
  "debit retirement-match <match>"
  "credit retirement <retirement+match>"
  "credit health <health>"
  "credit federal <federal+supplemental>"
  "credit state <state>"
  "credit fica <social-security+medicare+employer social security tax+employer Medicare tax>"
  "credit unemployment <unemployment>"
  "credit garnishment <garnishment>"
  "credit net-pay <net>"
Finally, print (indent 2) "total debit <sum of the values of the 3 debit lines> credit <sum of the values of the 8 credit lines>".
- REPORT: The first line is "REPORT period <number of closed periods>". All stubs in the stub records of all employees are summed per recorded department. For each department with at least one stub, one at a time in ascending order of department code (character code order), print (indent 2) "<department code> stubs <number of stubs> gross <G> pretax <retirement+health> taxes <federal+supplemental+state+social-security+medicare> garnishment <garnishment> net <net> employer <total employer contribution>" (each value is the sum over that department's stubs). Finally, print the sum over all stubs in the same format, with "total" written in place of the department code. The total line is printed even if there are no stubs at all.
- TOP: The first line is "TOP <k>". Sort the employees with at least 1 stub in decreasing order of YTD G, and, when G is equal, in increasing order of employee id. Print the first k of them (all of them if there are fewer than k) in order as (indent 2) "<rank> <employee id> <current department> gross <YTD G> net <YTD net>". The ranks are 1, 2, 3, ... from the first.
- EMPLOYEES: The first line is "EMPLOYEES". Then, for each registered employee, one at a time in increasing order of employee id, print (indent 2) "<employee id> <department> <pay type> <base amount> <employment status> retirement <retirement rate> health <health premium> allowances <allowance count> extra <extra withholding> bonus <pending bonus>", immediately followed by that employee's garnishment orders, one at a time in increasing order of order number, as (indent 4) "garnish <order number> <rate> balance <balance>".
- CONFIG: The first line is "CONFIG". Then print the 15 settings in the order of the table in [Processing] (STATE through NIGHT) as (indent 2) "<setting name> <value>" (the value is in rate notation for a rate setting and in amount notation for an amount setting). Next, print the pairs of the tax table in increasing order of lower bound as (indent 2) "bracket <lower bound> <rate>", and finally the pairs of the commission table in increasing order of lower bound as (indent 2) "tier <lower bound> <rate>".
For TOP and EMPLOYEES, if there are no lines to print after the first line, print instead one line (indent 2) "(none)".

== Error lines ==

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 1000.
- The input correctly follows the formats in [Input format] (there are no unknown commands, wrong numbers of tokens, or tokens that do not match their format). The only errors to detect are those listed in [Processing].
- The values of day and period number tokens are between 0 and 1000 inclusive, allowance counts are between 0 and 99 inclusive, and k of TOP is between 1 and 100 inclusive.
- At most 50 employees are registered, each employee has at most 10 garnishment orders, and the tax table and the commission table each have at most 20 pairs.
- PREVIEW is not given when the number of closed periods is 26.
- When computed according to the rules above, the absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values, is less than 2^53 (computing with 64-bit signed integers does not overflow).
