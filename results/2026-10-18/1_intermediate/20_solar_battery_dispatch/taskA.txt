[Overview]
Write a program that simulates, hour by hour, the flow of electricity in a house equipped with rooftop solar (PV) generation and a home battery. The electricity tariff is time-of-use, and the division of the day into time periods differs between weekdays and weekends. For each battery operating strategy (self-consumption first, charging and discharging according to the time-of-use tariff, peak shaving, outage preparedness, and exporting at peak times), the program determines the hourly charge and discharge, the import from the grid and export to the grid, and the curtailment of generation, and calculates the electricity bill for the whole period. Energy is handled as integers in Wh and money as integers in cents; floating-point numbers are not used.
The program first reads the battery and grid settings, the tariff prices and time-period divisions, and the hourly load and generation data. It then reads commands one line at a time, processes them in order, and writes the results to standard output. The commands run simulations with a specified strategy, change settings and prices, display and aggregate various results (hourly and daily tables, the bill, savings, totals by tariff period and by day type, import peaks, averages by hour of day, energy flows, self-sufficiency, charge/discharge cycle counts, how the battery is used, and the distribution and chart of the stored energy), compare with the case without a battery, compare strategies, select a battery capacity, run trial calculations while varying a setting, and evaluate the impact of a power outage.

[Input format]
The input consists of lines in the following order. Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by single ASCII spaces, with no spaces at the beginning or end of the line. In the description below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes. The symbols < > themselves never appear in the input or the output.

Line 1 (battery settings):
BATTERY <capacity> <initial> <charge_max> <discharge_max> <charge_eff> <discharge_eff> <reserve> <battery_cost>
Line 2 (grid settings):
GRID <export_max> <feedin_price> <fixed_charge> <demand_price>
Line 3:
PERIODS <k>
The next k lines (tariff periods): each line has the following form.
<period_name> <price>
The next 2 lines (time-period divisions):
WEEKDAY <hour_1> <period_1> <hour_2> <period_2> ... <hour_j> <period_j>
WEEKEND <hour_1> <period_1> <hour_2> <period_2> ... <hour_j> <period_j>
The next 1 line:
PROFILES <m>
The next m lines (profiles): each line has the following form.
<kind> <name> <v_0> <v_1> ... <v_23>
The next 1 line:
DAYS <D>
The next D lines (day specifications): the i-th line specifies day i, and each line has the following form.
<load_profile> <load_pct> <pv_profile> <pv_pct>
The next 1 line:
HOLIDAYS <h> <day_1> <day_2> ... <day_h>
(When h is 0, the line is just "HOLIDAYS 0".)
The next 1 line:
HOURS <n>
The next n lines (individual values): each line has the following form.
<day> <hour> <load> <pv>
Remaining lines: one command per line; the last line is END.

The meanings of the values so far are as follows.
- <capacity>: the maximum amount of energy (Wh) the battery can store.
- <initial>: the stored energy at the start of the simulation, as a percentage (%) of the capacity.
- <charge_max>: the upper limit of the amount (Wh) that can be put into the battery in one hour.
- <discharge_max>: the upper limit of the amount (Wh) that can be taken out of the battery in one hour.
- <charge_eff>, <discharge_eff>: the charge and discharge efficiencies (%).
- <reserve>: the stored energy kept without discharging, as a percentage (%) of the capacity.
- <battery_cost>: the price (cents) of the battery per 1 kWh of capacity.
- <export_max>: the upper limit of the amount (Wh) that can be sent to the grid in one hour.
- <feedin_price>: the price per 1 kWh of export, in units of 1/100 cent.
- <fixed_charge>: a fixed charge (cents) applied once for the whole period.
- <demand_price>: the charge (cents) per 1 kW of the maximum import (see section 6).
- <period_name>: the name of a tariff period. <price>: the price per 1 kWh for import during the hours of that period, in units of 1/100 cent (for example, 1250 means 12.5 cents per 1 kWh).
- The WEEKDAY and WEEKEND lines: each hour from hour <hour_i> up to just before the next hour <hour_(i+1)> (up to and including hour 23 when i = j) belongs to period <period_i>. For example, "WEEKDAY 0 OFF 7 MID 16 ON 21 OFF" means that on weekdays hours 0-6 and 21-23 are OFF, hours 7-15 are MID, and hours 16-20 are ON.
- <kind>: LOAD (a load profile) or PV (a generation profile). <name>: the name of the profile. <v_0> to <v_23>: the amount (Wh) for each hour from hour 0 to hour 23.
- <load_pct>, <pv_pct>: percentages (%) by which the profile values are multiplied.
- <day_1> to <day_h> of HOLIDAYS: the numbers of the days that are holidays.
- Each HOURS line: sets the load and generation of hour <hour> of day <day> to <load> and <pv> (Wh), respectively.

The list of commands is as follows.

Simulation:
RUN <strategy>

Changing settings:
SET <item> <value>
PRICE <period_name> <price>
RESET
CONFIG

Comparison and evaluation:
NOBATTERY
COMPARE <strategy> <strategy> ... <strategy>
SIZE <low> <high> <step>
SWEEP <item> <low> <high> <step> <strategy>
OUTAGE <day> <start_hour> <end_hour>
DIFF <day> <strategy>

Display and aggregation:
HOURLY <day>
DAILY
BILL
SAVINGS
BYPERIOD
DAYTYPE
PEAKS <count>
PROFILE
FLOWS
SELFSUFF
CYCLES
USAGE
HISTOGRAM
CHART <day>

Termination:
END

Notes on the values in the commands:
- <strategy> is a sequence of one or two tokens in one of the following five forms (their meanings are explained in sections 4 and 5).
SELF
TOU <target_pct>
PEAKSHAVE <limit>
BACKUP <reserve_pct>
EXPORTPEAK
However, the <strategy> part of a command may also contain a name that is none of the five, a missing parameter, or a parameter outside its range. The arguments of COMPARE are strategies written one after another with no separator symbol (there may be no arguments).
- <item> is a setting item name (section 3) and may not be a valid item name. <period_name> may be a name not defined by PERIODS.
- All other values are integers.

[Processing]
1. Days and hours
- The data period is the D days from day 1 to day D. Each day consists of the 24 hours from hour 0 to hour 23. An hour is denoted "hour h of day d", and the order of the 24D hours hour 0 of day 1, hour 1 of day 1, ..., hour 23 of day 1, hour 0 of day 2, ..., hour 23 of day D is called chronological order.
- Day 1 is a Monday. The weekday of day d is Monday if the remainder of d - 1 divided by 7 is 0, Tuesday if 1, Wednesday if 2, Thursday if 3, Friday if 4, Saturday if 5, and Sunday if 6.
- The days listed in HOLIDAYS are called holidays. Saturdays, Sundays and holidays are called weekend days, and all other days are called weekday days. Each hour of a weekday day belongs to the period given by the WEEKDAY line, and each hour of a weekend day belongs to the period given by the WEEKEND line. This is called the period of each hour.
- Day name: the name of a holiday is HOL. The name of a day that is not a holiday is the abbreviation of its weekday (from Monday on: MON, TUE, WED, THU, FRI, SAT, SUN).

2. Load and generation data
- The load is the energy used by the house in that hour, and the generation is the energy generated by the solar panels in that hour; both are integers in Wh.
- The load of hour h of day i is floor(A[h] * load_pct / 100), and the base generation value is floor(B[h] * pv_pct / 100). Here A is the value sequence of the LOAD profile named <load_profile> on the line for day i, B is the value sequence of the PV profile named <pv_profile> (in both, the hour-0 value is A[0] and B[0]), and load_pct and pv_pct are the values on that line. floor(y) denotes the largest integer less than or equal to y.
- Then the HOURS lines are applied in order from top to bottom, replacing the load and base generation value of the specified hour with <load> and <pv> of that line. If several lines specify the same hour, the values of the last line remain.
- The load and base generation values do not change after this. The generation used in the simulation is the base generation value multiplied by the setting PVSCALE (section 3): floor(base value * PVSCALE / 100). The load has no such multiplier.

3. Settings and price table
* Settings
The settings consist of the following 13 items, and the value of each item is an integer. The item name, its meaning, and its value range (the values that can be set with SET) are shown.
CAPACITY: capacity (1 to 100000)
INITIAL: initial percentage (0 to 100)
CHARGE: charge limit (1 to 20000)
DISCHARGE: discharge limit (1 to 20000)
CEFF: charge efficiency (1 to 100)
DEFF: discharge efficiency (1 to 100)
RESERVE: reserve percentage (0 to 100)
COST: battery price (0 to 100000)
EXPORT: export limit (0 to 100000)
FEEDIN: export price (0 to 10000)
FIXED: fixed charge (0 to 100000)
DEMAND: demand price (0 to 100000)
PVSCALE: generation multiplier (%, 0 to 300)
The first values of the settings are: the first 8 items take the values of the BATTERY line (in order), the next 4 items take the values of the GRID line (in order), and PVSCALE is 100.

* Price table
The price table holds the price of each tariff period defined by PERIODS. The order of the periods as defined by PERIODS is called definition order. Prices can be changed with PRICE, but periods are never added or removed. The following two periods are determined from the price table.
- Cheapest period: the period with the smallest price. If there are several such periods, the first of them in definition order.
- Peak period: the period with the largest price. If there are several such periods, the first of them in definition order.
In cases such as when all periods have the same price, the cheapest period and the peak period are the same period.

* Initial settings
The settings and the price table at the point when the first part of the input (up to the HOURS lines) has been read are called the initial settings and the initial price table, respectively.

4. Strategies
- An operating strategy (hereafter, strategy) consists of a name and, depending on the name, one parameter. The strategies with a parameter and their parameter ranges are TOU (target percentage, 1 to 100), PEAKSHAVE (limit amount, 0 to 100000) and BACKUP (reserve percentage, 0 to 100). SELF and EXPORTPEAK have no parameter.
- Strategy notation: a strategy without a parameter is written as its name only; a strategy with a parameter is written as the name, one ASCII space, and the parameter value (e.g. SELF, TOU 80).
- Reading a sequence of strategies: when reading strategies from the argument token sequence of a command, repeat steps 1-3 below from the beginning of the token sequence, finishing when no tokens remain. When an error occurs, stop processing that command at that point.
  1. Take the next token as the name. If the name is none of the five, the error is unknown strategy <name>.
  2. If it is a strategy without a parameter, the name alone forms one strategy.
  3. If it is a strategy with a parameter: if no tokens remain after the name, the error is missing parameter for <name>. Otherwise, take the next token as the parameter. If the parameter is outside its range, the error is invalid parameter for <name>. Otherwise, the name and the parameter form one strategy.
  For example, "SELF TOU 80 EXPORTPEAK" yields the three strategies SELF, TOU 80 and EXPORTPEAK.

5. Simulation
A simulation is run given a strategy, settings and a price table, and, when evaluating an outage, also the outage day, start hour and end hour. The following calculation is performed one hour at a time in chronological order, from hour 0 of day 1 to hour 23 of day D. Below, C is the value of CAPACITY, Ec is the value of CEFF, and Ed is the value of DEFF. ceil(y) denotes the smallest integer greater than or equal to y, and min(...) and max(...) denote the smallest and the largest of the values in parentheses, respectively.

* Preparation
- Let the reserve percentage r be RESERVE. However, when the strategy is BACKUP, r is the larger of RESERVE and the parameter. Let the reserve amount R be floor(C * r / 100).
- Set the stored energy (the amount of energy stored in the battery, Wh; written soc in the formulas below) to floor(C * INITIAL / 100). This value is called the starting stored energy.
- Determine the cheapest period Lo and the peak period Hi from the given price table.
- When the strategy is EXPORTPEAK and FEEDIN is greater than the price of Lo, arbitrage is said to be enabled.

* Calculation for one hour
For each hour, let load be the load of that hour, pv the generation used in the simulation (section 2), and p the period of that hour. When evaluating an outage, each hour from the start hour to the end hour of the outage day (both ends inclusive) is called an outage hour. Perform steps (1)-(7) below in order. The stored energy changes during the steps, and each formula uses the stored energy at that point.

(1) Direct consumption: let u = min(load, pv), surplus s = pv - u, and deficit n = load - u. Generation is used for the load first.
(2) Charging from generation: let x1 = min(s, CHARGE, ceil(100 * (C - soc) / Ec)). Add floor(x1 * Ec / 100) to the stored energy and subtract x1 from s. ceil(100 * (C - soc) / Ec) is the minimum charge amount needed to fill the battery.
(3) Discharging to the load: determine the discharge target t and the lower bound b as follows.
  - Outage hour: t = n, b = 0 (the same for every strategy; the battery discharges below the reserve amount).
  - Hour that is not an outage hour: b = R, and t depends on the strategy as follows.
    SELF, BACKUP: t = n.
    TOU: t = n if p is Hi, otherwise t = 0.
    PEAKSHAVE (limit amount a): t = max(0, n - a). That is, only the part of the deficit exceeding a is covered by discharging.
    EXPORTPEAK: t = 0 when arbitrage is enabled and p is Lo and not Hi; otherwise t = n.
  Let y1 = min(ceil(100 * t / Ed), DISCHARGE, max(0, soc - b)) and d1 = floor(y1 * Ed / 100). Subtract y1 from the stored energy and subtract d1 from n. y1 is the amount taken out of the battery and d1 is the amount supplied to the load; d1 does not exceed t.
(4) Exchange with the grid: if it is an outage hour, set g = 0, shortfall = n, e1 = 0, curtailed = s, and x2 = y2 = d2 = 0, skip (5) and (6), and go to (7). If it is not an outage hour, set g = n, shortfall = 0, e1 = min(s, EXPORT), and curtailed = s - e1. g is the import for the load, e1 is the export of generation, and curtailed is the generation that is discarded unused.
(5) Charging from the grid: let the charge headroom q be CHARGE - x1. In each of the following cases, determine the charge target v.
    TOU (target percentage a): when p is Lo and not Hi, v = floor(C * a / 100).
    PEAKSHAVE (limit amount a): when p is Lo, v = C, and q is replaced by min(q, max(0, a - g)).
    BACKUP: when p is Lo, v = R.
    EXPORTPEAK: when arbitrage is enabled and p is Lo and not Hi, v = C.
  If v was determined, let x2 = min(q, ceil(100 * max(0, v - soc) / Ec)) and add floor(x2 * Ec / 100) to the stored energy. If v was not determined, x2 = 0.
(6) Export from the battery: when the strategy is EXPORTPEAK, arbitrage is enabled, p is Hi, and x1 = 0, let y2 = min(ceil(100 * (EXPORT - e1) / Ed), DISCHARGE - y1, max(0, soc - R)) and d2 = floor(y2 * Ed / 100), and subtract y2 from the stored energy. Otherwise, y2 = d2 = 0.
(7) Recording: record the following values for that hour.
  - load, generation, period p
  - charge amount = x1 + x2 (the amount put into the battery), discharge amount = d1 + d2 (the amount supplied by the battery), drawn amount = y1 + y2 (the amount taken out of the battery)
  - stored energy (the value at the end of this hour)
  - import amount = g + x2, export amount = e1 + d2, curtailed amount, shortfall
  - loss = (x1 - floor(x1 * Ec / 100)) + (x2 - floor(x2 * Ec / 100)) + (y1 - d1) + (y2 - d2)
  - the 8 flows: PV>LOAD = u, PV>BATTERY = x1, PV>GRID = e1, PV>CURTAIL = curtailed amount, BATTERY>LOAD = d1, BATTERY>GRID = d2, GRID>LOAD = g, GRID>BATTERY = x2 (this order is called flow order)
  - import cost = floor(import amount * (price of p) / 100000), export income = floor(export amount * FEEDIN / 100000) (both in cents)
The amounts stored in (2) and (5) are rounded down separately for each charge amount. With these formulas, the stored energy is always at least 0 and at most C.

6. Simulation result
A simulation result consists of the records of the 24D hours, the starting stored energy, the reserve amount R, and copies of the strategy, settings and price table used in the simulation, together with the following values. Even if the settings or the price table are changed with SET, PRICE or RESET after the result has been obtained, the result does not change. Below, "sum" means the sum of the record values over all hours of the result (or over the specified hours).
- Import amount and import cost per period: the sum of the import amounts and the sum of the import costs over the hours of that period.
- Total export income: the sum of the export income.
- Maximum import: the largest import amount over all hours.
- Demand charge: floor(maximum import * DEMAND / 1000).
- Bill amount: total of the per-period sums of import costs - total export income + FIXED + demand charge. It can be negative.
- Self-sufficiency: with a the sum of the load and b the sum of the import amounts, max(0, a - b) / a written in percentage notation (section 8).
All money amounts are in cents, and FIXED and DEMAND are the values of the result's settings. In addition, the net cost of a set of hours is defined as the sum of their import costs - the sum of their export income.

7. The case without a battery
For given settings and a given price table, "the case without a battery" means the result of a simulation with strategy SELF using those settings with only CAPACITY replaced by 0, and that price table. In this case the battery neither charges nor discharges, the import amount of each hour is load - u, and the export amount is min(s, EXPORT). The bill amount of the case without a battery is called the baseline amount.

8. Number notation
- Integers are written in decimal, with a leading - for negative numbers.
- Money notation: to write a money amount c (cents), write - if c is negative, followed by the quotient of |c| divided by 100, ".", and the remainder of |c| divided by 100 (2 digits; if it is 1 digit, prefix a 0). For example, 7204 is written 72.04, -22 as -0.22, 5 as 0.05, and 0 as 0.00. Below, values described as "money" are written in this notation.
- Percentage notation: to write a / b (a >= 0, b >= 0) as a percentage, let v = floor(10000 * a / b) (v = 0 when b is 0), and write the quotient of v divided by 100, ".", and the remainder of v divided by 100 (2 digits; if it is 1 digit, prefix a 0). For example, a = 2, b = 3 gives 66.66.
- Tables: a table row is several columns concatenated with no separator character. Each column holds its value right-aligned, padded on the left with spaces up to the column width. If the length of the value is greater than or equal to the column width, the value is written as is. The header row of a table is exactly the string shown in the description of each command.
- Bar: the 22-character string consisting of "|", w copies of #, 20 - w copies of ., and "|", in this order, is called the bar of w.

9. Command processing flow
- The state consists of the settings, the price table and the current result. Initially, the settings and price table are the initial settings and initial price table, and there is no current result.
- Each command is checked in the following order.
  1. If the command name is not in the list, the error is unknown command <command name>.
  2. The arguments are checked in the order given under "Check" for each command in section 10. The error for a wrong number of arguments is wrong number of arguments.
  3. For a command described in section 10 as "Requires a result", if there is no current result, the error is no run.
- On an error, output only the single line "ERROR <error message>", and the state does not change at all.
- END finishes processing. END outputs nothing.
- Below, where "strategy notation" is written, write the strategy in the strategy notation of section 4, and where <strategy> is written, write the strategy notation of the strategy of the result displayed by that command. <day name> is the day name of section 1.

10. Commands
* RUN <strategy>
Check: read a sequence of strategies from the argument token sequence (section 4). If the number of strategies read is not 1, wrong number of arguments.
Processing: run a simulation of that strategy with the current settings and price table, and make its result the current result. The previous current result is discarded.
Output: RUN <strategy> bill <bill amount> import <sum of import amounts> export <sum of export amounts> soc <stored energy of the last hour>
(<bill amount> is money.)

* SET <item> <value>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 2, wrong number of arguments. If <item> is none of the 13 item names of section 3, unknown parameter <item>. If <value> is outside the range of that item, invalid value for <item>.
Processing: set that item of the settings to <value>.
Output: SET <item> <previous value> -> <value>

* PRICE <period_name> <price>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 2, wrong number of arguments. If <period_name> is not defined, unknown period <period_name>. If <price> is not between 0 and 10000 inclusive, invalid value for <period_name>.
Processing: set the price of that period in the price table to <price>.
Output: PRICE <period_name> <previous price> -> <price> cheapest <cheapest period> peak <peak period>
(The cheapest period and the peak period are those of the price table after the change.)

* RESET
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments.
Processing: restore the settings and the price table to the initial settings and the initial price table. The current result does not change.
Output: RESET

* CONFIG
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments.
Output: output the current settings and price table in the following 4 lines. The periods are written in definition order.
BATTERY capacity <CAPACITY> initial <INITIAL> charge <CHARGE> discharge <DISCHARGE> ceff <CEFF> deff <DEFF> reserve <RESERVE> cost <COST>
GRID export <EXPORT> feedin <FEEDIN> fixed <FIXED> demand <DEMAND>
SOLAR pvscale <PVSCALE>
TARIFF <period_name_1> <price_1> <period_name_2> <price_2> ... <period_name_k> <price_k> cheapest <cheapest period> peak <peak period>

* NOBATTERY
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments.
Processing: obtain the result of the case without a battery for the current settings and price table. The current result does not change.
Output: NOBATTERY bill <baseline amount> import <sum of import amounts> export <sum of export amounts> curtailed <sum of curtailed amounts> peak <maximum import>

* COMPARE <strategy> <strategy> ... <strategy>
Check: read a sequence of strategies from the argument token sequence. If the number of strategies read is 0, wrong number of arguments.
Processing: run a simulation of each strategy with the current settings and price table, and also obtain the baseline amount of the case without a battery. The current result does not change.
Output: the first line is COMPARE baseline <baseline amount>. Then output one line per strategy, in ascending order of bill amount (in the order written in the arguments when bill amounts are equal), as follows. If the same strategy is written more than once, output a line for each occurrence.
<rank>. <strategy notation> bill <bill amount> saving <baseline amount - bill amount> sufficiency <self-sufficiency>%
<rank> is the position of this line among the strategy lines (starting from 1). <bill amount> and <baseline amount - bill amount> are money.

* SIZE <low> <high> <step>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 3, wrong number of arguments. If any of the following holds, invalid range: <low> is less than 1, <high> is greater than 100000, <high> is less than <low>, <step> is less than 1, or floor((<high> - <low>) / <step>) + 1 is greater than 20.
Processing: vary the capacity c over <low>, <low> + <step>, <low> + 2 * <step>, ... in order, as long as it does not exceed <high>. For each c, run a simulation with strategy SELF using the current settings with only CAPACITY replaced by c, and the current price table. The baseline amount is the baseline amount of the case without a battery for the current settings and price table (it does not depend on c). Let saving = baseline amount - bill amount, and cost = floor(c * COST / 1000) (cents). Treating the data period as one month, write the payback period as follows. If the saving is positive, let w = floor(10 * cost / saving) and write the quotient of w divided by 10, ".", and the remainder of w divided by 10 (1 digit), concatenated (e.g. 88.8). If the saving is 0 or less, write never. The current result does not change.
Output: for each c, in ascending order of c, output the following line.
SIZE <c> bill <bill amount> saving <saving> cost <cost> payback <payback period>
(<bill amount>, <saving> and <cost> are money.) Finally, output BEST <c>. This c is, among the c whose saving is positive, the one with the smallest value of cost / saving (compared exactly as fraction values; if equal, the smaller c). If there is no c whose saving is positive, output BEST none.

* SWEEP <item> <low> <high> <step> <strategy>
Check: check in the following order.
  1. If there are fewer than 5 arguments, wrong number of arguments.
  2. If <item> (the 1st argument) is none of the 13 item names of section 3, unknown parameter <item>.
  3. For <low>, <high> and <step> (the 2nd to 4th arguments): if any of the following holds, invalid range: <low> is less than the minimum of that item's range, <high> is greater than the maximum of that item's range, <high> is less than <low>, <step> is less than 1, or floor((<high> - <low>) / <step>) + 1 is greater than 20.
  4. Read a sequence of strategies from the token sequence of the 5th and later arguments. If the number of strategies read is not 1, wrong number of arguments.
Processing: vary the value x over <low>, <low> + <step>, ... in order, as long as it does not exceed <high>. For each x, run a simulation of that strategy using the current settings with only the value of <item> replaced by x, and the current price table, and obtain the baseline amount of the case without a battery for the same settings and price table. The current result does not change.
Output: for each x, in ascending order of x, output the following line.
SWEEP <item> <x> bill <bill amount> saving <baseline amount - bill amount> sufficiency <self-sufficiency>%
(<bill amount> and <baseline amount - bill amount> are money.) Finally, output BEST <x>. This x is the x with the smallest bill amount; if there are several such x, the smallest x.

* OUTAGE <day> <start_hour> <end_hour>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 3, wrong number of arguments. If <day> is not between 1 and D inclusive, invalid day. If 0 <= <start_hour> <= <end_hour> <= 23 does not hold, invalid hours. Requires a result.
Processing: with the strategy, settings and price table of the current result, run a simulation that evaluates an outage, with the outage day <day>, start hour <start_hour> and end hour <end_hour>. This result is called the outage result. The current result does not change.
Output: the first line is as follows.
OUTAGE day <day> hours <start_hour>-<end_hour> strategy <strategy> soc <stored energy before>
<stored energy before> is the stored energy, in the outage result, of the hour immediately before hour <start_hour> of day <day> (when the outage starts at hour 0 of day 1, it is the starting stored energy). Then output a table consisting of the following header row and a row for each outage hour (in chronological order).
hour   load     pv  disch    soc  short   curt
The columns of each row are the hour (width 4), load, generation, discharge amount, stored energy, shortfall and curtailed amount (width 7 each). The values are the records of the outage result. Finally, output the following line.
TOTAL load <sum of load> unserved <sum of shortfall> served <supply rate>% bill <bill amount of the outage result>
<sum of load> and <sum of shortfall> are sums over the outage hours. <supply rate> is (sum of load - sum of shortfall) / sum of load in percentage notation, except that it is 100.00 when the sum of load is 0. <bill amount of the outage result> is money.

* DIFF <day> <strategy>
Check: check in the following order.
  1. If there are fewer than 2 arguments, wrong number of arguments.
  2. If <day> (the 1st argument) is not between 1 and D inclusive, invalid day.
  3. Read a sequence of strategies from the token sequence of the 2nd and later arguments. If the number of strategies read is not 1, wrong number of arguments.
  4. Requires a result.
Processing: with the settings and price table of the current result, run a simulation of the specified strategy. This result is called the comparison result. The current result does not change.
Output: the first line is as follows.
DIFF day <day> <day name> <strategy notation of the current result> vs <strategy notation of the specified strategy>
Then output a table consisting of the following header row and a row for each hour of day <day> (in chronological order).
hour   imp1   imp2   exp1   exp2   soc1   soc2
The columns of each row are the hour (width 4), the import amount of the current result, the import amount of the comparison result, the export amount of the current result, the export amount of the comparison result, the stored energy of the current result, and the stored energy of the comparison result (width 7 each). Finally, output the following line.
TOTAL cost1 <net cost 1> cost2 <net cost 2> difference <net cost 2 - net cost 1>
<net cost 1> and <net cost 2> are the net costs of the 24 hours of day <day> in the current result and in the comparison result, respectively. The three values are money.

* HOURLY <day>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 1, wrong number of arguments. If <day> is not between 1 and D inclusive, invalid day. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is HOURLY day <day> <day name> strategy <strategy>. Then output a table consisting of the following header row, a row for each hour of day <day> (in chronological order), and a total row.
hour   load     pv charge  disch    soc import export   curt
The columns of each hour row are the hour (width 4), load, generation, charge amount, discharge amount, stored energy, import amount, export amount and curtailed amount (width 7 each). In the total row, the 1st column is sum (width 4), the stored energy column is 7 spaces, and the other columns are the sums of the values over the 24 hours of that day.

* DAILY
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is DAILY strategy <strategy>. Then output a table consisting of the following header row and a row for each day from day 1 to day D.
day name    load      pv  import  export    curt     soc      cost
The columns of each day row are the day number (width 3), the day name (width 5), that day's sum of load, sum of generation, sum of import amounts, sum of export amounts, sum of curtailed amounts, the stored energy of hour 23 of that day (width 8 each), and that day's net cost (money, width 10). Finally, output the following line.
TOTAL load <sum of load> pv <sum of generation> import <sum of import amounts> export <sum of export amounts> curtailed <sum of curtailed amounts> cost <net cost of all hours>
(The sums are over all hours, and <net cost of all hours> is money.)

* BILL
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: output the following lines in order. The IMPORT line is output once for each period of the result's price table, in definition order, where <import amount> and <import cost> are the import amount and import cost of that period.
BILL strategy <strategy> days <D>
IMPORT <period_name> <import amount> Wh <import cost>
EXPORT <sum of export amounts> Wh <negated total export income>
FIXED <FIXED of the result's settings>
DEMAND <maximum import> Wh <demand charge>
TOTAL <bill amount>
<import cost>, <negated total export income>, <FIXED of the result's settings>, <demand charge> and <bill amount> are money.

* SAVINGS
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Processing: obtain the result of the case without a battery for the settings and price table of the current result. This is called the baseline result.
Output: the first line is SAVINGS strategy <strategy>. Then output lines of the following form in order.
<name> <baseline value> -> <current result value> saving <baseline value - current result value>
The three values are money. The lines are: first one line per period in definition order (<name> is the period name, and the value is that period's import cost), then the line whose <name> is EXPORT (the value is the negated total export income), the line whose <name> is DEMAND (the value is the demand charge), and the line whose <name> is TOTAL (the value is the bill amount).

* BYPERIOD
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is BYPERIOD strategy <strategy>. Then, for each period of the result's price table in definition order, output the following line.
<period_name> hours <number of hours> load <sum of load> pv <sum of generation> charge <sum of charge amounts> discharge <sum of discharge amounts> import <sum of import amounts> export <sum of export amounts>
<number of hours> is the number of hours of that period, and the sums are over the hours of that period.

* DAYTYPE
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is DAYTYPE strategy <strategy>. Then output the WEEKDAY line for weekday days and the WEEKEND line for weekend days, in this order.
<type> days <number of days> load <load> pv <generation> import <import amount> export <export amount> cost <net cost>
<number of days> is the number N of days of that type. <load>, <generation>, <import amount> and <export amount> are floor(S / N), where S is the sum of the values over all hours of the days of that type. <net cost> is, with T the net cost of all hours of the days of that type, the integer obtained by discarding the fractional part of |T| / N, given the sign of T (truncation toward zero; for example, T = -1000 and N = 7 give -142), written in money notation.

* PEAKS <count>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 1, wrong number of arguments. If <count> is less than 1, invalid count. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is PEAKS strategy <strategy>. Then sort the hours whose import amount is positive in descending order of import amount (in chronological order when equal), and output the following line for at most <count> hours from the front.
<rank>. day <day> hour <hour> <period_name> import <import amount> load <load> soc <stored energy>
<rank> is the row number starting from 1, and <period_name> is the period of that hour. If there is no hour whose import amount is positive, output a single line none after the first line.

* PROFILE
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is PROFILE strategy <strategy> days <D>. Then output a table consisting of the following header row and a row for each h = 0, 1, ..., 23.
hour   load     pv import export    soc
The columns of each row are h (width 4) and the averages of the load, generation, import amount, export amount and stored energy of hour h of day 1 to day D (width 7 each). The average is floor(S / D), where S is the sum of the D values.

* FLOWS
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is FLOWS strategy <strategy>. Then, for the 8 flows in flow order, output the line "<flow name> <sum of that flow>" (e.g. PV>LOAD 134123), and finally output the two lines LOSSES <sum of losses> and STORED <stored energy of the last hour - starting stored energy>.

* SELFSUFF
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: SELFSUFF sufficiency <self-sufficiency>% consumption <self-consumption rate>%
<self-consumption rate> is a / b in percentage notation, where a is the total of the sum of PV>LOAD and the sum of PV>BATTERY, and b is the sum of generation.

* CYCLES
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: CYCLES <p>/<q> <cycles> drawn <sum of drawn amounts> losses <sum of losses>
Let X be the sum of drawn amounts and C the CAPACITY of the result's settings. <p>/<q> is X / C as an irreducible fraction, with q at least 1 (if X is 0, write 0/1; when it divides evenly, also write it with q = 1). <cycles> is, with w = floor(1000 * X / C), the quotient of w divided by 1000, ".", and the remainder of w divided by 1000 (3 digits, padded with leading 0s if shorter), concatenated (e.g. 19.145, 0.000).

* USAGE
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: USAGE charging <a> discharging <b> idle <c> full <e> low <f> longest <g>
Then, only when g is positive, write " from day <day> hour <hour>" on the same line. The values are as follows. C is the CAPACITY of the result's settings, and R is the reserve amount of the result.
- a: the number of hours whose charge amount is positive. b: the number of hours whose drawn amount is positive. c: the number of hours whose charge amount and drawn amount are both 0.
- e: the number of hours whose stored energy equals C. f: the number of hours whose stored energy is R or less (hereafter called low hours).
- g: the length (number of hours) of the longest run of consecutive low hours in chronological order. A run may continue across days. It is 0 if there are no low hours. <day> and <hour> are the first hour of the longest run (if there are several such runs, the first one in chronological order).

* HISTOGRAM
Check: if the number of arguments is not 0, wrong number of arguments. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is HISTOGRAM strategy <strategy>. Let C be the CAPACITY of the result's settings, and assign each hour, by its stored energy, to band min(9, floor(10 * stored energy / C)). Then, for i = 0, 1, ..., 9 in order, with k the number of hours in band i, output the following line.
<10i right-aligned in width 3>% <bar of w> <k>
w is floor(20 * k / (24D)). For example, i = 1, k = 400, D = 30 gives " 10% |###########.........| 400".

* CHART <day>
Check: if the number of arguments is not 1, wrong number of arguments. If <day> is not between 1 and D inclusive, invalid day. Requires a result.
Output: the first line is CHART day <day> <day name> strategy <strategy>. Let C be the CAPACITY of the result's settings; then, for each hour of day <day> in chronological order, output the following line.
<hour right-aligned in width 2> <bar of w> <percentage>%
w is floor(20 * stored energy / C), and <percentage> is stored energy / C in percentage notation. For example, hour 8, stored energy 1570 and C 10000 give " 8 |###.................| 15.70%".

[Output format]
- For each command, output the lines specified in [Processing] in the specified order. On an error, output only the single line "ERROR <error message>".
- The output consists of ASCII characters only, and each line ends with a newline (LF). Do not put spaces at the end of lines, and do not output blank lines unless specified.
- Tokens are separated by a single ASCII space, except in table rows.

[Constraints]
- The input has at most 2000 lines.
- The first part of the input (up to the HOURS line and the n lines following it) is in the correct format as described in [Input format]. Each value of the BATTERY and GRID lines is within the range of the corresponding item in section 3.
- 1 <= k <= 6. Each <period_name> consists of 1 to 10 uppercase English letters, and the names are distinct. The <price> values of the PERIODS lines are between 0 and 10000 inclusive.
- In the WEEKDAY and WEEKEND lines, <hour_1> is 0 and <hour_1> < <hour_2> < ... < <hour_j> <= 23. Each <period_i> is a period name defined by PERIODS.
- 2 <= m <= 10. Each <name> consists of 1 to 10 lowercase English letters, and the names of profiles of the same kind are distinct. There is at least one LOAD profile and at least one PV profile. Profile values are between 0 and 20000 inclusive.
- 1 <= D <= 31. In the day specifications, <load_profile> is the name of a LOAD profile and <pv_profile> is the name of a PV profile, and <load_pct> and <pv_pct> are between 0 and 200 inclusive.
- 0 <= h <= D. The HOLIDAYS days are between 1 and D inclusive and are distinct.
- 0 <= n <= 100. In each HOURS line, <day> is between 1 and D inclusive, <hour> is between 0 and 23 inclusive, and <load> and <pv> are between 0 and 40000 inclusive.
- There is at least one weekday day and at least one weekend day.
- Each command line consists of at least one token, and the command name consists of uppercase English letters. END appears only on the last line.
- Among the command arguments, the following tokens are decimal integers (negative numbers have a leading -; no extra leading 0s) with absolute value at most 1000000: the 2nd argument of SET and PRICE (when there are 2 arguments), each argument of SIZE and OUTAGE (when there are 3 arguments), the 2nd to 4th arguments of SWEEP (when there are 5 or more arguments), the argument of HOURLY, CHART and PEAKS (when there is 1 argument), the 1st argument of DIFF (when there are 2 or more arguments), and the token immediately following TOU, PEAKSHAVE or BACKUP in a sequence of strategies. Each token of a command line consists of 1 to 20 characters among English letters, digits and -.
- There are at most 1000 command lines, and the SIZE, SWEEP, COMPARE, OUTAGE and DIFF lines total at most 100. COMPARE has at most 10 strategies.
- When computed exactly as by the formulas in [Processing], every integer appearing in the computation (including intermediate values) has absolute value less than 2^53.
