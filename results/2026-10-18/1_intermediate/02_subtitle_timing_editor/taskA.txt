[Overview]
Write a subtitle timing editor that reads subtitle data in SRT format, then executes the given commands in order to inspect and edit the display timing and the text of the subtitles, and outputs them in SRT, WebVTT and ASS formats or as frame numbers. All times are handled as integers in milliseconds, and all computations are done with integers (no floating-point numbers are used).

[Input format]
The input is read from standard input. It consists of the cue section, the terminator line and the command section, in this order.

The cue section is all lines before the first line consisting of exactly the 3 characters "###" (the terminator line). The cue section is divided into "blocks" separated by lines of length 0 (empty lines). A block is a maximal run of one or more consecutive non-empty lines (one that cannot be extended further in either direction). Empty lines may appear at the beginning or the end of the cue section, and any number of them may appear between blocks. The last block may be followed directly by the terminator line with no empty line in between. A valid block consists of the following lines.
- Line 1 (the index line): the index of the cue.
- Line 2 (the time line): of the form "HH:MM:SS,mmm --> HH:MM:SS,mmm", giving the display start time and the display end time.
- Line 3 onward (the text lines): the text of the cue, one or more lines.
However, the cue section may contain invalid blocks (see "1. Loading the cues" in [Processing]).

In the command section, the line after the terminator line holds the number of commands N as a decimal integer. Each of the following N lines holds one command.

[Processing]
## 1. Loading the cues
Number the blocks from the beginning as the 1st, 2nd, ... block (invalid blocks are numbered too). Check each block against the following (1)-(4) in this order and apply the first one that matches. k is the number of the block.
(1) The index line is not a string of 1 to 6 characters consisting only of digits (0-9): output "BAD CUE k: index".
(2) There is no line 2, or line 2 is not a valid time line: output "BAD CUE k: time".
(3) There is no line 3 (there is no text line at all): output "BAD CUE k: no text".
(4) None of the above: append the block as a cue to the end of the cue list.
Blocks that matched (1)-(3) are not added to the cue list. After all blocks have been checked, output "LOAD cues=n bad=b" (n is the number of cues added to the cue list, b is the number of invalid blocks).

A valid time line is exactly 29 characters long, with a time in characters 1-12, " --> " (the 5 characters space, "-->", space) in characters 13-17, and a time in characters 18-29. Here a time is 12 characters of the form "HH:MM:SS,mmm", where HH, MM and SS are each 2 digits, mmm is 3 digits, the 3rd and 6th characters are ":", the 9th character is ",", and furthermore the values of MM and SS are 59 or less. The value of a time is HH*3600000 + MM*60000 + SS*1000 + mmm (milliseconds). Whether the end time is after the start time is not checked when loading.

A cue has an index (the integer obtained by reading the index line as a decimal number; leading zeros are ignored), a start time and an end time (both integers in milliseconds), and a sequence of text lines (lines 3 onward, taken as they are). The subsequent commands inspect and modify this cue list.

## 2. Terms and common rules
- Position: the ordinal number counted 1, 2, 3, ... from the beginning of the cue list. When a command argument designates a cue, it always uses the position. The index of a cue keeps its original value unless changed by RENUMBER or similar, so it does not necessarily equal the position.
- Duration: end time - start time (milliseconds). It can be 0 or negative.
- Character count: the character count of a text line is the number of characters in the line (markup characters are counted too). The character count of a cue is the sum of the character counts of all its text lines (line breaks are not counted).
- Dialogue cue: a cue in which at least one text line starts with "- " (the 2 characters hyphen and space).
- Words: the sequence of non-empty strings containing no spaces obtained by joining all text lines of the cue with a single space each and splitting the resulting string at the spaces.
- Wrapping at width w: pack the sequence of words into lines from the beginning. The first word starts line 1; each following word is appended to the end of the current line after a single space if (character count of the current line) + 1 + (character count of the word) is w or less, and otherwise it starts a new line. A word longer than w is not split either; it forms a line by itself.
- floor(x): the largest integer less than or equal to x (for negative numbers it rounds away from zero). "Truncation" without further qualification means floor.
- Round half up: turning a rational number x into the largest integer less than or equal to x + 1/2. When the fractional part is exactly 1/2 the result is the larger integer, and the same rule applies when x is negative (for example, 2.5 becomes 3, -2.5 becomes -2 and -2.6 becomes -3). For an integer a and a positive integer b, a/b rounded half up is floor((2a + b)/(2b)).
- Two-decimal notation: a non-negative integer v written as the string formed by the quotient of v divided by 100, ".", and the remainder of v divided by 100 (padded with leading zeros to 2 digits if shorter) (for example, "21.30" when v is 2130 and "0.05" when v is 5).

Time notations (t is a non-negative integer in milliseconds):
- SRT notation: "HH:MM:SS,mmm". HH is floor(t/3600000), MM is the remainder of floor(t/60000) divided by 60, SS is the remainder of floor(t/1000) divided by 60, and mmm is the remainder of t divided by 1000. HH is padded with leading zeros to 2 digits if shorter, and MM and SS are padded with leading zeros to 2 digits and mmm to 3 digits.
- VTT notation: the SRT notation with "," changed to ".".
- ASS notation: with c = floor((t + 5)/10) (rounding half up to centiseconds), "H:MM:SS.cc". H is floor(c/360000) written without leading zeros, MM is the remainder of floor(c/6000) divided by 60, SS is the remainder of floor(c/100) divided by 60 (both 2 digits), and cc is the remainder of c divided by 100 (2 digits).

## 3. Markup
Scan a text line from the beginning. When the part starting at the current position matches one of the following (a)-(c), that part is one markup item, and the scan continues from the position right after it. When none matches, the single character at the current position is an ordinary character, and the scan advances to the next character.
(a) Format tag: one of "<i>", "</i>", "<b>", "</b>", "<u>", "</u>" (lowercase letters only).
(b) Font tag: "</font>". Or, when the character right after "<font" is a space or ">" and there is a ">" somewhere after "<font", the part from "<font" up to and including that first ">".
(c) Position code: the 6 characters "{\an", one digit from 1 to 9, and "}". This digit is called the code digit of that position code.
Any other "<", ">", "{", "}" and so on are all ordinary characters. For example, each character of "<I>" or "{\an0}", and the "<" of "<font color=red" with no ">" after it, are ordinary characters.

## 4. Command syntax and errors
A command line is a command name and zero or more arguments, separated by a single space each. Command names are written in uppercase letters and are case-sensitive. The last argument of SEARCH, INSERT, TEXT and REPLACE is a string argument: the whole rest of the line after the space immediately before it (it may contain spaces).

The argument types are as follows.
- Non-negative integer: a string of 1 to 9 characters consisting only of digits (0-9), read as a decimal number (leading zeros are ignored).
- Signed integer: a non-negative integer, or "-" immediately followed by a non-negative integer ("+" is not allowed).
- Position: a non-negative integer whose value is 1 or more.
- Time: 12 characters in the same format as a time in a time line (the values of MM and SS are 59 or less).
- fps: either a non-negative integer a alone, or a non-negative integer a, "/", and a non-negative integer b in this order, where a and b are both between 1 and 100000 inclusive. When only a is given, b = 1. The frame rate is a/b (frames per second).
- Text: the sequence of one or more parts obtained by splitting the string argument at "|"; each part becomes one text line. No part may be empty (that is, the argument must not contain "||" and must not start or end with "|").

Each command is checked against the following (1)-(4) in this order. When an error is found, output only the one line of the first error found, and change nothing for that command.
(1) The command name is none of those defined in "5. Commands": "ERROR unknown command"
(2) The number of arguments is wrong, an argument does not match the format of its type, or an argument value is outside the specified range: "ERROR bad arguments"
(3) The value of a position argument is greater than the number of cues: "ERROR no cue p" (p is the value of that argument; when there are several position arguments, they are checked from left to right)
(4) The errors specified for each command
If there is no error, execute the command and output the specified lines.

## 5. Commands
Below, n denotes the number of cues before execution, and "every time" means each of the start times and end times of all cues. In output formats, only the symbols that are explained (n, p, S and so on) are replaced by values; all other characters are output as they are.

-- Inspection and display --
LIST
In order of position, output each cue on one line in the form "p i S E T". p is the position, i is the index, S and E are the SRT notations of the start time and the end time, and T is the text lines joined with "|". If there are no cues, output nothing.

VALIDATE
In order of position, check each cue against the following (1)-(7) in this order, and output one line for each problem found. p is the position and d is the duration.
(1) The index i differs from p: "cue p: index i (expected p)"
(2) d is 0 or less: "cue p: end not after start"
(3) p is 2 or more and the start time is less than the end time of the cue at position p-1: "cue p: overlaps previous by x ms" (x = (end time of the cue at position p-1) - (start time of the cue at position p))
(4) d is at least 1 and less than 700: "cue p: too short (d ms)"; d is greater than 7000: "cue p: too long (d ms)"
(5) For each text line whose character count is greater than 42, in increasing order of its line number j within the cue (counted from 1): "cue p: line j too long (x chars)" (x is the character count of that line)
(6) The cue has 3 or more text lines: "cue p: too many lines (x)" (x is the number of text lines)
(7) d is 1 or more and the character count c of the cue satisfies c*1000 > 21*d: "cue p: too fast (v cps)" (v is floor(c*100000/d) in two-decimal notation)
Finally, with k the total number of problems output, output "VALIDATE problems=k".

SEARCH string
For each cue in which some text line contains the string argument as a substring (case-sensitive), in order of position, output "p S L". p is the position, S is the SRT notation of the start time, and L is the first text line that contains the string. Finally, with k the number of matching cues, output "SEARCH found=k".

AT t (t is a time)
Output the cues satisfying start time <= t < end time in order of position in the same form as LIST, and finally, with k their number, output "AT active=k".

GAPS min (min is a non-negative integer from 0 to 60000)
For p = 1, 2, ..., n-1 in this order, if g = (start time of the cue at position p+1) - (end time of the cue at position p) is less than min, output "p q g" (q = p+1). Finally, with k the number of lines output, output "GAPS count=k".

STATS
First output "STATS cues=n". If n = 0, that is all. Otherwise, the effective duration of each cue is its duration if the duration is 0 or more, and 0 if it is negative; then output the following 4 lines.
- "duration total=T mean=M": T is the sum of the effective durations and M is floor(T/n), both in SRT notation.
- "speed v": with c the sum of the character counts of the cues whose effective duration is 1 or more, v is floor(c*100000/T) in two-decimal notation. However, when T = 0, output "speed -".
- "longest cue=p duration=D": p is the position of the cue with the largest effective duration (if several are largest, the smallest position), and D is the SRT notation of that effective duration.
- "gap cue=p ms=g": the p for which g = (start time of the cue at position p+1) - (end time of the cue at position p) is largest (if several, the smallest p), and that g. However, when n = 1, output "gap none".

-- Writing out --
PRINT
In order of position, output each cue in SRT format. For each cue, output the index line, the line "S --> E" (S and E are the SRT notations of the start time and the end time), each text line, and one empty line, in this order. If there are no cues, output nothing.

TOVTT
Output in WebVTT format. First output the line "WEBVTT" and one empty line. Then, in order of position, do the following for each cue.
Scan each text line by the markup rules and build the string in which format tags are kept as they are, font tags and position codes are removed, among ordinary characters "&" is replaced by "&amp;", "<" by "&lt;" and ">" by "&gt;", and all other ordinary characters are kept as they are; then remove all leading and trailing spaces from it. Lines that become empty are discarded. If no line remains, output nothing for that cue.
Build the settings string using k, the code digit of the first position code that appears in the cue (searched in the order of the text lines, and from left to right within a line; discarded lines are included). If k is 7, 8 or 9 it starts with " line:0", if 4, 5 or 6 with " line:50%", and if 1, 2 or 3 with nothing; after that comes " align:start" if k is 1, 4 or 7, " align:end" if 3, 6 or 9, and nothing if 2, 5 or 8 (" line:0" and the others include the leading space). If there is no position code, the settings string is empty.
The output is the index line, the line "S --> E" followed by the settings string (S and E are the VTT notations of the start time and the end time), the remaining text lines, and one empty line, in this order.

TOASS
Output event lines in ASS format. First output the line "[Events]" and the line "Format: Layer, Start, End, Style, Name, MarginL, MarginR, MarginV, Effect, Text". Then, in order of position, output "Dialogue: 0,S,E,Default,,0,0,0,,T" for each cue. S and E are the ASS notations of the start time and the end time, and T is the converted text lines joined with "\N" (the 2 characters backslash and N). To convert a text line, scan it by the markup rules, replace the format tags "<i>", "</i>", "<b>", "</b>", "<u>", "</u>" with "{\i1}", "{\i0}", "{\b1}", "{\b0}", "{\u1}", "{\u0}" respectively, remove font tags, and keep position codes and ordinary characters as they are. Lines that become empty after conversion are joined as they are as well.

FRAMES fps
In order of position, output "p f g" for each cue. p is the position, and f and g are the frame numbers floor(t*a/(1000*b)) computed with t being the start time and the end time respectively. If there are no cues, output nothing.

-- Changing times --
SHIFT ms (ms is a signed integer)
Add ms to every time. A time whose result is negative becomes 0 (clamping). With c the number of cues in which at least one of the start time and the end time was clamped, output "SHIFT cues=n clamped=c".

SHIFTFROM p ms (p is a position, ms is a signed integer)
Change only the times of the cues at position p and after (including p) by the same rules as SHIFT, and with m the number of cues changed this way (= n-p+1) and c counted the same way as for SHIFT, output "SHIFTFROM cues=m clamped=c".

SCALE num den (num and den are non-negative integers from 1 to 100000)
Replace every time t with t*num/den rounded half up, and output "SCALE cues=n".

SYNC p1 t1 p2 t2 (p1 and p2 are positions, t1 and t2 are times)
Let s1 and s2 be the start times of the cues at positions p1 and p2 before execution. If s1 = s2, it is the (4) error "ERROR sync points coincide". Otherwise, replace every time t with t1 + (t-s1)*(t2-t1)/(s2-s1) rounded half up (the linear map that sends s1 to t1 and s2 to t2, computed exactly as a fraction and then rounded). A time whose result is negative is clamped to 0, and with c counted the same way as for SHIFT, output "SYNC cues=n clamped=c".

SNAP fps
Align every time t to the nearest frame boundary. First, let f, the frame number, be t*a/(1000*b) rounded half up, and replace t with f*1000*b/a rounded half up. With k the number of times whose value changed (start times and end times are counted separately), output "SNAP changed=k".

FIXOVERLAP gap (gap is a non-negative integer from 0 to 60000)
For p = 1, 2, ..., n-1 in this order, let s be the start time and e the end time of the cue at position p, and s' the start time of the cue at position p+1; if e > s'-gap, do the following. If s'-gap > s, set the end time to s'-gap (a fix). Otherwise change nothing and output "cue p: cannot fix". Finally, with k the number of cues fixed and f the number of cues that could not be fixed, output "FIXOVERLAP fixed=k failed=f".

EXTEND min (min is a non-negative integer from 1 to 60000)
In order of position, for each cue whose duration is less than min, take start time + min as the candidate, and if there is a cue at the next position, make the candidate the smaller of it and that cue's start time. If the candidate is greater than the current end time, change the end time to the candidate (an extension). After all cues have been processed, with k the number of cues extended and s the number of cues whose duration is less than min at that point (including cues that are still shorter than min after being extended), output "EXTEND extended=k short=s".

TIME p S E (p is a position, S and E are times)
Set the start time of the cue at position p to S and its end time to E, and output "TIME cue=p".

-- Reordering and restructuring --
SORT
Sort the cue list by start time ascending, then by end time ascending when the start times are equal, then by original position ascending when those are also equal. With m the number of cues whose position changed through the sort, output "SORT moved=m" (cues with completely identical contents are still counted as separate cues).

RENUMBER
Set the index of each cue to the value of its position, and with k the number of cues whose index changed, output "RENUMBER changed=k".

MERGE gap (gap is a non-negative integer from 0 to 60000)
Join cues that are close together. Prepare a new empty list, take the cues one at a time in order of position, and for each cue B taken, do the following. If the new list is not empty and its last cue A and B satisfy all 3 of the following conditions, join B into A. Otherwise, append B to the end of the new list.
- (start time of B) - (end time of A) is less than gap (including when it is negative).
- Neither A nor B is a dialogue cue.
- Building the words from the sequence of A's text lines followed by B's text lines and wrapping them at width 42 gives 2 lines or fewer.
When joining, set A's end time to the larger of the end times of A and B, and set A's text lines to the result of that wrapping. A's index and start time do not change. A, after a join, may be joined further with following cues. Finally, the new list becomes the cue list, and with k the number of joins and m the number of cues after merging, output "MERGE merged=k cues=m".

SPLIT width (width is a non-negative integer from 10 to 80)
For each cue whose duration is 1 or more and which is not a dialogue cue, wrap its words at width width (other cues are not changed). If the result is 2 lines or fewer, replace the text lines with that result. If it is 3 lines or more, split the cue into several cues whose text lines are the wrapped lines taken 2 at a time from the beginning (the last one may have 1 line), and place them, in this order, at the original cue's position. All the split cues have the original cue's index. Their times are determined as follows. Let D be the original duration, c1, c2, ... the character counts of the parts (the sum of the character counts of the part's text lines), and C their sum. First allot floor(D*ci/C) milliseconds to each part, then distribute the remainder, D minus the sum of these, 1 millisecond at a time to the parts in decreasing order of the remainder of D*ci divided by C (if the remainders are equal, the earlier part first). The start time of the first part is the original start time, the end time of each part is its start time + its allotted time, and the start time of the next part is the end time of the previous part. With k the number of original cues that were split and m the number of cues after execution, output "SPLIT split=k cues=m".

INSERT p S E text (p is a position, S and E are times)
For INSERT only, check (3) of "4. Command syntax and errors" by comparing p with n+1 instead of the number of cues n: if p is greater than n+1, it is "ERROR no cue p". Insert a new cue whose index is p, start time is S, end time is E and text lines are the parts of the text argument, so that it ends up at position p (if p = n+1, append it to the end). With m the number of cues after the insertion, output "INSERT cue=p cues=m".

DELETE p, DELETE p q (p and q are positions)
With one argument, delete the cue at position p; with two arguments, delete the cues from position p to position q (both inclusive). q < p is treated as an argument value outside the range and gives "ERROR bad arguments" of (2) of "4. Command syntax and errors" (checked before (3)). With k the number of cues deleted and m the number of cues after the deletion, output "DELETE removed=k cues=m".

-- Changing text --
TEXT p text (p is a position)
Replace the text lines of the cue at position p with the parts of the text argument, and with k the number of new text lines, output "TEXT cue=p lines=k".

BALANCE
Rearrange the text lines of each cue into at most 2 lines of lengths as even as possible. Dialogue cues are not changed. For any other cue, build the string T by joining its words with a single space each; if T is 42 characters or fewer, the single line T becomes the new text lines. If T is longer than 42 characters, with w the number of words, for each j = 1, 2, ..., w-1 split into the 2 lines formed by joining the first j words with a single space each and by joining the remaining words with a single space each; the splits in which both lines are 42 characters or fewer are candidates. The candidate with the smallest absolute difference between the character counts of the 2 lines (if there are several, the one whose first line is shorter) becomes the new text lines. If there is no candidate, the cue is not changed. With c the number of cues whose text lines became different from before execution, s the number of cues left unchanged because there was no candidate, and d the number of dialogue cues, output "BALANCE changed=c skipped=s dialogue=d".

STRIP
From each text line, remove all markup found by the markup rules (format tags, font tags, position codes), and then also remove all leading and trailing spaces. Text lines that become empty are deleted, and cues left with no text lines at all are deleted from the cue list. With t the number of markup items removed, l the number of text lines deleted, and c the number of cues deleted, output "STRIP tags=t removed_lines=l removed_cues=c".

CLEANHI
Remove descriptions for the hearing impaired (descriptions of sound effects and the like, and speaker names). Apply the following (1)-(4) to each text line.
(1) Scan the line from the beginning; when there is a "[" with a "]" somewhere after it, remove the part from the "[" up to and including the first "]" after it, and continue scanning right after it. The same applies to "(" and ")". A "[" with no "]" after it and a "(" with no ")" after it are kept as ordinary characters. Each removed part is counted as a bracketed segment.
(2) Collapse runs of consecutive spaces into a single space, and remove leading and trailing spaces (below, this operation is called normalizing).
(3) If the line starts with "- ", those 2 characters are the prefix and the rest is the body. If it does not, the prefix is empty and the whole line is the body. If the body contains ":" and the part from the beginning of the body up to just before the first ":" starts with an uppercase letter (A-Z) and consists only of uppercase letters, digits and spaces, remove that part and the ":" as a speaker name. In this case, the new line is the result of joining the prefix and the rest of the body and normalizing it, and it is counted as a speaker name.
(4) If the result is empty or is the single character "-", delete that text line.
Cues left with no text lines at all are deleted from the cue list. With s the number of bracketed segments, a the number of speaker names, l the number of text lines deleted, and c the number of cues deleted, output "CLEANHI segments=s labels=a removed_lines=l removed_cues=c".

REPLACE old|new
Split the string argument at the first "|" into the part before it, old, and the part after it, new. If there is no "|", if old or new is empty, or if new contains "|", it is "ERROR bad arguments". Scan each text line from the beginning, replace each part that matches old with new, and continue scanning right after the replaced part (all non-overlapping matches are replaced from left to right, and the replaced result is not examined again). With k the number of replacements and m the number of cues in which at least one replacement was made, output "REPLACE count=k cues=m".

-- Undo --
UNDO
Undo the most recently executed modifying command, restoring the whole cue list (the sequence of cues, indexes, times and text lines) to its state before that command was executed. The modifying commands are SHIFT, SHIFTFROM, SCALE, SYNC, SNAP, FIXOVERLAP, EXTEND, TIME, SORT, RENUMBER, MERGE, SPLIT, INSERT, DELETE, TEXT, BALANCE, STRIP, CLEANHI and REPLACE. A modifying command executed without an error is recorded even if nothing actually changed. Commands that ended in an error and commands that are not modifying commands are not recorded. Executing UNDO repeatedly undoes the records one at a time, newest first (UNDO itself is not recorded, and an undone command cannot be redone). If there is no record to undo, it is the (4) error "ERROR nothing to undo". After undoing, with C the name of the undone command, output "UNDO command=C".

[Output format]
Write to standard output the output of loading the cues, followed by the output of each command in command order. The lines to output and their formats are exactly as specified in [Processing]; no other lines (headings, separating empty lines, etc.) are output. Empty lines are output only where specified by PRINT and TOVTT. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros (except for the zero padding specified for the time notations and the two-decimal notation), and negative numbers are preceded by "-". No line has trailing spaces, and every line ends with a newline (LF).

[Constraints]
- The input consists only of printable ASCII characters (including the space) and newlines (LF), and the last line also ends with a newline.
- The cue section has between 0 and 300 blocks, and each line of the cue section is at most 100 characters long. Text lines do not start or end with a space.
- 1 <= N <= 1000. A command line is 1 to 200 characters long, does not start or end with a space, and does not contain 2 or more consecutive spaces.
- The parts of a text argument, and old and new of REPLACE, do not start or end with a space.
- Every time appearing in the input (the times in time lines and time arguments) is less than 36000000 milliseconds (10 hours). Also, after any command is executed, every time is less than 36000000 milliseconds.
- The number of cues is always 1000 or less, and the length of a text line is always 200 characters or less.
- Intermediate values of computations stay below 2^53 in absolute value, but may exceed the 32-bit integer range (SCALE, SYNC, SNAP, FRAMES, STATS, etc.).
