[Overview]
Write a program for a "kinship calculator" that manages a family tree and answers, with English kinship terms, how two persons are related. It reads commands from standard input; it registers and removes persons, adds and removes parent-child links, records marriages, divorces and deaths, and undoes changes. As queries, it handles the relationship between two persons (ancestors and descendants, siblings, aunts and uncles, nieces and nephews, which degree of cousin and how many generations removed, the distinction of half and double relationships, spouses, bereavement, divorce, co-parents, step-parents and step-children, step-siblings, and in-laws), lists of relatives, search by kinship term, blood-relationship paths, nearest common ancestors, lists of ancestors and descendants, lists of cousins, lists of siblings, per-generation summaries, descendant tree charts, numbered ancestor pedigrees, personal timelines, lists of residents in a given year, kinship coefficients, search by following relationships, generation of kinship terms, statistics, and checks for consanguineous marriages, and writes the results to standard output.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N (1 <= N <= 1000), and each of the following N lines contains one command. Each line consists of several tokens separated by single spaces; there is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. Each command has one of the following 28 forms (<...> marks the position of a value).

PERSON <id> <sex> <birthyear> <name>
DEATH <id> <year>
REMOVE <id>
PARENT <child> <parent>
UNPARENT <child> <parent>
SPOUSE <id1> <id2> <year>
DIVORCE <id1> <id2> <year>
UNDO
INFO <id>
RELATION <id1> <id2>
RELATIVES <id>
WHO <id> <words>
PATH <id1> <id2>
COMMON <id1> <id2>
ANCESTORS <id>
DESCENDANTS <id>
COUSINS <id> <degree>
SIBLINGS <id>
GENERATIONS <id>
TREE <id>
PEDIGREE <id> <depth>
TIMELINE <id>
CENSUS <year>
KINSHIP <id1> <id2>
FIND <id> <route>
TERM <i> <j> <sex>
STATS
AUDIT

The format of each value is as follows.
- <id>, <id1>, <id2>, <child>, <parent>: a person identifier (hereafter "id"), a string of 1 to 10 characters consisting of one lowercase English letter followed by 0 to 9 lowercase English letters or digits (e.g. ann, bob2).
- <sex>: one of the single characters M, F and X. M means male, F means female, and X means a sex that is neither.
- <birthyear>, <year>: an integer from 1800 to 2025 inclusive.
- <name>: a string of 1 to 20 English letters (A to Z, a to z).
- <words>: 1 to 6 words separated by single spaces, continuing to the end of the line. Each word is a string of 1 to 30 characters consisting of lowercase English letters, digits and "-" (hyphen) (e.g. first cousin once removed, half-sibling, 11th cousin).
- <degree>: an integer from 1 to 20 inclusive.
- <depth>: an integer from 0 to 10 inclusive.
- <route>: a single token made of 1 to 8 "steps" joined by "." (period). Each step is one of parent, father, mother, child, son, daughter, sibling, brother, sister, spouse, husband, wife (e.g. parent.sibling.child).
- <i>, <j>: an integer from 0 to 200 inclusive.
All integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Every line is guaranteed to follow this format (it is not guaranteed, for example, that the person with a given id is registered).

[Processing]
Process the commands one by one in input order. For each command, examine the conditions listed under "Checks" in its description in the order written, output only the line of the first error that applies, and do nothing else for that command (the data is not changed). If none of the conditions applies, perform the "Action".

== Data ==
- The program keeps a collection of persons, a collection of parent-child links, and a sequence of marriage records. All of them are initially empty.
- A person has an id, a sex, a birth year, a name and a death year. Initially there is no death year; it is set by DEATH. A person without a death year is called "living", and a person with a death year is called "deceased". The ids of persons registered at the same time are all distinct.
- A parent-child link is a pair meaning "the parent of person c is person p". Each person has 0 to 2 parents. When p is a parent of c, c is called a "child" of p.
- A marriage record consists of two persons (called the "first person" and the "second person" in the order written in SPOUSE), a start year, an end year and a status. The status is one of current (ongoing), divorced (ended by divorce) and widowed (ended by the death of one of the two). A record with status current has no end year. A record with status widowed also holds which of the two persons died. Marriage records are kept in the order they were created, and this order is called "record order".

== Terms ==
- "id order": the order of ids by character code (ASCII), smallest first. Compare character by character from the first character; if one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first (e.g. "ann" comes before "ann2", and "ann2" comes before "bob").
- "age order": the order by birth year, smallest first; persons with the same birth year are ordered in id order.
- "person list": the person list of a collection of persons is the ids of those persons in age order, separated by single spaces. When there is nobody, it is "-" (a single hyphen).
- "ancestor" and "generation distance": a person reached from person x by following parents one or more times (parent, parent's parent, ...) is called an "ancestor" of x (x itself is not included). When x is an ancestor of y, y is called a "descendant" of x. When y can be reached from x by following parents some number of times, the minimum such number is called the "generation distance from x to y". The generation distance from x to x itself is 0.
- "grandparent": an ancestor of person b whose generation distance from b is 2 is called a grandparent of b.
- "common ancestor": a person who is a or an ancestor of a, and who is also b or an ancestor of b, is called a common ancestor of a and b.
- "siblings": two different persons who have one or more common parents are said to be siblings.
- "spouse": when there is a marriage record with status current that contains person x, the other person of that record is called the spouse of x (a person has at most one spouse).
- "last marriage record": among the marriage records consisting of the two persons x and y, the last one in record order is called the last marriage record of x and y.
- "partner": when x and y have a last marriage record and its status is current or widowed, y is called a partner of x (a spouse lost to death is included among partners; a divorced spouse is not).
- "life years": for a deceased person, "<birthyear>-<deathyear>"; for a living person, "<birthyear>-" (e.g. 1850-1925, 1950-).
- "fraction notation": a rational number reduced to lowest terms and written as "<numerator>/<denominator>" (the denominator is positive; 0 is "0/1").

== Apex ==
For two persons a and b (they may be the same person), the "apex" and two generation distances da and db are determined as follows.
(1) If a is a common ancestor of a and b (that is, a and b are the same person, or a is an ancestor of b), the apex is a, da = 0, and db = the generation distance from b to a.
(2) Otherwise, if b is a common ancestor of a and b (that is, b is an ancestor of a), the apex is b, da = the generation distance from a to b, and db = 0.
(3) If neither holds and a and b have common ancestors, then for each common ancestor c let da(c) = the generation distance from a to c and db(c) = the generation distance from b to c, and the apex is the c that minimizes the triple (da(c)+db(c), da(c), id of c). Triples are compared starting from the first value, and ids are compared in id order. For the apex c, let da = da(c) and db = db(c). Furthermore, the number of common ancestors c with da(c) = da and db(c) = db is called the "share count".
(4) If there is no common ancestor, a and b have no apex.
When a and b are different persons and have an apex, a and b are said to be "blood-related".

== Kinship terms ==
From da, db and a sex s, a term T(da, db, s) is built as follows. T(da, db, s) is the term for what a person A of sex s is to a person B, where da is the generation distance from A to the apex and db is the generation distance from B to the apex. Wherever something is written below in the form "word1/word2/word3", use word1 if s is M, word2 if s is F, and word3 if s is X.
- G(g): if g = 1, the empty string; if g = 2, "grand"; if g >= 3, "great-" repeated g-2 times followed by "grand" (e.g. G(3) = great-grand, G(4) = great-great-grand).
- da = 0, db = 0: self
- da = 0, db >= 1: G(db) followed by father/mother/parent (e.g. father, grandmother, great-grandparent).
- da >= 1, db = 0: G(da) followed by son/daughter/child (e.g. son, granddaughter, great-great-grandchild).
- da = 1, db = 1: brother/sister/sibling
- da = 1, db >= 2: G(db-1) followed by uncle/aunt/pibling (e.g. uncle, grandaunt, great-grandpibling).
- da >= 2, db = 1: G(da-1) followed by nephew/niece/nibling (e.g. niece, grandnephew, great-grandnibling).
- da >= 2, db >= 2: "<ordinal> cousin<removal>". <ordinal> is the ordinal for k = min(da, db)-1: for k = 1 to 10 it is first, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, tenth respectively. For k >= 11, it is the decimal representation of k followed by "th" if k mod 100 is 11, 12 or 13, and otherwise followed by "st" if k mod 10 is 1, "nd" if it is 2, "rd" if it is 3, and "th" otherwise (e.g. 11th, 12th, 21st, 22nd, 23rd, 111th). <removal> depends on r = |da-db|: if r = 0, the empty string; if r = 1, " once removed"; if r = 2, " twice removed"; if r >= 3, " <r> times removed" (each of them begins with a single space). Examples: first cousin, second cousin once removed, third cousin 3 times removed.
- "blood term": when a and b are blood-related, the blood term of a with respect to b is T(da, db, sex of a) (da and db are the values for the apex of a and b). However, if the apex was determined by (3), prefix "half-" if the share count is 1 (e.g. half-brother, half-uncle, half-first cousin), and prefix "double " (double followed by a single space) if the share count is 4 or more (e.g. double first cousin). If the share count is 2 or 3, prefix nothing.

== Relationship list ==
For two different persons a and b, the "relationship list of a with respect to b" is the sequence of the terms of those of the following conditions (1) to (13) that hold, in this numbered order (if none holds, it is an empty sequence). Terms are chosen by the sex of a.
(1) a and b are blood-related: the blood term of a with respect to b
(2) the status of the last marriage record of a and b is current: husband/wife/spouse
(3) the status of the last marriage record of a and b is widowed: if the person who died in that record is a, late husband/late wife/late spouse; if it is b, widower/widow/surviving spouse
(4) the status of the last marriage record of a and b is divorced: ex-husband/ex-wife/ex-spouse
(5) there is no marriage record consisting of a and b, and there are one or more persons who have both a and b as parents: co-parent (this word regardless of sex)
(6) a is a partner of some parent of b, and a is not a parent of b: stepfather/stepmother/stepparent
(7) b is a partner of some parent of a, and b is not a parent of a: stepson/stepdaughter/stepchild
(8) a and b have no common parent, and for some parent p of a and some parent q of b, q is a partner of p: stepbrother/stepsister/stepsibling
(9) a is not an ancestor of b, and a is a partner of some grandparent of b: stepgrandfather/stepgrandmother/stepgrandparent
(10) b is not an ancestor of a, and b is a partner of some grandparent of a: stepgrandson/stepgranddaughter/stepgrandchild
(11) a is a parent of some partner of b: father-in-law/mother-in-law/parent-in-law
(12) some partner of a is a child of b: son-in-law/daughter-in-law/child-in-law
(13) a and some partner of b are siblings, or some partner of a and b are siblings: brother-in-law/sister-in-law/sibling-in-law
When a and b are the same person, the relationship list of a with respect to b is the sequence consisting of the single word "self".
The "relationship string" is the terms of the relationship list separated by ", " (a comma and a single space). When the list is empty, it is "no relation" (e.g. first cousin, husband).

== Kinship coefficient and inbreeding coefficient ==
The "kinship coefficient" phi(a, b) of persons a and b is the rational number defined by the following rules. phi(a, b) = phi(b, a).
- When a and b are the same person: if a has two parents p and q, phi(a, a) = (1+phi(p, q))/2; if a does not have two parents, phi(a, a) = 1/2.
- When a and b are different persons: let x be the one of a and b with the larger birth year, and y the other one (if the birth years are equal, either may be taken as x; the result is the same). phi(a, b) is the sum of phi(p, y) over each parent p of x, divided by 2 (0 if x has no parents).
The "inbreeding coefficient" F(x) of person x is phi(p, q) if x has two parents p and q, and 0 otherwise. Since a parent is born at least 12 years before the child, this computation always terminates.

== Undo ==
Among PERSON, DEATH, REMOVE, PARENT, UNPARENT, SPOUSE and DIVORCE, the commands processed without an error are called "modifying commands". UNDO undoes the last processed modifying command that has not yet been undone, and restores all persons, parent-child links and marriage records to their state immediately before that command was processed. A modifying command that has been undone is never a target of undoing again. UNDO itself is not a modifying command, and there is no command that redoes an undo.

== Common check ==
To "check person x" means to output "ERROR UNKNOWN PERSON <x>" if there is no person whose id is x. In commands that take two ids, they are checked in the order written.

== Commands ==
PERSON <id> <sex> <birthyear> <name>
- Checks: if a person whose id is <id> already exists, output "ERROR DUPLICATE PERSON <id>".
- Action: register the person (with no parents, living) and output "ADDED <id> <name> <sex> <birthyear>".

DEATH <id> <year>
- Checks: check person <id>. Next, if <id> is deceased, output "ERROR ALREADY DECEASED <id>". Next, if <year> is less than the birth year of <id>, or less than any of the start years and end years of the marriage records that contain <id>, output "ERROR BAD YEAR <id>". Next, if <id> has children whose birth year is greater than <year>+1, output "ERROR AFTER DEATH <c> <id>" for the first such child c in id order.
- Action: set the death year of <id> to <year>. If <id> has a spouse s, set the end year of that marriage record to <year> and its status to widowed (the person who died is <id>), and output "DIED <id> <year> spouse=<s>"; if <id> has no spouse, output "DIED <id> <year>".

REMOVE <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: remove all parent-child links in which <id> is the parent or the child and all marriage records that contain <id>, then remove person <id>, and output "REMOVED <id>". Afterwards, a new person with the same id may be registered.

PARENT <child> <parent>
- Checks: check person <child> and person <parent>. Next, examine the following conditions in this order.
  If <child> and <parent> are the same person: "ERROR SAME PERSON <child>"
  If <parent> is already a parent of <child>: "ERROR ALREADY PARENT <child> <parent>"
  If <child> already has two parents: "ERROR TOO MANY PARENTS <child>"
  If <parent> is a descendant of <child>: "ERROR CYCLE <child> <parent>"
  If the birth year of <parent> + 12 is greater than the birth year of <child>: "ERROR AGE GAP <child> <parent>"
  If <parent> is deceased and the death year of <parent> + 1 is less than the birth year of <child>: "ERROR AFTER DEATH <child> <parent>"
- Action: add the parent-child link meaning that the parent of <child> is <parent>, and output "LINKED <child> <parent> <term>". <term> is father/mother/parent according to the sex of <parent>.

UNPARENT <child> <parent>
- Checks: check person <child> and person <parent>. Next, if <parent> is not a parent of <child>, output "ERROR NOT PARENT <child> <parent>".
- Action: remove that parent-child link and output "UNLINKED <child> <parent>".

SPOUSE <id1> <id2> <year>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>. Next, examine the following conditions in this order. In a condition that says "looking at <id1>, <id2> in this order", x is the person looked at first among the persons that satisfy it.
  If <id1> and <id2> are the same person: "ERROR SAME PERSON <id1>"
  Looking at <id1>, <id2> in this order, if there is a deceased person: "ERROR DECEASED <x>"
  Looking at <id1>, <id2> in this order, if there is a person who has a spouse: "ERROR ALREADY MARRIED <x>"
  If one is an ancestor of the other, or the two are siblings: "ERROR CLOSE KIN <id1> <id2>"
  Looking at <id1>, <id2> in this order, if there is a person such that <year> is less than that person's birth year + 16: "ERROR TOO YOUNG <x>"
  Looking at <id1>, <id2> in this order, if there is a person such that, among the marriage records that contain that person and have an end year, there is one whose end year is greater than or equal to <year>: "ERROR BAD YEAR <x>"
- Action: append a marriage record whose first person is <id1>, second person is <id2>, start year is <year> and status is current, and output "MARRIED <id1> <id2> <year>".

DIVORCE <id1> <id2> <year>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>. Next, if <id2> is not the spouse of <id1> (including the case where <id1> and <id2> are the same person), output "ERROR NOT MARRIED <id1> <id2>". Next, if <year> is less than or equal to the start year of the marriage record of the two persons with status current, output "ERROR BAD YEAR <id1>".
- Action: set the end year of that marriage record to <year> and its status to divorced, and output "DIVORCED <id1> <id2> <year>".

UNDO
- Checks: if there is no modifying command that can be undone, output "ERROR NOTHING TO UNDO".
- Action: perform the undo and output "UNDONE <input line of the undone command>". The input line is the entire line exactly as written in the input (e.g. UNDONE PARENT cal ann).

INFO <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: output the following lines in this order.
  "INFO <id> <name> <sex> <life years>"
  "  parents: <person list of the parents of <id>>"
  For each marriage record that contains <id>, in record order, one line "  marriage: <other> <start year>-<end year> <status>" (<other> is the other person of that record. If there is no end year, it is empty; for example, "  marriage: bob 1922- current").
  "  children: <person list of the children of <id>>"
  "  siblings: <person list of the persons who are siblings of <id>>"
  "  inbreeding: <fraction notation of F(<id>)>"

RELATION <id1> <id2>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>.
- Action: output "RELATION <id1> <id2>: <relationship string of <id1> with respect to <id2>>".

RELATIVES <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: the persons y other than <id> whose relationship list with respect to <id> is not empty are called "relatives". Output "RELATIVES <id> <number of relatives>", then for each relative y in id order output "  <y>: <relationship string of y with respect to <id>>".

WHO <id> <words>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: let Q be <words> (the string of the words separated by single spaces). For each person y other than <id>, compute the relationship list L of y with respect to <id>, and the relationship list L' built by the same rules while treating the sex of y as X. L and L' have the same length, and the words at the same position represent the relationship with the same number. If there is a position I such that the I-th word of L or the I-th word of L' equals Q, y is said to "match", and for the earliest such position I, the I-th word of L is called the "matched term". Output "WHO <id> <Q> <number of matching persons>", then for each matching person y in id order output "  <y>: <matched term>".

PATH <id1> <id2>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>.
- Action: if <id1> and <id2> have no apex, output "PATH <id1> <id2> none". If they have an apex, let c be the apex, and build the "upward path" from a person x to c as follows. Start with x; while the last person y is not c, append the first in id order among the parents of y whose "generation distance from that parent to c" equals "the generation distance from y to c" - 1 (the generation distance from c to c is 0). Let R be the sequence of persons formed by the upward path from <id1> to c followed by the upward path from <id2> to c with its last person c removed and then reversed, and output "PATH <id1> <id2> <da+db>: <ids of the persons in R separated by single spaces>". (da and db are the values for the apex of <id1> and <id2>.)

COMMON <id1> <id2>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>.
- Action: among the common ancestors c of <id1> and <id2>, those for which no child of c is a common ancestor of <id1> and <id2> are called "nearest common ancestors". For each c, let da(c) = the generation distance from <id1> to c and db(c) = the generation distance from <id2> to c. Output "COMMON <id1> <id2> <number of nearest common ancestors>", then output "  <c> <da(c)> <db(c)>" for the nearest common ancestors c sorted in increasing order of (da(c)+db(c), id of c).

ANCESTORS <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: output "ANCESTORS <id> <number of ancestors of <id>>", then for each ancestor y of <id>, with g the generation distance from <id> to y, output "  <g> <y> <T(0, g, sex of y)>" in increasing order of (g, id of y).

DESCENDANTS <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: output "DESCENDANTS <id> <number of descendants of <id>>", then for each descendant y of <id>, with g the generation distance from y to <id>, output "  <g> <y> <T(g, 0, sex of y)>" in increasing order of (g, id of y).

COUSINS <id> <degree>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: the persons y other than <id> such that y and <id> have an apex, both da (the generation distance from y to the apex) and db (the generation distance from <id> to the apex) for that apex are 2 or more, and min(da, db)-1 equals <degree>, are called "matches". Output "COUSINS <id> <degree> <number of matches>", then output "  <y> <blood term of y with respect to <id>>" for the matches y sorted in increasing order of (|da-db|, id of y).

SIBLINGS <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: classify each person y other than <id> as follows. If y and <id> have two common parents, y is full; if they have one, y is half. If they have no common parent, and for some parent p of <id> and some parent q of y, q is a partner of p, y is step. A person who fits none of these is not classified. Let K be the number of classified persons, and M the number of persons classified as full or half that come before <id> in age order. Output "SIBLINGS <id> <K> order=<M+1>", then for each classified person y in age order output "  <y> <class> <birth year of y>".

GENERATIONS <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: group the descendants y of <id> by the generation distance g from y to <id>. Output "GENERATIONS <id> <number of distinct values of g>", then for each value of g in increasing order of g output "  <g> <count> <living count> <minimum birth year>-<maximum birth year>". Here the count, the living count, the minimum birth year and the maximum birth year are the values for the descendants with that g.

TREE <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: output "TREE <id> <number of descendants of <id>>", then "visit" <id> at depth 0. Visiting person x at depth d means the following. If x has already been visited during the processing of this TREE command, output a line consisting of 2*(d+1) spaces followed by "<x> (see above)", and finish. Otherwise, output a line consisting of 2*(d+1) spaces followed by "<x> <name> <life years>" (of x), further followed by " = <s>" if x has a spouse s, and then visit each child of x in age order at depth d+1.

PEDIGREE <id> <depth>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: to "enter" person x with number k at generation g means to create a line consisting of 2*(g+1) spaces followed by "<k> <x> <name> <life years>" (of x), and then, if g is less than <depth>, to enter the i-th parent (i = 0, 1) of x, when the parents of x are ordered by sex in the order M, F, X (in id order if the sex is the same), with number 2k+i at generation g+1 (the entry of the parent with i = 0, including all entries made within it, is completed before the parent with i = 1 is entered). Create the lines by entering <id> with number 1 at generation 0. The same person may be entered more than once. Output "PEDIGREE <id> <depth> entries=<number of lines created> distinct=<number of distinct persons entered>", then output the created lines in the order they were created.

TIMELINE <id>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: collect the following "events" for <id>. Each event consists of a year, a rank, a counterpart and a text.
  Birth: the year is the birth year of <id>, rank 0, no counterpart, text "born".
  For each marriage record that contains <id> (let p be the other person): the year is the start year, rank 1, counterpart p, text "married <p>". In addition, if the status of that record is divorced: the year is the end year, rank 3, counterpart p, text "divorced <p>". If the status is widowed and the person who died is p: the year is the end year, rank 4, counterpart p, text "widowed <p>".
  For each child c of <id>: the year is the birth year of c, rank 2, counterpart c, text "child <c>".
  For each deceased parent p of <id>, if the death year of p is greater than or equal to the birth year of <id>, and either <id> is living or the death year of p is less than or equal to the death year of <id>: the year is the death year of p, rank 5, counterpart p, text "lost parent <p>". Likewise, for each deceased child c of <id> under the same condition (with c in place of p): the year is the death year of c, rank 5, counterpart c, text "lost child <c>".
  If <id> is deceased: the year is the death year, rank 6, no counterpart, text "died".
  Output "TIMELINE <id> <number of events>", then output "  <year> <text>" for the events sorted in increasing order of (year, rank, id of counterpart). (An event without a counterpart never has the same year and rank as another event, so counterparts never need to be compared for it.)

CENSUS <year>
- Action: the persons whose birth year is less than or equal to <year> and who are living or whose death year is greater than <year> are called "residents". The "status" of a resident x is determined as follows. If there is no marriage record that contains x and whose start year is less than or equal to <year>, it is single. Otherwise, let m be the last such record in record order; if m has no end year or its end year is greater than <year>, the status is "married <the other person of m>"; otherwise it is the status of m (divorced or widowed). Output "CENSUS <year> <number of residents>", then for each resident x in age order output "  <x> <<year> minus the birth year of x> <status>".

KINSHIP <id1> <id2>
- Checks: check person <id1> and person <id2>.
- Action: output "KINSHIP <id1> <id2> <fraction notation of phi(<id1>, <id2>)>".

FIND <id> <route>
- Checks: check person <id>.
- Action: start with the set S of persons consisting of <id> alone, and apply each step of <route> to S from first to last. Applying a step parent, child, sibling or spouse replaces S by the set of all the parents of x, all the children of x, all the persons who are siblings of x, or the spouse of x (if any), respectively, collected over every person x in S. The steps father, son, brother and husband collect only those with sex M among the persons collected as for parent, child, sibling and spouse respectively, and mother, daughter, sister and wife collect only those with sex F. After all steps, remove <id> from S if it is in S. Output "FIND <id> <route> <number of persons in S>", then for each person y in S in id order output "  <y>: <relationship string of y with respect to <id>>".

TERM <i> <j> <sex>
- Action: output "TERM <i> <j> <sex> <T(i, j, <sex>)>" (without half- or double).

STATS
- Action: output "STATS persons=<number of persons> living=<number of living persons> links=<number of parent-child links> couples=<number of marriage records with status current> founders=<number of persons with no parents> generations=<L>". L is the maximum length k of a sequence of persons x1, x2, ..., xk in which each x(i+1) is a parent of x(i) (sequences with k = 1 are included), and 0 if there are no persons.

AUDIT
- Action: first, go through the marriage records in record order and, for each one whose first person a and second person b are blood-related, create a line "  marriage <a> <b> <blood term of a with respect to b>". Next, for each person x whose inbreeding coefficient is not 0, in id order, create a line "  inbred <x> <fraction notation of F(x)>". Output "AUDIT <number of lines created>", then output the created lines in the order they were created.

[Output format]
- For each command, output only the lines described in [Processing]. Every command outputs at least one line.
- The only whitespace at the beginning of a line is what the descriptions specify (spaces), and no whitespace is added at the end of a line. Do not output empty lines. End every line with a newline.
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Ids and names are written exactly as in the input.
- The error lines are of the following 17 kinds (the <...> parts are replaced by the corresponding ids).
ERROR UNKNOWN PERSON <id>
ERROR DUPLICATE PERSON <id>
ERROR SAME PERSON <id>
ERROR ALREADY PARENT <child> <parent>
ERROR TOO MANY PARENTS <child>
ERROR CYCLE <child> <parent>
ERROR AGE GAP <child> <parent>
ERROR AFTER DEATH <child> <parent>
ERROR NOT PARENT <child> <parent>
ERROR ALREADY DECEASED <id>
ERROR BAD YEAR <id>
ERROR DECEASED <id>
ERROR ALREADY MARRIED <id>
ERROR CLOSE KIN <id1> <id2>
ERROR TOO YOUNG <id>
ERROR NOT MARRIED <id1> <id2>
ERROR NOTHING TO UNDO

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 1000
- Each line is at most 200 characters long.
- At most 200 persons are registered at the same time.
- Since a parent is born at least 12 years before the child and all years are between 1800 and 2025 inclusive, any sequence obtained by following parents has length at most 19.
- Every value of phi and F (including the values computed in intermediate steps) is between 0 and 1 inclusive and can be represented as a fraction whose denominator is a power of 2 not exceeding 2^37. Every other integer that appears during the computation has an absolute value less than 10^6.

In this example, the common ancestors ann and bob of eve and fox both have da = 2 and db = 2, and the share count is 2, so eve is fox's first cousin. gil is a partner of dee, who is a sibling of cal, so gil is cal's brother-in-law. Since the sex of fox is X, the term is grandchild. eve is a descendant of ann, so eve cannot be made a parent of ann. bob's death makes the marriage record of ann and bob widowed, and ann, the survivor, is bob's widow. For the kinship coefficient, following the parents of the one with the larger birth year gives phi(eve, fox) = phi(dee, eve)/2, phi(dee, eve) = phi(cal, dee)/2, and phi(cal, dee) = (phi(ann, cal)+phi(bob, cal))/2; since phi(ann, cal) = (phi(ann, ann)+phi(bob, ann))/2 = (1/2+0)/2 = 1/4 and likewise phi(bob, cal) = 1/4, phi(eve, fox) = 1/16.
