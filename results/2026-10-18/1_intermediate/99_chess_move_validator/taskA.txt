[Overview]
Write a program that validates chess game records. Following the commands read from standard input, it sets and prints positions in FEN, validates and plays moves given in Standard Algebraic Notation (SAN) and in UCI format, lists legal moves, determines check, checkmate, stalemate and draw conditions, takes back moves, counts positions (perft), shows attacks and pins, prints the game record in PGN format, and so on, and writes the results to standard output.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. Each line consists of a command name and zero or more arguments separated by single spaces, with no whitespace at the beginning or end of the line. The input consists of exactly these N+1 lines.

[Processing]
Process the commands one at a time in input order, writing the output of each command immediately. Below, strings enclosed in double quotes "" are strings that appear exactly as written in the input or output. In descriptions of output, a part such as <v> is replaced by its value.

1. Board and pieces
- The board is 8 squares tall and 8 squares wide. Files are denoted by "a" to "h" and ranks by "1" to "8", and a square is denoted by the two characters formed by its file letter followed by its rank digit (example: "e4"). This is called a square name. File letters are lowercase only.
- Let c be the file position (a is 1, b is 2, ..., h is 8) and r the rank digit; the number of a square is 8*(r - 1) + (c - 1). a1 is 0, b1 is 1, ..., h1 is 7, a2 is 8, ..., h8 is 63. "Square order" means increasing order of this number.
- A square with c + r even is called a dark square, and a square with c + r odd is called a light square.
- Pieces come in two colors, white and black. There are six piece types: king "K", queen "Q", rook "R", bishop "B", knight "N" and pawn "P". The letter of a piece is the letter of its type (uppercase) for a white piece, and the lowercase form of that letter for a black piece. Each square is either empty or holds exactly one piece.
- When one of white and black is called color X, the other one is called the opposing color. "Forward" for white is the direction in which the rank digit increases, "forward" for black is the direction in which the rank digit decreases, and "backward" is the opposite direction. White's last rank is rank 8, and black's last rank is rank 1.

2. Position
A position consists of the following six items.
- The piece placement (for each square, whether it is empty or which piece is on it)
- The side to move (the color that moves next)
- The castling rights: zero or more of "K", "Q", "k" and "q". "K" denotes white's kingside castling right, "Q" white's queenside, "k" black's kingside and "q" black's queenside castling right.
- The en passant target square: none, or one square.
- The halfmove clock: an integer >= 0.
- The move number: an integer >= 1.
The standard initial position is the position written in FEN (section 13) as "rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w KQkq - 0 1".

3. Attacks and check
- A piece of color X on square s is said to "attack" a square t other than s when the following holds for its piece type. Whether t is empty, and the color of a piece on t, do not matter.
  Pawn: t is the square one rank forward from s and one file to the left or right.
  Knight: of the difference in file positions and the difference in rank digits between s and t, one is 1 and the other is 2.
  King: the difference in file positions and the difference in rank digits between s and t are both at most 1.
  Rook: s and t are on the same file or the same rank, and all squares between them are empty.
  Bishop: the difference in file positions and the difference in rank digits between s and t are equal, and all squares between s and t on that diagonal line are empty.
  Queen: the rook condition or the bishop condition holds.
- When some piece of color X attacks t, we say that "X attacks t".
- When the opposing color attacks the square of color X's king, we say that "X is in check".

4. Moves and legal moves
A move is a triple of a from-square, a to-square, and a promotion piece type (one of "Q", "R", "B" and "N", or none).
The pseudo-legal moves of a position P are all the moves generated as follows for each piece of the side to move X in P.
- A piece other than a pawn: moves to the squares that the piece attacks and that do not hold a piece of X (no promotion).
- A pawn: if the square one rank forward is empty, a move to that square. If the square one rank forward is empty, the pawn is on rank 2 if it is white or on rank 7 if it is black, and the square two ranks forward is also empty, a move to that square two ranks forward. Also, for a square that the pawn attacks, a move to that square if it holds a piece of the opposing color or it is the en passant target square of P. A move whose to-square is on the last rank becomes four moves whose promotion piece types are "Q", "R", "B" and "N"; all other moves have no promotion.
Castling is not included in the pseudo-legal moves.
The legal moves of a position P are all the pseudo-legal moves after which X is not in check in the resulting position (section 5), together with all castling moves that satisfy the conditions of section 6. A castling move is represented as a move that moves the king two files (white kingside: e1 to g1, white queenside: e1 to c1, black kingside: e8 to g8, black queenside: e8 to c8; no promotion).
- A pawn move that changes file and whose to-square is empty is called en passant.
- Moves whose to-square holds a piece, and en passant moves, are called captures.
- A move that has a promotion piece type is called a promotion.

5. The position after a move
The position after the side to move X plays move m (from-square s, to-square t) in position P is P with all of the following changes applied.
- If m is en passant, remove the piece on the square one rank backward from t.
- If m is castling, also move the rook on the same rank: from the h-file to the f-file on the kingside, and from the a-file to the d-file on the queenside.
- Move the piece on s to t. If t holds a piece, that piece is removed. If there is a promotion piece type, place a piece of X of that type on t. s becomes empty.
- The halfmove clock becomes 0 if m is a pawn move or a capture, and otherwise increases by 1.
- Castling rights: if the piece that was on s is a king, remove both rights of X. In addition, remove "Q" if s or t is a1, "K" if s or t is h1, "q" if s or t is a8, and "k" if s or t is h8.
- The en passant target square becomes the square between s and t if m is a pawn move that advances two ranks, and none otherwise.
- If X is black, increase the move number by 1.
- The side to move becomes the opposing color.

6. Castling
For kingside (or queenside) castling of the side to move X in position P, check the following (1) to (5) in this order; the quoted string of the first one that applies is the reason why castling is not possible. Here the base rank is rank 1 for white and rank 8 for black, and all squares below are squares on the base rank.
  (1) X does not have that castling right: "no castling right"
  (2) Some square between the king and the rook (on the kingside, the f-file and g-file squares; on the queenside, the b-file, c-file and d-file squares) holds a piece: "path blocked"
  (3) X is in check: "in check"
  (4) In P, the opposing color attacks the rook's destination square (the f-file square on the kingside, the d-file square on the queenside): "through check"
  (5) In the position after that castling move (section 5), X is in check: "into check"
If none of them applies, that castling move is a legal move of P. Note that whenever a castling right exists, the king and the rook concerned are always on their original squares (the king on the e-file; the rook on the h-file for the kingside, on the a-file for the queenside).

7. SAN (move notation)
The SAN of a legal move m in position P is built as follows.
- If m is castling, it is "O-O" for the kingside and "O-O-O" for the queenside ("O" is the uppercase letter O).
- If m is a pawn move, it is: the file letter of the from-square and "x" if m is a capture, then the to-square name, then "=" and the letter of the promotion piece type if there is one.
- Otherwise, it is the concatenation of the letter of the piece type, the disambiguation string, "x" if m is a capture, and the to-square name. The disambiguation string is defined as follows. Consider the legal moves of P that are not castling, whose to-square is the same as m's, whose from-square differs from m's, and whose piece on the from-square is the same as the piece on m's from-square (same color and same type), and let O be the set of their from-squares. If O is empty, it is the empty string. If every square in O is on a different file from m's from-square, it is the file letter of the from-square. Otherwise, if every square in O is on a different rank from m's from-square, it is the rank digit of the from-square. Otherwise, it is the from-square name.
- Finally, for the position Q after m, append "#" if the side to move in Q is in check and Q has no legal moves, and append "+" if that side is in check and Q has legal moves. If that side is not in check, append nothing. This mark ("#", "+" or none) is called the check mark of m.
Sorting strings in "SAN order" means sorting them in lexicographic order by ASCII code (compare character by character from the start; when one string equals the beginning of the other, the shorter one comes first). The SANs of the legal moves of one position are all distinct.

8. Reading SAN
A string w given to the MOVE command is examined in position P according to (1) to (4) below. As soon as a reason is determined, the examination stops.
(1) Syntax: read w with the following steps. If a condition is not met during the steps, the reason is "syntax".
  - If the last character of w is "+" or "#", remove that one character as the mark. Otherwise there is no mark. Call the rest u.
  - If u is "O-O", treat it as kingside castling, and if u is "O-O-O", treat it as queenside castling, and go to (2).
  - If u has 2 or more characters and its second-to-last character is "=", take the last character as the promotion piece type and remove those two characters. In this case, the promotion piece type must be one of "Q", "R", "B" and "N".
  - The last two characters of the remainder must be a square name (the condition is also not met when fewer than 2 characters remain). Remove them as the to-square.
  - If the last character of the remainder is "x", remove that one character, and the capture mark is present. Otherwise the capture mark is absent.
  - If the first character of the remainder is one of "K", "Q", "R", "B" and "N", remove it as the piece type. After it there must be zero or one file letter followed by zero or one rank digit, and these must make up the whole remainder. They are the specified file and the specified rank. Also, there must be no promotion piece type.
  - Otherwise, the piece type is pawn. If the capture mark is present, the remainder must be exactly one file letter, which becomes the specified file. If the capture mark is absent, the remainder must be empty.
(2) If P has no legal moves at all, the reason is "game over".
(3) If it is castling, the reason is the section 6 reason if there is one. If there is none, let m be that castling move and check (g) of (4).
(4) If it is not castling, the moves that satisfy all of the following conditions are called "matching moves".
  - The type of the piece on the from-square is the piece type read.
  - The to-square is the to-square read.
  - It is not castling.
  - If a file is specified, the from-square is on that file. If a rank is specified, the from-square is on that rank.
  - If the piece type is pawn, whether the move changes file agrees with whether the capture mark is present.
  Then check the following (a) to (g) in this order.
  (a) If there is no matching legal move, the reason is "leaves king in check" if there is a matching pseudo-legal move, and "no such move" if there is not.
  (b) If the matching legal moves have two or more from-squares, the reason is "ambiguous".
  (c) From here on, let s be the from-square of the matching legal moves. If whether the legal move from s to the to-square is a capture does not agree with whether the capture mark is present, the reason is "capture mark".
  (d) If the legal move from s to the to-square is a promotion but there is no promotion piece type, the reason is "missing promotion".
  (e) If there is a promotion piece type but the legal move from s to the to-square is not a promotion, the reason is "invalid promotion".
  (f) If the piece type is not pawn and the string formed by the specified file followed by the specified rank (an empty string where nothing is specified) does not equal the disambiguation string (section 7) of the legal move from s to the to-square, the reason is "disambiguation".
  If no reason has been determined so far, let m be the legal move from s to the to-square with the promotion piece type read (no promotion if there is none).
  (g) If the check mark of m (section 7) does not equal the mark read in (1), the reason is "check mark".
If no reason is determined, w has been read correctly as the SAN representing the legal move m.

9. Reading UCI notation
A string w given to the UCI command is examined in position P according to (1) to (6) below. As soon as a reason is determined, the examination stops.
  (1) If w does not satisfy the condition that its length is 4 or 5, the first two characters and the next two characters are each a square name, and, if the length is 5, the last character is one of "q", "r", "b" and "n", the reason is "syntax". Let the square given by the first two characters be the from-square s and the square given by the next two characters be the to-square t, and if there is a fifth character, let its uppercase form be the promotion piece type.
  (2) If P has no legal moves at all, the reason is "game over".
  (3) If s does not hold a piece of the side to move, the reason is "no piece".
  (4) If there is no legal move (castling included) whose from-square is s and whose to-square is t, the reason is "leaves king in check" if there is a pseudo-legal move whose from-square is s and whose to-square is t, and "no such move" if there is not.
  (5) If that legal move is a promotion but there is no promotion piece type, the reason is "missing promotion".
  (6) If there is a promotion piece type but that legal move is not a promotion, the reason is "invalid promotion".
If no reason is determined, let m be the legal move from s to t with the promotion piece type read in (1) (no promotion if there is none); w has been read correctly as representing m.

10. The game
The program holds one game. A game consists of three things: the start position, the list of moves played, and whether the start position was set by LOAD. For each move in the list of moves played, the position before that move, its SAN, its UCI notation and the captured piece are recorded. The current position is the position after the last move (the start position if there are no moves).
- In the initial state, the start position is the standard initial position, the list of moves played is empty, and the start position was not set by LOAD. The program starts in the initial state.
- To "play" a legal move m in the current position P means to append the record of m in P (the position P before the move, the SAN of m in P, the UCI notation and the captured piece) to the end of the list of moves played. As a result, the current position becomes the position after m.
- The UCI notation is the concatenation of the from-square name, the to-square name and, if there is a promotion piece type, its lowercase letter (examples: "e2e4", "e1g1", "b7b8q").
- The captured piece is the lowercase form of the letter of the piece on m's to-square if there is a piece there, "p" if m is en passant, and "-" otherwise.
- The position sequence of the game is the start position followed by the positions after each move in the list of moves played, in order.

11. Repeated positions and the game state
- The position key of a position is the 4-tuple of the piece placement, the side to move, the castling rights and the en passant target square. However, if the legal moves of that position include no en passant move, the en passant target square is treated as none.
- The repetition count is the number of positions in the position sequence of the game whose position key equals the position key of the current position (the current position itself included).
- The state of the current position is "checkmate" if the side to move is in check and has no legal moves, "stalemate" if it is not in check and has no legal moves, "check" if it is in check and has legal moves, and "normal" otherwise.
- The draw marks are those of the following three that apply to the current position, listed in this order.
  "fifty": the halfmove clock is 100 or more.
  "threefold": the repetition count is 3 or more.
  "insufficient": there is no pawn, rook or queen on the board (of either color). In addition, there is at most one piece other than kings in total, or all pieces other than kings are bishops and their squares are either all dark squares or all light squares.
- The result of the game is, if the state is "checkmate", "1-0" when black is to move and "0-1" when white is to move. Otherwise, it is "1/2-1/2" if the state is "stalemate" or there is at least one draw mark. Otherwise, it is "*".
- Even when there are draw marks the game does not end, and play can continue if there are legal moves.

12. perft
A sequence of d moves (m1, m2, ..., md), where P1 is the position after playing legal move m1 from position P, P2 is the position after playing legal move m2 from P1, and so on, is called a line of depth d from P. Let perft(P, d) be the number of lines of depth d from P, with perft(P, 0) = 1. The last move md of each line of depth d from P (a legal move in position P(d-1)) is called a leaf move; compute the following seven numbers.
  nodes: the number of leaf moves (this equals perft(P, d))
  captures: the number of leaf moves that are captures (en passant included)
  ep: the number of leaf moves that are en passant
  castles: the number of leaf moves that are castling
  promotions: the number of leaf moves that are promotions (moves with different promotion piece types are counted as different moves)
  checks: the number of leaf moves after which, in the resulting position, the side to move is in check
  mates: the number of leaf moves after which, in the resulting position, the side to move is in check and has no legal moves
Draw marks are not considered. A line that reaches a position with no legal moves partway does not reach depth d and is not counted.

13. FEN
FEN is a string representing a position, consisting of the following fields (1) to (6) separated by single spaces.
  (1) Piece placement: the ranks in the order rank 8, rank 7, ..., rank 1, separated by "/". Each rank lists its squares in order from the a-file to the h-file, writing a square that holds a piece as that piece's letter and a run of consecutive empty squares as a single digit ("1" to "8") giving their number.
  (2) Side to move: "w" for white, "b" for black.
  (3) Castling rights: the rights held, among "K", "Q", "k" and "q", listed in this order. "-" if there are none.
  (4) En passant target square: its square name. "-" if there is none.
  (5) Halfmove clock.
  (6) Move number.
When writing FEN, a run of consecutive empty squares is always merged into a single digit. In (4), write the en passant target square of the position, regardless of whether there is a legal en passant move.
When LOAD reads FEN, take the arguments in order as fields (1) to (6), check the following [1] to [9] in this order, and the quoted string of the first one that applies is the reason.
  [1] "fields": there are not exactly 6 arguments.
  [2] "placement": splitting (1) at "/" (an empty part also counts as one part) does not give exactly 8 parts, or one of them is not a valid rank. A rank is read character by character from the left, where a digit "1" to "8" stands for that many empty squares and a piece letter (one of "K", "Q", "R", "B", "N", "P", "k", "q", "r", "b", "n" and "p") stands for a square holding that piece; it is a valid rank if the number of squares read is exactly 8. However, a rank in which a digit is immediately followed by a digit, and a rank that contains any other character, are not valid.
  [3] "kings": at least one of the number of white kings and the number of black kings is not 1.
  [4] "pawns": there is a pawn on rank 1 or rank 8.
  [5] "side": (2) is neither "w" nor "b".
  [6] "castling": (3) is not "-" and is also not one or more of "K", "Q", "k" and "q", without duplicates, listed in this order. Or, for some right contained in (3), the king and the rook are not on their original squares. The original squares are: for "K", the white king on e1 and a white rook on h1; for "Q", the white king on e1 and a white rook on a1; for "k", the black king on e8 and a black rook on h8; for "q", the black king on e8 and a black rook on a8.
  [7] "en passant": (4) is not "-" and one of the following applies. It is not a square name. White is to move, and the square is not on rank 6, or the square is not empty, or the square on rank 7 of the same file is not empty, or the square on rank 5 of the same file does not hold a black pawn. Black is to move, and the square is not on rank 3, or the square is not empty, or the square on rank 2 of the same file is not empty, or the square on rank 4 of the same file does not hold a white pawn.
  [8] "clocks": (5) is not a valid number from 0 to 999, or (6) is not a valid number from 1 to 999, or (4) is a square name but the value of (5) is not 0.
  [9] "check": in the position read, the side that is not to move is in check.
If none of them applies, the position read is the position that the FEN represents.
A number is valid when it is a string consisting only of "0" to "9", its first character is not "0" unless the string is "0" itself, and its value lies in the specified range. The same applies to numeric command arguments.

14. Commands
The arguments, behavior and output of each command are as follows.

NEW
Reset the game to the initial state and print "OK".

LOAD <f1> <f2> <f3> <f4> <f5> <f6>
Read the arguments as the FEN fields as described in section 13. If a reason is determined, print "ERROR bad fen (<reason>)" and leave the game unchanged. If there is no reason, make the game one whose start position is the position read, whose list of moves played is empty, and whose start position was set by LOAD, and print "OK". This processing applies whatever the number of arguments is.

FEN
Print the FEN of the current position.

BOARD
Print the board in 9 lines. Lines 1 to 8 are for the ranks whose rank digit r is 8, 7, ..., 1 respectively, and each consists of r followed by, for each square from the a-file to the h-file, one space and the character of that square (the piece letter if there is a piece, "." if it is empty). Line 9 is "  a b c d e f g h" (two spaces followed by a to h separated by single spaces).

MOVE <w1> <w2> ...
There are one or more arguments. For k = 1, 2, ... in order, read the k-th argument wk in the current position as described in section 8. If a reason is determined, print "ILLEGAL move <k>: <wk> (<reason>)" and finish processing MOVE. The remaining arguments are ignored, and the moves played up to that point remain. If there is no reason, play the legal move that wk represents (section 10). After the moves of all the arguments have been played, print "OK".

UCI <w1> <w2> ...
There are one or more arguments. For k = 1, 2, ... in order, read the k-th argument wk in the current position as described in section 9, and if there is no reason, play the legal move that wk represents. When a reason is determined, the remaining arguments are ignored, and the moves played up to that point remain. When processing is finished, print the following lines.
- If this command played one or more moves, one line consisting of "san" followed by the SANs of the moves played, in the order they were played, each written in the form " <SAN>".
- If processing ended because a reason was determined, after that, the line "ILLEGAL move <k>: <wk> (<reason>)".

LEGAL [<s>]
There are zero or one arguments, and if there is one, it is a square name. Sort the SANs of the legal moves of the current position (if the argument is given, only those whose from-square is <s>) in SAN order. With n being their number, print one line consisting of "legal <n>" followed by each SAN written in the form " <SAN>".

STATUS
Print one line consisting of "status <side to move> <state> rep <repetition count>" followed by the draw marks in order, each written in the form " <mark>". <side to move> is "white" if white is to move in the current position and "black" if black is to move. <state> and the draw marks are as in section 11.

UNDO <n>
n is from 1 to 9999. If n is greater than the number of moves played, print "ERROR cannot undo". Otherwise, remove the last n moves from the list of moves played (the current position goes back to the position before the oldest of the removed moves) and print "OK".

PERFT <d>
d is from 1 to 3. Compute the seven numbers of section 12 for depth d from the current position P, and print "perft <d> nodes <nodes> captures <captures> ep <ep> castles <castles> promotions <promotions> checks <checks> mates <mates>".

DIVIDE <d>
d is from 1 to 3. For each legal move m of the current position, let Q be the position after m and compute perft(Q, d - 1); print them one move per line in the form "<SAN> <value>", in SAN order of the SANs of m. Finally, print the sum of those values in the form "total <sum>". If there are no legal moves, print only "total 0".

ATTACKS <s>
There is exactly one argument, and it is a square name. Take the squares of the pieces that attack <s> (section 3) in the current position, split them into white pieces and black pieces, and sort each group in square order. Print one line consisting of "attacks <s> white <number of white pieces>", followed by each square name of the white pieces in the form " <square name>", then " black <number of black pieces>" and each square name of the black pieces in the same form.

PINS
Let X be the side to move in the current position. From the square of X's king, for each of the 8 directions (up, down, left, right and the 4 diagonals), trace the squares in that direction in order, starting from the adjacent square. If the first piece found is a piece of X, and the next piece found is a piece of the opposing color that is a queen, or is a rook when the direction is up, down, left or right, or is a bishop when the direction is diagonal, the first piece is called a "pinned piece" and the next piece a "pinning piece". With n being the number of pinned pieces, print one line consisting of "pins <n>" followed, in square order of the pinned pieces, by entries written in the form " <square name of pinned piece>-<square name of pinning piece>".

HISTORY
Print "history <number of moves played>", then print the moves played from oldest to newest, numbered i = 1, 2, ..., one per line in the form "<i> <UCI notation> <SAN> <captured piece>".

PGN
With R being the result of the game (section 11), print the following lines.
- The first line is [Result "<R>"] (the double quotes inside the brackets are part of the output).
- If the start position of the game was set by LOAD, next print the two lines [SetUp "1"] and [FEN "<FEN of the start position>"].
- The last line consists of the following words for each move in the list of moves played, in order, with R added at the end, separated by single spaces. If white is to move in the position before the move, the two words "<move number>." and the SAN; if black is to move and it is the first move, the two words "<move number>..." and the SAN; if black is to move and it is not the first move, the single word SAN. <move number> is the move number of the position before the move. If there are no moves, this line is just R.

15. Errors
Each command is checked in the following order.
  (1) If the command name is none of those in section 14, print "ERROR unknown command". Uppercase and lowercase letters in command names are distinguished.
  (2) If the number or the values of the arguments do not match the description in section 14, print "ERROR bad arguments". The same applies when a command that takes no arguments (NEW, FEN, BOARD, STATUS, PINS, HISTORY, PGN) is given arguments. However, LOAD is processed as described in section 14 regardless of the number of arguments, and never produces this error.
  (3) Errors and ILLEGAL lines described for each command in section 14 are printed as described there.
If (1) or (2) applies, print only that one line and go on to the next command without changing the game.

[Output format]
For each command, print the lines defined in [Processing], in that order. The output consists only of ASCII characters; no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline. No blank lines are printed. All numbers are written as decimal integers without extra leading zeros.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500
- Each line has from 1 to 200 characters and consists only of printable ASCII characters and spaces.
- An argument consisting only of "0" to "9" has at most 9 characters.
- The value of nodes computed by PERFT and the total printed by DIVIDE are at most 100000.

[Example]
Input:
10
MOVE f3 e5 g4 Qh4
MOVE Qh4#
STATUS
PGN
UNDO 1
LEGAL d8
UCI d8h4 a2a3
HISTORY
FEN
ATTACKS f2
