[Overview]
Write a calculator that handles units of physical quantities (a unit converter with dimensional analysis). It reads a sequence of commands from standard input, performs unit conversion, display of dimensions, reduction and simplification of unit expressions, the four arithmetic operations between quantities, temperature conversion, comparison of quantities, listing and conversion tables of the units of the same dimension, automatic choice of prefixes, splitting into several units, user-defined units, and evaluation of quantity expressions that use variables, and writes the results to standard output.
A unit is represented by the tuple of exponents (its dimension) of the seven base units kg, m, s, A, K, mol, cd, together with an exact factor (a positive rational number) relative to the coherent SI unit. Perform every computation exactly with rational numbers; do not use floating-point numbers.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. The input consists of exactly these N+1 lines. Each command consists of a command name and zero or more arguments, separated by single spaces; no line has leading or trailing spaces, and there are no empty lines. There are 21 command forms, listed below ([TO e3] and [TO e] are optional parts).

  DEFINE name = v e
  UNDEF name
  DEFS
  CONVERT v e1 TO e2
  INVERT v e1 TO e2
  DIM e
  REDUCE e
  SIMPLIFY e
  ADD v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
  SUB v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
  MUL v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
  DIV v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
  TEMP v s1 TO s2
  COMPARE v1 e1 v2 e2
  LIST a
  TABLE v e
  AUTO v e
  SPLIT v e TO list
  LET name = q
  EVAL q [TO e]
  VARS

The arguments mean the following.
- v, v1, v2 are "numbers", e, e1, e2, e3 are "unit expressions", and q is a "quantity expression". How each of them is written is defined in the subsections "== Numbers ==", "== Unit expressions ==" and "== Quantity expressions ==" of [Processing].
- name is the name of a unit or of a variable, s1 and s2 are strings denoting temperature scales, and a is the name of a kind of quantity or a unit expression. list consists of at least 2 and at most 5 "parts" joined by single "," (comma) characters; each part is treated as a unit expression.
- "=" and "TO" are arguments consisting of exactly that string.
Some lines have a command name that is none of the 21 above. If the command name is one of the 21, the number of arguments and the positions of "=" and "TO" always match the forms above. ADD, SUB, MUL and DIV have 4 or 6 arguments; when there are 6, the fifth argument is TO. EVAL has 1 or 3 arguments; when there are 3, the second argument is TO. Whether the argument values are valid is checked as described in [Processing].

[Processing]
Process the commands in order starting from the first one, and output the result of each command as described in [Output format]. Adding and removing units with DEFINE and UNDEF, and setting variables with LET, affect the subsequent commands. Apart from these, no command affects later commands.

== Notation for rational numbers and integers ==
- Fraction notation: write the rational number as an irreducible fraction p/q (q>=1), and write p if q=1, otherwise p/q. A negative number has a leading "-", and 0 is written as 0 (examples: 5/2, -1/3, 1000, 0).
- Decimal notation: write the absolute value of the rational number x as an irreducible fraction p/q, let W be the quotient of p divided by q, r the remainder, and Z the quotient of r*10^6 divided by q. Write W in decimal, followed by "." and Z written as a 6-digit decimal number (padded with leading zeros when it has fewer digits). If x is negative, prefix "-" (even when both W and Z are 0). In other words, it is the value of x with the digits from the 7th decimal place onward truncated (examples: 25/2 is 12.500000, 2/3 is 0.666666, -160/9 is -17.777777, -1/1000000000000 is -0.000000, 0 is 0.000000).
- Quantity notation: for a value x and a unit string u, the string "<fraction notation of x> <u> (<decimal notation of x>)" (example: 78125/25146 mi (3.106855)).
- An integer is written in decimal without extra leading zeros, with a leading "-" if it is negative.

== Dimensions ==
A dimension is the tuple of the (integer) exponents of each of the seven base units kg, m, s, A, K, mol, cd. Below, this order is called the "base-unit order".
- The sum and the difference of two dimensions are the exponent-wise sum and difference; k times a dimension (k an integer) multiplies each exponent by k.
- The dimension whose exponents are all 0 is called "dimensionless".
- Dimension notation: the base units with a non-zero exponent, in base-unit order, each written as the symbol alone if the exponent is 1 and as "symbol^exponent" otherwise (e.g. m^2, s^-2), separated by single spaces. The dimension notation of dimensionless is "1" (examples: kg m^2 s^-2, s^-1 mol, kg^-1 m^-2 s^4 A^2, 1).

== Unit list and user units ==
Units defined with DEFINE are called "user units". User units cannot take a prefix. The "unit list" consists of the built-in units in table order, followed by the user units in the order in which they were defined. A user unit removed with UNDEF is removed from the list. When a unit is defined again with a removed name, that unit is added at the end of the list.

== Resolving units ==
A name t is "resolved" as a unit as follows.
(1) If the unit list has a unit whose symbol is t, it is that unit.
(2) Otherwise, if t is the concatenation, in this order, of the symbol p of some prefix and the symbol u of a built-in unit that can take a prefix, it is the unit whose factor is (factor of p)*(factor of u) and whose dimension is the dimension of u. There is at most one such pair of p and u.
(3) Otherwise, t cannot be resolved.
For example, km resolves by (2) to a unit with factor 1000 and dimension m, and kg to a unit with factor 1 and dimension kg. dC and dF resolve by (1) to the temperature-difference units, not to decicoulomb or decifarad. min is the minute and h is the hour. kkm (only one prefix is allowed), kmin and Mt (units that cannot take a prefix), Kg (uppercase and lowercase letters are distinguished) and d cannot be resolved.

== Unit expressions ==
A unit expression is an expression that combines units; it has a factor and a dimension.
A unit expression is read from the beginning and split into the following "tokens".
  Identifier: a longest run of consecutive letters (A to Z, a to z).
  Digit string: a longest run of consecutive digits (0 to 9).
  Punctuation: one of the single characters "*", "/", "^", "(", ")", "-".
  Invalid token: a single character that matches none of the above.
The syntax of a unit expression is as follows.
  - A unit expression is one or more "terms" separated by "*" or "/".
  - A term is an "atom" alone, or an atom, "^" and an "exponent" in this order.
  - An atom is one of: an identifier; a digit string whose first character is not 0; or "(", a unit expression and ")" in this order.
  - An exponent is a digit string alone, or "-" and a digit string in this order.
A unit expression is checked in the following order, and the first error found is reported.
(1) Syntax: if the token sequence does not follow the syntax, the error is "ERROR SYNTAX <unit expression> AT <position>". <position> is determined as follows. Adding tokens one at a time from the beginning, check whether the token sequence so far is an initial part of some valid unit expression (a whole valid unit expression included); for the first token for which it is not, <position> is the position within the unit expression of the first character of that token (the first character being position 1). If it remains an initial part through the last token (the unit expression ends prematurely), <position> is END. For example, m**s gives AT 3, (m gives AT END, m) gives AT 2, 2m gives AT 2, 0*m gives AT 1, m^2^3 gives AT 4, and m^2.5 gives AT 4.
(2) Identifiers and exponents: check the identifiers and exponents that appear in the unit expression from left to right. If an identifier cannot be resolved, the error is "ERROR UNKNOWN UNIT <identifier>". If the value of an exponent (the digit string read as a decimal number, negated if there is a "-") is none of -3, -2, -1, 1, 2, 3, the error is "ERROR BAD EXPONENT <exponent>", where <exponent> is the string exactly as written, including the "-". For example, m^0 gives BAD EXPONENT 0 and m^-04 gives BAD EXPONENT -04 (m^03 is valid and the same as m^3).
If there is no error, the factor and dimension of the unit expression are computed as follows.
  - Identifier atom: the factor and dimension of the resolved unit.
  - Digit-string atom: the factor is that integer and the dimension is dimensionless.
  - Atom "(" unit expression ")": the factor and dimension of the inner unit expression.
  - Term atom "^" exponent: with k the value of the exponent, raise the factor of the atom to the k-th power (if k is negative, raise the reciprocal of the factor to the |k|-th power), and multiply the dimension by k.
  - Unit expression: starting from factor 1 and dimensionless, process the terms from left to right: for the first term and a term after "*", multiply by its factor and add its dimension; for a term after "/", divide by its factor and subtract its dimension.
For example, km/h has factor 5/18 and dimension m s^-1, and L/(100*km) has factor 1/100000000 and dimension m^2.

== Numbers ==
A number is a string of one of the following three forms, or such a string preceded by a single "-". If there is a "-", the sign of the value is inverted. Here a digit string is a sequence of one or more digits (leading zeros are allowed).
  Integer: a digit string alone (examples: 7, 007, 0). Its value is that integer.
  Decimal: a digit string, "." and a digit string concatenated in this order (examples: 0.50, 1013.25). Its value is the value of the written decimal number.
  Fraction: a digit string, "/" and a digit string concatenated in this order (example: 3/6). Its value is the value of the first digit string divided by the value of the second digit string.
Strings that are not of these forms, and fractions whose second digit string has the value 0, are invalid numbers (examples: 1., .5, 1/0, 1e3, --1, +1, 1,5, -).

== Quantity expressions ==
A quantity expression is an expression that combines numerals, units and variables with operations; it has a value (a rational number) and a dimension. The value is the numerical value of the quantity expressed in the coherent SI unit.
A quantity expression is read from the beginning and split into the following tokens.
  Numeral: a longest run of consecutive digits. However, if it is immediately followed by "." and that "." is immediately followed by a digit, the "." and the longest run of digits following it are included in the same single numeral (examples: 12, 1.5, 007).
  Identifier: a longest run of consecutive letters.
  Unit: when "[" is immediately followed by one or more characters other than "]" and then by a "]", the sequence from the "[" up to the first "]" after it. The string between "[" and "]" is called the "unit string".
  Punctuation: one of the single characters "+", "-", "*", "/", "^", "(", ")", ",".
  Invalid token: a single character that matches none of the above (such as a "[" that does not form a unit token, a "]" or a ".").
The syntax of a quantity expression is as follows.
  - A quantity expression is one or more "products" separated by "+" or "-".
  - A product is one or more "unaries" separated by "*" or "/".
  - A unary is "-" followed by a unary, or a "power".
  - A power is a "primary" alone, or a primary, "^" and an exponent in this order. An exponent is a numeral not containing "." alone, or "-" and a numeral not containing "." in this order.
  - A primary is one of the following.
    A numeral alone.
    A numeral immediately followed by a unit.
    A unit alone.
    An identifier alone (a variable reference).
    An identifier, "(", one or more quantity expressions separated by ",", and ")" in this order (a function call).
    "(", a quantity expression and ")" in this order.
A quantity expression is checked in the following order.
(1) Syntax: checked in the same way as for unit expressions; if it does not follow the syntax, the error is "ERROR SYNTAX <quantity expression> AT <position>". For example, 2^2^2 gives AT 4, 3x gives AT 2, 1. gives AT 2, abs() gives AT 5, and 1+ gives AT END.
(2) Evaluation: if the syntax is valid, compute the value and dimension of the quantity expression by the following rules. If an error occurs during the computation, report that error and stop the computation.
  - Order: every operation is performed after the values and dimensions of all its operands have been computed (except that, for a function call, the name and the number of arguments are checked first, as described below). For an operation with two operands, the left operand is computed first. Operations are grouped according to the syntax; "+" and "-", and likewise "*" and "/", associate from left to right. For example, 10-4-3 is (10-4)-3, 1+2*3 is 1+(2*3), and -2^2 is -(2^2).
  - Numeral alone: the value is the decimal number denoted by the numeral, and the dimension is dimensionless.
  - Numeral and unit: check the unit string as a unit expression and, if it has an error, report that error (in an ERROR SYNTAX line, <unit expression> and <position> refer to the unit string). The value is (the decimal number denoted by the numeral)*(the factor of the unit expression), and the dimension is the dimension of the unit expression.
  - Unit alone: treated in the same way as a numeral and unit, with the numeral taken to be 1.
  - Identifier alone: the value and dimension of the variable with that name. If there is no variable with that name, the error is "ERROR UNKNOWN VARIABLE <identifier>".
  - "(" quantity expression ")": the value and dimension of the inner quantity expression.
  - Power: after computing the value and dimension of the primary, check the exponent in the same way as an exponent of a unit expression (ERROR BAD EXPONENT). Let k be the value of the exponent; if the value of the primary is 0 and k is negative, the error is "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". Raise the value to the k-th power (if k is negative, raise the reciprocal of the value to the |k|-th power), and multiply the dimension by k.
  - Unary "-": inverts the sign of the value. The dimension does not change.
  - a+b and a-b: if the dimensions of a and b differ, the error is "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of a> vs <dimension notation of b>". The value is the sum or the difference, and the dimension is the dimension of a.
  - a*b: the value is the product, and the dimension is the sum of the dimensions of a and b.
  - a/b: if the value of b is 0, the error is "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". The value is the quotient, and the dimension is the difference of the dimensions of a and b.
  - Function call: first, if the name is none of abs, sqrt, min, max, the error is "ERROR UNKNOWN FUNCTION <identifier>"; if the number of arguments (quantity expressions separated by ",") is not 1 for abs and sqrt, or not 2 for min and max, the error is "ERROR BAD ARGUMENTS <identifier>". Next, compute the values and dimensions of the arguments from left to right, and determine the result as follows.
    abs(a): the value is the absolute value of the value of a, and the dimension is the dimension of a.
    sqrt(a): if the value of a is negative, "ERROR NEGATIVE ROOT"; if the dimension of a has an odd exponent, "ERROR ODD DIMENSION <dimension notation of a>"; if, with the value of a written as an irreducible fraction p/q, either p or q is not a perfect square (the square of an integer >= 0), "ERROR NOT EXACT" (checked in this order). Otherwise, the value is sqrt(p)/sqrt(q), and the dimension is the dimension of a with each exponent divided by 2.
    min(a,b) and max(a,b): if the dimensions of a and b differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of a> vs <dimension notation of b>". min takes the one with the smaller value as the result, and max the one with the larger value (if the values are equal, either gives the same result).
Variables are created by LET and have a name and a pair of a value and a dimension. Variable names are unrelated to unit symbols (the variable m and the unit m are different things). Since a variable holds only a value and a dimension, it is not changed by DEFINE or UNDEF executed after the variable was created.

== Simplified unit ==
The "simplified unit" of a dimension d is the symbol of the first unit whose factor is exactly 1 and whose dimension is d, scanning from the beginning the unit list as it is when the command is processed. If there is no such unit, it is the dimension notation of d. For example, when there are no user units, the simplified unit of the dimension kg m^2 s^-2 is J, that of m s^-1 is m s^-1, that of kg is kg (the factor of g is not 1), that of K is K (it comes before dC), and that of dimensionless is 1.

== Character code order ==
The "character code order" of strings compares two strings character by character from the beginning and puts first the one whose first differing character has the smaller ASCII code; if one string is an initial part of the other, the shorter one comes first (uppercase letters come before lowercase letters).

== Error checking ==
If the command name is none of the 21, output "ERROR UNKNOWN COMMAND <command name>".
Otherwise, first check the arguments from left to right. Each kind of argument is checked as follows ("=" and "TO" are not checked).
- Number: if it is invalid, "ERROR BAD NUMBER <number>".
- Unit expression: as described in "== Unit expressions ==".
- Quantity expression: as described in "== Quantity expressions ==", check the syntax and then evaluate.
- name (DEFINE and LET): if it is not a string of length 1 to 12 consisting of letters only, "ERROR BAD NAME <name>". For LET, also "ERROR BAD NAME <name>" when name is one of abs, sqrt, min, max. The name of UNDEF is not checked here.
- s1, s2, a, list: as described for each command.
When an error is found, output only the error line of the first error found and do nothing else for that command (neither the unit list nor the variables change). If the arguments have no error, perform the "Check" steps given in the description of each command in the order written; if one of them applies, likewise output only its error line.
In error lines, <command name>, <number>, <name>, <unit expression>, <quantity expression>, <identifier> and <scale> are replaced by the string exactly as written in the input.

== Commands ==
Below, the values of the numbers v, v1, v2 are written x, x1, x2, and the factors of the unit expressions e, e1, e2, e3 are written f, f1, f2, f3 and their dimensions d, d1, d2, d3. In the output descriptions, an item enclosed in < > stands for the fraction notation of the value if it is a value, and for the string itself if it is an argument (e, name, etc.) or a string (U, etc.). Arguments are the strings exactly as written in the input.

DEFINE name = v e
Check: if name is the name of a kind of quantity, or can be resolved as a unit (matches (1) or (2) of "== Resolving units =="), "ERROR NAME TAKEN <name>". If x<=0, "ERROR NOT POSITIVE <v>".
Action: append a user unit with symbol name, factor x*f and dimension d at the end of the unit list. Output "DEFINE <name> factor=<x*f> dim=<dimension notation of d> kind=<kind name of d>".

UNDEF name
Check: if name is the symbol of a built-in unit, "ERROR BUILTIN UNIT <name>". If there is no user unit whose symbol is name, "ERROR NOT DEFINED <name>".
Action: remove the user unit name from the unit list and output "UNDEF <name>".

DEFS
Action: output "DEFS count=<number of user units>", followed by the user units in unit-list order, one line each: "DEF <symbol> = <value> <unit expression> factor=<factor> dim=<dimension notation>". <value> and <unit expression> are the fraction notation of the value of the number v and the unit expression e (as written) of the DEFINE that defined that unit.

CONVERT v e1 TO e2
Check: if d1 and d2 differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of d1> vs <dimension notation of d2>".
Action: let r=(x*f1)/f2 and output "CONVERT <x> <e1> = <quantity notation of r and e2>".

INVERT v e1 TO e2
Conversion between quantities that are reciprocals of each other (frequency and period, fuel economy in km/L and in L/(100*km), etc.).
Check: if d1 is dimensionless, or if the sum of d1 and d2 is not dimensionless, "ERROR NOT RECIPROCAL <dimension notation of d1> vs <dimension notation of d2>". If x=0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO".
Action: let r=(1/(x*f1))/f2 and output "INVERT <x> <e1> = <quantity notation of r and e2>".

DIM e
Action: output "DIM <e> = <dimension notation of d> factor=<f> kind=<kind name of d>".

REDUCE e
Reduces a unit expression, treating the unit names as the strings they are written as.
Action: compute the "reduced form" of the unit expression, as a pair of a coefficient (a rational number) and an exponent (an integer) for each name, as follows. Names are distinguished as strings (km and m, and J and N, are different names).
  - Identifier atom: the coefficient is 1 and the exponent of that name is 1.
  - Digit-string atom: the coefficient is that integer, and there are no names.
  - Atom "(" unit expression ")": the reduced form of the inner unit expression.
  - Term atom "^" exponent: with k the value of the exponent, raise the coefficient of the atom to the k-th power and multiply the exponent of each name by k.
  - Unit expression: starting from coefficient 1 and no names, process the terms from left to right: for the first term and a term after "*", multiply by its coefficient and add the exponent of each name; for a term after "/", divide by its coefficient and subtract the exponent of each name.
Next, order the names by their first appearance in the unit expression, remove the names whose exponent is 0, and write each remaining name as the name alone if its exponent is 1 and as "name^exponent" otherwise, separated by single spaces. If the coefficient is not 1, add its fraction notation at the front (even when no names remain). If nothing remains (no names remain and the coefficient is 1), use "1". Taking this as <reduced form>, output "REDUCE <e> = <reduced form>". For example, N*m/N becomes m, km/h*h becomes km, L/(100*km) becomes 1/100 L km^-1, (m/s)^-2*m becomes m^-1 s^2, 2*m/m becomes 2, and m/m becomes 1.

SIMPLIFY e
Action: output "SIMPLIFY <e> = <quantity notation of f and the simplified unit of d>". For example, the output of SIMPLIFY km/h is "SIMPLIFY km/h = 5/18 m s^-1 (0.277777)".

ADD v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
SUB v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
Check: if d1 and d2 differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of d1> vs <dimension notation of d2>". If TO e3 is present and d1 and d3 differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of d1> vs <dimension notation of d3>".
Action: the "result unit expression" is e3 if TO e3 is present and e1 otherwise; let g be its factor. For ADD let r=(x1*f1+x2*f2)/g, and for SUB let r=(x1*f1-x2*f2)/g, and output "ADD <x1> <e1> + <x2> <e2> = <quantity notation of r and the result unit expression>". For SUB, the command name is SUB and "-" is written instead of "+".

MUL v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
DIV v1 e1 v2 e2 [TO e3]
Below, let D=d1+d2 for MUL and D=d1-d2 for DIV.
Check: for DIV, if x2=0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". If TO e3 is present and D and d3 differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of D> vs <dimension notation of d3>".
Action: for MUL, let P=x1*x2 and S=(x1*f1)*(x2*f2), and let the "combined unit expression" U be the string formed by concatenating e1, "*" and e2 in this order. For DIV, let P=x1/x2 and S=(x1*f1)/(x2*f2), and let U be the string formed by concatenating e1, "/" and E in this order. Here E is "(", e2 and ")" concatenated in this order if e2 contains "*" or "/", and e2 otherwise (example: for DIV 1 J 2 N*m, U is J/(N*m)).
If TO e3 is present, let r=S/f3 and output "MUL <x1> <e1> * <x2> <e2> = <quantity notation of r and e3>". If TO e3 is absent, output "MUL <x1> <e1> * <x2> <e2> = <P> <U> = <quantity notation of S and the simplified unit of D>". For DIV, the command name is DIV and "/" is written instead of the "*" between <e1> and <x2>.

TEMP v s1 TO s2
Conversion between scales of absolute temperature. There are four scales: C (degrees Celsius), F (degrees Fahrenheit), K (kelvins) and R (degrees Rankine).
Argument check: s1 and s2 are checked in this order, after v. If a scale is none of C, F, K, R, the error is "ERROR UNKNOWN SCALE <scale>".
Action: let k, the temperature x (on scale s1) expressed in kelvins, be x+5463/20 if s1 is C, (x+45967/100)*5/9 if F, x if K, and x*5/9 if R.
Check: if k<0, "ERROR BELOW ABSOLUTE ZERO".
Action (continued): let r be k-5463/20 if s2 is C, k*9/5-45967/100 if F, k if K, and k*9/5 if R, and output "TEMP <x> <s1> = <quantity notation of r and s2>".
(5463/20=273.15 and 45967/100=459.67. Temperature differences are converted not with this command but with CONVERT and the units K, dC, dF.)

COMPARE v1 e1 v2 e2
Check: if d1 and d2 differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of d1> vs <dimension notation of d2>".
Action: let A=x1*f1 and B=x2*f2, and let the "relation" be "<" if A<B, "=" if A=B, and ">" if A>B. Output "COMPARE <x1> <e1> <relation> <x2> <e2> ratio=<ratio>". <ratio> is the fraction notation of A/B if B!=0, and undefined if B=0.

LIST a
Argument check: if a is the name of a kind of quantity, let D be the dimension of that kind. Otherwise, check a as a unit expression (if it has an error, report it as described in "== Unit expressions ==") and let D be its dimension.
Action: select all units in the unit list whose dimension is D, and sort them in increasing order of factor, units with equal factors in character code order of their symbols. Output "LIST <a> dim=<dimension notation of D> kind=<kind name of D> count=<number of selected units>", followed by, in sorted order, one line per unit: "UNIT <symbol> factor=<factor> origin=<origin> prefix=<prefix flag>". <origin> is builtin for a built-in unit and user for a user unit. <prefix flag> is yes if the unit can take a prefix and no if it cannot.

TABLE v e
Action: select and sort the units whose dimension is d in the same way as LIST. Output "TABLE <x> <e> dim=<dimension notation of d> count=<number of selected units>", followed by, in sorted order, for each unit, with u its symbol and g its factor and r=(x*f)/g, the line "ROW <quantity notation of r and u>".

AUTO v e
Expresses a quantity in a unit with a prefix.
Check: scanning the built-in units in table order, let the "reference unit" B be the first unit that can take a prefix and whose dimension is d. If there is no such unit, "ERROR NO PREFIXABLE UNIT <dimension notation of d>".
Action: let w=(x*f)/(factor of B), and determine the integer k as follows.
  - If w=0, k=0.
  - If |w|>=1, start from k=0 and add 3 to k while k<9 and |w|>=10^(k+3).
  - If 0<|w|<1, start from k=0 and subtract 3 from k while k>-9 and |w|<10^k.
Let z=w/10^k. For k equal to 9, 6, 3, 0, -3, -6, -9, take as the unit string the symbol of B preceded by G, M, k, (nothing), m, u, n respectively, and output "AUTO <x> <e> = <quantity notation of z and the unit string>". For example, 0.0075 m becomes 15/2 mm, 2500 g becomes 5/2 kg (the reference unit of mass is g), 30000 GW becomes 30000 GW, and 0.2 nm becomes 1/5 nm.

SPLIT v e TO list
Splits a quantity into whole numbers of units, starting from the largest unit.
Argument check: check each part of list as a unit expression, from left to right.
Check: if x<0, "ERROR NEGATIVE VALUE". Check the dimension of each unit expression of list from left to right; if one differs from d, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of d> vs <dimension notation of that unit expression>". If the factors of the unit expressions of list are not strictly decreasing from left to right (if some unit expression has a factor less than or equal to that of the unit expression immediately to its right), "ERROR UNIT ORDER".
Action: let the unit expressions of list be u1, u2, ..., un from the left, and their factors g1, g2, ..., gn. Let T=x*f, and for i=1, 2, ..., n-1 in this order, let ci=floor(T/gi) (the largest integer not exceeding T/gi) and then T=T-ci*gi. Finally let w=T/gn. Output "SPLIT <x> <e> = <c1> <u1> <c2> <u2> ... <c(n-1)> <u(n-1)> <quantity notation of w and un>". Each ci is an integer, and each ui is the part as written. For example, the output of SPLIT 50 in TO yd,ft,in is "SPLIT 50 in = 1 yd 1 ft 2 in (2.000000)".

LET name = q
Action: let V be the value and D the dimension of the quantity expression q, and set the value and dimension of the variable named name to V and D (if a variable with that name already exists, it is replaced). Output "LET <name> = <quantity notation of V and the simplified unit of D>".

EVAL q [TO e]
Let V be the value and D the dimension of the quantity expression q.
Check: if TO e is present and D and d differ, "ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation of D> vs <dimension notation of d>".
Action: if TO e is present, let r=V/f and output "EVAL <q> = <quantity notation of r and e>". If TO e is absent, output "EVAL <q> = <quantity notation of V and the simplified unit of D>".

VARS
Action: output "VARS count=<number of variables>", followed by the variables in character code order of their names, one line each: "VAR <variable name> = <quantity notation of the value and the simplified unit of the dimension>".

[Output format]
- For each command, output only the lines described in [Processing]. Every command outputs at least one line.
- Words and values within a line are separated by single spaces, as described. Lines have no leading or trailing spaces, and no empty lines are output. Every line ends with a newline.
- There are the following 24 kinds of error lines (the parts in < > are replaced by the strings described in [Processing]).
  ERROR UNKNOWN COMMAND <command name>
  ERROR BAD NUMBER <number>
  ERROR BAD NAME <name>
  ERROR SYNTAX <unit expression or quantity expression> AT <position>
  ERROR UNKNOWN UNIT <identifier>
  ERROR BAD EXPONENT <exponent>
  ERROR UNKNOWN SCALE <scale>
  ERROR UNKNOWN VARIABLE <identifier>
  ERROR UNKNOWN FUNCTION <identifier>
  ERROR BAD ARGUMENTS <identifier>
  ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO
  ERROR NEGATIVE ROOT
  ERROR ODD DIMENSION <dimension notation>
  ERROR NOT EXACT
  ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH <dimension notation> vs <dimension notation>
  ERROR NAME TAKEN <name>
  ERROR NOT POSITIVE <number>
  ERROR BUILTIN UNIT <name>
  ERROR NOT DEFINED <name>

  ERROR UNIT ORDER

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500
- Each line is at least 1 and at most 200 characters long and consists only of printable ASCII characters (character codes 0x21 to 0x7E) and spaces.
- The length of each digit string in numbers and unit expressions (including the unit strings inside quantity expressions), and the length of each run of digits in a numeral of a quantity expression, is at least 1 and at most 9. In a decimal number, and in a numeral of a quantity expression that contains ".", the total number of digits is at most 12.
- Each part of list is at least 1 character long.
- There are at most 30 user units at any one time, and at most 50 variables.
- The test data are constructed so that, when computed as follows, every integer that appears (including values before reduction) has an absolute value less than 2^53. Values are always held as irreducible fractions a/b (b>=1). Each formula in [Processing] is computed in the order written. The sum and the difference of two values a/b and c/d are obtained as (a*d+c*b)/(b*d) and (a*d-c*b)/(b*d), the product as (a*c)/(b*d), and the quotient as (a*d)/(b*c) (if the denominator becomes negative, the signs of the numerator and the denominator are inverted), and then reduced to lowest terms; comparisons are decided by comparing a*d and c*b. The k-th power is obtained by multiplying by the value k times (if k is negative, by its reciprocal |k| times), and 10^k is also obtained this way. floor(a/b) is obtained by integer division. The r*10^6 in the decimal notation is also less than 2^53.

[Example]
Input:
14
DEFINE furlong = 220 yd
CONVERT 2 mi TO furlong
CONVERT 72 km/h TO m/s
INVERT 25 Hz TO ms
DIM N*m/s
SIMPLIFY km*N
REDUCE kg*m/(s*kg)
DIV 150 km 2 h
TEMP 212 F TO C
AUTO 0.0035 A
LET v = 90[km/h]
EVAL 1/2*1000[kg]*v^2 TO kJ
SPLIT 4000 s TO h,min,s
CONVERT 1 m TO s

Output:
DEFINE furlong factor=25146/125 dim=m kind=length
CONVERT 2 mi = 16 furlong (16.000000)
CONVERT 72 km/h = 20 m/s (20.000000)
INVERT 25 Hz = 40 ms (40.000000)
DIM N*m/s = kg m^2 s^-3 factor=1 kind=power
SIMPLIFY km*N = 1000 J (1000.000000)
REDUCE kg*m/(s*kg) = m s^-1
DIV 150 km / 2 h = 75 km/h = 125/6 m s^-1 (20.833333)
TEMP 212 F = 100 C (100.000000)
AUTO 7/2000 A = 7/2 mA (3.500000)
LET v = 25 m s^-1 (25.000000)
EVAL 1/2*1000[kg]*v^2 = 625/2 kJ (312.500000)
SPLIT 4000 s = 1 h 6 min 40 s (40.000000)
ERROR DIMENSION MISMATCH m vs s

In this example, 1 furlong=220 yd=25146/125 m and 1 mi=201168/125 m, so 2 mi is 16 furlong. The combined unit expression of DIV is km/h, and 75 km/h expressed in the simplified unit is 125/6 m s^-1. In TEMP, 212 F expressed in kelvins is (212+459.67)*5/9=373.15, which is 100 C. The reference unit of AUTO is A, and since w=7/2000 is at least 10^-3 and less than 10^0, k=-3. The value of EVAL is 1/2*1000*25^2=312500 J, which is expressed in kJ.
