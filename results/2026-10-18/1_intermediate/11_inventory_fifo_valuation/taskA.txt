[Overview]
Write an inventory management program that manages the stock of goods spread over several warehouses and computes inventory value and cost of goods sold with the first-in, first-out method (FIFO). The program reads commands from standard input one line at a time and writes, for each command, a result in the prescribed format to standard output.
All money amounts are handled as integers counted in cents (units of 1/100); floating-point numbers are not used. Every receipt creates a "lot" carrying a unit cost, and a shipment issues stock from the oldest lots first to obtain the cost of goods sold and the gross profit. When a shipment finds too little stock, the shortfall is kept as a backorder (unshipped remainder), which is filled automatically when stock arrives later. In addition, the program handles transfers between warehouses, stocktaking, inventory adjustments, returns, write-downs to market price, lot recall and tracing, disposal of expired stock, and various reports (inventory valuation, lot list, reorder points, gross profit, aging buckets, stock card, and so on).

[Input format]
The first line contains N, the number of command lines that follow. Lines 2 through N+1 each contain one command. Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by single ASCII spaces, with no whitespace at the start or end of the line. The program ends after all N commands have been processed.

The token formats are as follows.
- Warehouse code: 1 to 8 characters that start with an uppercase letter (A-Z) and consist only of uppercase letters and digits (0-9) (e.g. TKY, W2).
- Item code: 1 to 12 characters that start with an uppercase letter and consist only of uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen "-" (e.g. BOLT-M6).
- Item name: 1 to 20 characters that start with a lowercase letter (a-z) and consist only of lowercase letters, digits and the underscore "_" (e.g. hex_bolt_m6).
- Money amount: an integer part and a fractional part joined by ".". The integer part is a decimal number from 0 to 99999 with no extra leading zeros. The fractional part has exactly 2 digits (e.g. 0.07, 12.50, 333.43). The money amount "a.bc" represents the integer a*100+bc counted in cents. Unit costs, selling prices and market prices are written in this format.
- Adjustment quantity: "+" or "-" immediately followed by a decimal non-negative integer with no extra leading zeros (e.g. +40, -25, -0).
- Other numbers (day, receipt day, quantity, actual quantity, reorder level, order number, lot number, number of days, k): decimal non-negative integers with no extra leading zeros.

There are the following 26 commands. Each <...> part is a token of the format above.
DAY <day>
WAREHOUSE <warehouse code>
ITEM <item code> <reorder level> <item name>
RECEIVE <warehouse code> <item code> <quantity> <unit cost> <receipt day>
SHIP <warehouse code> <item code> <quantity> <selling price>
CANCEL <order number>
TRANSFER <source warehouse code> <destination warehouse code> <item code> <quantity>
Either ADJUST <warehouse code> <item code> <adjustment quantity starting with "+"> <unit cost> or ADJUST <warehouse code> <item code> <adjustment quantity starting with "-">
RETURN <order number> <quantity> <unit cost>
COUNT <warehouse code> <item code> <actual quantity>
MARKET <item code> <market price>
RECALL <lot number>
EXPIRE <number of days>
Either VALUE or VALUE <warehouse code>
LOTS <warehouse code> <item code>
REORDER
ITEMS
MARGIN

[Processing]
== State and initial state ==
- Current day: initially 1. It is advanced by DAY.
- Warehouses: initially there are none. They are registered by WAREHOUSE.
- Items: initially there are none. They are registered by ITEM. Each item has a reorder level, an item name and a last receipt cost. The last receipt cost is the unit cost of the last successful RECEIVE for that item; for an item with no successful RECEIVE yet it is "none".
- Lots: created by RECEIVE, ADJUST (+), RETURN, and COUNT (when the actual quantity is larger). Lots are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in order of creation with a serial number shared by all items (the lot number). Each lot has an item, a lot day, an initial quantity, an initial unit cost, a creating warehouse and a creation kind. The lot day is the receipt day for a lot created by RECEIVE, and otherwise the current day at the time of creation. The creation kind is one of receive, adjust, return, count, according to the command that created the lot.
- Stock lots: one lot may be split over several warehouses by transfers between warehouses. The part of a lot that is held in one warehouse is called a "stock lot". A stock lot has a lot number, a lot day, a quantity and a unit cost. A warehouse holds at most one stock lot with a given lot number. When the quantity of a stock lot becomes 0, the stock lot ceases to exist. The unit cost may be changed by MARKET, but stock lots with the same lot number always have equal unit costs.
- Orders: one order is created for each successful SHIP, numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in order of creation (the order number). An "existing order number" is a number that is at least 1 and at most the number of orders created so far. Each order has a warehouse, an item, an order day (the current day when the SHIP was processed), a selling price, an ordered quantity, a shipped quantity, a backorder quantity, a cancelled quantity, a returned quantity, revenue, COGS (cost of goods sold), and an issue record. The issue record is the list of the parts (lot number, quantity, unit cost) issued from stock lots for that order, in the order they were issued.
- Removed quantity: for each lot, the total quantity removed by ADJUST (-), COUNT (when the actual quantity is smaller), EXPIRE and RECALL is recorded. It is initially 0.
- Stock card: each item has a sequence of lines (entries) recording increases and decreases of stock. It is initially empty.
- Cumulative totals: for each of the 8 categories received, returned, adjusted-in, adjusted-out, shipped, written-down, expired, recalled, a total quantity and a total value are kept. All are initially 0.
A command that results in an error changes no state at all; it only outputs one error line.

== Terms and orderings ==
- "Ascending order" of warehouse codes and item codes is the lexicographic order by character code (ASCII) (when one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first).
- The "FIFO order" of the stock lots of one item in one warehouse is ascending order of lot day, and for equal lot days ascending order of lot number.
- The "on-hand quantity" of an item in a warehouse is the total quantity of the stock lots of that item in that warehouse. The "value" of a stock lot is quantity * unit cost.
- The "total on-hand quantity" of an item is the sum of its on-hand quantities over all warehouses, and the "total backorder" of an item is the sum of the backorder quantities of all orders for that item.
- The "age" of a stock lot is the current day minus the lot day.

== FIFO issue ==
To "issue" q units of an item from a warehouse, repeat the following until q becomes 0. Take the first stock lot in FIFO order, let t = min(q, the quantity of that stock lot), take t units out of that stock lot (decrease its quantity by t), and decrease q by t. The list of the parts taken out (lot number, lot day, t, unit cost), in the order they were taken out, is called the "issued parts", and the sum of t * unit cost is called the "issue cost". An issue is performed only when the on-hand quantity of that item in that warehouse is at least q (this is guaranteed by the checks and rules of each command).

== Adding stock and allocating backorders ==
To "add" a stock lot (lot number, lot day, quantity, unit cost) of item s to warehouse w: if w already has a stock lot of s with the same lot number, add the quantity to it; otherwise place it as a new stock lot.
RECEIVE, TRANSFER (destination), ADJUST (+), RETURN, and COUNT (when the actual quantity is larger) add stock, then output the command's output line, and then perform the following "allocation" for that warehouse w and item s. As long as there is an order for w and s with a backorder quantity of 1 or more and the on-hand quantity of s in w is 1 or more, repeat the following.
  Among such orders choose the one with the smallest order number and let f = min(backorder quantity, on-hand quantity). Issue f units of s from w, add f to the order's shipped quantity, subtract f from its backorder quantity, add f * selling price to its revenue and the issue cost to its COGS, and append the issued parts to the end of its issue record. Add (f, issue cost) to the cumulative total shipped and add a fill entry to the stock card. Then output one FILL line.
Because of this rule, for any pair of warehouse and item that has an order with a backorder quantity of 1 or more, the on-hand quantity is always 0.

== Stock card ==
An entry has a day (the current day at that time), a kind, a warehouse, a quantity change and a value change. Where a command description says "add (kind, warehouse, quantity change, value change) to the stock card", append this entry to the end of the stock card of that item. The kind is one of receive, ship, fill, transfer-out, transfer-in, adjust, return, count, writedown, expire, recall. The fill entry added by allocation is (fill, w, -f, -issue cost).

== Processing of each command ==
Errors are checked in the order written, and only the first error that applies is output. The text of the error lines is given in [Output format].
1. DAY d: if d is less than or equal to the current day, it is an invalid day error. Otherwise set the current day to d.
2. WAREHOUSE w: if w is already registered, it is a duplicate warehouse error. Otherwise register w.
3. ITEM s L name: there are no errors. If s is not registered, register it with reorder level L, item name name, and last receipt cost "none". If it is registered, replace only its reorder level and item name with L and name.
4. RECEIVE w s q c d: check in the following order. (a) w is not registered: unknown warehouse, (b) s is not registered: unknown item, (c) q is 0: invalid quantity, (d) d is greater than the current day: future day. If there is no error, create a new lot with lot day d, quantity q, unit cost c and kind receive, add it to w, and set the last receipt cost of s to c. Add (q, q*c) to the cumulative total received and add (receive, w, +q, +q*c) to the stock card. Then perform allocation for w and s.
5. SHIP w s q p: perform the same checks as (a)-(c) of RECEIVE. If there is no error, create a new order (order day = current day, selling price p, ordered quantity q). Let f = min(q, the on-hand quantity of s in w), issue f units of s from w, and set the order's shipped quantity to f, backorder quantity to q-f, revenue to f*p, COGS to the issue cost, and issue record to the issued parts. If f is 1 or more, add (f, issue cost) to the cumulative total shipped and add (ship, w, -f, -issue cost) to the stock card.
6. CANCEL n: if n is not an existing order number, it is an unknown order error; if the order's backorder quantity is 0, it is a no backorder error. Otherwise add the order's backorder quantity to its cancelled quantity and set its backorder quantity to 0.
7. TRANSFER a b s q: check in the following order. (a) a is not registered: unknown warehouse, (b) b is not registered: unknown warehouse, (c) a and b are the same: same warehouse, (d) s is not registered: unknown item, (e) q is 0: invalid quantity, (f) the on-hand quantity of s in a is less than q: insufficient stock. If there is no error, issue q units of s from a, and add the issued parts one by one, in order, to b as stock lots with the same lot number, lot day, quantity and unit cost. Add (transfer-out, a, -q, -issue cost) and (transfer-in, b, +q, +issue cost) to the stock card, in this order. Then perform allocation for b and s.
8. ADJUST w s +q c / ADJUST w s -q: perform the same checks as (a)-(c) of RECEIVE (both "+0" and "-0" are invalid quantity). For "-", next, if the on-hand quantity of s in w is less than q, it is an insufficient stock error.
  For "+": create a new lot with lot day = current day, quantity q, unit cost = the command's unit cost c, and kind adjust, and add it to w. Add (q, q*c) to the cumulative total adjusted-in and add (adjust, w, +q, +q*c) to the stock card. Then perform allocation for w and s.
  For "-": issue q units of s from w, and add the quantity of each issued part to the removed quantity of its lot. Add (q, issue cost) to the cumulative total adjusted-out and add (adjust, w, -q, -issue cost) to the stock card.
9. RETURN n q c: check in the following order. (a) n is not an existing order number: unknown order, (b) q is 0: invalid quantity, (c) q is greater than the order's (shipped quantity - returned quantity): return exceeds shipped. If there is no error, for the order's warehouse w and item s, create a new lot with lot day = current day, quantity q, unit cost c and kind return, and add it to w. Add q to the order's returned quantity, subtract q * selling price from its revenue, and subtract q*c from its COGS. Add (q, q*c) to the cumulative total returned and add (return, w, +q, +q*c) to the stock card. Then perform allocation for w and s (the stock may also be allocated to the backorder of the returned order itself).
10. COUNT w s a: perform the same checks as (a) and (b) of RECEIVE. If there is no error, let h be the on-hand quantity of s in w and do one of the following.
  If a = h: do nothing.
  If a < h: issue h-a units of s from w, and add the quantity of each issued part to the removed quantity of its lot. Add (h-a, issue cost) to the cumulative total adjusted-out and add (count, w, -(h-a), -issue cost) to the stock card.
  If a > h: let c be the last receipt cost of s (c = 0 if it is "none"), create a new lot with lot day = current day, quantity a-h, unit cost c and kind count, and add it to w. Add (a-h, (a-h)*c) to the cumulative total adjusted-in and add (count, w, +(a-h), +(a-h)*c) to the stock card. Then perform allocation for w and s.
11. MARKET s p: if s is not registered, it is an unknown item error. Otherwise examine the warehouses one by one in ascending order, and for every stock lot of s in that warehouse whose unit cost is greater than p, compute the write-down loss quantity*(unit cost - p) and set its unit cost to p. For each warehouse whose total write-down loss is 1 or more, add (writedown, that warehouse, 0, -total write-down loss of that warehouse) to the stock card. Let k be the number of stock lots whose unit cost was changed, u the total quantity of those stock lots, and L the total write-down loss; add (u, L) to the cumulative total written-down. The last receipt cost and the initial unit costs of lots do not change.
12. RECALL n: if n is not the lot number of a created lot, it is an unknown lot error; if no warehouse holds a stock lot with lot number n, it is a nothing to recall error. Otherwise examine the warehouses in ascending order, and for each warehouse that holds a stock lot with lot number n, remove that stock lot and add (recall, that warehouse, -quantity, -value) to the stock card (the one of the item of lot n). Add the total quantity removed to the removed quantity of lot n, and add (total quantity, total value) to the cumulative total recalled.
13. EXPIRE d: there are no errors. Examine, one by one, the pairs of warehouse and item that have stock lots, in ascending order of item, and for the same item in ascending order of warehouse, and remove all stock lots whose age is greater than d. For each pair where one or more stock lots were removed, add the quantity of each removed stock lot to the removed quantity of its lot, add the total quantity and total value removed to the cumulative total expired, and add (expire, that warehouse, -total quantity, -total value) to the stock card.
14. VALUE w, AGING w: if w is not registered, it is an unknown warehouse error. The state does not change.
15. LOTS w s: perform the same checks as (a) and (b) of RECEIVE. The state does not change.
16. ORDER n: if n is not an existing order number, it is an unknown order error. The state does not change.
17. TRACE n: if n is not the lot number of a created lot, it is an unknown lot error. The state does not change.
18. CARD s: if s is not registered, it is an unknown item error. The state does not change.
19. VALUE, AGING, REORDER, ITEMS, MARGIN, TOP k, BACKORDERS, STATUS, SUMMARY: there are no errors. The state does not change.

== Definitions of the summaries ==
- Sales summary of an item: for an item with one or more orders, the sum of (shipped quantity - returned quantity) over all orders of that item is the quantity sold, the sum of their revenue is the revenue R, the sum of their COGS is the COGS C, and R-C is the gross profit. The overall sales summary takes the same sums over all orders. The revenue R is always 0 or more.
- Ranking for TOP k: sort the items with one or more orders by gross profit in descending order, for equal gross profit by revenue in descending order, and if that is also equal by item code in ascending order; from the top they are ranked 1st, 2nd, ...

[Output format]
For each command, output one or more lines in the formats below. Text inside double quotes is written literally as shown (ASCII characters), and each <...> part is replaced by a value. Apart from indentation, every space between tokens is a single ASCII space. "(indent 2)" means two ASCII spaces at the start of the line, and "(indent 4)" means four ASCII spaces at the start of the line. The output consists of ASCII characters only, no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No blank lines are output.

== Number notation ==
- Money notation: if the number of cents c is 0 or more, write the integer part of c/100 (a decimal number with no extra leading zeros), ".", and c mod 100 as 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has one digit), in this order (e.g. 0.07, 12.50, 1000.29). If c is negative, write "-" followed by the money notation of -c (e.g. -7.80).
- Signed quantity notation: if x > 0, "+" followed by x; if x < 0, "-" followed by -x; if x = 0, "0" (e.g. +40, -25).
- Signed money notation: if c > 0, "+" followed by the money notation of c; if c < 0, "-" followed by the money notation of -c; if c = 0, "0.00" (e.g. +3.60, -12.20).
- Margin notation: for gross profit P and revenue R, if R = 0 it is "n/a". If R > 0, compute x = floor(|P|*1000/R); prefix "-" only when P < 0 and x > 0, then write the integer part of x/10, ".", x mod 10 as 1 digit, and "%". In other words, P/R expressed as a percentage, truncated toward 0 after the first decimal place (e.g. 7.6%, 100.0%, -12.3%, 0.0%).
Below, every money amount is written in money notation unless stated otherwise. Integers such as quantities, days, numbers and counts are written as decimal numbers with no extra leading zeros.

== Output of each command (on success) ==
- FILL line: (indent 2) "FILL order <order number> qty <f> revenue <f*selling price> cogs <issue cost> profit <f*selling price - issue cost> backorder <backorder quantity after the allocation>". In a command that performs allocation, one such line is output for each repetition of the allocation, after the command's output line.
- DAY: "DAY <d>"
- WAREHOUSE: "WAREHOUSE <warehouse code>"
- ITEM: for a new registration "ITEM <item code> added <item name> level <reorder level>", for a replacement "ITEM <item code> updated <item name> level <reorder level>".
- RECEIVE: "RECEIVE <warehouse code> <item code> lot <lot number> qty <q> cost <c> day <d> value <q*c>", followed by the FILL lines.
- SHIP: "SHIP order <order number> <warehouse code> <item code> shipped <f> revenue <f*p> cogs <issue cost> profit <f*p - issue cost> backorder <q-f>"
- CANCEL: "CANCEL order <order number> qty <cancelled quantity>"
- TRANSFER: "TRANSFER <a> -> <b> <item code> qty <q> value <issue cost> lots <number of issued parts>", followed by the FILL lines.
- ADJUST: for "+", "ADJUST <warehouse code> <item code> +<q> lot <lot number> value <q*c>", followed by the FILL lines. For "-", "ADJUST <warehouse code> <item code> -<q> value <issue cost>".
- RETURN: "RETURN order <order number> <warehouse code> <item code> qty <q> lot <lot number> refund <q*selling price> value <q*c>", followed by the FILL lines.
- COUNT: if a = h, "COUNT <warehouse code> <item code> qty <a> ok". If a < h, "COUNT <warehouse code> <item code> qty <h> -> <a> shortage <h-a> value <issue cost>". If a > h, "COUNT <warehouse code> <item code> qty <h> -> <a> surplus <a-h> lot <lot number> value <(a-h)*c>", followed by the FILL lines.
- MARKET: "MARKET <item code> price <p> lots <k> qty <u> loss <L>"
- RECALL: the first line is "RECALL lot <n> <item code> qty <total quantity> value <total value>". Then, for each warehouse from which a stock lot was removed, in ascending order of warehouse, output (indent 2) "<warehouse code> qty <quantity> value <value>".
- EXPIRE: the first line is "EXPIRE <d>". Then, for each pair from which stock lots were removed, in the order examined, output (indent 2) "<item code> <warehouse code> lots <number of stock lots removed> qty <total quantity> value <total value>". Finally output (indent 2) "total qty <total quantity over all pairs> value <total value over all pairs>".
- VALUE: the first line is "VALUE" if no warehouse is given, or "VALUE <warehouse code>" if one is given. For the stock lots in the target warehouses (all warehouses if none is given), list the items whose total quantity is 1 or more in ascending order of item, and for each item output (indent 2) "<item code> qty <total quantity q> value <total value v> avg <floor(v/q)>". If no warehouse is given, immediately after that line output, for each warehouse where the item's on-hand quantity is 1 or more, in ascending order of warehouse, (indent 4) "<warehouse code> qty <on-hand quantity> value <total value>". Finally output (indent 2) "total qty <grand total quantity of the target> value <grand total value of the target>".
- LOTS: the first line is "LOTS <warehouse code> <item code>". Then output the stock lots of that item in that warehouse one by one in FIFO order as (indent 2) "lot <lot number> day <lot day> qty <quantity> cost <unit cost> value <value> age <age>". Next, output the orders for that warehouse and that item whose backorder quantity is 1 or more, one by one in ascending order of order number, as (indent 2) "backorder order <order number> qty <backorder quantity>".
- REORDER: the first line is "REORDER". Then examine the registered items in ascending order of item, and for each item whose (total on-hand quantity - total backorder) is less than or equal to its reorder level, output (indent 2) "<item code> <item name> onhand <total on-hand quantity> backorder <total backorder> level <reorder level>".
- ITEMS: the first line is "ITEMS". Then output the registered items one by one in ascending order of item as (indent 2) "<item code> <item name> level <reorder level> onhand <total on-hand quantity> backorder <total backorder> cost <last receipt cost>". For an item whose last receipt cost is "none", that part is written as "none".
- MARGIN: the first line is "MARGIN". Then output the items with one or more orders one by one in ascending order of item as (indent 2) "<item code> sold <quantity sold> revenue <R> cogs <C> profit <R-C> margin <margin notation>". Finally output the overall sales summary as (indent 2) "total sold <quantity sold> revenue <R> cogs <C> profit <R-C> margin <margin notation>".
- TOP: the first line is "TOP <k>". Then output the items ranked 1st through k-th (all of them if there are fewer than k items) one by one in rank order as (indent 2) "<rank> <item code> profit <gross profit> revenue <revenue> margin <margin notation>".
- AGING: the first line is "AGING day <current day>" if no warehouse is given, or "AGING <warehouse code> day <current day>" if one is given. The stock lots in the target warehouses (all warehouses if none is given) are divided into 4 buckets by age: 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, and 91 or more. List the items whose total quantity is 1 or more in ascending order of item, and for each item output (indent 2) "<item code> 0-30 <quantity> 31-60 <quantity> 61-90 <quantity> 91+ <quantity>". Then, over all target stock lots, output (indent 2) "total 0-30 <quantity> 31-60 <quantity> 61-90 <quantity> 91+ <quantity>" and (indent 2) "value 0-30 <value> 31-60 <value> 61-90 <value> 91+ <value>". Each quantity and value is the total quantity and total value of the stock lots that fall into that bucket.
- BACKORDERS: the first line is "BACKORDERS". Then output the orders whose backorder quantity is 1 or more, one by one in ascending order of order number, as (indent 2) "order <order number> <warehouse code> <item code> qty <backorder quantity> day <order day> price <selling price>".
- ORDER: the first line is "ORDER <order number> <warehouse code> <item code> day <order day> price <selling price>". The second line is (indent 2) "ordered <ordered quantity> shipped <shipped quantity> backorder <backorder quantity> cancelled <cancelled quantity> returned <returned quantity>". Then output each part of the issue record one by one, in order, as (indent 2) "lot <lot number> qty <quantity> cost <unit cost>". Finally output (indent 2) "revenue <revenue> cogs <COGS> profit <revenue - COGS>".
- TRACE: the first line is "TRACE lot <lot number> <item code> <creation kind> <creating warehouse> day <lot day> qty <initial quantity> cost <initial unit cost>". Then output the warehouses that hold a stock lot with that lot number one by one in ascending order of warehouse as (indent 2) "held <warehouse code> qty <quantity> cost <current unit cost>". Next, output the orders whose issue record contains parts with that lot number one by one in ascending order of order number as (indent 2) "order <order number> qty <total quantity of the parts with that lot number in that order's issue record>". Finally, if the lot's removed quantity is 1 or more, output (indent 2) "removed qty <removed quantity>".
- CARD: the first line is "CARD <item code> <item name>". Then output the entries of that item's stock card one per line in the order they were added, as (indent 2) "day <day> <kind> <warehouse code> <quantity change in signed quantity notation> <value change in signed money notation> balance <running quantity> <running value>". The running quantity and running value start from 0 and are the sums, taken in order, of the quantity changes and of the value changes up to and including that line.
- STATUS: the first line is "STATUS". Then output the registered warehouses one by one in ascending order of warehouse as (indent 2) "<warehouse code> skus <number of items whose on-hand quantity in that warehouse is 1 or more> qty <total quantity of the stock lots in that warehouse> value <total value of the stock lots in that warehouse> backorder <total backorder quantity of the orders for that warehouse>".
- SUMMARY: the first line is "SUMMARY". Then output the 8 categories of the cumulative totals in the order received, returned, adjusted-in, adjusted-out, shipped, written-down, expired, recalled as (indent 2) "<category> qty <total quantity> value <total value>". Next output, over all stock lots, (indent 2) "on-hand qty <total quantity> value <total value>", and finally (indent 2) "orders <number of orders created> open <number of orders whose backorder quantity is 1 or more> backorder <total backorder quantity of all orders>".
For LOTS, REORDER, ITEMS, TOP, BACKORDERS, CARD and STATUS, if there is no line to output after the first line, output one line (indent 2) "(none)" instead. VALUE, EXPIRE, MARGIN and AGING output their total lines even when there is no applicable item or pair (for example, with no stock the total line of VALUE is "  total qty 0 value 0.00", and with no orders the total line of MARGIN is "  total sold 0 revenue 0.00 cogs 0.00 profit 0.00 margin n/a").

"ERROR invalid day <d>"
"ERROR duplicate warehouse <warehouse code>"
"ERROR unknown warehouse <warehouse code>"
"ERROR unknown item <item code>"
"ERROR invalid quantity"

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 2000.
- The input correctly follows the formats in [Input format] (there are no unknown commands, wrong numbers of tokens, or tokens that do not match their format). The only errors to detect are those listed in [Processing].
- The day of DAY and the receipt day of RECEIVE are between 1 and 100000 inclusive. The number of days of EXPIRE is between 0 and 100000 inclusive, k of TOP is between 1 and 100 inclusive, and the reorder level is between 0 and 1000000 inclusive.
- Quantities, the numeric part of adjustment quantities, and actual quantities are between 0 and 100000 inclusive. Order numbers and lot numbers are between 0 and 1000000 inclusive.
- At most 20 warehouses and at most 100 items are registered.
- When computed according to the rules above, the absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values (including |P|*1000 in the margin notation), is less than 2 to the 53rd power (computing with 64-bit signed integers does not overflow).
