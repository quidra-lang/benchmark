[Overview]
Write a program that simulates a supply chain shaped like the beer distribution game, in discrete time with one week as the unit. The chain consists of four stages, in order from the one closest to the customer: the retailer (RETAILER), the wholesaler (WHOLESALER), the distributor (DISTRIBUTOR) and the factory (FACTORY). Every week, each stage receives the goods that arrived from upstream, fills the orders received from downstream out of its inventory, carries the unfilled part over as backlog, and places an order upstream according to its ordering policy. The factory's orders are sent to the production plant. Shipping goods and transmitting orders each take a delay, and inventory and backlog incur a cost every week.
The program first reads the settings of the four stages, then reads commands one line at a time, processes them in order, and writes the results to standard output. The commands define the customer demand (by listing the values or by generating them with random numbers), run the simulation, change the ordering policies and various settings, save and restore the state, show various displays and analyses (weekly records, costs, fill rates, the bullwhip effect, peaks, actual lead times, plots, frequency distributions), compare policies, and optimize base-stock levels. All numbers are handled as integers; floating-point numbers are not used.

[Input format]
Lines 1 to 4 are the stage settings, one line each in the order RETAILER, WHOLESALER, DISTRIBUTOR, FACTORY, in the following form.
<stage> <initial inventory> <initial quantity> <shipping delay> <order delay> <holding rate> <backlog rate> <policy>
From line 5 on there is one command per line, and the last line is END. The command on line 5 is always DEMAND or GENERATE and never causes an error.
Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by single ASCII spaces, with no spaces at the beginning or end of a line. In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes, and [...] denotes a part that may be omitted. The bracket symbols < > [ ] themselves never appear in the input, and they do not appear in the output either, except for the "->" in the SHOCK line.

<policy> is a sequence of tokens in one of the following 7 forms (their meanings are explained in section 6).
FIXED <q>
PASS
BASESTOCK <S>
MINMAX <s> <S>
MOVAVG <k> <S>
SMOOTH <k> <S>
STERMAN <theta> <alpha> <beta> <Q>
<theta>, <alpha> and <beta> are decimals written as one digit, ".", and two digits (e.g. 0.25, 1.00); all other parameters are integers.

The commands are listed below.

Defining the demand:
DEMAND <d1> <d2> ... <dn>
GENERATE <weeks> <base> <spread> <seed> <change>...

Operating the simulation:
RUN <weeks>
RESET
SAVE <name>
LOAD <name>

Changing settings:
SET <target> <policy>
BATCH <stage> <lot>
CAPACITY <target> <capacity>
COST <stage> <holding rate> <backlog rate>
VISIBILITY <mode>
SHOCK <week> <demand>

Displays and analyses:
STATUS
SETTINGS
WEEK <week>
TABLE <stage> [<from> <to>]
CHAIN [<from> <to>]
DEMANDS [<from> <to>]
COSTS
SUMMARY
BALANCE
FILLRATE
BULLWHIP
PEAKS
LEADTIME <stage>
PLOT <stage> <kind> [<from> <to>]
HISTOGRAM <stage> <kind> <width>
COMPARE <policy> / <policy> / ... / <policy>
OPTIMIZE <target> <low> <high>

End:
END

Notes on the values:
- <stage> and <target> in a command are sequences of uppercase English letters and may not be valid stage names.
- In the <policy> part of SET and COMPARE, the policy name may be none of the 7 kinds, and the number of parameters may be wrong. When the name is valid and the number of parameters is also correct, each parameter has the form given above.
- The arguments of COMPARE are one or more policies separated by the single token "/". Each policy consists of one or more tokens and does not contain "/". When there is only one policy, there is no "/".
- A <change> of GENERATE consists of the 3 tokens "<kind> <week> <amount>", and zero or more of them follow. <kind> is one of STEP, PULSE and RAMP, and <amount> is an integer (preceded by - when negative).
- <capacity> of CAPACITY is OFF or an integer >= 0. <mode> of VISIBILITY and <kind> of PLOT and HISTOGRAM are sequences of uppercase English letters and may not be valid values.
- <name> is the name given to a saved state.
- All other values are integers >= 0.

[Processing]
Section 1. Stages and weeks
- The order of the four stages RETAILER, WHOLESALER, DISTRIBUTOR, FACTORY is called the stage order. For each stage, the stage immediately before it in the stage order is called its downstream stage, and the stage immediately after it is called its upstream stage. The downstream of RETAILER is the customer, and the upstream of FACTORY is the production plant. Goods flow from upstream to downstream, and orders flow from downstream to upstream.
- The simulation advances one week at a time. Weeks are numbered from 1. Let w be the number of weeks simulated so far. The length of the current demand series (section 2) is called the horizon and is denoted by H; no week after week H is simulated.

Section 2. State
Each stage has the following values.
- Inventory: the quantity of goods on hand.
- Backlog: the quantity of the orders received from downstream (for RETAILER, the customer demand) that has not been shipped yet.
- Shipping pipeline: the quantities of goods in transit from upstream to this stage, listed in order of arrival. Between weeks, the number of elements equals the shipping delay, and the first element arrives in the next week.
- Order pipeline: the quantities of the orders placed by this stage that have not yet reached upstream (for FACTORY, the production plant), listed in the order they were placed. Between weeks, the number of elements equals the order delay, and the first element reaches upstream in the next week.
- The policy, and, when the policy is STERMAN, the expected demand (section 6).
- The holding rate and the backlog rate (the cost, in cents, of holding one unit of goods as inventory or as backlog for one week), the lot (an integer >= 1), and the capacity (an integer >= 0, or "none").
- The weekly records (section 4).
The production plant has an unprocessed quantity (the quantity of the received orders that has not been produced yet), a capacity (an integer >= 0, or "none"), and weekly records.
In addition, there are the demand-signal setting (ON or OFF), the current demand series, the base demand series, and the list of saved states. A demand series is the list of the customer demand of each week, in order from week 1.

Section 3. Initial state
The following state is called the initial state, and setting the state to the initial state is called "initializing".
- w is 0, and there are no records at all.
- The inventory of each stage is the initial inventory from that stage's settings, and the backlog is 0. The shipping pipeline consists of as many elements as the shipping delay, the order pipeline consists of as many elements as the order delay, and every element of both is the initial quantity of that stage.
- The policy of each stage is the policy from the settings, the holding rate and backlog rate are the values from the settings, the lot is 1, and the capacity is none.
- The unprocessed quantity of the production plant is 0, and its capacity is none. The demand-signal setting is OFF.
- The current demand series is the same list as the base demand series.
The list of saved states and the base demand series do not change when initializing.

Section 4. Processing one week
When w is less than H, week t (t = w + 1) is simulated by the following steps (1) to (6). Each step is performed for all stages before proceeding to the next step.
(1) Receiving: each stage removes the first element of its shipping pipeline and adds that quantity, as the received quantity, to its inventory.
(2) Incoming orders: the incoming order quantity of RETAILER is the demand of week t in the current demand series. Each other stage removes the first element of the order pipeline of its downstream stage, and that quantity is its incoming order quantity. The production plant likewise removes the first element of FACTORY's order pipeline, and that quantity is its incoming order quantity.
(3) Shipping and production: the stages are processed in the stage order. Let b, the backlog before shipping, be the backlog at that moment, and let the requested quantity be "b + incoming order quantity". The shipped quantity is the smaller of the inventory and the requested quantity (if the capacity is not none, the smallest of the inventory, the requested quantity and the capacity). The shipped quantity is subtracted from the inventory, and the backlog becomes "requested quantity - shipped quantity". Each stage other than RETAILER appends the shipped quantity to the end of the shipping pipeline of its downstream stage. RETAILER's shipment goes to the customer. Also, the smaller of "shipped quantity - b" (0 if negative) and the incoming order quantity is the on-time shipped quantity. This is the part of that week's incoming order that could be shipped in that week, when the shipment is regarded as filling the oldest backlog first.
After the four stages, the production plant is processed. Let the requested quantity be "unprocessed quantity + incoming order quantity", let the produced quantity be the requested quantity (if the capacity is not none, the smaller of the requested quantity and the capacity), and let the unprocessed quantity become "requested quantity - produced quantity". The produced quantity is appended to the end of FACTORY's shipping pipeline.
(4) Ordering: for each stage, the supply line and the inventory position are computed by the following formulas.
  supply line = sum of the elements of the order pipeline + backlog of the upstream stage (for FACTORY, the unprocessed quantity of the production plant) + sum of the elements of the shipping pipeline
  inventory position = inventory + supply line - backlog
For every stage, the values at the moment this step starts are used (the order pipeline is the list after its first element was removed in (2), and the shipping pipeline is the list after the element was appended in (3)). The policy order quantity x is determined by the policy (section 6), and x rounded up to a multiple of the lot, that is, "ceil(x / lot) * lot", is the order quantity (if x is 0, the order quantity is also 0). The order quantity is appended to the end of the order pipeline.
(5) Costs: for each stage, the holding cost is "holding rate * inventory", the backlog cost is "backlog rate * backlog", and the cost is "holding cost + backlog cost" (in cents).
(6) Recording: for each stage, the following are kept as the record of week t: the received quantity, the incoming order quantity, the shipped quantity, the backlog before shipping, the on-time shipped quantity, the inventory, the backlog, the order quantity, the holding cost, the backlog cost, and the cost. The inventory and the backlog are the values after (3) (they do not change in (4) and (5)). For the production plant, the incoming order quantity, the produced quantity and the unprocessed quantity are kept. Finally, w is set to t.

Section 5. Demand signal
The policies MOVAVG, SMOOTH and STERMAN use the demand signal. The demand signal of a stage for week u is that stage's incoming order quantity of week u if the demand-signal setting is OFF, and the demand of week u in the current demand series (the customer demand) if it is ON. The demand signal is determined by the setting at the moment it is used. When MOVAVG uses the demand signals of past weeks, they are also determined by the setting at that moment.

Section 6. Policies
* Policy order quantity
In step (4) of week t, let P be the inventory position of the stage, L the supply line, R the incoming order quantity of week t, and D the demand signal of week t; the policy order quantity x is determined as follows. max(a, b) denotes the larger of a and b, and floor(y) denotes the largest integer less than or equal to y (e.g. floor(7 / 2) = 3, floor(-7 / 3) = -3).
- FIXED q: x = q.
- PASS: x = R.
- BASESTOCK S: x = max(0, S - P).
- MINMAX s S: if P <= s then x = S - P, otherwise x = 0.
- MOVAVG k S: let m be the smaller of k and t, divide the sum of the m demand signals from week t - m + 1 to week t by m, and let F = floor(sum / m). x = max(0, S + F * (shipping delay + order delay) - P).
- SMOOTH k S: x = max(0, D + floor((S - P) / k)). When S - P is negative, floor is still applied as defined.
- STERMAN theta alpha beta Q: let a, b and c be the integers obtained by multiplying theta, alpha and beta by 100, respectively (e.g. 25 for 0.25). The expected demand E is an integer that represents the demand forecast in units of 1/100. First, E is updated to floor((100 * a * D + (100 - a) * E) / 100). Next, with N = inventory - backlog, x = max(0, floor((100 * E + b * (100 * (Q - N) - c * L)) / 10000)). This corresponds to updating the forecast E/100 by exponential smoothing, then taking "E/100 + alpha * (Q - N - beta * L)" with its fractional part truncated, and using 0 if that is less than 0.

* Initial value of the expected demand
When a STERMAN policy is set for a stage (including at initialization, at SET, and at the start of a trial in section 7), the expected demand of that stage is set to 100 times that stage's demand signal of week w if w is 1 or more, and to 100 times that stage's initial quantity if w is 0. The expected demand is used and updated only for stages whose policy is STERMAN.

* Policy validation
SET and COMPARE validate a policy in the following order, and the first condition that applies is the error.
1. The name is none of the 7 kinds: unknown policy <name>
2. The number of parameters is wrong (the correct number is 0 for PASS, 1 for FIXED and BASESTOCK, 2 for MINMAX, MOVAVG and SMOOTH, and 4 for STERMAN): <name> needs <correct number> parameters
3. A parameter is out of range: invalid parameter for <name>
The ranges are as follows. FIXED: 0 <= q <= 1000; BASESTOCK: 0 <= S <= 10000; MINMAX: 0 <= s < S <= 10000; MOVAVG: 1 <= k <= 52 and 0 <= S <= 10000; SMOOTH: 1 <= k <= 10 and 0 <= S <= 10000; STERMAN: theta, alpha and beta each between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive, and 0 <= Q <= 10000.

* Policy notation
When a policy is output, the name and the parameters are written exactly as written in the input, separated by single ASCII spaces (e.g. STERMAN 0.30 0.25 0.50 14, PASS). This is called the policy notation.

Section 7. Full-horizon trial
COMPARE and OPTIMIZE perform the following trial, separately from the current simulation. The state is the same as the initial state, except that the lot, capacity, holding rate and backlog rate of each stage, the capacity of the production plant, and the demand-signal setting are the current values, and the demand series is the current demand series. The policy of each stage is the one specified in the description of each command (the expected demand of STERMAN starts at 100 times the initial quantity). Then weeks 1 to H are simulated as in section 4. A trial does not change anything in the state of the current simulation.
In a trial, the total cost of a stage is the sum of that stage's costs from week 1 to week H, and the total cost of the chain is the sum of the total costs of the four stages.

Section 8. Number notation
- Money: an integer c >= 0 representing cents is written as "the quotient of c / 100", ".", and "the remainder of c / 100 written with 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit)", concatenated in this order (e.g. 12.34 for 1234, 0.05 for 5). All costs are written as money.
- Truncated decimal: to write a non-negative rational number p / q as a truncated decimal with d decimal places, let r = floor(p * 10^d / q), and write "the quotient of r / 10^d", ".", and "the remainder of r / 10^d written with d digits (missing digits padded with leading 0s)", concatenated in this order (e.g. 23 / 3 with 4 places is 7.6666, 1 / 50 with 2 places is 0.02).
- Lists: a pipeline is written as its elements from the first, separated by "," (e.g. 4,4,0).
- All other integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, with a leading "-" for negative numbers.
- Right-aligned in width n: if the string of the written value is shorter than n, ASCII spaces are added on its left to make its length n. If it is n or longer, it is written as is.

Section 9. Error handling
For each command, the error conditions listed in its description are checked in the listed order. For the first condition that applies, output only one line consisting of ERROR followed by one ASCII space and the message (e.g. ERROR unknown stage SHOP), and the command ends without changing anything. If no condition applies, process the command as described and output the lines as described. <target>, <week>, <name> and the like in a message are the values exactly as written in the command.
The descriptions of the commands use the following terms.
- "The stage name is invalid": the value is none of RETAILER, WHOLESALER, DISTRIBUTOR and FACTORY. The message is unknown stage <that value>.
- "No weeks have been simulated": w is 0. The message is no weeks simulated.
- "The range is invalid": <from> and <to> are written, and 1 <= from <= to <= limit does not hold (the limit is determined by each command). The message is bad range <from> <to>. If <from> and <to> are not written, from is 1 and to is the limit.
If the command name is none of those in section 10, it is the error unknown command <command name>.

Section 10. Commands
Command: DEMAND <d1> <d2> ... <dn>
No errors. Initialize, with d1, d2, ..., dn as the base demand series (so H = n). Then output "DEMAND weeks <H> total <sum of demands> min <minimum demand> max <maximum demand>".

Command: GENERATE <weeks> <base> <spread> <seed> <change>...
Generates the demand series with random numbers.
Errors:
1. The <week> of a <change> is not between 1 and <weeks> inclusive (the <change>s are checked in the order written, and the first one that applies is reported): week <that week> outside horizon
2. There is a week whose level is negative (let t be the earliest such week): negative level at week <t>
Random numbers are generated by x(0) = seed, x(i) = the remainder of 48271 * x(i - 1) divided by 2147483647 (i = 1, 2, ...). For t = 1, 2, ..., <weeks>, the level of week t is <base> plus the amounts from all <change>s. The amount from one <change> is: for kind STEP, <amount> when t >= <week>; for PULSE, <amount> when t = <week>; for RAMP, <amount> * (t - <week> + 1) when t >= <week>; and 0 otherwise. The demand of week t is "level + the remainder of x(t) divided by <spread>". Initialize with the resulting list of length <weeks> as the base demand series, and output a line of the same form as DEMAND (it starts with DEMAND).

Command: RUN <weeks>
Errors:
1. w is equal to H: horizon reached at week <H>
Simulate, one week at a time, as many weeks as the smaller of <weeks> and H - w. After step (6) of each week, for each stage in the stage order: if the backlog before shipping is 0 and the backlog is positive, output "STOCKOUT <stage> week <t> backlog <backlog>"; if the backlog before shipping is positive and the backlog is 0, output "RECOVER <stage> week <t>" (t is the number of that week). Finally, output "RUN <first week>-<last week> demand <sum of demands> cost <sum of costs>". The sum of demands is the sum of the demands of the simulated weeks, and the sum of costs is the sum of the costs of the four stages over those weeks (as money). If w has become H as a result, then output "HORIZON <H>".

Command: RESET
No errors. Initialize and output "RESET week 0".

Command: SAVE <name>
No errors. Save, under <name>, a copy of everything in the current state except the list of saved states and the base demand series (w, the values and records of each stage and of the production plant, the policies and expected demands, the lots, capacities, holding rates, backlog rates, the demand-signal setting, and the current demand series), and output "SAVE <name> week <w>". If a state with the same name already exists, it is replaced.

Command: LOAD <name>
Errors:
1. No state has been saved under that name: no snapshot <name>
Make the saved state the current state (the saved state itself remains), and output "LOAD <name> week <w>". w is the value after restoring.

Command: SET <target> <policy>
Errors:
1. <target> is not ALL and the stage name is invalid: unknown stage <target>
2. The error found by policy validation (section 6)
Set the policy of the stage <target> (all stages if ALL) to <policy>, and output "SET <target> <policy notation>". The new policy is used from the next week to be simulated.

Command: BATCH <stage> <lot>
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. The lot is not between 1 and 100 inclusive: invalid batch size <lot>
Change the lot of that stage and output "BATCH <stage> <lot>".

Command: CAPACITY <target> <capacity>
Errors:
1. <target> is not PLANT and the stage name is invalid: unknown stage <target>
2. The capacity is not OFF and is greater than 10000: invalid capacity <capacity>
Set the capacity of the stage <target> (the production plant if PLANT) to none if OFF, and to the value otherwise, and output "CAPACITY <target> <capacity>".

Command: COST <stage> <holding rate> <backlog rate>
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. The holding rate or the backlog rate is greater than 1000: invalid cost
Change the holding rate and backlog rate of that stage, and output "COST <stage> holding <holding rate> backlog <backlog rate>". Costs already recorded do not change.

Command: VISIBILITY <mode>
Errors:
1. The mode is neither ON nor OFF: unknown visibility <mode>
Set the demand-signal setting to the mode and output "VISIBILITY <mode>".

Command: SHOCK <week> <demand>
Errors:
1. The week is not between 1 and H inclusive: week <week> outside horizon
2. The week is w or less: week <week> already simulated
Change the demand of week <week> in the current demand series to <demand> (the base demand series does not change), and output "SHOCK week <week> <original demand> -> <demand>".

Command: STATUS
No errors. First output "STATUS week <w> of <H>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> inv <inventory> back <backlog> transit <shipping pipeline> pending <order pipeline> supply <supply line> position <inventory position> policy <policy notation>". The supply line and the inventory position are the values obtained by applying the formulas of section 4 (4) to the current state. For a stage whose policy is STERMAN, append " expect <expected demand>" to the end of the line. The expected demand E is written in the same way as money (the quotient of E / 100, ".", the remainder with 2 digits). Finally, output "PLANT back <unprocessed quantity of the production plant>".

Command: SETTINGS
No errors. First output "SETTINGS". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> holding <holding rate> backlog <backlog rate> batch <lot> capacity <capacity> delays <shipping delay> <order delay>", and then output "PLANT capacity <capacity of the production plant>". A capacity of none is written as OFF. Finally, output "VISIBILITY <demand-signal setting>".

Command: WEEK <week>
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
2. The week is not between 1 and w inclusive: bad week <week>
First output "WEEK <week> demand <demand of that week>". The demand of that week is the value in the current demand series. Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> recv <received quantity> in <incoming order quantity> ship <shipped quantity> inv <inventory> back <backlog> order <order quantity> cost <cost>" from that week's record, and finally output "PLANT in <incoming order quantity> made <produced quantity> back <unprocessed quantity>" from the production plant's record of that week.

Command: TABLE <stage> [<from> <to>]
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. No weeks have been simulated
3. The range is invalid (the limit is w)
First output "TABLE <stage> <from>-<to>". Then output a header line, followed by one line per week from week <from> to week <to> with that stage's record of that week. The header line consists of week right-aligned in width 4, in, ship, recv, inv, back and order each right-aligned in width 6, and cost right-aligned in width 9, in this order, separated by single ASCII spaces; that is, "week     in   ship   recv    inv   back  order      cost". Each week's line consists of the week number, the incoming order quantity, the shipped quantity, the received quantity, the inventory, the backlog, the order quantity and the cost (as money), in the same order as the header, right-aligned in the same widths, separated by single ASCII spaces.

Command: CHAIN [<from> <to>]
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
2. The range is invalid (the limit is w)
First output "CHAIN <from>-<to>". Then output a header line consisting of week right-aligned in width 4, demand right-aligned in width 6, and the four stage names in the stage order, each right-aligned in width 11, separated by single ASCII spaces. Then, for each week from week <from> to week <to>, output a line consisting of the week number, the demand of that week (the value in the current demand series) and each stage's order quantity of that week, in the same order, right-aligned in the same widths, separated by single ASCII spaces.

Command: DEMANDS [<from> <to>]
Errors:
1. The range is invalid (the limit is H)
First output "DEMANDS <from>-<to> total <sum> min <minimum> max <maximum>", then output one line with the values of the current demand series from week <from> to week <to>, separated by single ASCII spaces. The sum, minimum and maximum are over those values.

Command: COSTS
No errors. First output "COSTS week <w>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> holding <sum of holding costs> backlog <sum of backlog costs> total <sum of costs>", and finally output a line of the same form with TOTAL as its label, giving the totals of each sum over the four stages. All sums are over the records from week 1 to week w and are written as money.

Command: SUMMARY
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
First output "SUMMARY weeks 1-<w>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> avg_inv <average inventory> avg_back <average backlog> orders <sum of order quantities> min <minimum order quantity> max <maximum order quantity> zero_weeks <number of weeks with order quantity 0>". The average inventory is "sum of inventories / w" and the average backlog is "sum of backlogs / w", each written as a truncated decimal with 2 decimal places. All of these are over the records from week 1 to week w.

Command: BALANCE
No errors. First output "BALANCE week <w>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> start <initial inventory> received <sum of received quantities> shipped <sum of shipped quantities> end <inventory> owed <backlog>". Finally, output "SYSTEM initial <initial goods> produced <sum of produced quantities> delivered <sum of RETAILER's shipped quantities> stock <sum of the inventories of the four stages> transit <sum of the elements of the shipping pipelines of the four stages>". The initial goods is the sum over the four stages of "initial inventory + initial quantity * shipping delay". All sums of records are over weeks 1 to w, and the inventory, backlog and shipping pipelines are the current values.

Command: FILLRATE
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
First output "FILLRATE weeks 1-<w>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> fill <fill rate> ontime <sum of on-time shipped quantities> ordered <sum of incoming order quantities> stockout_weeks <stockout weeks> service <service rate>%". All sums are over the records from week 1 to week w. The fill rate is n/a if the sum of incoming order quantities is 0, and otherwise "sum of on-time shipped quantities * 100 / sum of incoming order quantities" written as a truncated decimal with 2 decimal places followed by %. The stockout weeks is the number of weeks whose backlog is positive, and the service rate is "(w - stockout weeks) * 100 / w" written as a truncated decimal with 2 decimal places.

Command: BULLWHIP
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
Let n = w. For a list of n values, the mean is "sum / n", the variation V is "n * (sum of the squares of the values) - (square of the sum)", and the variance is "V / (n * n)". First, for the list of the current demand series from week 1 to week n, output "BULLWHIP weeks 1-<n> demand mean <mean> var <variance>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, for the list of that stage's order quantities from week 1 to week n, output one line "<stage> mean <mean> var <variance> ratio <ratio> step <step ratio>". The ratio is "V of that stage / V of the demand", and the step ratio is "V of that stage / V of the list of order quantities of the downstream stage" (for RETAILER, divide by the V of the demand). The mean, variance, ratio and step ratio are written as truncated decimals with 4 decimal places. However, the ratio and the step ratio are written as n/a if the V divided by is 0.

Command: PEAKS
Errors:
1. No weeks have been simulated
First output "PEAKS weeks 1-<w>". Then, for each stage in the stage order, output one line "<stage> max_inv <maximum inventory> week <week> max_back <maximum backlog> week <week> longest_stockout <longest stockout>". The maximum inventory and the maximum backlog are the maximum values of the inventory and the backlog in the records from week 1 to week w. Each following <week> is the earliest week in which that maximum was reached. However, if the maximum is 0, - is written in place of <week>. The longest stockout is written as "<number of weeks> weeks <first week>-<last week>" for the longest run of consecutive weeks whose backlog is positive (if several have the same length, the earliest one). If there is no week whose backlog is positive, it is written as 0.

Command: LEADTIME <stage>
Determines how many weeks it actually took for that stage's orders to arrive.
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. No weeks have been simulated
Let I = (shipping delay + order delay) * initial quantity (the amount that was in the pipelines in the initial state). For each week u with 1 <= u <= w, let C(u) be "sum of the received quantities from week 1 to week u - I". For each week t whose order quantity is positive, let O(t) be the sum of the order quantities from week 1 to week t; if there is a smallest u that satisfies t < u <= w and C(u) >= O(t), the order of week t is completed and its lead time is u - t. If there is no such u, the order of week t is open (since goods arrive for earlier orders first, u corresponds to the week in which the last unit of the order of week t arrived).
Output "LEADTIME <stage> nominal <shipping delay + order delay> orders <number of weeks whose order quantity is positive> completed <number of completed orders> open <number of open orders> min <minimum> avg <average> max <maximum>". The minimum and maximum are the minimum and maximum lead times of the completed orders, and the average is "sum of the lead times of the completed orders / number of completed orders" written as a truncated decimal with 2 decimal places. If there are no completed orders, write "min - avg - max -".

Command: PLOT <stage> <kind> [<from> <to>]
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. The kind is none of ORDERS, INV, BACK and NET: unknown mode <kind>
3. No weeks have been simulated
4. The range is invalid (the limit is w)
The value of each week is, from that stage's record of that week, the order quantity if the kind is ORDERS, the inventory if INV, the backlog if BACK, and "inventory - backlog" if NET.
- When the kind is ORDERS, INV or BACK: let M be the maximum of the values in the range, and first output "PLOT <stage> <kind> <from>-<to> max <M>". Then, for each week from week <from> to week <to>, output one line "<week number right-aligned in width 3> |<bar> <value>". The bar is n copies of #, where n = floor(value * 50 / M) (if M is 0, n = 0).
- When the kind is NET: first output "PLOT <stage> NET <from>-<to> min <minimum of the values in the range> max <maximum of the values in the range>". Let A be the maximum of the absolute values of the values in the range, and for each week let n = floor(absolute value of the value * 25 / A) (if A is 0, n = 0); output one line "<week number right-aligned in width 3> <left part>|<right part> <value>". If the value is negative, the left part is 25 - n ASCII spaces followed by n copies of -, and the right part is empty. If the value is 0 or more, the left part is 25 ASCII spaces, and the right part is n copies of + if the value is positive, and empty if it is 0. For example, if A is 28 and the value is -12, then n = 10, and the left part is 15 ASCII spaces followed by 10 copies of -.

Command: HISTOGRAM <stage> <kind> <width>
Errors:
1. The stage name is invalid
2. The kind is none of ORDERS, INV, BACK and NET: unknown mode <kind>
3. The width is not between 1 and 100 inclusive: invalid width <width>
4. No weeks have been simulated
Output the frequency distribution of the values of the weeks from week 1 to week w (determined in the same way as PLOT). The bin number of a value v is floor(v / width) (e.g. if the width is 5, the bin number of -1 and -5 is -1, and the bin number of -6 is -2). First output "HISTOGRAM <stage> <kind> width <width> weeks 1-<w>". Then, for each number j from the minimum to the maximum bin number (including numbers into which no value falls), in increasing order, output one line "<j * width>..<j * width + width - 1> <count>". The count is the number of weeks that fall into that bin. If the count is 1 or more, append one ASCII space and as many * as the count to the end of the line.

Command: COMPARE <policy> / ... / <policy>
Errors:
1. The error found by policy validation (section 6) (the policies are checked in the order written, and the error is for the first policy that causes an error)
For each policy written, perform a trial (section 7) with that policy as the policy of all four stages. First output "COMPARE <number of policies> weeks <H>". Then output one line per policy, in increasing order of the total cost of the chain (ties in the order written): "<rank> <policy notation> total <total cost of the chain> fill <fill rate> bullwhip <ratio>". Ranks are numbered from 1. The fill rate is computed in the same way as FILLRATE from the trial's RETAILER records from week 1 to week H, and the ratio is computed in the same way as the ratio of BULLWHIP (with n = H) from the trial's FACTORY list of order quantities and the demand series (both from week 1 to week H).

Command: OPTIMIZE <target> <low> <high>
Errors:
1. <target> is not ALL and the stage name is invalid: unknown stage <target>
2. low is greater than high: bad range <low> <high>
3. high - low is greater than 200: range too large
For each integer S from low to high, perform a trial (section 7) with BASESTOCK S as the policy of the stage <target> (all stages if ALL) and the current policies as the policies of the other stages. The objective value is the total cost of that stage if <target> is a stage name, and the total cost of the chain if ALL. For the S that minimizes the objective value (if there are several such S, the smallest), output "OPTIMIZE <target> BASESTOCK <S> cost <objective value> total <total cost of the chain in that trial>".

Command: END
Marks the end of the input. Terminate without outputting anything.

[Output format]
- For each command, output the lines specified in [Processing] in the specified order. On an error, output only one line starting with ERROR.
- In the second and later lines output by TABLE, CHAIN and PLOT, there may be consecutive ASCII spaces at the beginning of a line or in the middle of a line, for right alignment or bars. In all other lines, every separator is a single ASCII space and there is no space at the beginning of a line. No line has trailing spaces.
- The output consists only of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No empty lines are output.

[Constraints]
- The input has at most 1005 lines.
- All setting lines are in the correct form, with 0 <= initial inventory <= 1000, 0 <= initial quantity <= 100, 1 <= shipping delay <= 4, 1 <= order delay <= 4, 0 <= holding rate <= 1000, 0 <= backlog rate <= 1000. The policies in the settings do not cause errors in policy validation.
- Command names consist of uppercase English letters. END appears only on the last line. For the commands in section 10, the number of arguments and the form of each token are as described (except that the policy parts of SET and COMPARE are as stated in [Input format]).
- Integer tokens are in decimal without extra leading zeros. The <seed> of GENERATE is between 1 and 2147483646 inclusive, and the absolute value of every other integer is at most 99999.
- The number n of values of DEMAND is between 1 and 104 inclusive, and each value is at most 1000.
- The <weeks> of GENERATE is between 1 and 104 inclusive, <base> is at most 1000, and <spread> is between 1 and 100 inclusive. There are at most 6 <change>s, and each <amount> is between -100 and 100 inclusive.
- The <weeks> of RUN is at least 1, the <demand> of SHOCK is at most 1000, and the <low> and <high> of OPTIMIZE are at most 10000.
- <stage>, <target>, <mode> and <kind> consist of 1 to 12 uppercase English letters, and <name> consists of 1 to 8 lowercase English letters and digits.
- The number of policies in COMPARE is at most 8.
- When computed exactly by the formulas in [Processing], every integer that appears in the computation (including intermediate values) has an absolute value less than 2^53.
