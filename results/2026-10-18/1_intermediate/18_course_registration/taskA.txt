[Overview]
Write a program that handles university course registration. It covers registering courses and students; parsing and evaluating prerequisites (expressions that combine courses that must have been passed beforehand and year conditions with "&", "|" and parentheses); detecting overlapping timetable slots; credit limits; capacities and waitlists (with priority by year); dropping courses and promotion from waitlists; swapping courses; stopping and reopening registration; changing capacities and credit limits; changing prerequisites; cancelling courses; and various queries (whether a request can be accepted, rosters, schedules, free time, course information, checks of grades and credits, histories, the list of students, and summary reports). The program reads commands from standard input one line at a time and, for each command, writes the result in the specified format to standard output.
All computations are done with integers; no floating-point numbers are used.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number N of command lines that follow. Starting from the second line, the next N lines contain one command per line. Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by a single space, with no whitespace at the beginning or end of the line. The program ends after processing all N commands.

The token formats are as follows. An "integer" below is written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- Course code: 2 to 4 uppercase letters (A-Z) followed by exactly 3 digits (0-9) (e.g. CS101, MATH210).
- Student ID: 1 uppercase letter followed by 1 to 7 digits (e.g. S1001, S9).
- Credits: an integer from 1 to 6.
- Capacity: an integer from 0 to 500.
- Year: an integer from 1 to 6.
- Credit limit: an integer from 1 to 40.
- Timetable: the single character "-", or 1 to 6 "slots" joined by commas ",". A slot is 11 characters: 2 uppercase letters (day), 4 digits (start time), a hyphen "-", and 4 digits (end time), in this order (e.g. MO0900-1030). The characters always have this shape, but values that are not a valid day or time (e.g. QQ0900-1000, MO0900-0975) may appear.
- Prerequisite: the single character "-", or a string of 1 to 60 characters consisting only of uppercase letters, digits, "&", "|", "(" and ")". A string that does not conform to the grammar defined in [Processing] may appear.
- Grade records: the single character "-", or 1 to 20 "records" joined by commas ",". A record is a course code, a colon ":", 1 uppercase letter (the grade), ":", and 1 digit from 1 to 6 (the credits), in this order (e.g. CS101:A:3). A character that is not a valid grade (e.g. E) may appear.
- Day: 2 uppercase letters. An invalid day (e.g. XX) may appear.

There are the following 21 commands. Each <...> part holds a token of the format above.
COURSE <course code> <credits> <capacity> <timetable> <prerequisite>
STUDENT <student ID> <year> <credit limit> <grade records>
ENROLL <student ID> <course code>
CHECK <student ID> <course code>
OPTIONS <student ID>
DROP <student ID> <course code>
SWAP <student ID> <course code> <course code>
CLOSE <course code>
OPEN <course code>
CAPACITY <course code> <capacity>
LIMIT <student ID> <credit limit>
PREREQ <course code> <prerequisite>
CANCEL <course code>
ROSTER <course code>
SCHEDULE <student ID>
FREE <student ID> <day>
INFO <course code>
AUDIT <student ID>
HISTORY <student ID>
STUDENTS
REPORT

[Processing]
== State and initial state ==
- Courses: initially there are none. A course is registered with COURSE and deleted with CANCEL. Each course has a course code, credits, a capacity, a list of slots (in the order written in the timetable), a prerequisite, a registration status (open or closed), a set of enrolled students (the students currently taking that course), and a waitlist. Right after registration, the registration status is open and the enrolled students and the waitlist are empty.
- Students: initially there are none. A student is registered with STUDENT. Students are never deleted. Each student has a student ID, a year, a credit limit, grade records (a list of records), a set of courses being taken, and a history. Right after registration, both the courses being taken and the history are empty. The year and the grade records never change after registration.
- Waitlist: a per-course queue of students who requested a course when its capacity had no room. The places from the front are called "positions" 1, 2, 3, .... Under the rules below, a student is never both an enrolled student and on the waitlist of the same course, and never appears twice in the same waitlist.
A command that results in an error does not change the state at all; it only outputs one error line.

== Terms and orderings ==
- "Ascending order of course code", "ascending order of student ID" and "ascending order of department" mean lexicographic order by character code (ASCII) (when one string matches the beginning of the other, the shorter one comes first; e.g. S10 < S1001 < S9, and MA < MATH).
- The "department" of a course is the leading uppercase-letter part of its course code (e.g. the department of MATH210 is MATH, and the department of CS101 is CS).
- The "enrolled count" of a course is the number of its enrolled students. When enrolled count < capacity, the course "has a free seat".
- The "enrolled credits" of a student is the sum of the credits of the courses the student is taking. The "waitlist count" of a student is the number of waitlists the student is on (counted over all courses).
- Where the output format says "<a>/<b>", write the two integers joined by a slash "/" with no space in between (e.g. 3/30).
- A registration status is written as open or closed.
- A line marked "(indented)" in an output format is printed with 2 spaces at the beginning of the line.
- floor(x/y) is the integer obtained by discarding the fractional part of the quotient of x divided by y (every division in this task is between numbers >= 0).

== Slots and times ==
- A slot "DDhhmm-HHMM" (day DD, start at hh hours mm minutes, end at HH hours MM minutes) is "valid" only when DD is one of MO, TU, WE, TH, FR, SA, SU, 0 <= hh <= 23, 0 <= mm <= 59, 0 <= HH <= 23, 0 <= MM <= 59, and start minute < end minute. Here hh, mm, HH and MM are the values of the 2-digit strings read as decimal numbers, start minute = hh*60 + mm, and end minute = HH*60 + MM. The "length" of a slot is end minute - start minute.
- The order of days is MO, TU, WE, TH, FR, SA, SU (Monday through Sunday).
- Two slots "overlap" when they are on the same day and both "start minute of one < end minute of the other" and "start minute of the other < end minute of the one" hold. A slot that ends at some time and a slot that starts exactly at that time do not overlap (e.g. MO0900-1030 and MO1030-1200 do not overlap).
- Two courses "conflict in time" when some slot of one overlaps some slot of the other. A course with no slots conflicts in time with no course.
- The "weekly time" of a course is the sum of the lengths of its slots.
- The "time notation" of an integer m >= 0 (minutes) is the concatenation of floor(m/60) written with at least 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit), ":", and the remainder of m divided by 60 written with 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit) (e.g. 0 is 00:00, 450 is 07:30, 1320 is 22:00). This notation is used both for times (start minutes and end minutes) and for durations (weekly times, etc.).

== Grades ==
- The valid grades are the five letters A, B, C, D and F, whose "grade points" are 4, 3, 2, 1 and 0 respectively.
- Student s "has passed" course code c when s's grade records contain a record whose course code is c and whose grade is A, B or C (D and F are failing).
- The "earned credits" of a student is the sum of the credits of the records in the student's grade records whose grade is not F.
- The "GPA" of a student is as follows. If the grade records are empty, it is "n/a". Otherwise, let P = (the sum over the records of grade point * credits), C = (the sum over the records of credits), and v = floor((200*P + C)/(2*C)) (v is the integer obtained by rounding 100*P/C half up at the first decimal place). The GPA is the concatenation of floor(v/100), ".", and the remainder of v divided by 100 written with 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit) (e.g. if P = 25 and C = 8, then v = 313 and the GPA is 3.13; if P = 0, it is 0.00).

== Prerequisite expressions ==
A prerequisite other than "-" is handled as follows.
1. Lexical analysis: read the string from left to right. Each of "&", "|", "(" and ")" is one symbol of 1 character, and each run of consecutive uppercase letters and digits, taken as long as possible, is one "word". A word that matches the course code format (2 to 4 uppercase letters followed by exactly 3 digits) is a "course condition", and a word consisting of the uppercase letter Y followed by exactly 1 digit from 1 to 9 (Y1 to Y9) is a "year condition". If there is even one word that is neither, the string does not conform to the grammar.
2. Grammar: if the whole sequence of symbols and words does not exactly match the following "expression", the string does not conform to the grammar.
  expression: 1 or more terms joined by "|".
  term: 1 or more factors joined by "&".
  factor: 1 word, or "(", an expression, ")" in this order.
  ("&" binds more tightly than "|". For example, A&B|C means "A and B" or "C".)
  Examples that do not conform to the grammar: EE101&, (EE101, EE101), (), EE101||EE102, &EE101, EE101(EE102), EE1010, E101, Y0, Y10.
3. Evaluation: for student s, a course condition c is satisfied when s has passed c, and a year condition Yk is satisfied when s's year >= k (the course code of a course condition need not belong to a registered course; only the grade records are used for the decision). A term is satisfied when all of its factors are satisfied, and an expression is satisfied when any of its terms is satisfied. A parenthesized expression is, as a whole, one factor. The prerequisite "-" is satisfied by every student.
4. Canonical form: in output, a prerequisite is written in the following "canonical form". First, turn the expression into a tree as follows. An expression consisting of 2 or more terms is an "OR node" whose children are those terms in order, and a term consisting of 2 or more factors is an "AND node" whose children are those factors in order. An expression with only 1 term is that term, a term with only 1 factor is that factor, a factor of the form "(" expression ")" is the inner expression, and a word factor is a "leaf" of that word. Next, if an OR node has a child that is an OR node, replace that child with the sequence of that child's children (at the same place, keeping their order). Do the same when an AND node has a child that is an AND node. Repeat this until nothing more can be replaced. Then write a leaf as its word, an OR node as its children in order joined by "|", and an AND node as its children in order joined by "&". However, a child of an AND node that is an OR node is written enclosed in "(" and ")". Duplicate words are not removed, and nothing is reordered. The canonical form of the prerequisite "-" is "-".
  Examples: ((EE101)) becomes EE101, (EE101|EE102)|Y2 becomes EE101|EE102|Y2, (EE101&EE102)|EE103 becomes EE101&EE102|EE103, and EE101&((EE102|(EE103))&Y2) becomes EE101&(EE102|EE103)&Y2.
5. Prerequisite validation: the "prerequisite validation" for course code c and prerequisite x is: (1) if x is not "-" and does not conform to the grammar, "ERROR invalid prereq <c>"; (2) if x contains the course condition c, "ERROR self prereq <c>".

== Adding to a waitlist ==
- When student s is added to the waitlist of course c, s is inserted immediately after the last of the students on c's waitlist whose year is greater than or equal to s's year (at the front if there is no such student). As a result, the waitlist is ordered by year in descending order, and within the same year in the order in which students were added.
- "The position s would have if s joined c's waitlist" is 1 + (the number of students on c's waitlist whose year is greater than or equal to s's year), which equals s's position when s is actually added.
- When a student is removed from a waitlist, the positions of the students behind that student each move forward by 1.

== Judging a request ==
In the "eligibility check" of whether student s can take course c, check (1) to (7) below in order; the "reason" of the first one that applies is the result. SWAP performs the check while "excluding" some course x. When there is no excluded course, ignore "excluding x" in (6) and (7).
(1) c's registration status is closed: the reason is "course closed".
(2) s is an enrolled student of c: the reason is "already enrolled".
(3) s is on c's waitlist: the reason is "already waitlisted".
(4) s has passed c: the reason is "already completed <the grade of that record>".
(5) s does not satisfy c's prerequisite: the reason is "prereq not met".
(6) among the courses s is taking (excluding x), there is one that conflicts in time with c: the reason is "time conflict <the course code of the course with the smallest course code among such courses>".
(7) t = (the sum of the credits of the courses s is taking (excluding x)) + (the credits of c) is greater than s's credit limit: the reason is "credit limit <t> <s's credit limit>".
If none of these applies, the result of the eligibility check is "OK".
ENROLL, CHECK and OPTIONS use the following "request judgment" (there is no excluded course). If the result of the eligibility check is a reason, the result of the request judgment is that reason. If the result of the eligibility check is OK, it is as follows.
(8) If c has a free seat, "enroll". Even if there are students on c's waitlist (in that case, only students who cannot be promoted remain on the waitlist), the result is enroll if there is a free seat.
(9) If there is no free seat and s's waitlist count is 3 or more, the reason "waitlist limit".
(10) Otherwise, "waitlist <the position s would have if s joined c's waitlist>".
For a reason, the leading lowercase part (one of course closed, already enrolled, already waitlisted, already completed, prereq not met, time conflict, credit limit, waitlist limit) is called the "reason word", and the <...> part after it is called the "extra value". The extra value is the grade for already completed, the course code for time conflict, and "<t> <credit limit>" (two integers separated by 1 space) for credit limit; the other reasons have no extra value.
When a result of the request judgment is output, enroll is written as is, waitlist is written as "waitlist <position>", and a reason is written as the reason word followed (if there is an extra value) by 1 space and the extra value (e.g. time conflict CS101, credit limit 13 12, prereq not met).

== Promotion process ==
When DROP, SWAP, OPEN, CAPACITY, LIMIT or CANCEL succeeds, the following "promotion process" is performed after that command's output.
Examine the registered courses once each, in ascending order of course code. If the registration status of the examined course c is closed, do nothing and move on to the next course. If it is open, repeat the following.
- If c has no free seat, finish processing c.
- Look through c's waitlist in order from the front, and let s be the first student who "can be promoted" to c. A student can be promoted to c when none of the courses the student is taking conflicts in time with c, and (the student's enrolled credits) + (the credits of c) <= (the student's credit limit) (the prerequisite and the waitlist count are not checked again). If there is no such student, finish processing c.
- Remove s from c's waitlist and add s to c's enrolled students, add "promote <c>" to s's history, and output (indented) "PROMOTE <s> <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> credits <enrolled credits of s>/<credit limit of s>" (the values are those after the addition).
Students who cannot be promoted remain in the same place in the waitlist. A later course is examined in the state after the earlier courses have been processed.

== History ==
- The history of each student is a list of (command number, event) pairs in the order in which they occurred. The command number tells which of the N command lines the command is (the first command is 1). For a pair added by the promotion process, the command number is the number of the command that performed that promotion process.
- A pair is added only where [Processing] says to "add" it to the history. The event is one of the following 9 kinds of strings: "enroll <course code>", "waitlist <course code> <position>", "promote <course code>", "drop <course code>", "drop <course code> waitlist", "swap <course code> <course code>", "unlist <course code>", "cancel <course code>", "cancel <course code> waitlist".

== Order of checks ==
Each command performs the checks in the order written below, outputs the line of the first error that applies, and stops. If none applies, the command succeeds, changes the state and outputs its result. Frequently used checks are defined here.
- Student check: if no student with that student ID is registered, "ERROR unknown student <student ID>".
- Course check: if no course with that course code is registered, "ERROR unknown course <course code>" (a course deleted by CANCEL is not registered until it is registered again).
The student IDs, course codes, slots, grades and days in the <...> parts of error lines are the tokens exactly as written in the input (or parts of them).

== Commands ==
- COURSE <c> <k> <m> <t> <x>: (1) if c is registered, "ERROR duplicate course <c>"; (2) examine the slots of timetable t in the order written, and if there is an invalid slot, "ERROR invalid slot <that slot>" for the first such slot; (3) call the slots of t the 1st, 2nd, ... in the order written; if there is a pair with i < j such that the i-th slot and the j-th slot overlap, then for the pair with the smallest i, and among those the smallest j, "ERROR overlapping slots <the i-th slot> <the j-th slot>"; (4)(5) prerequisite validation for c and x. On success, register a course with course code c, credits k, capacity m, the slots of t as its list of slots (0 slots if t is "-"), and prerequisite x, and output "COURSE <c> credits <k> capacity <m> meetings <number of slots> weekly <time notation of the weekly time> prereq <canonical form of x>".
- STUDENT <s> <y> <L> <r>: (1) if s is registered, "ERROR duplicate student <s>"; (2) examine the records of grade records r one at a time in the order written: if the grade of the record is not valid, "ERROR invalid grade <s> <course code of that record> <grade of that record>"; if the grade is valid but the course code of the record also appears in an earlier record, "ERROR duplicate record <s> <that course code>" (stop at the first record to which one of these applies). On success, register a student with student ID s, year y, credit limit L, and grade records r (empty if r is "-"), and output "STUDENT <s> year <y> limit <L> records <number of records> earned <earned credits> gpa <GPA>".
- ENROLL <s> <c>: (1) student check, (2) course check, (3) if the result of the request judgment for s and c is a reason, "ERROR <reason word> <s> <c>" (if there is an extra value, followed by 1 space and the extra value). On success, do the following according to the result of the request judgment.
  When it is enroll: add s to c's enrolled students, add "enroll <c>" to s's history, and output "ENROLL <s> <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> credits <enrolled credits of s>/<credit limit of s>" (the values are those after the addition).
  When it is waitlist <p>: add s to c's waitlist (s's position becomes p), add "waitlist <c> <p>" to s's history, and output "ENROLL <s> <c> waitlist <p>".
- CHECK <s> <c>: (1) student check, (2) course check. On success, output "CHECK <s> <c> <result of the request judgment for s and c>". The state is not changed.
- OPTIONS <s>: (1) student check. On success, output "OPTIONS <s> courses <number of registered courses>", then, for each registered course c in ascending order of course code, output (indented) "<c> <result of the request judgment for s and c>". The state is not changed.
- DROP <s> <c>: (1) student check, (2) course check, (3) if s is neither an enrolled student of c nor a student on c's waitlist, "ERROR not registered <s> <c>". On success, do the following. This is the same even if c's registration status is closed.
  When s is an enrolled student of c: remove s from c's enrolled students, add "drop <c>" to s's history, and output "DROP <s> <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> credits <enrolled credits of s>/<credit limit of s>" (the values are those after the removal).
  When s is on c's waitlist: remove s from c's waitlist, add "drop <c> waitlist" to s's history, and output "DROP <s> <c> waitlist removed".
  After that, perform the promotion process.
- SWAP <s> <a> <b>: (1) student check, (2) course check for a, (3) course check for b, (4) if s is not an enrolled student of a, "ERROR not enrolled <s> <a>" (this includes the case where s is only on a's waitlist), (5) if a and b are the same course code, "ERROR same course <a>", (6) if the result of the eligibility check for s and b, excluding a, is a reason, "ERROR <reason word> <s> <b>" (if there is an extra value, followed by 1 space and the extra value), (7) if b has no free seat, "ERROR course full <b>". On success, remove s from a's enrolled students and add s to b's enrolled students (these two are done at once), add "swap <a> <b>" to s's history, and output "SWAP <s> <a> -> <b> credits <enrolled credits of s>/<credit limit of s>". After that, perform the promotion process. SWAP never adds a student to a waitlist, and it does not check the waitlist count.
- CLOSE <c>: (1) course check, (2) if c's registration status is closed, "ERROR already closed <c>". On success, set c's registration status to closed and output "CLOSE <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> waitlist <number of students on c's waitlist>". The enrolled students and the waitlist stay as they are.
- OPEN <c>: (1) course check, (2) if c's registration status is open, "ERROR already open <c>". On success, set c's registration status to open and output "OPEN <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> waitlist <number of students on c's waitlist>". After that, perform the promotion process.
- CAPACITY <c> <n>: (1) course check, (2) if n < (enrolled count of c), "ERROR capacity below enrollment <c> <enrolled count of c>". On success, change c's capacity to n and output "CAPACITY <c> <capacity before the change> -> <n>". After that, perform the promotion process.
- LIMIT <s> <n>: (1) student check, (2) if n < (enrolled credits of s), "ERROR limit below enrolled <s> <enrolled credits of s>". On success, change s's credit limit to n and output "LIMIT <s> <credit limit before the change> -> <n>". After that, perform the promotion process.
- PREREQ <c> <x>: (1) course check, (2)(3) prerequisite validation for c and x. On success, change c's prerequisite to x and output "PREREQ <c> <canonical form of x>". Then examine the students on c's waitlist in order from the front, and for each student who does not satisfy the new prerequisite, remove that student from the waitlist, add "unlist <c>" to that student's history, and output (indented) "UNLIST <student ID of that student> <c>". The enrolled students of c stay as they are. The promotion process is not performed.
- CANCEL <c>: (1) course check. On success, output "CANCEL <c> enrolled <enrolled count of c> waitlist <number of students on c's waitlist>", add "cancel <c>" to the history of each enrolled student of c, and add "cancel <c> waitlist" to the history of each student on c's waitlist. Then delete c (c is no longer a course being taken by any student, and c's waitlist ceases to exist). A deleted course code can later be registered again as a new course with COURSE. After that, perform the promotion process.
- ROSTER <c>: (1) course check. On success, output "ROSTER <c> <registration status> enrolled <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> waitlist <number of students on c's waitlist>", then output the enrolled students of c in ascending order of student ID as (indented) "enrolled <student ID> year <year>", and then the students on c's waitlist in order from the front as (indented) "waitlist <position> <student ID> year <year>".
- SCHEDULE <s>: (1) student check. On success, output "SCHEDULE <s> courses <number of courses s is taking> credits <enrolled credits of s> weekly <time notation of the sum of the weekly times of the courses s is taking>". Then, for each day d in the order of days, if at least one slot of the courses s is taking is on day d, output a line consisting of (indented) "<d>" followed, for each of those slots in ascending order of start minute, by 1 space and "<time notation of the start minute>-<time notation of the end minute> <course code of the course of that slot>" (by the rules, the start minutes of those slots are all different). Finally, if some of the courses s is taking have no slots, output a line consisting of (indented) "TBA" followed by their course codes in ascending order, each appended after 1 space.
- FREE <s> <d>: (1) student check, (2) if d is none of MO, TU, WE, TH, FR, SA, SU, "ERROR invalid day <d>". Among the integers u with 480 <= u < 1320 (each minute from 08:00 to 22:00), those for which "start minute <= u < end minute" holds for none of the day-d slots of the courses s is taking are "free". Group the free values of u into consecutive ranges a <= u < b (u = a-1 and u = b are not free or are outside 480 <= u < 1320), and order the ranges by ascending a. On success, output a line consisting of "FREE <s> <d> total <time notation of the number of free values of u>" followed, for each range, by 1 space and "<time notation of a>-<time notation of b>" (if there is no free value, the line ends with "total 00:00").
- INFO <c>: (1) course check. On success, output "INFO <c> <registration status> credits <credits> capacity <capacity> prereq <canonical form of the prerequisite>". Then sort the slots of c by (order of days, start minute) and output each as (indented) "meet <day> <time notation of the start minute>-<time notation of the end minute>". If there are no slots, output (indented) "meet TBA" instead. Finally, if there are registered courses whose prerequisite contains the course condition c, output a line consisting of (indented) "required by" followed by their course codes in ascending order, each appended after 1 space; if there are none, output (indented) "required by none".
- AUDIT <s>: (1) student check. On success, output the following lines in order.
  "AUDIT <s> year <year> limit <credit limit>".
  For each record of the grade records, in ascending order of course code: (indented) "record <course code> <grade> <credits>".
  (indented) "earned <earned credits> gpa <GPA>".
  For each course being taken, in ascending order of course code: (indented) "enrolled <course code> <credits of that course>".
  (indented) "credits <enrolled credits> remaining <credit limit - enrolled credits>".
  For each course whose waitlist s is on, in ascending order of course code: (indented) "waitlist <course code> <position of s in that waitlist>".
- HISTORY <s>: (1) student check. On success, output "HISTORY <s> events <number of pairs in the history>", then output each pair of the history in order as (indented) "<command number> <event>".
- STUDENTS: output "STUDENTS <number of registered students>", then output each student s in ascending order of student ID as (indented) "<s> year <year> credits <enrolled credits>/<credit limit> courses <number of courses being taken> waitlists <waitlist count>".
- REPORT: the "rate" for integers k and m is floor(k*100/m) followed by "%" if m >= 1, and "n/a" if m = 0. First output "REPORT courses <number of registered courses> enrolled <sum K of the enrolled counts of all courses>/<sum M of the capacities of all courses> rate <rate for K and M> waitlisted <sum of the numbers of students on the waitlists of all courses>". Then output each course c in ascending order of course code as (indented) "<c> <registration status> <enrolled count of c>/<capacity of c> <rate for those two> waitlist <number of students on c's waitlist>". Finally, list the departments of the registered courses in ascending order without duplicates, and for each department g output (indented) "dept <g> courses <number of courses whose department is g> enrolled <sum of the enrolled counts of those courses>/<sum of the capacities of those courses> rate <rate for those two> waitlisted <sum of the numbers of students on the waitlists of those courses>".

[Output format]
For each command, output the lines defined in [Processing] in order. A line marked (indented) starts with 2 spaces; every other line has no whitespace at its beginning. Tokens within a line are separated by 1 space, and there is no whitespace at the end of a line. No empty lines are output. All output consists of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline character (LF).
For a command that performs the promotion process, the command's line may be followed by (indented) lines output by the promotion process.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 1000.
- The input correctly follows the formats of [Input format] (there are no unknown commands, wrong numbers of tokens, or tokens that do not match their format). The only errors to detect are those listed in [Processing].
- At most 50 courses are registered at the same time, and at most 100 students are registered.
- When everything is computed according to the rules above, the absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values, is less than 2^31 (computing with 32-bit signed integers does not overflow).

In canonical form, the prerequisite of EE201 is written as (EE101|Y3)&EE100. A2 has passed EE101 with grade B, so A2 cannot request EE101, and since A2 has no record for EE100, A2 does not satisfy the prerequisite of EE201 either. A3 failed EE101 with D, but as a year-3 student A3 satisfies the prerequisite of EE201 through Y3. On the waitlist of EE101, A3 (year 3), who requested later, is placed ahead of A4 (year 1). When A1 drops EE101, the promotion process is performed, but A3 cannot be promoted because EE102, which A3 is taking (MO 10:00-11:00), conflicts in time with EE101 (MO 09:00-10:30); so the next student, A4, is promoted. The GPA of A3 is 1.40, since P = 1*3 + 2*2 = 7 and C = 5 give v = floor(1405/10) = 140.
