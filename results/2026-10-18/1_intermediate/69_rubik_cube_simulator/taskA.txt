[Overview]
Write a program that represents a 3x3x3 Rubik's Cube as the arrangement of the colors of its 54 stickers (colored labels) and performs operations and analyses on it. The program reads a list of commands from standard input, executes them in order, and writes the results to standard output. The commands cover interpreting and applying move sequences written in move notation, displaying the net, checking and loading states, undoing and redoing moves, transforming sequences (inverse, simplification, mirroring, rotation), the order of a sequence and the cycle structure of the pieces, generating scrambles with random numbers, searching for short solutions, and so on.

[Input format]
- The first line is the number of commands N.
- It is followed by N commands, one per line.
A command line consists either of the command name alone, or of the command name, a single space, and the argument string concatenated in this order. The command name runs from the start of the line up to just before the first space (the whole line if there is no space), and the argument string runs from just after the first space to the end of the line. The command formats are as follows (the parts other than the command name are argument names).
RESET
SHOW
STATE
ISSOLVED
HISTORY
SOLUTION
ANALYZE
STAGE
MACROS
CHECK facelets
LOAD facelets
UNDO n
REDO n
SEARCH d
WHERE piece
SAVE name
RESTORE name
SCRAMBLE n seed
APPLY seq
INVERT seq
SIMPLIFY seq
METRICS seq
ORDER seq
CYCLES seq
UNROTATE seq
UNTIL seq
DEFINE name seq
MIRROR plane seq
ROTATE rot seq
The commands fall into the following 4 kinds, and their arguments are written as follows.
- Commands without arguments: RESET, SHOW, STATE, ISSOLVED, HISTORY, SOLUTION, ANALYZE, STAGE, MACROS. They have no argument string.
- Word commands: CHECK, LOAD, UNDO, REDO, SEARCH, WHERE, SAVE, RESTORE, SCRAMBLE. Each part of the argument string separated by spaces is called a word (if there is no argument string, there are 0 words); the words, in the number given in the format, are the arguments in the order of the format.
- Sequence commands: APPLY, INVERT, SIMPLIFY, METRICS, ORDER, CYCLES, UNROTATE, UNTIL. The whole argument string is the sequence string seq. If there is no argument string, seq is the empty string.
- Head-word commands: DEFINE, MIRROR, ROTATE. The part of the argument string from its start up to just before the first space (the whole argument string if there is no space) is the head word, and it is the name, plane, or rot of the format. The sequence string seq runs from the character after the space that immediately follows the head word to the end of the line. If nothing follows the head word, seq is the empty string.
Each argument is written as follows.
- n, d, and seed are number words. A number word is a string of 1 to 10 characters consisting only of digits (0-9) that, when it has 2 or more characters, does not start with 0. Its value is the value read as a decimal number. For example, 0, 7, and 2147483646 are number words, while 07, -1, +3, 12345678901, and x are not.
- name is a name. A name is a string of 1 to 8 characters consisting only of lowercase letters (a-z).
- plane is one of LR, UD, FB.
- rot is one of x, x', x2, y, y', y2, z, z', z2.
- facelets and piece are arbitrary words (their contents are examined by the processing of each command).
- seq is a sequence string (explained in section 4 of [Processing]).

[Processing]
Process the commands one at a time in input order, writing the output of each immediately. Below, a string enclosed in double quotes is a string that appears as is in the input or output. In the output descriptions, a part such as <n> is replaced by its value.

1. Cube, faces, and stickers
- The cube has 6 faces, called U (up), R (right), F (front), D (down), L (left), and B (back). The order in which the faces are listed here is called the face order. U and D, R and L, and F and B are called opposite faces of each other.
- Colors are represented by the 6 uppercase letters W, R, G, Y, O, B. The standard color of each face is W for U, R for R, G for F, Y for D, O for L, and B for B. The order of the colors listed in this order (W, R, G, Y, O, B) is called the color order.
- Each face has 9 stickers arranged in 3 rows and 3 columns. When each face is viewed in the orientation below, the sticker in row i from the top and column j from the left is written as (i, j) of that face; for a face X it is written X(i, j) (1 <= i <= 3, 1 <= j <= 3).
  U: viewed from above, with the side adjacent to face B at the top, the side adjacent to face F at the bottom, the side adjacent to face L on the left, and the side adjacent to face R on the right
  R: viewed from the right, with the side adjacent to face U at the top, the side adjacent to face F on the left, and the side adjacent to face B on the right
  F: viewed from the front, with the side adjacent to face U at the top, the side adjacent to face L on the left, and the side adjacent to face R on the right
  D: viewed from below, with the side adjacent to face F at the top, the side adjacent to face B at the bottom, the side adjacent to face L on the left, and the side adjacent to face R on the right
  L: viewed from the left, with the side adjacent to face U at the top, the side adjacent to face B on the left, and the side adjacent to face F on the right
  B: viewed from behind, with the side adjacent to face U at the top, the side adjacent to face R on the left, and the side adjacent to face L on the right
  These are the orientations of the net in which face U is placed above face F, face D below it, face L to its left, face R to its right, and face B further to the right of face R. X(i, j) of face X is also written Xk (k = 3(i - 1) + j). For example, U9 is U(3, 3) and R1 is R(1, 1).
- Sticker numbers: the number of Xk of the p-th face in face order (U is the 1st, B is the 6th) is 9(p - 1) + k. The numbers run from 1 to 54. For example, U9 is number 9, R1 is number 10, F3 is number 21, and B9 is number 54.
- A state is the color of each of the 54 stickers. The state string is the 54-character string of the color letters of the stickers in number order. The state in which every sticker has the standard color of its face is called the solved state.
- A state is solved when, on every face, the colors of all 9 stickers are the same as the color of (2, 2) (the center) of that face. Even if the orientation of the whole cube has changed, the state is solved if each face is a single color.

2. Pieces
- The piece positions of the cube are the following 8 corners, 12 edges, and 6 centers. Each letter of a position's name denotes a face on which a sticker of that position lies, and the stickers of the position are listed in parentheses in the order of the letters of the name. The 1st, 2nd (and 3rd) stickers in this order are called the 1st, 2nd (and 3rd) stickers of that position. For corners, this order is clockwise when the corner is viewed from outside.
  Corners: URF (U9 R1 F3), UFL (U7 F1 L3), ULB (U1 L1 B3), UBR (U3 B1 R3), DFR (D3 F9 R7), DLF (D1 L9 F7), DBL (D7 B9 L7), DRB (D9 R9 B7)
  Edges: UR (U6 R2), UF (U8 F2), UL (U4 L2), UB (U2 B2), DR (D6 R8), DF (D2 F8), DL (D4 L8), DB (D8 B8), FR (F6 R4), FL (F4 L6), BL (B6 L4), BR (B4 R6)
  Centers: U (U5), R (R5), F (F5), D (D5), L (L5), B (B5)
  For each of corners, edges, and centers, the order listed above is called the position order of that kind.
- Pieces are called by the same names as the positions. The colors of a piece P are the standard colors of the faces denoted by the letters of P's name, in the order of the letters of the name. For example, the colors of piece URF are W, R, G, the colors of piece UF are W, G, and the color of piece F is G. In the solved state, each position contains the piece with the same name as the position.

3. Moves
- A move is a pair of a move letter (1 character) and an amount. The move letters are the 18 letters U, D, F, B, L, R, M, E, S, u, d, f, b, l, r, x, y, z, and the amount is 1, 2, or 3. A move with amount a means performing the move with amount 1 a times in a row. The notation of a move is the move letter followed by nothing if the amount is 1, by "2" if it is 2, and by "'" if it is 3 (e.g. R, R2, R').
- Performing a move carries stickers (with their colors) to other places on the cube. The effects of the moves with amount 1 are as follows. "X(i, j) -> Y(i', j')" means that the sticker that was at X(i, j) moves to Y(i', j'), and an expression containing i or j holds for all i, j = 1, 2, 3. Stickers not listed do not move.
  U: U(i, j) -> U(j, 4 - i), F(1, j) -> L(1, j), L(1, j) -> B(1, j), B(1, j) -> R(1, j), R(1, j) -> F(1, j)
  R: R(i, j) -> R(j, 4 - i), F(i, 3) -> U(i, 3), U(i, 3) -> B(4 - i, 1), B(i, 1) -> D(4 - i, 3), D(i, 3) -> F(i, 3)
  F: F(i, j) -> F(j, 4 - i), U(3, j) -> R(j, 1), R(i, 1) -> D(1, 4 - i), D(1, j) -> L(j, 3), L(i, 3) -> U(3, 4 - i)
  D: D(i, j) -> D(j, 4 - i), F(3, j) -> R(3, j), R(3, j) -> B(3, j), B(3, j) -> L(3, j), L(3, j) -> F(3, j)
  L: L(i, j) -> L(j, 4 - i), U(i, 1) -> F(i, 1), F(i, 1) -> D(i, 1), D(i, 1) -> B(4 - i, 3), B(i, 3) -> U(4 - i, 1)
  B: B(i, j) -> B(j, 4 - i), U(1, j) -> L(4 - j, 1), L(i, 1) -> D(3, i), D(3, j) -> R(4 - j, 3), R(i, 3) -> U(1, i)
  M: U(i, 2) -> F(i, 2), F(i, 2) -> D(i, 2), D(i, 2) -> B(4 - i, 2), B(i, 2) -> U(4 - i, 2)
  E: F(2, j) -> R(2, j), R(2, j) -> B(2, j), B(2, j) -> L(2, j), L(2, j) -> F(2, j)
  S: U(2, j) -> R(j, 2), R(i, 2) -> D(2, 4 - i), D(2, j) -> L(j, 2), L(i, 2) -> U(2, 4 - i)
  That is, U, R, F, D, L, B turn the layer of their face 90 degrees clockwise as seen from outside that face; M turns the layer between L and R in the same direction as L, E turns the layer between U and D in the same direction as D, and S turns the layer between F and B in the same direction as F (the centers move too). For each remaining move letter, the move with amount 1 has the same effect as performing the following moves in succession (the listed moves give the same result in any order).
  r: R, M'; l: L, M; u: U, E'; d: D, E; f: F, S; b: B, S'
  x: R, M', L'; y: U, E', D'; z: F, S, B'
  u, d, f, b, l, r are moves that turn two layers, and x, y, z are rotations of the whole cube. Moves with the letters U, D, F, B, L, R are called outer moves, those with M, E, S slice moves, those with u, d, f, b, l, r wide moves, and those with x, y, z rotation moves.
- Axis of a move letter: the axis of R, L, M, r, l, x is x; the axis of U, D, E, u, d, y is y; the axis of F, B, S, f, b, z is z.
- A move sequence (or simply sequence) is zero or more moves in a row. Applying a sequence to a state means performing its moves in order, starting from the first. The inverse of a sequence is the sequence with the order of the moves reversed and the amount a of each move replaced by 4 - a (the inverse of R is R', and the inverse of R2 is R2). The sequence formed by concatenating c copies of a sequence X is called the c-th power of X.
- The effect of a sequence is the correspondence that tells, for each sticker number, to the place of which numbered sticker that sticker moves when the sequence is applied. Two sequences have the same effect when this correspondence agrees for every number (then they give the same result whatever state they are applied to). The effects of the 54 moves formed from the 18 move letters and the 3 amounts are pairwise distinct.
- Sequence notation: the notation of a sequence containing k moves is, if k is 1 or more, k followed by the notation of each move in order, separated by single spaces (e.g. 3 R U2 F'), and if k is 0, it is 0. In the outputs, the parts that stand for a sequence (<sequence>, <that sequence>, <the sequence found>, and <history>) are written in sequence notation.

4. Interpreting a sequence string
The sequence string seq is converted into a move sequence by reading it one character at a time from the start with the following procedure. Character positions are counted with the first character of the command line as position 1. The reading position starts at the position of the first character of seq (the length of the command name + 2 for sequence commands, and the length of the command name + the length of the head word + 3 for head-word commands). The current character is the character at the reading position; when the reading position exceeds the length of the line, "there is no current character". Reading a character means advancing the reading position by 1. Skipping spaces means reading characters while the current character is a space. A "syntax error" means outputting error: syntax at <p>, where p is the reading position at that moment, and finishing the processing of that command (when the reading position exceeds the length of the line, p is the length of the line + 1). Only the first error that occurs in the procedure is output.
(1) Reading a sequence: start with the empty sequence and repeat the following. Skip spaces; if the current character is a move letter, read a move by (2); if it is "(", read by (3); if it is "[", read by (4); if it is "{", read by (5); and append the resulting sequence to the end of the sequence so far. Otherwise (including when there is no current character), end the repetition; the sequence so far is the result.
(2) Reading a move: read the move letter. If the following current character is "2", the amount is 2 and that character is also read; if it is "'", the amount is 3 and that character is also read; otherwise the amount is 1 (nothing is read). The result is the sequence consisting of that one move. No space may be placed between the move letter and "2" or "'".
(3) Group: read "(", read a sequence X by (1), and skip spaces. If the current character is not ")", it is a syntax error. Read ")"; the result is the result of reading a power by (6) for X.
(4) Bracket expression: read "[", read a sequence A by (1), and skip spaces. If the current character is neither "," nor ":", it is a syntax error. Read that character, read a sequence B by (1), and skip spaces. If the current character is not "]", it is a syntax error. Read "]". Let X be, for ",", the concatenation of A, B, the inverse of A, and the inverse of B in this order (commutator), and for ":", the concatenation of A, B, and the inverse of A in this order (conjugate); the result is the result of reading a power by (6) for X.
(5) Macro reference: read "{", then read as many of the following lowercase letters (a-z) as possible. The string formed by concatenating the lowercase letters read is the name. If the name is the empty string or the current character is not "}", it is a syntax error. If there is no macro with that name (DEFINE in section 12), output error: undefined <name> and finish the processing of that command. Read "}"; the result is the result of reading a power by (6) for that macro's sequence X.
(6) Reading a power: let c = 1. If the current character is a digit from 1 to 9, read it and set c to its value; further, if the current character is then a digit from 0 to 9, read it too and set c to 10c + its value. Next, if the current character is "'", read it. The result is the c-th power of X, and if "'" was read, the inverse of that. No space may be placed before the digits or "'".
(7) Whole: read a sequence by (1) and skip spaces. If there is a current character, it is a syntax error. If there is none, then if the number of moves in the sequence read exceeds 500, output error: too long and finish the processing of that command; if it is 500 or less, that sequence is the result of interpreting seq.
For example, "RUR'U'", "R U R' U'", and "[R, U]" all become the sequence R, U, R', U'; "(R U)2'" becomes the sequence U', R', U', R'; and "[R: U2]" becomes the sequence R, U2, R'. "R2'", "R3", "(R U)0", and "(R U) 2" are all syntax errors ("(R)100" is a syntax error because "0" remains after the 10th power of R).

5. Identifying pieces and cycle notation
- For a state and a corner position q, let c1, c2, c3 be the colors of the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd stickers of q. For a corner piece P with colors p1, p2, p3 and t in {0, 1, 2}, when c(t+1), c(t+2), c(t+3) are equal to p1, p2, p3 (a subscript that exceeds 3 has 3 subtracted from it), we say that "q contains piece P with orientation t". For example, if q = URF and c1, c2, c3 are G, W, R, then q contains piece URF with orientation 1. If there are no such P and t, q contains no piece.
- For the sticker colors c1, c2 of an edge position q and the colors p1, p2 of an edge piece P: if c1 = p1 and c2 = p2, q contains P with orientation 0; if c1 = p2 and c2 = p1, q contains P with orientation 1. If neither holds (for every piece), q contains no piece.
- If the sticker color of a center position q is the same as the color of a center piece P, q contains P with orientation 0.
- Cycle notation: for one kind (corners, edges, or centers), when each position contains one piece and each piece is contained in exactly one position, the cycle notation of that kind is built as follows. "Piece P is contained in position q with orientation t" is regarded as "P moves to q, and its orientation is t". Starting from the first position p1 in the position order of that kind that is not yet contained in any cycle, let p2 be the position to which piece p1 (the piece with the same name as position p1) moves, p3 the position to which piece p2 moves, ..., and follow them; the positions up to pk, the position just before returning to p1, form one cycle (p1, p2, ..., pk) (k may be 1). Let s be the sum of the orientations of pieces p1, ..., pk. Repeat this until every position is contained in a cycle. Each cycle is written, only when k >= 2 or the mark below is not empty, as the concatenation of "(", the names p1 through pk separated by single spaces, ")", and the mark, in this order. The mark is, for corners, empty if the remainder of s divided by 3 is 0, "+" if it is 1, and "-" if it is 2; for edges, empty if the remainder of s divided by 2 is 0 and "+" if it is 1; for centers, always empty. The cycle notation is the written cycles, in the order in which they were built, separated by single spaces; if no cycle is written, it is none. For example, the corner cycle notation of the state obtained by applying R to the solved state is (URF UBR DRB DFR).

6. Normalizing the orientation
- The following 24 rotation sequences, in this order, are called the rotation table.
  (1) the empty sequence (2) y (3) y2 (4) y' (5) x (6) x y (7) x y2 (8) x y' (9) x2 (10) x2 y (11) x2 y2 (12) x2 y' (13) x' (14) x' y (15) x' y2 (16) x' y' (17) z (18) z y (19) z y2 (20) z y' (21) z' (22) z' y (23) z' y2 (24) z' y'
  Their effects are pairwise distinct, and the effect of any sequence consisting only of rotation moves is the same as the effect of exactly one of them.
- When applying some sequence in the rotation table to a state makes the colors of all 6 centers the standard colors of their faces, there is exactly one such sequence; it is called the normalizing rotation, and the state obtained by applying the normalizing rotation is called the normalized state. A state obtained by applying moves, and a state that passed the check of section 7, always have a normalizing rotation.

7. Checking a state
For a string w, examine (1)-(10) below in this order; the string on the right of the first one that applies is the invalidity reason. A w to which none of them applies is valid. (5)-(7) are examined for the state whose state string is w, and (8)-(10) for the normalized state of that state.
(1) The length of w is not 54: length <length of w>
(2) There is a character other than W, R, G, Y, O, B: char <position of the first such character> (positions count the start of w as 1)
(3) In color order, there is a color whose number of occurrences in w is not 9: count <the first such color in color order> <number of occurrences of that color>
(4) There is no normalizing rotation: centers
(5) Some corner position contains no piece: corner <the first such position in position order>
(6) Some edge position contains no piece: edge <the first such position in position order>
(7) Some piece is contained in no position: missing <the first such piece, examining the corners in position order and then the edges in position order>
(8) The sum of the orientations of the pieces contained in the corner positions is not a multiple of 3: twist
(9) The sum of the orientations of the pieces contained in the edge positions is not a multiple of 2: flip
(10) The parity of the corner permutation differs from the parity of the edge permutation: parity. The parity of the corner permutation is whether the inversion count (the number of pairs in which the earlier number is larger) of the following list is even or odd: list the corner positions in position order and, for each, take the index in position order of the piece contained in it. The same applies to edges.
A valid string is the state string of a state that can be made from the solved state by applying outer moves and rotation moves; also, the state string of any state obtained by applying moves is valid.

8. Transforming and measuring move sequences
- Simplification: a move sequence X is simplified as follows. (a) Split X into maximal intervals in which moves of the same axis are consecutive (runs). (b) Replace each run with the following: list the move letters appearing in the run in the order of their first appearance, and for each letter compute the remainder e of the sum of the amounts of the moves with that letter in the run divided by 4; if e is not 0, put one move (that letter, e) (letters with e equal to 0 are removed). (c) If the sequence formed by concatenating the results of (b) in order is the same as X, it is the result of simplification. If it differs, return to (a) with that sequence as the new X. Since moves of the same axis give the same result in any order, simplification does not change the effect of the sequence. For example, "R L R" becomes R2, L; "L U U' R L" becomes L2, R; and "R U R' U' U R U' R'" becomes the empty sequence.
- Mirroring: if plane is LR, the mirror axis is axis x; if UD, axis y; if FB, axis z. Replace each move (letter m, amount a) of the sequence as follows. If the axis of m is the mirror axis and m is a letter of an outer move or a wide move, change m to its opposite letter (swap R and L, r and l, U and D, u and d, F and B, f and b) and change the amount to 4 - a. If the axis of m is the mirror axis and m is a letter of a slice move or a rotation move, leave it unchanged. If the axis of m is not the mirror axis, keep the letter and change the amount to 4 - a. This gives the move sequence as seen when the cube is reflected in a mirror.
- Rotation conversion: let rho be the move of rot. Replace each move m of the sequence with the move having the same effect as "rho, m, the inverse of rho" (exactly one of the 54 moves does).
- Rotation removal: rewrite a sequence X as a sequence of outer moves only followed by a sequence of rotation moves. (a) Replace the slice moves and wide moves of X with the following sequences (the left side is the move with amount 1; the replacement for the move with amount a is the right side with the amount of each move multiplied by a and taken as the remainder after division by 4).
  M: R L' x'; E: U D' y'; S: F' B z; r: L x; l: R x'; u: D y; d: U y'; f: B z; b: F z'
  For example, M2 is replaced by R2 L2 x2, and r' by L' x'. (b) Let the rotation sequence H be the empty sequence, and look at the moves of the replaced sequence in order from the first: if it is a rotation move, append it to the end of H (it is not added to the output); if it is an outer move m, append to the end of the output sequence the outer move having the same effect as "H, m, the inverse of H" (exactly one exists). (c) Finally, append to the end of the output sequence the sequence of the rotation table whose effect is the same as that of H. The output sequence built this way is the result, and its effect is the same as that of X.
- Metrics: sum the following values over the moves of the sequence to obtain 4 values.
  htm: 1 for outer moves and wide moves, 2 for slice moves, 0 for rotation moves
  qtm: for outer moves and wide moves, 2 if the amount is 2 and 1 otherwise; for slice moves, twice that; 0 for rotation moves
  stm: 0 for rotation moves, 1 for every other move
  etm: 1 for every move
- Order: under the effect of a sequence X, let pi(i) be the number to which the sticker with number i moves. The order of X is the least common multiple of the lengths of all cycles of pi (followed as i, pi(i), pi(pi(i)), ... until returning to i), and it is the minimum number of times X must be applied in succession to return any state to its original. The order of the empty sequence is 1.

9. Random numbers and scrambles
- Random numbers are generated with MINSTD. The initial value of a number x is seed; each time one random number is taken, x is replaced by (48271 * x) mod 2147483647, and that new x is the value taken.
- An n-move scramble is built as follows. Decide the i-th move for i = 1, 2, ..., n in this order. The usable faces are the 6 faces listed in face order, excluding the face of the (i - 1)-th move (when i >= 2) and, when the faces of the (i - 1)-th move and the (i - 2)-th move have the same axis (when i >= 3), the two faces of that axis (the axis of a face is the axis of its letter). Take one random number and call its value v; with m the number of usable faces, the face of the i-th move is the usable face at index v mod m from the start (the first counts as index 0). Then take one more random number and call its value v'; the amount is 1 if v' mod 3 is 0, 3 if it is 1, and 2 if it is 2. This produces a sequence of outer moves in which the same face never appears twice in a row and faces of the same axis never appear in 3 consecutive moves.

10. Searching for a solution
The candidate moves of the search are the 18 outer moves in the order U, U2, U', R, R2, R', F, F2, F', D, D2, D', L, L2, L', B, B2, B'. Among the sequences of candidate moves, those in which no two moves of the same face are consecutive and U never comes immediately after D, R never immediately after L, and F never immediately after B are called search sequences. Find the shortest among the search sequences of length d or less that make the current state solved when applied to it. If there are several shortest ones, choose the lexicographically smallest in the order of the candidate moves (comparing from the first move, the one whose first differing move comes earlier in the order of the candidate moves). The sequence of length 0 (the empty sequence) qualifies when the current state is solved.

11. Program data and command checks
The program holds the following data. Initially, the current state is the solved state and everything else is empty.
- The current state
- History: the sequence of applied moves.
- Redo sequence: the sequence of moves undone by UNDO.
- Macros: a collection of pairs of a name and a move sequence.
- Saves: a collection of pairs of a name and a pair of a state and a history.
For each command, examine (1)-(5) below in order and process the first one that applies. A command that outputs an error changes none of this data.
(1) The command name is not in the formats (uppercase and lowercase letters are distinguished): error: unknown command <command name>
(2) The arguments have the wrong form: error: bad arguments. The arguments have the wrong form when any of the following holds: a command without arguments has an argument string; the number of words of a word command differs from the format; n, d, or seed is not a number word; the name of SAVE or RESTORE is not a name; a head-word command has no argument string; the head word does not match how it is written in [Input format].
(3) For sequence commands and head-word commands, interpret seq as described in section 4, and if there is an error, output it.
(4) An argument value is out of range: error: bad value. The ranges are: n of UNDO and REDO is 1 or more; d of SEARCH is 4 or less; n of SCRAMBLE is from 1 to 50 and seed is from 1 to 2147483646. Also, if piece of WHERE is not the name of a corner or edge position (one of the 20), output error: unknown piece <piece>.
(5) Process as described in section 12 (errors defined there also exist).

12. Processing of each command
- RESET: set the current state to the solved state, empty the history and the redo sequence, and output reset. The macros and saves do not change.
- SHOW: output the net of the current state in 9 lines. Lines 1-3 are 6 spaces followed by the 3 colors of row i (i = 1, 2, 3) of U, separated by single spaces. Lines 4-6 are the 12 colors of row i of L, F, R, B, in this order, separated by single spaces. Lines 7-9 are row i of D written in the same form as lines 1-3.
- STATE: output state <state string of the current state>.
- ISSOLVED: if the current state is solved, output solved; otherwise, with k the number of stickers whose color differs from the color of the center of their face, output unsolved <k>.
- HISTORY: output history <history> (<history> is in sequence notation).
- SOLUTION: compute the sequence obtained by simplifying the inverse of the history, and output solution <that sequence>.
- ANALYZE: for the normalized state of the current state, output the following 3 lines. Line 1 is rotation none if the normalizing rotation is the empty sequence, and otherwise rotation followed by the notation of each move of the normalizing rotation, separated by single spaces (e.g. rotation z' y). Line 2 is corners <corner cycle notation>, and line 3 is edges <edge cycle notation>.
- STAGE: for the normalized state of the current state, let cross be the number of positions among DR, DF, DL, DB that contain the piece with the same name with orientation 0; let slots be the number of position pairs among (DFR, FR), (DLF, FL), (DBL, BL), (DRB, BR) in which both positions contain the pieces with the same names with orientation 0; and let top be the number of stickers among the 9 stickers of U whose color is W. The stage is solved if the state is solved; otherwise oll if cross = 4 and slots = 4 and top = 9; otherwise f2l if cross = 4 and slots = 4; otherwise cross if cross = 4; and none otherwise. Output stage <stage> cross <cross> slots <slots> top <top>.
- MACROS: output macros <number of macros> on the first line, followed by one line macro <name> <sequence> per macro, in lexicographic order of the names (character code order).
- CHECK facelets: check facelets as described in section 7; if it is valid, output valid, and otherwise output invalid <invalidity reason> (e.g. invalid count W 8).
- LOAD facelets: check facelets as described in section 7; if it is not valid, output error: invalid <invalidity reason>. If it is valid, set the current state to the state whose state string is facelets, empty the history and the redo sequence, and output loaded.
- UNDO n: if the history is empty, output error: nothing to undo. Otherwise, with k = min(n, length of the history), repeat k times "remove the last move of the history, apply the inverse of that move to the current state, and append that move to the end of the redo sequence", and output undone <k>.
- REDO n: if the redo sequence is empty, output error: nothing to redo. Otherwise, with k = min(n, length of the redo sequence), repeat k times "remove the last move of the redo sequence, apply that move to the current state, and append it to the end of the history", and output redone <k>.
- SEARCH d: perform the search of section 10. If a sequence is found, output found <the sequence found> (found 0 if the current state is solved); if none is found, output not found. The state does not change.
- WHERE piece: in the normalized state of the current state, let q be the position containing piece piece and t its orientation; if piece is a corner, output <piece> at <q> twist <t>, and if it is an edge, output <piece> at <q> flip <t> (e.g. UFL at UBR twist 0).
- SAVE name: make copies of the current state and the history the save named name (if a save with the same name exists, replace it). Output saved <name>. The redo sequence does not change.
- RESTORE name: if there is no save named name, output error: no save <name>. If there is, set the current state and the history to copies of the state and history of that save, empty the redo sequence, and output restored <name>. The save itself does not change.
- SCRAMBLE n seed: build the n-move scramble of section 9, apply it to the current state, append it to the end of the history, and empty the redo sequence. Output scramble followed by the notation of each move of the scramble, separated by single spaces (e.g. scramble L2 B2 F').
- APPLY seq: apply the sequence of seq to the current state, append it to the end of the history, and empty the redo sequence (empty it even if the sequence is empty). With k the number of moves of the sequence, output applied <k>.
- INVERT seq: compute the inverse of the sequence of seq and output inverse <that sequence>.
- SIMPLIFY seq: simplify the sequence of seq and output simplified <that sequence>.
- METRICS seq: compute the metrics of the sequence of seq and output metrics htm <htm> qtm <qtm> stm <stm> etm <etm>.
- ORDER seq: compute the order of the sequence of seq and output order <order>.
- CYCLES seq: for the state obtained by applying the sequence of seq to the solved state (without normalization), output the 3 lines corners <corner cycle notation>, edges <edge cycle notation>, centers <center cycle notation>.
- UNROTATE seq: remove the rotations of the sequence of seq and output unrotated <that sequence>.
- UNTIL seq: let T be the order of the sequence of seq. For k = 1, 2, ..., T in this order, check whether the state obtained by applying the sequence of seq k times in succession to the current state is solved, and output until <k> for the first k for which it is solved. If it is not solved for any k, output until never. The state does not change.
- DEFINE name seq: make the sequence of seq the macro named name (if a macro with the same name exists, replace it). With k the number of moves of the sequence, output defined <name> <k>. A macro stores the interpreted sequence, so it does not change even if another macro is redefined later.
- MIRROR plane seq: mirror the sequence of seq with plane and output mirror <that sequence>.
- ROTATE rot seq: apply the rotation conversion with rot to the sequence of seq and output rotated <that sequence>. For example, the output of ROTATE y R U R' U' is rotated 4 B U B' U'.

[Output format]
- For each command, output the lines described in [Processing] in that order. Unless otherwise specified, each command outputs one line.
- Words in a line are separated by a single space (except the 6 spaces at the start of the SHOW lines). No line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline.
- Numbers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- In the descriptions in [Processing], the period that ends each sentence (including the one at the end of each item) is a sentence separator and is not included in the output.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 400.
- Each command line has from 1 to 200 characters and consists of printable ASCII characters (! through ~) and spaces. There is no space at the start or end of a line, and two or more spaces never appear in a row.
- The number of moves of every sequence built by the procedure of section 4 (including sequences built partway through) is at most 10000.
- The length of the history is always at most 1000.
