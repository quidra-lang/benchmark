[Overview]
Write a program that plans journeys on a subway based on its timetable. It registers stations (zone, transfer time), lines (stops, travel times between stops, first and last departure times, headway), walking links between stations, and a zone-based tariff, and then finds the journey that departs at a given time and arrives earliest, journeys with few rides, fares, the last train, journeys with a specified arrival time, the arrival time for each departure time, the stations reachable within a given time, and journeys via a specified station. It also handles timetable displays per station and per line, closures of stations and segments, and delays and suspensions of lines. Journeys are searched exactly by the procedure defined in [Processing]; even when several journeys have the same arrival time, the procedure determines exactly one. Read commands from standard input, process them one at a time in the order given, and write the results to standard output.

[Input format]
The first line contains N, the number of command lines that follow. Each of the following N lines is one command. Each line consists of one or more tokens separated by single ASCII spaces, with no space at the beginning or end of the line. The first token is the command name, and the second and later tokens are the arguments of the command.
In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes, and ... denotes repetition of the same form. The < > around a value are notation for the description and are written neither in the input nor in the output (the "->" that appears in the output is a symbol consisting of the two characters - and >).

The commands are as follows.

Registering the network and the tariff:
STATION <station> <zone> <name>
LINE <line> <first> <last> <headway> <stop_0> <min_1> <stop_1> <min_2> <stop_2> ... <min_m> <stop_m>
TRANSFER <station> <minutes>
WALK <stationA> <stationB> <minutes>
TARIFF <discount> <window> <price_1> <price_2> ... <price_q>

Changing the service status:
CLOSE <station>
CLOSE <stationA> <stationB>
OPEN <station>
OPEN <stationA> <stationB>
DELAY <line> <minutes>
SUSPEND <line>
RESUME <line>

Searching journeys:
ROUTE <origin> <destination> <time>
VIA <origin> <via> <destination> <time>
FEWEST <origin> <destination> <time>
PARETO <origin> <destination> <time>
FARE <origin> <destination> <time> <class>
LASTTRAIN <origin> <destination>
ARRIVEBY <origin> <destination> <time>
PROFILE <origin> <destination> <time1> <time2>
REACH <station> <time> <minutes>

Displaying timetables and information:
TIMETABLE <station> <line> <toward> <time> <count>
DEPARTURES <station> <time> <count>
ARRIVALS <station> <time> <count>
TRIP <line> <toward> <time>
SHOWLINE <line>
INFO <station>
STATUS

The meaning of each value is as follows.
- <station>, <stationA>, <stationB>, <stop_0> to <stop_m>, <origin>, <destination>, <via>, and <toward> are station IDs (strings that identify stations). <line> is a line ID (a string that identifies a line).
- <name> is the name of a station and is used only for output.
- <first>, <last>, <time>, <time1>, and <time2> are times of the form "HH:MM". HH and MM are each two-digit decimal numbers.
- <class> is a fare class. The valid classes are the two classes ADULT (adult) and CHILD (child), but other strings may also be given.
- <zone>, <headway>, <minutes>, <min_1> to <min_m>, <discount>, <window>, <price_1> to <price_q>, and <count> are decimal integers.
- The arguments of LINE are <line>, <first>, <last>, <headway>, followed by station IDs and integers alternating, starting with a station ID and ending with a station ID (m >= 1).
- For CLOSE and OPEN, one argument specifies a station, and two arguments specify a segment (Section 5).

[Processing]
Section 1. Terms and notation
- Strings are compared in lexicographic order of character codes (ASCII). They are compared one character at a time from the beginning; the string whose character code is smaller at the first differing character is the smaller one, and when one string equals the beginning part of the other, the shorter one is the smaller (e.g. AX < BLU, N1 < N10 < N2). The order in which stations or lines are arranged in increasing order of their IDs is called "ID order".
- Times are handled as integer numbers of minutes elapsed since 0:00. An input "HH:MM" represents HH * 60 + MM. To output a time t, write the quotient of t / 60 and the remainder of t / 60, each with two digits (prefixing a 0 if it has one digit), joined by : (e.g. 485 is 08:05, and 1440 is 24:00). This form is called time notation.
- All durations are integers in minutes. Service runs until 24:00, and no train departs from or arrives at a station later than 24:00 (Section 4). Walks that would arrive later than 24:00 are not used either (Sections 7 and 8).
- The quotient of x / y is the integer obtained by truncating the fractional part of x / y (in this task, x is an integer >= 0 and y is an integer >= 1).

Section 2. Initial state
Initially there are no stations, no lines, and no walking links, no station or segment is closed, and no tariff is registered.

Section 3. Stations and walking links
- Each station has a station ID, a zone, a station name, a transfer time, and whether it is closed. At registration, its transfer time is 0 and it is not closed.
- The transfer time is the minimum time needed, after arriving at a station, before boarding a train at that station, when the station is not the origin (Sections 7 and 8).
- A walking link connects two different stations and has a travel time (minutes). It has no direction: one can walk from either station with the same travel time. There is at most one walking link connecting the same two stations. A station connected to station u by a walking link is called a walk neighbor of u.

Section 4. Lines and trains
- A line registered by LINE <line> <first> <last> <headway> <stop_0> <min_1> <stop_1> ... <min_m> <stop_m> connects its stops stop_0, stop_1, ..., stop_m in this order. The stops are all distinct (ensured by the LINE checks in Section 12). min_i is the travel time from stop_(i-1) to stop_i. Let c_0 = 0 and c_i = min_1 + min_2 + ... + min_i; T = c_m is called the total travel time of the line.
- A line "contains" a station when that station is one of the stops of the line.
- A line runs trains in two directions. The direction from stop_0 to stop_m is called "toward stop_m", and the direction from stop_m to stop_0 is called "toward stop_0"; the station that gives a direction its name is called the terminal of that direction. The order of the directions is: toward stop_m first, toward stop_0 second.
- For each direction, the stop sequence p_0, p_1, ..., p_m and the travel time o_j from p_0 to p_j are defined as follows. p_0 is called the starting station of that direction.
  Toward stop_m: p_j = stop_j, o_j = c_j
  Toward stop_0: p_j = stop_(m-j), o_j = T - c_(m-j)
- A line has a delay (minutes) and whether it is suspended. At registration, its delay is 0 and it is not suspended.
- Each direction has (the quotient of (last - first) / headway) + 1 trains. Let n be this number; the trains are given the train numbers 0, 1, ..., n-1. The train with train number q departs from p_0 at time first + delay + q * headway, and arrives at each p_j at time first + delay + q * headway + o_j and departs at that same time (there is no dwell time). This time is called the time of that train at p_j. The larger the train number, the later the train's time at every station.
- Because of the checks in LINE and DELAY, last + delay + T <= 1440 always holds.
- The trains of a suspended line are treated as nonexistent in the searches of Sections 7 and 8, and in DEPARTURES and ARRIVALS.

Section 5. Closures
- At a closed station, one can neither board nor alight from a train, nor go to that station by a walking link. Trains pass through closed stations (their times do not change).
- A segment is a pair of two stations (the order is not distinguished). When a segment is closed, on every line and in either direction, wherever the two adjacent stations p_(j-1), p_j in the stop sequence form that segment, one can no longer pass between them while staying on a train. The trains themselves run on schedule.
- Closures do not affect the results of TIMETABLE, DEPARTURES, and ARRIVALS (specifying a closed station is an error).
- A segment is output as its two station IDs joined by -. In the output of CLOSE and OPEN, they are written in the order given in the command; in the output of STATUS, the smaller one is written first (e.g. PAR-CEN, CEN-PAR).

Section 6. Legs, journeys, and labels
- There are three kinds of legs.
  Ride leg: consists of the seven values line, terminal, boarding station, boarding time, alighting station, alighting time, and stop count, and represents riding a train of that line in the direction of that terminal from the boarding station to the alighting station. The stop count is the index of the alighting station in the stop sequence minus the index of the boarding station (closed stations passed through are also counted).
  Walk leg: consists of the four values departure station, departure time, arrival station, and arrival time, and represents walking along a walking link.
  Stay leg: consists of the three values station, start time, and end time, and represents staying at the via station in VIA.
- The end time of a leg is the alighting time for a ride leg, the arrival time for a walk leg, and the end time for a stay leg.
- A journey is a sequence of legs in order. A journey containing no legs is called the empty journey. "Journey J with leg x added" means the journey obtained by appending x to the end of J.
- In the searches (Sections 7 and 8), stations are given labels (t, J), each a pair of an arrival time and a journey.
- Printing a journey: for a heading H, a departure time t0, an arrival time t1, and a journey J, outputting the following lines in this order is called "printing the journey".
  First line: "<H> <t0> -> <t1> total <duration> rides <number of ride legs> walks <number of walk legs>". The duration is t1 - t0.
  Then one line for each leg of J, in order: a ride leg is written as "RIDE <line> toward <terminal> <boarding station> <boarding time> -> <alighting station> <alighting time> wait <wait time> stops <stop count>", a walk leg as "WALK <departure station> <departure time> -> <arrival station> <arrival time>", and a stay leg as "STAY <station> <start time> -> <end time>". The wait time is the boarding time minus the end time of the immediately preceding leg (t0 for the first leg).
  All times are written in time notation.

Section 7. Earliest-arrival search
The earliest-arrival search with origin s and departure time t0 is performed by the following procedure.
(1) Set the label of s to (t0, the empty journey). The other stations have no label. Mark all stations as unsettled.
(2) Among the unsettled stations that have a label, let u be the station whose label has the smallest arrival time (if there are several, the one with the smallest station ID), and settle u. If there is no such station, end the search. Let (a, J) be the label of u.
(3) If u = s, let r = a; otherwise let r = a + the transfer time of u. r is called the ready time.
(4) Choose the lines that are not suspended and contain u, in ID order, and for each of them examine its two directions in the order of Section 4. In each direction, let u = p_i. If i = m (if u is the terminal of that direction), do nothing for that direction. Otherwise, choose, among the trains whose time at p_i is r or later, the one with the smallest train number. If there is no such train, do nothing for that direction. Let d be the time of the chosen train at p_i, and do the following for j = i+1, i+2, ..., m in this order.
  - If the segment (p_(j-1), p_j) is closed, end the processing of that direction.
  - If p_j is closed, do nothing and proceed to the next j.
  - Let t be the time of the chosen train at p_j. If p_j has no label, or the arrival time of the label of p_j is greater than t, set the label of p_j to (t, J with the ride leg (that line, p_m, u, d, p_j, t, j - i) added).
(5) Examine the walking links of u in ID order of the walk neighbor. Let v be the walk neighbor and w the travel time, and let t = a + w. If v is not closed, t <= 1440, and v has no label or the arrival time of the label of v is greater than t, set the label of v to (t, J with the walk leg (u, a, v, t) added).
(6) Go back to (2).
When the search has ended, if station v has the label (t, J), v can be reached at time t by journey J. Stations that have no label cannot be reached.
In (4) and (5), a label is replaced only when the arrival time becomes strictly smaller. Therefore, when there are several journeys with the same arrival time, the label that was set first remains. Also, since a newly set arrival time is always a or greater, the label of a settled station never changes afterward.

Section 8. Round search
In the round search with origin s and departure time t0, rounds 0, 1, 2, ... are performed in order. The label of each station when round k has finished is called "the round k label", and the set of stations updated in round k is called M_k. Round k sets and rewrites only round k labels.

Walk step: within round k, performing the following on a set U of stations is called the "walk step". First, record the label of each station of U at that point. Next, take the stations of U as u in ID order, and examine the walking links of u in ID order of the walk neighbor. Let (a, J) be the recorded label of u, v the walk neighbor, and w the travel time, and let t = a + w. If v is not closed, t <= 1440, and v has no label or the arrival time of the label of v is greater than t, set the label of v to (t, J with the walk leg (u, a, v, t) added), and add v to M_k. Even if the label of a station of U is rewritten during the walk step, the walks from that station use the recorded label.

Round 0: set the label of s to (t0, the empty journey); the other stations have no label. Let M_0 = {s}, and perform the walk step with U = {s}.

Round k (k >= 1): if M_(k-1) is empty, round k is not performed and the round search ends. Otherwise, let a copy of all round k-1 labels be the initial state of the round k labels, and make U and M_k empty. Then choose the lines that are not suspended in ID order, and for each of them examine its two directions in the order of Section 4. In each direction, with the boarded train set to "none", perform the following (a) to (d) for j = 0, 1, ..., m in this order.
(a) If j >= 1 and the segment (p_(j-1), p_j) is closed, set the boarded train to none.
(b) If p_j is closed, proceed to the next j without performing (c) and (d).
(c) If there is a boarded train, let t be the time of that train at p_j. If p_j has no label, or the arrival time of the label of p_j is greater than t, set the label of p_j to (t, J_b with the ride leg (that line, p_m, p_b, d_b, p_j, t, j - b) added), and add p_j to U and M_k. b, d_b, and J_b are the values recorded when the boarded train was determined in (d).
(d) If j < m and p_j is contained in M_(k-1), let (a, J) be the round k-1 label of p_j, and let r = a if p_j = s, otherwise r = a + the transfer time of p_j. If there is a train with the smallest train number among the trains whose time at p_j is r or later, and either there is no boarded train or the train number of that train is smaller than the train number of the boarded train, make that train the boarded train and record b = j, d_b = the time of that train at p_j, and J_b = J.
When all lines have been examined, perform the walk step on U. This ends round k.

With this procedure, the journey of a label set in round k contains exactly k ride legs. Also, journeys with two consecutive walk legs are never found. In each round as well, a label is replaced only when the arrival time becomes strictly smaller.

Section 9. Fares
The tariff is registered by TARIFF and has a discount amount D, a discount window W, and the prices price_1, ..., price_q. Running TARIFF again replaces the whole tariff.
The half of x is defined as ceil(x / 20) * 10 (the smallest multiple of 10 that is at least half of x; e.g. the half of 250 is 130, and the half of 200 is 100).
The fare of journey J for fare class C is computed over the ride legs of J one at a time, in order, as follows (walk legs and stay legs are free).
- Zone count: in the stop sequence of the direction toward the terminal of that ride leg on the line of that ride leg, let z be the number of distinct zones of the stations from the boarding station to the alighting station (including both ends, and including the stations passed through and closed stations).
- Price: price_z (price_q if z > q). If C is CHILD, the price is the half of that.
- Discount: the discount cap is D if C is ADULT, and the half of D if C is CHILD. If the ride leg is not the first ride leg of J and (the boarding time of the ride leg - the alighting time of the immediately preceding ride leg) is W or less, the discount is the smaller of the discount cap and the price. Otherwise, the discount is 0.
- Payment: price - discount.
The fare of J is the sum of the payments of all ride legs.

Section 10. Error handling
The error conditions listed in the description of each command are checked in the order listed. For the first condition that holds, output only one line consisting of ERROR followed by a single ASCII space and the message (e.g. ERROR unknown station QQQ), and the command ends without changing anything. If none of the conditions holds, process the command as described and output the lines as described. <station>, <line>, <time>, etc. in a message are exactly the values written in that command.
Below, "is not registered" means that no station (or line) with that ID has been registered yet.

Section 11. Common checks for journey searches
ROUTE, FEWEST, PARETO, FARE, LASTTRAIN, ARRIVEBY, and PROFILE first check the following error conditions in this order.
1. The origin is not registered: unknown station <origin>
2. The destination is not registered: unknown station <destination>
3. The origin and the destination are the same: origin and destination are the same
4. The origin is closed: station <origin> is closed
5. The destination is closed: station <destination> is closed

Section 12. Commands
* STATION <station> <zone> <name>
Errors:
1. A station with that station ID is already registered: station <station> already exists
Register the station, and output "STATION <station> zone <zone> <name>".

* LINE <line> <first> <last> <headway> <stop_0> <min_1> <stop_1> ... <min_m> <stop_m>
Errors:
1. A line with that line ID is already registered: line <line> already exists
2. Some of stop_0 to stop_m is not registered: unknown station <the earliest-written such station>
3. The same station appears two or more times among stop_0 to stop_m: station <station> appears twice on line <line> (<station> is, among the stations for which the same station is written earlier, the one written earliest)
4. first is later than last: line <line> first departure is after last departure
5. last + T is greater than 1440: line <line> runs past 24:00
Register the line, and output "LINE <line> stops <number of stops> time <T> trips <number of trains>". The number of stops is m+1, and the number of trains is the number of trains in one direction.

* TRANSFER <station> <minutes>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
Change the transfer time of the station to <minutes>, and output "TRANSFER <station> <minutes>".

* WALK <stationA> <stationB> <minutes>
Errors:
1. stationA is not registered: unknown station <stationA>
2. stationB is not registered: unknown station <stationB>
3. stationA and stationB are the same: walk from <stationA> to itself
If there is no walking link connecting stationA and stationB, register a walking link with travel time <minutes>, and output "WALK <stationA> <stationB> <minutes>". If there already is one, change its travel time to <minutes>, and output "WALK <stationA> <stationB> <minutes> was <travel time before the change>".

* TARIFF <discount> <window> <price_1> <price_2> ... <price_q>
Errors:
1. There is an i such that price_(i+1) is smaller than price_i: tariff prices must not decrease
Register the tariff (replacing it if one already exists), and output "TARIFF zones <q> discount <discount> window <window>".

* CLOSE <station>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The station is already closed: station <station> is already closed
Close the station, and output "CLOSE <station>".

* CLOSE <stationA> <stationB>
Errors:
1. stationA is not registered: unknown station <stationA>
2. stationB is not registered: unknown station <stationB>
3. There is no line on which stationA and stationB are adjacent stops: no segment <stationA>-<stationB>
4. The segment is already closed: segment <stationA>-<stationB> is already closed
Close the segment (stationA, stationB), and output "CLOSE <stationA>-<stationB> lines <line> <line> ...". The lines are the line IDs, in ID order, of all lines (including suspended ones) on which stationA and stationB are adjacent stops. Since the closure applies to the pair of stations, it also applies to lines registered later.

* OPEN <station>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The station is not closed: station <station> is not closed
Remove the closure of the station, and output "OPEN <station>".

* OPEN <stationA> <stationB>
Errors:
1. stationA is not registered: unknown station <stationA>
2. stationB is not registered: unknown station <stationB>
3. The segment (stationA, stationB) is not closed: segment <stationA>-<stationB> is not closed
Remove the closure of the segment, and output "OPEN <stationA>-<stationB>".

* DELAY <line> <minutes>
Errors:
1. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
2. (last of the line) + <minutes> + T is greater than 1440: line <line> runs past 24:00
Set the delay of the line to <minutes> (replacing the previous delay, not adding to it), and output "DELAY <line> <minutes>".

* SUSPEND <line>
Errors:
1. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
2. The line is already suspended: line <line> is already suspended
Suspend the line, and output "SUSPEND <line>".

* RESUME <line>
Errors:
1. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
2. The line is not suspended: line <line> is not suspended
Remove the suspension of the line, and output "RESUME <line>".

* ROUTE <origin> <destination> <time>
The errors are as in Section 11. Perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time <time>. If the destination has the label (t, J), print the journey with heading "ROUTE <origin> <destination>", departure time <time>, arrival time t, and journey J. If it has no label, output "ROUTE <origin> <destination> <time> unreachable".

* VIA <origin> <via> <destination> <time>
Errors:
1. The origin is not registered: unknown station <origin>
2. The via station is not registered: unknown station <via>
3. The destination is not registered: unknown station <destination>
4. The origin and the destination are the same: origin and destination are the same
5. The via station is the same as the origin or the destination: via station <via> must differ from origin and destination
6. The origin is closed: station <origin> is closed
7. The via station is closed: station <via> is closed
8. The destination is closed: station <destination> is closed
First, perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time <time>. If the via station has the label (a1, J1), let t1 = a1 + the transfer time of the via station, and perform the earliest-arrival search with the via station and time t1 (in this search, the via station is the origin). If the destination has the label (a2, J2), let J be J1 with the stay leg (via station, a1, t1) added and then the legs of J2 added in order, and print the journey with heading "VIA <origin> <via> <destination>", departure time <time>, arrival time a2, and journey J. If in either search the target station has no label, output "VIA <origin> <via> <destination> <time> unreachable".

* FEWEST <origin> <destination> <time>
The errors are as in Section 11. Perform the round search with the origin and time <time>, and find, in the order of rounds 0, 1, 2, ..., the first round k in which the destination has a label. With (t, J) the label of the destination in that round, print the journey with heading "FEWEST <origin> <destination> trips <k>", departure time <time>, arrival time t, and journey J. If the round search ends with the destination having no label in any round, output "FEWEST <origin> <destination> <time> unreachable".

* PARETO <origin> <destination> <time>
Lists journeys in order from those with fewer rides, where increasing the number of rides makes the arrival time earlier.
The errors are as in Section 11. Perform the round search with the origin and time <time> until it ends. Round k is an "option" when the destination has the label (t, J) in round k, and in round k-1 the destination has no label (including the case k = 0) or the arrival time of the round k-1 label is greater than t. With c the number of options, first output "PARETO <origin> <destination> <time> options <c>". Then, for each option in increasing order of k, print the journey with heading "OPTION trips <k>", departure time <time>, arrival time t, and journey J.

* FARE <origin> <destination> <time> <class>
Errors:
1-5. As in Section 11
6. The class is neither ADULT nor CHILD: unknown fare class <class>
7. No tariff is registered: no tariff
Perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time <time>. If the destination has no label, output "FARE <origin> <destination> <time> <class> unreachable". If it has the label (t, J), compute the fare of J for fare class <class> as in Section 9, and output "FARE <origin> <destination> <time> <class> rides <number of ride legs> total <fare of J>". Then, for each ride leg of J in order, output one line "TICKET <line> <boarding station> <alighting station> zones <zone count> price <price> discount <discount> pay <payment>".

* LASTTRAIN <origin> <destination>
The errors are as in Section 11. Varying the departure time t over 1439, 1438, ..., 0 in this order, perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time t, and find the first t for which the destination has a label. With (t1, J) the label of the destination at that point, print the journey with heading "LASTTRAIN <origin> <destination>", departure time t, arrival time t1, and journey J. If there is no such t, output "LASTTRAIN <origin> <destination> none".

* ARRIVEBY <origin> <destination> <time>
The errors are as in Section 11. Let D be <time>. Varying the departure time t over D-1, D-2, ..., 0 in this order, perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time t, and find the first t for which the destination has a label whose arrival time is D or earlier. With (t1, J) the label of the destination at that point, print the journey with heading "ARRIVEBY <origin> <destination> by <time>", departure time t, arrival time t1, and journey J. If there is no such t (including the case D = 0), output "ARRIVEBY <origin> <destination> by <time> none".

* PROFILE <origin> <destination> <time1> <time2>
Errors:
1-5. As in Section 11
6. time1 is later than time2: empty interval <time1> <time2>
For each time t from time1 to time2 (including both ends), perform the earliest-arrival search with the origin and time t, and let A(t) be the arrival time of the label of the destination (if there is no label, A(t) does not exist). Select, in increasing order, all t such that A(t) exists and, in addition, t = time2, or A(t+1) does not exist, or A(t) < A(t+1) (these are the departure times such that departing even one minute later makes the arrival later or makes the destination unreachable). With c the number of selected times, first output "PROFILE <origin> <destination> <time1> <time2> count <c>", then for each selected t output one line "<t> -> <A(t)> total <A(t) - t>" (t and A(t) are in time notation).

* REACH <station> <time> <minutes>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The station is closed: station <station> is closed
Perform the earliest-arrival search with the station and time <time>. Arrange the stations whose label arrival time is at most <time> + <minutes> (including the station itself) in increasing order of arrival time, and in ID order when equal. With c their number, first output "REACH <station> <time> <minutes> count <c>", then for each of these stations output one line "<station ID> <station name> <arrival time> <arrival time - time> rides <number of ride legs in the journey of the label>".

* TIMETABLE <station> <line> <toward> <time> <count>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
3. The line does not contain the station: station <station> is not on line <line>
4. <toward> is neither stop_0 nor stop_m of the line: <toward> is not a terminal of line <line>
5. <station> and <toward> are the same: no departures from <station> toward <toward>
6. The line is suspended: line <line> is suspended
7. The station is closed: station <station> is closed
Among the trains of the line in the direction toward <toward>, arrange those whose time at the station is <time> or later in increasing order of time, and select the first <count> of them (all of them if there are fewer). With c the number of selected trains, output one line consisting of "TIMETABLE <station> <line> toward <toward> <time> next <c>" followed by the times of the selected trains at the station, in increasing order, each preceded by a single ASCII space.

* DEPARTURES <station> <time> <count>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The station is closed: station <station> is closed
Over the lines that are not suspended and contain the station, in each direction in which the station is not the terminal, collect all trains whose time at the station is <time> or later. Arrange them in increasing order of their time at the station, in ID order of the line when equal, and within the same line in the direction order of Section 4, and select the first <count> of them (all of them if there are fewer). With c the number of selected trains, first output "DEPARTURES <station> <time> count <c>", then for each selected train output one line "<time at the station> <line> toward <terminal>".

* ARRIVALS <station> <time> <count>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
2. The station is closed: station <station> is closed
The same as DEPARTURES, but instead of the directions in which the station is not the terminal, collect the trains of the directions in which the station is not the starting station. The output is first "ARRIVALS <station> <time> count <c>", then for each selected train "<time at the station> <line> from <starting station of that direction>".

* TRIP <line> <toward> <time>
Errors:
1. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
2. <toward> is neither stop_0 nor stop_m of the line: <toward> is not a terminal of line <line>
3. The line is suspended: line <line> is suspended
Among the trains of the line in the direction toward <toward>, select the one with the smallest train number whose time at p_0 is <time> or later. If there is no such train, output "TRIP <line> toward <toward> <time> none". Otherwise, with q its train number, first output "TRIP <line> toward <toward> <time> trip <q+1> departs <time of that train at p_0>", then for j = 0, 1, ..., m in this order output one line "<time of that train at p_j> <p_j>". However, if p_j is closed, append " closed" to the end of that line, and if j >= 1 and the segment (p_(j-1), p_j) is closed, further append " blocked" after that.

* SHOWLINE <line>
Errors:
1. The line is not registered: unknown line <line>
First output "SHOWLINE <line> <state> first <first> last <last> headway <headway> delay <delay> trips <number of trains>". <state> is suspended if the line is suspended, and running otherwise. <first> and <last> are the values written in LINE (times not including the delay), written in time notation. Then, for i = 0, 1, ..., m in this order, output one line "<c_i> <stop_i> zone <zone of stop_i>". However, if stop_i is closed, append " closed" to the end of that line, and if i >= 1 and the segment (stop_(i-1), stop_i) is closed, further append " blocked" after that.

* INFO <station>
Errors:
1. The station is not registered: unknown station <station>
Output the following three lines.
First line: "INFO <station> <name> zone <zone> transfer <transfer time> <state>". <state> is closed if the station is closed, and open otherwise.
Second line: "LINES" followed by the line IDs of all lines that contain the station (including suspended ones), in ID order, each preceded by a single ASCII space. If there is no such line, "LINES none".
Third line: "WALKS" followed by " <walk neighbor> <travel time>" for each walking link of the station, in ID order of the walk neighbor. If there is no walking link, "WALKS none".

* STATUS
There are no errors. First output "STATUS stations <number of stations> lines <number of lines> walks <number of walking links>". Then output, one line each and in this order: "CLOSED <station>" for each closed station, in ID order; "CLOSED <stationX>-<stationY>" (stationX is the smaller of the two stations) for each closed segment, in increasing order of stationX, and in increasing order of stationY when equal; "SUSPENDED <line>" for each suspended line, in ID order; and "DELAYED <line> <delay>" for each line whose delay is not 0, in ID order.

[Output format]
- For each command, output the lines specified in [Processing] in the specified order. On an error, output only one line starting with ERROR.
- Every separator within a line is a single ASCII space, and no space is placed at the beginning or end of a line.
- Every time (a value expressed as minutes elapsed since 0:00, including the train times of TIMETABLE, DEPARTURES, ARRIVALS, and TRIP and the arrival times of REACH) is written in time notation. Every other number (including durations such as travel times, wait times, delays, headways, c_i, and T) is written as a decimal integer without extra leading zeros.
- The output consists of ASCII characters only, every line ends with a newline (LF), and no empty lines are output.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500. Each command name is one of those listed in [Input format], and the number of arguments and the form of each token are always correct. However, a command may refer to a station or line that is not registered.
- A station ID is 1 to 6 characters that start with an uppercase letter and consist only of uppercase letters and digits, and a line ID is 1 to 4 characters of the same form. <name> is 1 to 16 characters consisting only of letters (A-Z, a-z), and <class> is 1 to 8 characters consisting only of uppercase letters.
- Times are between 00:00 and 23:59 inclusive (HH is 00 to 23, and MM is 00 to 59). Integers in the input are written without extra leading zeros.
- 1 <= zone <= 9.
- In LINE, 1 <= m <= 11, 1 <= headway <= 180, and 1 <= min_i <= 60.
- <minutes> is between 0 and 30 inclusive for TRANSFER, between 1 and 120 inclusive for WALK, between 0 and 180 inclusive for DELAY, and between 1 and 1440 inclusive for REACH.
- In TARIFF, 1 <= q <= 9, 0 <= discount <= 1000, 0 <= window <= 120, and 0 <= price_i <= 10000.
- <count> of TIMETABLE, DEPARTURES, and ARRIVALS is between 1 and 30 inclusive. In PROFILE, when time1 <= time2, time2 - time1 is at most 180.
- At most 30 stations and at most 12 lines are registered.
- The absolute value of every integer appearing in the computation is less than 2^31.
