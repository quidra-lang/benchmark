[Overview]
Write a program that analyzes the access log of a web server. From standard input, it first reads the lines of the access log. It checks each line, records every valid line as a "record", and counts the lines that are not valid, by reason. It then reads the query lines (hereafter, queries), processes them one at a time in the order they are written, and writes the results to standard output. The queries compute an overall summary, a list of the malformed lines, breakdowns by status and by method, rankings of the most requested paths and IP addresses, bar charts of the counts per hour of day and per fixed time interval, the transfer volume and the number of visitors per day, response-time statistics and a histogram, Apdex scores, error rates per path, slow endpoints, reconstructed sessions, detection of error bursts and of the traffic peak with a sliding window, gaps in access, and details per IP address. The records to aggregate can also be narrowed down by a time range and by conditions, and those settings can be cleared.

[Input format]
The input consists of a first part, the "log part", and a second part, the "query part".
- Log part: each line from the 1st line of the input up to, but not including, the line consisting only of "---" (three hyphens) is a "log line". There may be zero log lines. The log lines are given "line numbers" 1, 2, 3, ... in order from the first.
- Query part: each line from the line after the "---" line up to, but not including, the line consisting only of "END" is a "query line". There is at least one query line. The "END" line is the last line of the input and is not treated as a query.

A valid log line consists of the following 8 "fields" separated by spaces, and represents one request.
<date> <time> <ip> <method> <path> <status> <bytes> <latency>
For example, "2026-01-01 09:15:30 10.0.0.2 GET /api/users/42?full=1 200 5120 87" means that at 9:15:30 on January 1, 2026, /api/users/42?full=1 was requested with the GET method from the IP address 10.0.0.2, and the server returned 5120 bytes with status 200 and took 87 milliseconds to respond. However, log lines are not necessarily valid (their handling is described in [Processing] 1.).

A query line consists of a query name and zero or more arguments separated by spaces (the list is in [Processing] 5.). However, the content of a query line is not necessarily valid.

Both log lines and query lines are "split by spaces" as follows. A run of one or more consecutive spaces in a line is treated as one separator, and the longest sequences of characters that contain no space are extracted from left to right. Spaces at the start and at the end of the line are ignored (for example, " a  b" yields the two parts a and b). Each extracted part is called a "field" for a log line and a "token" for a query line.

In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes, and [...] denotes a part that may be omitted. The symbols < > [ ] themselves appear in neither the input nor the output.

[Processing]
1. Checking log lines
Split each log line by spaces into fields and perform the following checks in order from top to bottom. If a check is not satisfied, the line is a "malformed line", and the name of that check (the word written to the left of the ":") is the "reason" of that line (the checks below it are not performed). A line that satisfies all the checks is valid and is recorded as a "record". A malformed line is not recorded; only its reason and its line number are remembered.
  fields: there are exactly 8 fields.
  date: the 1st field is a valid date.
  time: the 2nd field is a valid time.
  ip: the 3rd field is a valid IP address.
  method: the 4th field matches one of GET, HEAD, POST, PUT, DELETE, PATCH, OPTIONS (uppercase and lowercase letters are distinguished).
  path: the first character of the 5th field is "/".
  status: the 6th field is a sequence of exactly 3 digits, and its value is between 100 and 599 inclusive.
  bytes: the 7th field is a canonical decimal number of at most 8 characters.
  latency: the 8th field is a canonical decimal number of at most 6 characters.

The terms used here mean the following.
- A digit is one of the characters 0 to 9. A "canonical decimal number" is a string of one or more characters consisting only of digits whose first character is not 0 when it has two or more characters. Its value is the value obtained by reading it as a decimal number. For example, 0, 7 and 512 are canonical decimal numbers, but 007, +5, -5 and 1e3 are not.
- A "valid date" is a string YYYY-MM-DD of exactly 10 characters whose 5th and 8th characters are "-" and whose other 8 characters are digits, such that the year YYYY is between 2000 and 2059 inclusive, the month MM is between 1 and 12 inclusive, and the day DD is at least 1 and at most the number of days in that month. The numbers of days in the months, from January on, are 31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, except that February of a leap year has 29 days. A leap year is a year that is divisible by 4 but not by 100, or a year that is divisible by 400 (for example, 2000 and 2024 are leap years, and 2025 is not).
- A "valid time" is a string HH:MM:SS of exactly 8 characters whose 3rd and 6th characters are ":" and whose other 6 characters are digits, such that the hour HH is at most 23, the minute MM is at most 59, and the second SS is at most 59.
- A "valid IP address" is a string that, when split at each "." (in this split, the part before a ".", the part after a ".", and the part between two "." characters can also be empty), splits into exactly 4 parts, each of which is a canonical decimal number of at most 3 characters whose value is at most 255. For example, 0.0.0.0 and 192.168.1.5 are valid, while 10.0.0, 10.00.0.1, 256.1.1.1, 10..0.1 and 10.0.0.1. are not.

A record has a line number, a date, a time, an IP address, a method, a path (normalized as in 3.), a status, a byte count, and a response time (in milliseconds). The "timestamp" of a record is the string formed by joining its date and its time with one space (example: 2026-01-01 09:15:30).
When the quotient of the status divided by 100 is c (1 to 5), we say that it "is cxx" (for example, 404 is 4xx). A record whose status is 400 or more is called an "error".

2. Times in seconds
Dates and times are handled by converting them to the number of seconds elapsed since 00:00:00 on January 1, 2000. The "day number" of a date YYYY-MM-DD is the sum of the total of the numbers of days of the years from 2000 to YYYY-1 (366 for a leap year, 365 otherwise) (0 if YYYY is 2000), the total of the numbers of days of the months from January to month MM-1 of year YYYY (0 if MM is 1), and DD-1 (the day number of 2000-01-01 is 0). The "seconds" of a date and a time is "day number * 86400 + HH * 3600 + MM * 60 + SS". For example, the seconds of 2000-03-01 00:00:00 is 5184000, and the seconds of 2026-01-01 00:00:00 is 820540800. The seconds of a record is the seconds of its date and its time.
All comparisons and differences of times are done with seconds. Sorting records in "time order" means sorting them in ascending order of seconds, and records with equal seconds in ascending order of line number.

3. Path normalization
The path of a record is the string of the 5th field converted by the following procedure. This conversion is called "normalization".
(1) If the string contains "?", remove the first "?" and everything after it.
(2) Split the rest at each "/" into parts, and discard the empty parts.
(3) Look at the parts from left to right. Discard ".". Discard "..", and in addition, if any parts have been kept so far, remove the one part that was kept last. Keep every other part.
(4) Replace each kept part as follows. A part consisting only of digits is replaced with "{id}". Otherwise, a part that has 8 or more characters, each of which is a digit or one of the lowercase letters a to f, and that contains at least one digit, is replaced with "{hex}". Any other part is left as it is.
(5) The result is "/" followed by the kept parts joined in order with "/" between them. If no part is kept, the result is "/".
Examples:
  /api/users/42?full=1 -> /api/users/{id}
  /static/../img//logo.png -> /img/logo.png
  /files/3fa85f64c0de11ee/ -> /files/{hex}
  /files/deadbeef -> /files/deadbeef (not replaced because it contains no digit)
  /api/users/123/../456 -> /api/users/{id}
  /./../ -> /
Paths given as query arguments (LATENCY and FILTER PATH) are normalized in the same way.

4. Target records
The program keeps the following state for narrowing down the records to aggregate. Initially, each of them is "none".
- Range: "none", or two timestamps, a start and an end.
- Conditions: for each of the 4 "keys" METHOD, STATUS, IP, PATH, "none" or one value.
A record that satisfies all of the following is called a "target record".
- The range is "none", or the seconds of the record is at least the seconds of the start and at most the seconds of the end.
- The METHOD condition is "none", or the method of the record matches its value.
- The STATUS condition is "none", or the following holds. If the value has the form "cxx", the status of the record is cxx. If the value is a sequence of exactly 3 digits, the status of the record is equal to that value.
- The IP condition is "none", or the IP address of the record matches its value.
- The PATH condition is "none", or the path of the record matches its value.
Except for the 1st line of SUMMARY and for MALFORMED, every query uses only the target records. Below, n denotes the number of target records.

5. Processing queries
Split each query line by spaces into tokens; the first token is the "query name", and the remaining tokens, in order, are the "arguments". The forms of the queries are as follows (uppercase and lowercase letters are distinguished).
  SUMMARY
  MALFORMED
  STATUS
  METHODS
  TOP <kind> <limit>
  HOURLY
  TIMELINE <minutes>
  BANDWIDTH
  VISITORS
  LATENCY [<path>]
  HISTOGRAM <width>
  APDEX <threshold>
  ERRORS
  SLOWEST <limit>
  SESSIONS <ip> [<minutes>]
  BURST <length> <times>
  PEAK <length>
  GAPS <length>
  IP <ip>
  RANGE <date> <time> <date> <time>
  FILTER <key> <value>
  UNFILTER <key>
  RESET
  STATE

Each query line is processed in the order (1) to (4) below. If an error occurs along the way, output only the single line "ERROR <reason>" and go on to the next query line without changing the state (the range and the conditions). <reason> is the name of the error, such as unknown query.
(1) If the query name is not in the list above, the error is unknown query.
(2) If the number of arguments does not match the form above, the error is wrong arguments. LATENCY takes 0 or 1 arguments, SESSIONS takes 1 or 2 arguments, and every other query takes the number of arguments shown in its form above.
(3) Examine the arguments in order from the first; the error is determined as follows by the type of the first invalid argument found.
  - Numeric arguments (<limit>, <minutes>, <width>, <threshold>, <length>, <times>): valid if it is a canonical decimal number of at most 6 characters whose value is in the following range, otherwise invalid number. <limit> is between 1 and 100 inclusive, <minutes> is between 1 and 1440 inclusive, <width> and <threshold> are between 1 and 100000 inclusive, <length> is between 1 and 86400 inclusive, and <times> is between 1 and 1000 inclusive. The unit of <length> is seconds, and the unit of <width> and <threshold> is milliseconds.
  - <kind>: valid if it is PATHS or IPS, otherwise invalid kind.
  - <path>: valid if its first character is "/", otherwise invalid path.
  - <ip>: valid if it is a valid IP address, otherwise invalid ip.
  - <date> and <time> of RANGE: valid if the 1st and 3rd arguments are valid dates and the 2nd and 4th arguments are valid times, otherwise invalid timestamp.
  - <key>: valid if it is one of METHOD, STATUS, IP, PATH for FILTER, or one of those and RANGE for UNFILTER, otherwise invalid key.
  - <value> of FILTER: valid if, when the key is METHOD, it is one of the 7 methods listed in the method check of 1.; when the key is STATUS, it is one of 1xx, 2xx, 3xx, 4xx, 5xx (x is a lowercase letter) or a sequence of exactly 3 digits whose value is between 100 and 599 inclusive; when the key is IP, it is a valid IP address; and when the key is PATH, it is a string whose first character is "/". Otherwise invalid value.
(4) Execute the query (7.). An error can also occur during execution.
For the following queries, if the collection of records they use is empty at the start of execution, output only the single line "<query name> no records" and finish the execution (example: STATUS no records).
- STATUS, METHODS, TOP, HOURLY, TIMELINE, BANDWIDTH, VISITORS, HISTOGRAM, APDEX, ERRORS, SLOWEST, BURST, PEAK, GAPS: when there is no target record.
- LATENCY, SESSIONS, IP: when the collection of records defined for each of them in 7. is empty.

6. Common formats and rules
- Decimals: for integers a >= 0 and b > 0, "writing a/b to d decimal places" means finding the quotient q of a * 10^d divided by b (the fractional part is truncated), and writing, concatenated in this order, the quotient of q divided by 10^d, ".", and the remainder of q divided by 10^d written with d digits (padded with leading 0s if it has fewer than d digits). For example, 2/3 written to 2 decimal places is 0.66, 7/2 written to 1 decimal place is 3.5, and 5/1 written to 2 decimal places is 5.00. The computation is done exactly with integers.
- Percentage: the percentage of a relative to b is (a * 100)/b written to 2 decimal places, followed by "%" (for example, the percentage of 1 relative to 3 is 33.33%).
- Bar: the bar for a count c and a maximum M (M >= 1) is "#" repeated ceil(c * 30 / M) times. A "line with a bar" is a line of the form "<label> <count> <bar>", except that when the count is 0, the bar and the space before it are not written, giving "<label> 0".
- Byte notation: the notation of a byte count b is b followed by "B" if b < 1024; b/1024 written to 1 decimal place followed by "KB" if 1024 <= b < 1048576; b/1048576 written to 1 decimal place followed by "MB" if 1048576 <= b < 1073741824; and b/1073741824 written to 1 decimal place followed by "GB" if 1073741824 <= b (examples: 512B, 1.0KB, 2.3MB).
- String order: when strings are compared, they are compared one character at a time from the start by ASCII code, and the one whose first differing character has the smaller code comes first. When one of them matches the beginning of the other, the shorter one comes first.
- IP address order: when IP addresses are compared, the values of their 4 parts are compared as numbers from left to right (for example, the order is 8.8.8.8, 10.0.0.2, 10.0.0.10).
- Window: for records r1, r2, ..., rm (m >= 1) sorted in time order and a length L (in seconds), for each j (1 <= j <= m), let i(j) be the smallest i that satisfies "seconds of rj - seconds of ri < L", and let j - i(j) + 1 be the window size at j. Let W be the maximum window size, let j* be the smallest j whose window size is W, and let r(i(j*)) be the start of the window and r(j*) the end of the window. In other words, W is the largest number of records that are consecutive in time order and whose seconds differ by less than L.
- Comparing rates and averages: the rates, averages and scores compared when sorting in ERRORS, SLOWEST and APDEX are compared by their exact values as fractions, not by the values written as decimals.

7. The queries
SUMMARY
  Output the following 3 lines.
  SUMMARY lines <number of log lines> valid <number of records> malformed <number of malformed lines>
  requests <n> bytes <total byte count> ips <number of distinct IP addresses> paths <number of distinct paths>
  span <first timestamp> <last timestamp> <difference in seconds>
  The 1st line gives counts over all log lines, regardless of the range and the conditions. The 2nd and 3rd lines give values for the target records. The first and the last in the 3rd line are the first and the last record when the target records are sorted in time order, and the difference in seconds is the seconds of the last record minus the seconds of the first record. When n = 0, all values in the 2nd line are 0, and the 3rd line is "span none".

MALFORMED
  On the 1st line, output "MALFORMED <number of malformed lines>". Then, for the reasons fields, date, time, ip, method, path, status, bytes, latency in this order, for each reason that has at least one malformed line, output one line "<reason> <number of lines with that reason> <line number> <line number> ..." (listing all the line numbers of the lines with that reason in ascending order). All log lines are covered, regardless of the range and the conditions.

STATUS
  On the 1st line, output "STATUS <n>". Then output the 5 lines "<c>xx <count> <percentage>" for c = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 in this order (output them even if the count is 0). The count is the number of target records that are cxx, and the percentage is the percentage of the count relative to n. Then, for each status that appears in the target records, in ascending order of status, output "<status> <count> <percentage>".

METHODS
  On the 1st line, output "METHODS <n>". Then, for each method that appears in the target records, output "<method> <count> <percentage>". The percentage is the percentage of the count relative to n. The order is descending order of count, and for equal counts, the string order of the method names.

TOP <kind> <limit>
  If <kind> is PATHS, the path is the "key"; if it is IPS, the IP address is the key. Count the target records for each key. Let m be the number of distinct keys, and let s be the smaller of <limit> and m. Sort the keys in descending order of count, and for equal counts, in key order (string order for paths, IP address order for IP addresses). On the 1st line, output "TOP <kind> <s> of <m>", and then, for the first s keys in this sorted order, output "<rank> <key> <count> <percentage>". The rank is the number of keys whose count is larger than that of this key, plus 1 (keys with equal counts get the same rank). The percentage is the percentage of the count relative to n.

HOURLY
  For each h = 0, 1, ..., 23, count the target records whose hour HH is h. Let M be the maximum of these counts, and let p be the smallest h whose count is M. On the 1st line, output "HOURLY <n> peak <p> <M>". Then, for h = 0, 1, ..., 23 in this order, output 24 lines with a bar, each with h as the label and its count as the count. The maximum for the bars is M. p and the label h of each line are written with 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit).

TIMELINE <minutes>
  Let w = <minutes> * 60, and let the quotient of the seconds of each target record divided by w be the "interval number" of that record. Let a be the minimum and b the maximum interval number; if b - a + 1 is greater than 60, the error is too many buckets. Otherwise, on the 1st line, output "TIMELINE <minutes> <b - a + 1>". Then, for k = a, a + 1, ..., b in this order, output a line with a bar whose label is the start time of the interval with interval number k and whose count is the number of target records whose interval number is k. The maximum for the bars is the maximum of those counts.
  The start time of an interval is the date and time corresponding to the seconds k * w, written in the form "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM" (the month, day, hour and minute each with 2 digits; the seconds are not written). The date and time corresponding to seconds s are obtained by reversing the conversion of 2. That is, letting D be the quotient and R the remainder of s divided by 86400, the date is the date whose day number is D, the hour is the quotient of R divided by 3600, and the minute is the quotient of the remainder of R divided by 3600, divided by 60.

BANDWIDTH
  Group the target records by date. Let d be the number of distinct dates, and let B be the total byte count of the target records. On the 1st line, output "BANDWIDTH <d> <B> <notation of B>", and then, in order from the oldest date (the same as the string order of the dates), output "<date> <count> <total byte count> <notation of that total>". The count and the total byte count are values over the target records of that date.

VISITORS
  For each IP address, the oldest of the dates of the target records with that IP address is the "first day" of that IP address. On the 1st line, output "VISITORS <number of distinct dates> <number of distinct IP addresses>" (both are counts over the target records). Then, for each date that appears in the target records, from the oldest, output "<date> unique <u> new <w> returning <u - w>". u is the number of distinct IP addresses of the target records of that date, and w is the number of those IP addresses whose first day is that date.

LATENCY [<path>]
  If <path> is absent, use all target records; if it is present, use the target records whose path matches the normalization of <path>. Let c be the number of records used, and let v1 <= v2 <= ... <= vc be their response times sorted in ascending order. The P-th percentile (P = 50, 90, 99) is vr, where r is ceil(P * c / 100). Let k be the quotient of c divided by 10, and call the c - 2k values from v(k+1) to v(c-k) the "trimmed values". Output the following one line.
  LATENCY <target> count <c> min <v1> p50 <50th percentile> p90 <90th percentile> p99 <99th percentile> max <vc> mean <mean> trimmed <trimmed mean>
  <target> is all if <path> is absent, or the normalization of <path> if it is present. The mean is "total of the response times/c" and the trimmed mean is "total of the trimmed values/(c - 2k)", each written to 2 decimal places.

HISTOGRAM <width>
  Let the quotient of the response time of each target record divided by <width> be the "class number" of that record. Let a be the minimum and b the maximum class number; if b - a + 1 is greater than 60, the error is too many buckets. Otherwise, on the 1st line, output "HISTOGRAM <width> <b - a + 1>". Then, for k = a, a + 1, ..., b in this order, output a line with a bar whose label is "<k * width>-<k * width + width - 1>" (example: 100-199) and whose count is the number of target records whose class number is k. The maximum for the bars is the maximum of those counts.

APDEX <threshold>
  Let T be <threshold>, and classify each target record as follows. If its status is 500 or more, it is "frustrated". Otherwise, it is "satisfied" if its response time is at most T, "tolerating" if the response time is greater than T and at most 4 * T, and "frustrated" if the response time is greater than 4 * T. For a collection of N records of which S are satisfied and U are tolerating, the "score" of the collection is the fraction (2 * S + U)/(2 * N), and this value written to 3 decimal places is the notation of the score. The "rating" of a score is excellent if the score is 0.94 or more, otherwise good if it is 0.85 or more, otherwise fair if it is 0.70 or more, otherwise poor if it is 0.50 or more, and unacceptable otherwise.
  On the 1st line, for all target records, output "APDEX <T> <notation of the score> <rating> satisfied <S> tolerating <U> frustrated <number of frustrated records>". Then, for each path that appears in the target records, output "<path> <notation of the score> <rating> <N>" for the target records of that path. The order is ascending order of score, for equal scores descending order of N, and if those are also equal, the string order of the paths.

ERRORS
  Let E be the number of target records that are errors. On the 1st line, output "ERRORS <E> of <n> <percentage of E relative to n>". Then, for each path that has at least one target record that is an error, output "<path> <count> <4xx count> <5xx count> <error rate>". The count is the number of target records of that path, the 4xx count and the 5xx count are the numbers of those records that are 4xx and 5xx, respectively, and the error rate is the percentage of (4xx count + 5xx count) relative to the count. The order is descending order of (4xx count + 5xx count)/count, for equal values descending order of (4xx count + 5xx count), and if those are also equal, the string order of the paths.

SLOWEST <limit>
  A pair of a method and a path is called an "endpoint"; group the target records by endpoint. For each endpoint, let c be the number of records, t the total of the response times, and x the maximum response time, and let the average be t/c. Let m be the number of distinct endpoints, and let s be the smaller of <limit> and m. Sort the endpoints in descending order of average, for equal averages in descending order of x, if those are also equal in the string order of the paths, and if those are also equal in the string order of the method names. On the 1st line, output "SLOWEST <s> of <m>", and then, for the first s endpoints in this sorted order, for i = 1, 2, ..., s in this order, output "<i> <method> <path> count <c> avg <t/c written to 2 decimal places> max <x>".

SESSIONS <ip> [<minutes>]
  If <minutes> is absent, it is 30. Use the target records whose IP address is <ip>, and let r1, r2, ..., rm be these records sorted in time order. Divide them into "sessions" as follows. r1 starts the first session. For i >= 2, if (seconds of ri) - (seconds of r(i-1)) is at most <minutes> * 60, ri belongs to the same session as r(i-1); otherwise, ri starts a new session. The "duration" of a session is the seconds of the last record of the session minus the seconds of its first record. Let q be the number of sessions, and on the 1st line, output the following.
  SESSIONS <ip> timeout <minutes> count <q> requests <m> avg_duration <total of the durations/q written to 1 decimal place> avg_requests <m/q written to 1 decimal place>
  Then number the sessions 1, 2, ... in order of their start, earliest first, and for each session output "<number> <timestamp of the first record> <timestamp of the last record> <duration> <number of records> <number of distinct paths>".

BURST <length> <times>
  Group the target records that are errors by IP address, sort each group in time order, and for each group find the window (6.) with length L = <length>. An IP address whose maximum window size W is at least <times> is a "matching IP address". On the 1st line, output "BURST <length> <times> <number of matching IP addresses>", and then, for each matching IP address, output "<ip> <W> <timestamp of the start of the window> <timestamp of the end of the window>". The order is descending order of W, and for equal W, IP address order. If there is no target record that is an error, output only the 1st line.

PEAK <length>
  Sort all target records in time order and find the window with length L = <length>. Output the single line "PEAK <length> <W> <timestamp of the start of the window> <timestamp of the end of the window>".

GAPS <length>
  Let r1, r2, ..., rn be all target records sorted in time order. Each i with 1 <= i <= n - 1 such that (seconds of r(i+1)) - (seconds of ri) is at least <length> is a "gap". On the 1st line, output "GAPS <length> <number of gaps>", and then, for each gap, in ascending order of i, output "<timestamp of ri> <timestamp of r(i+1)> <difference in seconds>".

IP <ip>
  Use the target records whose IP address is <ip>. Output the following lines.
  IP <ip> requests <number of records> bytes <total byte count> errors <number of errors> paths <number of distinct paths>
  first <first timestamp> last <last timestamp>
  methods <method> <count> <method> <count> ...
  classes 1xx <count> 2xx <count> 3xx <count> 4xx <count> 5xx <count>
  path <path> <count>
  The first and the last on the 2nd line are the first and the last record when these records are sorted in time order. The 3rd line lists the pairs of each method that appears in these records and its count, in descending order of count, and for equal counts, in the string order of the method names. The 4th line gives the numbers of records that are cxx (written even if 0). For the final "path" lines, sort the paths that appear in these records in descending order of count, and for equal counts, in the string order of the paths, and output one line for each of the first ones, at most 3.

RANGE <date> <time> <date> <time>
  The start timestamp is the 1st and 2nd arguments joined with one space, and the end timestamp is the 3rd and 4th arguments joined with one space. If the seconds of the start is greater than the seconds of the end, the error is invalid range. Otherwise, change the range to the range from the start to the end (the previous range is discarded), and output "RANGE <start timestamp> <end timestamp> active <n>". n is the number of target records after the change.

FILTER <key> <value>
  Change the condition of <key> to <value> (the previous value is discarded), and output "FILTER <key> <value> active <n>". When the key is PATH, the normalization of <value> is used as the value of the condition, and the normalization is also written as <value> in the output. n is the number of target records after the change.

UNFILTER <key>
  If <key> is RANGE, the subject is the range; otherwise, the subject is the condition of that key. If the subject is "none", the error is not filtered. Otherwise, change the subject to "none", and output "UNFILTER <key> active <n>". n is the number of target records after the change.

RESET
  Change the range and all 4 conditions to "none", and output "RESET active <n>". n is the number of target records after the change.

STATE
