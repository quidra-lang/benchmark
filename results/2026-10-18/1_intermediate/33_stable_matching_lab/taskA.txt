[Overview]
Write a program for a "stable matching lab" that runs representative algorithms for matching markets, in which participants rank one another by preference, with every detail of each procedure specified. It reads a sequence of commands from standard input and handles: creating multiple named markets (two-sided markets, roommate markets and housing markets); setting preference lists and capacities; generating preference lists with MINSTD pseudo-random numbers; deferred acceptance (the Gale-Shapley algorithm) with proposals made from the proposer side and from the receiver side, and recording its proposals; checking for blocking pairs; enumerating all stable matchings by eliminating rotations; selecting the egalitarian stable matching and the minimum-regret stable matching; the hospitals/residents problem and checking the rural hospitals theorem; the stable roommates problem by Irving's algorithm; the top trading cycles algorithm; serial dictatorship; the Boston mechanism (immediate acceptance) and searching for manipulations by swapping adjacent preferences; and rank statistics. It writes the results to standard output. Every search order and every tie-break is specified, so the output is uniquely determined.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. Each command consists of a command name and zero or more arguments, separated by single ASCII spaces; there is no whitespace at the start or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. There are the following 20 command formats ([VERBOSE] means that the word VERBOSE may or may not be present).

  MARKET m p1 p2 ... pk | r1 r2 ... rl
  ROOMS m a1 a2 ... ak
  HOUSES m a1:h1 a2:h2 ... ak:hk
  RANDOM m n seed
  PREF m a c1 c2 ... ck
  CAPACITY m r q
  SHOW m
  GS m [VERBOSE]
  GSR m [VERBOSE]
  CHECK m x1-y1 x2-y2 ... xk-yk
  LATTICE m
  EGALITARIAN m
  MINREGRET m
  HR m
  ROOMMATES m [VERBOSE]
  TTC m
  SERIAL m a1 a2 ... ak
  BOSTON m
  MANIPULATE m a
  STATS m

The arguments mean the following.
- m is a market name, h1 to hk are house names, and all the others (p1 to pk, r1 to rl, a, a1 to ak, r, x1 to xk, y1 to yk) are agent names. c1 to ck of PREF are agent names or house names. A name is a string consisting of one English letter (A to Z or a to z) followed by zero or more English letters or digits (0 to 9). Names are case-sensitive.
- The "|" of MARKET is a word consisting of the single ASCII vertical bar character only. A MARKET line contains exactly one "|", with one or more names before it (after the market name) and one or more names after it.
- ai:hi of HOUSES is a single word made of an agent name and a house name joined by ":" (ASCII colon). xi-yi of CHECK is a single word made of two names joined by "-" (ASCII hyphen).
- n, seed and q are positive integers written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- k and l are symbols denoting the number of names or words on the line; they are not written in the input. k of PREF, CHECK and SERIAL may be 0; in that case the line ends with the agent name a for PREF, and with the market name m for CHECK and SERIAL.
- VERBOSE is the word VERBOSE written in uppercase English letters.
It is guaranteed that every line follows these formats and the ranges in [Constraints].

[Processing]
Process the commands in order from the first line, and print the result of each command according to [Output format]. Check the errors listed under "Checks" in the description of each command in the order listed; print only the line of the first error that applies, and do nothing else for that command (no market is created or changed). If no error applies, perform the "Action".

== Markets and agents ==
- The program holds any number of named markets. Initially there are no markets. Market names are distinct from one another regardless of market type. There is no command that deletes or replaces a market.
- There are the following 3 types of markets.
  Two-sided market (created by MARKET and RANDOM): the agents are divided into two groups, "proposers" and "receivers". The names of the agents in the same market are distinct across both groups. The proposers are ordered in the order given when the market was created; this is called the "proposer order". The receivers are ordered likewise; this is called the "receiver order". Each receiver has a "capacity" (an integer >= 1), which is 1 when the market is created. The capacity of a proposer is always 1.
  Roommate market (created by ROOMS): there is only one group of agents, and their names are distinct.
  Housing market (created by HOUSES): each agent owns one house, and that agent is called the "owner" of that house. Agent names are distinct from one another, and house names are distinct from one another (an agent and a house may have the same name).
- In roommate markets and housing markets, the agents are ordered in the order given when the market was created; this is called the "agent order".
- Each agent has a "preference list". It is empty when the agent is created, and is set by PREF and RANDOM. A preference list lists the agent's "possible partners" from most preferred to least preferred, without duplicates. The possible partners are: for a proposer of a two-sided market, the receivers of that market; for a receiver, the proposers of that market; in a roommate market, the agents of the same market other than the agent itself; in a housing market, the houses of the same market (including the agent's own house). Anything not on the preference list is an "unacceptable" partner for that agent.
- When b is on the preference list of agent a, the position of b in the list counted from the front (starting from 1) is called "the rank of b for a". The rank of a b that is not on a's preference list, and the rank of "no partner", are both (length of a's preference list)+1. When the rank of b for a is smaller than the rank of c for a, we say that "a prefers b to c".

== Matchings ==
- A "matching" of a two-sided market is a set of proposer-receiver pairs in which each proposer is in at most one pair and each receiver is in at most (its capacity) pairs. When there is a pair containing proposer p, the receiver of that pair is called p's "partner" and is written M(p). The proposers of the pairs containing receiver r are called r's "partners" (there may be more than one).
- The "pair notation" of a matching lists all proposers in proposer order, writing "p-r" for each proposer p if it has a partner r and "p-*" if it has none, separated by single spaces (example: Ann-Fay Bob-* Cid-Eve).
- Among the pairs (p, r) of a matching, a pair where r is not on p's preference list or p is not on r's preference list is called an "unacceptable pair".
- A pair (p, r) of a proposer p and a receiver r that is not in the matching and satisfies all of (1) to (3) below is called a "blocking pair" of that matching.
  (1) r is on p's preference list, and p is on r's preference list.
  (2) p prefers r to M(p) (if p has no partner, compare with "no partner").
  (3) The number of r's partners is less than r's capacity. Or, letting w be the one among r's partners whose rank for r is the largest, r prefers p to w.
- A matching with no unacceptable pairs and no blocking pairs is called a "stable matching".
- Each two-sided market has a "current matching" and its "source". When the market is created, there is no current matching. When GS, GSR, BOSTON, EGALITARIAN or MINREGRET succeeds, the matching it computed becomes the current matching of that market, and the source becomes that command name (one of GS, GSR, BOSTON, EGALITARIAN, MINREGRET). When PREF or CAPACITY on that market succeeds, the market no longer has a current matching. Other commands do not change the current matching.

== Common checks ==
- "Check new market m" means: if a market named m already exists, print "ERROR DUPLICATE MARKET <m>".
- "Check market m" means: if no market named m exists, print "ERROR UNKNOWN MARKET <m>". "Check two-sided market m" means: check market m, and then, if m is not a two-sided market, print "ERROR WRONG TYPE <m>". "Check roommate market m" and "check housing market m" are defined likewise for each type.
- "Check agent a" means: if the target market has no agent named a, print "ERROR UNKNOWN AGENT <a>" (house names are not agent names). "Check proposer a" means: check agent a, and then, if a is a receiver, print "ERROR NOT PROPOSER <a>". "Check receiver a" means: check agent a, and then, if a is a proposer, print "ERROR NOT RECEIVER <a>".

== Deferred acceptance ==
GS, GSR, HR, LATTICE, EGALITARIAN, MINREGRET and MANIPULATE use the following "deferred acceptance" procedure on a two-sided market. One of the two groups (proposers and receivers) is the "proposing side" and the other is the "receiving side". In "proposer-proposing deferred acceptance" the proposers are the proposing side, and in "receiver-proposing deferred acceptance" the receivers are the proposing side. On either side, the capacity of each agent is that agent's capacity (1 for a proposer, the set value for a receiver).
- Each agent x on the proposing side has a "next proposal position" in its own preference list, initially the front of the list. Each agent y on the receiving side has a set of agents it is "holding", initially empty. The number of agents on the receiving side that are holding x is called x's "hold count". When x's hold count is less than x's capacity and x's preference list still contains partners that x has not yet proposed to, x is said to be "able to propose".
- Prepare a queue, and put into it all agents on the proposing side that are able to propose, in proposer order (in receiver order if the proposing side is the receivers). Repeat (1) to (3) below until the queue is empty.
  (1) Remove the agent x at the front of the queue. Let y be the partner at x's next proposal position in x's preference list, and advance that position by one. x "proposes" to y.
  (2) If x is not on y's preference list, y rejects x. Otherwise, if the number of agents y is holding is less than y's capacity, y holds x. Otherwise, let w be the agent whose rank for y is the largest among the agents y is holding. If y prefers x to w, y stops holding w (rejects w) and holds x; in this case, if w is able to propose and is not in the queue, append w to the end of the queue. If y does not prefer x to w, y rejects x.
  (3) If x is able to propose, append x to the end of the queue.
- When the queue becomes empty, the set of pairs formed by each agent y on the receiving side and each agent that y is holding is the resulting matching (the result is always a stable matching). The number of times (1) was performed is called the "number of proposals".
- Each proposal is represented by the following "proposal line". When in (2) y holds x without rejecting anyone, it is "PROPOSE <x> <y> HOLD"; when y rejects w and holds x, it is "PROPOSE <x> <y> HOLD REJECT <w>"; when y rejects x, it is "PROPOSE <x> <y> REJECT".

== Enumerating stable matchings ==
LATTICE, EGALITARIAN and MINREGRET enumerate all stable matchings by the following procedure, on a two-sided market in which every receiver has capacity 1.
- For a proposer p that has a partner in a stable matching M, examine the receivers w that come after M(p) in p's preference list, from front to back, and find the first w satisfying the condition "p is on w's preference list, and w has no partner or w prefers p to w's partner". If such a w exists and that w has a partner, w's partner is called p's "successor". If no such w exists, or the w found has no partner, p has no successor.
- Build the "rotation sequence" in M as follows. Look at the proposers one at a time in proposer order; if the proposer p being looked at is not yet contained in any rotation in the sequence, follow successors from p one after another. Stop when reaching a proposer that has no successor, or a proposer already visited during this traversal from p (including p itself). If the reason for stopping is returning to p, append to the end of the sequence, as a "rotation", the sequence of proposers p0, p1, ..., p(t-1) (p0=p) traversed as p, p's successor, its successor, ..., up to just before returning to p. The "notation" of a rotation is "p0-M(p0) p1-M(p1) ... p(t-1)-M(p(t-1))".
- To "eliminate" rotation p0, ..., p(t-1) in M means to build the matching in which, for each p(i) with i=0, 1, ..., t-1, the partner of p(i) is changed to M(p(i+1)) (M(p0) when i=t-1), and the other pairs are left unchanged.
- Start the "enumeration list" as a list consisting of only one matching: the result of proposer-proposing deferred acceptance. The matchings in the enumeration list are numbered from 1 in the order they were added, and the matching numbered i is written Mi. The "origin" and "via" of M1 are "-".
- For i=1, 2, ... in order, while Mi is in the enumeration list, do the following. For each rotation rho in the rotation sequence in Mi, in sequence order: if no rotation with the same notation as rho has a number yet, assign the next number (consecutive numbers starting from 1) to that notation. Hereafter, the number assigned to the notation of rho is called "the number of rho". Then, if the matching M' obtained by eliminating rho in Mi differs from every matching in the enumeration list (comparing them as sets of pairs), append M' to the end of the enumeration list, with origin i and via the number of rho.
- By this procedure, every stable matching of the market appears in the enumeration list exactly once. When there are 51 or more stable matchings (when a 51st matching would be added to the enumeration list), it is the error "ERROR TOO MANY MATCHINGS".
- The "cost" of a matching is the sum, over all pairs (p, r), of "the rank of r for p" and "the rank of p for r". The "regret" of a matching is the maximum of those ranks (if there are no pairs, both the cost and the regret are 0).

== Boston mechanism ==
BOSTON and MANIPULATE use the following "Boston mechanism" on a two-sided market.
- Initially no proposer has a partner, and the "remaining slots" of each receiver equal its capacity. Let L be the maximum length of the proposers' preference lists, and perform rounds j=1, 2, ..., L in this order.
- In round j, first, every proposer that has no partner and whose preference list has length j or more "applies" to the j-th receiver on its own preference list. Then, for each receiver r, look at the proposers that applied to r in this round and are on r's preference list, in increasing order of their rank for r: if r's remaining slots are 1 or more, that proposer becomes r's partner and r's remaining slots decrease by 1; if the remaining slots are 0, that proposer gets no partner. Applicants that are not on r's preference list also get no partner. A pair, once decided, never changes afterward.
- The total number of applications over all rounds is called the "number of applications".

== Effective list ==
The "effective list" of agent a in a housing market is: if a's house is on a's preference list, the part of the preference list from the front up to that house (including that house); otherwise, the preference list with a's house appended at the end.

== Commands ==
MARKET m p1 ... pk | r1 ... rl
- Checks: check new market m. Then look at p1, ..., pk, r1, ..., rl in this order, and for the first one x whose name already appeared earlier, print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <x>".
- Action: create two-sided market m with p1, ..., pk as proposers and r1, ..., rl as receivers, and print "MARKET <m> proposers=<k> receivers=<l>".

ROOMS m a1 ... ak
- Checks: check new market m. Then look at a1, ..., ak in this order, and for the first one x whose name already appeared earlier, print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <x>".
- Action: create roommate market m with a1, ..., ak as its agents, and print "ROOMS <m> agents=<k>".

HOUSES m a1:h1 ... ak:hk
- Checks: check new market m. Then, for i=1, 2, ..., k in order: if ai is equal to any of a1, ..., a(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <ai>"; otherwise, if hi is equal to any of h1, ..., h(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE HOUSE <hi>" (print only the first one found).
- Action: create housing market m in which each ai owns house hi, and print "HOUSES <m> agents=<k>".

RANDOM m n seed
- Checks: check new market m.
- Action: create two-sided market m consisting of proposers P1, P2, ..., Pn and receivers R1, R2, ..., Rn (names made of the uppercase letter P or R followed by an integer from 1 to n). All capacities are 1. Then set the pseudo-random state x to seed, and build the preference list of each agent, in the order P1, ..., Pn, R1, ..., Rn, as follows. First, make a list L of R1, ..., Rn in this order for a proposer, or of P1, ..., Pn in this order for a receiver, and number the positions of L from the front as 1, 2, ..., n. Next, for i=n, n-1, ..., 2 in order, replace x with "the remainder of 48271 * x divided by 2147483647", let j be ("the remainder of x divided by i")+1, and swap the i-th and j-th elements of L (do nothing if i=j). The resulting L is that agent's preference list. The state x carries over unchanged to the next agent. Finally print "RANDOM <m> n=<n> seed=<seed>".

PREF m a c1 ... ck
- Checks: check market m, and check agent a. Then, for i=1, 2, ..., k in order: if ci is not the name of a possible partner of a, print "ERROR BAD CHOICE <ci>"; otherwise, if ci is equal to any of c1, ..., c(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE CHOICE <ci>" (print only the first one found).
- Action: replace a's preference list with c1, ..., ck (empty if k=0); if m is a two-sided market, remove its current matching; and print "PREF <m> <a> length=<k>".

CAPACITY m r q
- Checks: check two-sided market m, and check receiver r.
- Action: set r's capacity to q, remove the current matching, and print "CAPACITY <m> <r> <q>".

SHOW m
- Checks: check market m.
- Action: the "list notation" of a preference list is the elements of the list from front to back separated by single spaces ("-" if the list is empty).
  For a two-sided market, print "SHOW <m> MARKET proposers=<number of proposers> receivers=<number of receivers>", then, for each proposer p in proposer order, "PROPOSER <p> <list notation of p's preference list>", then, for each receiver r in receiver order, "RECEIVER <r> cap=<capacity of r> <list notation of r's preference list>", and finally "CURRENT <source of the current matching>" ("CURRENT -" if there is no current matching).
  For a roommate market, print "SHOW <m> ROOMS agents=<number of agents>", then, for each agent a in agent order, "AGENT <a> <list notation of a's preference list>".
  For a housing market, print "SHOW <m> HOUSES agents=<number of agents>", then, for each agent a in agent order, "AGENT <a> house=<a's house> <list notation of a's preference list>".

GS m [VERBOSE]
- Checks: check two-sided market m.
- Action: run proposer-proposing deferred acceptance, and make the result the current matching (source GS). Print "GS <m> proposals=<number of proposals>", then, if VERBOSE is present, all proposal lines in the order the proposals were made, and finally "MATCHING <pair notation of the result>".

GSR m [VERBOSE]
- Checks: check two-sided market m.
- Action: run receiver-proposing deferred acceptance, and make the result the current matching (source GSR). The output has the same form as for GS, and starts with "GSR <m> proposals=<number of proposals>", that is, the first line with GS replaced by GSR (in the proposal lines, x is a receiver and y is a proposer).

CHECK m x1-y1 ... xk-yk
- Checks: check market m, and if m is a housing market, print "ERROR WRONG TYPE <m>". Then examine the pairs xi-yi for i=1, 2, ..., k in order as follows, and print only the first error found.
  When m is a two-sided market: check proposer xi, and check receiver yi. Then, if xi is equal to any of x1, ..., x(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <xi>". Then, if the number of those among y1, ..., yi that are equal to yi is greater than the capacity of yi, print "ERROR OVER CAPACITY <yi>".
  When m is a roommate market: check agent xi, and check agent yi. Then, if xi is equal to any of x1, ..., x(i-1), y1, ..., y(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <xi>". Then, if yi is equal to xi or to any of x1, ..., x(i-1), y1, ..., y(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <yi>".
- Action (two-sided market): for the matching consisting of the pairs (x1, y1), ..., (xk, yk) (the matching with no pairs if k=0), find the number U of unacceptable pairs and the number B of blocking pairs. If U and B are both 0, print only "CHECK <m> STABLE". Otherwise, print "CHECK <m> UNSTABLE unacceptable=<U> blocking=<B>", then each unacceptable pair (p, r) in proposer order of p, in the form "UNACCEPTABLE <p> <r>", then each blocking pair (p, r) in proposer order of p, and for the same p in receiver order of r, in the form "BLOCKING <p> <r>".
- Action (roommate market): consider the roommate matching consisting of the pairs {x1, y1}, ..., {xk, yk}. Each agent is in at most one pair, and the other member of the same pair is that agent's partner. A pair {a, b} where b is not on a's preference list or a is not on b's preference list is an "unacceptable pair"; two agents a and b that are not in the same pair, such that b is on a's preference list, a is on b's preference list, a prefers b to its own partner, and b prefers a to its own partner (comparing with "no partner" if there is no partner), form a "blocking pair". For both kinds, the one of the two agents that comes first in agent order is taken as a and the pair is written (a, b); the pairs are sorted in agent order of a, and for the same a in agent order of b. The output has the same form as for a two-sided market.

LATTICE m
- Checks: check two-sided market m. Then, if there is a receiver whose capacity is not 1, print "ERROR NOT ONE TO ONE". Then run the enumeration of stable matchings, and if there are 51 or more stable matchings, print "ERROR TOO MANY MATCHINGS".
- Action: print "LATTICE <m> matchings=<number of matchings in the enumeration list> rotations=<number of numbered rotations>". Then, for each matching Mi in number order, print "MATCHING <i> from=<origin of Mi> via=<via of Mi> cost=<cost of Mi> regret=<regret of Mi> <pair notation of Mi>", and finally, for each rotation in number order, print "ROTATION <number> <notation of the rotation>".

EGALITARIAN m
- Checks: same as LATTICE.
- Action: let Mi be the matching in the enumeration list with the minimum cost (if there are several, the one with the smallest number), and make Mi the current matching (source EGALITARIAN). Print "EGALITARIAN <m> matching=<i> cost=<cost of Mi>" and "MATCHING <pair notation of Mi>".

MINREGRET m
- Checks: same as LATTICE.
- Action: let Mi be the matching in the enumeration list with the minimum regret (if there are several, the one with the smallest number), and make Mi the current matching (source MINREGRET). Print "MINREGRET <m> matching=<i> regret=<regret of Mi>" and "MATCHING <pair notation of Mi>".

HR m
- Checks: check two-sided market m.
- Action: regard the proposers as residents and the receivers as hospitals. Let A be the result of proposer-proposing deferred acceptance, and H the result of receiver-proposing deferred acceptance (the current matching is not changed). Print the following lines in this order.
  "HR <m> residents=<number of proposers> hospitals=<number of receivers>"
  "RESIDENT-OPTIMAL <pair notation of A>"
  "HOSPITAL-OPTIMAL <pair notation of H>"
  "UNMATCHED <the proposers that have no partner in A, in proposer order, separated by single spaces>" ("UNMATCHED -" if there is no such proposer)
  For each receiver h in receiver order, "HOSPITAL <h> cap=<capacity of h> resident=<number of h's partners in A> hospital=<number of h's partners in H> same=<S>". S is YES if the set of h's partners in A equals the set of h's partners in H, and NO otherwise.
  Finally, "RURAL HOLDS" if all of (1) to (3) below hold, and "RURAL FAILS" otherwise. (1) The set of proposers that have a partner in A equals the set of proposers that have a partner in H. (2) For every receiver, the values of resident and hospital are equal. (3) For every receiver whose resident value is less than its capacity, S is YES. (By the rural hospitals theorem, these 3 conditions always hold.)

ROOMMATES m [VERBOSE]
- Checks: check roommate market m. Then, if there are agents whose preference list is shorter than (number of agents in m)-1 (that is, who have not listed everyone other than themselves), print "ERROR INCOMPLETE <a>" for the first such agent a in agent order.
- Action: perform Irving's algorithm as follows. Each agent has a "working list", initially a copy of its own preference list. The elements of a working list always stay in the same order as in the original preference list. Below, "remove the pair of a and b" means removing b from a's working list and a from b's working list. "Print the table" means printing, for each agent a in agent order, "TABLE <a> <list notation of a's working list>".
  Phase 1: each agent has a "held partner", initially none. Put all agents into a queue in agent order, and repeat the following until the queue is empty. Remove the agent x at the front of the queue; x proposes to the agent y at the front of x's working list (at this time x is always on y's working list). If y has a held partner z, append z to the end of the queue. Make x the held partner of y, and for each agent w that comes after x in y's working list, remove the pair of y and w (if z exists, z is among these w). After each such proposal and its removals (that is, still inside the repetition), if there are agents whose working list is empty, end the procedure with "no stable matching (a)" for the first such agent a in agent order.
  If phase 1 did not end with no stable matching, print the table if VERBOSE is present, and proceed to phase 2.
  Phase 2: if the working lists of all agents have length 1, pair each agent with the only agent in its working list and end the procedure. Otherwise, let p1 be the first agent in agent order whose working list has length 2 or more, and for i=1, 2, ... in order, find q(i) as the second agent in p(i)'s working list and p(i+1) as the last agent in q(i)'s working list. Stop when p(i+1) becomes equal to one of p1, ..., p(i), say p(j) (the working list of every p(i) that appears along the way always has length 2 or more). Let r=i-j+1, and for s=0, 1, ..., r-1, let x(s)=p(j+s) and y(s)=the agent at the front of x(s)'s working list. (x(0), y(0)), ..., (x(r-1), y(r-1)) is called the "rotation" of this phase; give it the number t, which is the number of rotations found so far plus 1, and print "ROTATION <t> <x(0)>-<y(0)> <x(1)>-<y(1)> ... <x(r-1)>-<y(r-1)>".
  Then eliminate this rotation. For s=0, 1, ..., r-1, letting u be y(s+1) (y(0) when s=r-1), remove the pair of u and w for each agent w that comes after x(s) in u's working list. All pairs to remove are determined from the working lists as they were before the elimination started (changes during the elimination do not affect which pairs are removed). After the elimination, print the table if VERBOSE is present. After that, if there are agents whose working list is empty, end the procedure with "no stable matching (a)" for the first such agent a in agent order; otherwise return to the beginning of phase 2.
  The output is first the line "ROOMMATES <m> proposals=<number of proposals in phase 1>", followed by the TABLE lines and ROTATION lines described above, in the order they were printed. Finally, if the procedure ended with no stable matching (a), print "NO STABLE MATCHING <a>"; if it ended by forming pairs, print "MATCHING <pair list>". The pair list writes each pair as "<a>-<b>" (where a is the one of the two that comes first in agent order) and lists the pairs in agent order of a, separated by single spaces. (When this procedure ends by forming pairs, those pairs form a stable roommate matching; when it ends with no stable matching, no stable roommate matching exists.)

TTC m
- Checks: check housing market m.
- Action: let the set of agents called the "remaining set" initially be all agents. A house whose owner is in the remaining set is called a "remaining house". Repeat (1) to (3) below until the remaining set is empty.
  (1) For each agent a in the remaining set, the owner of the remaining house that comes first in a's effective list is called the agent that a "points to" (a's own house is always remaining, so this is always determined).
  (2) Starting from the first agent in agent order in the remaining set, follow the agents pointed to one after another; when an already visited agent is reached for the first time, call that agent b1. Let b1, b2, ..., bt be the agents visited, in order, when following the pointers one step at a time from b1 until just before returning to b1; this sequence is called a "cycle" (t=1 when b1 points to itself). Cycles are numbered from 1 in the order they are found.
  (3) Each bi receives the house owned by the agent it points to. Remove b1, ..., bt from the remaining set.
  The output is "TTC <m> cycles=<number of cycles>", then, for each cycle in number order, "CYCLE <number> <b1>:<house b1 receives> <b2>:<house b2 receives> ... <bt>:<house bt receives>", and finally "ALLOCATION <allocation notation>". The allocation notation lists all agents in agent order, writing "a-h" for each agent a if it received a house h and "a-*" if it received none, separated by single spaces.

SERIAL m a1 ... ak
- Checks: check housing market m. Then, for i=1, 2, ..., k in order, check agent ai, and if ai is equal to any of a1, ..., a(i-1), print "ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <ai>". Finally, if k differs from the number of agents in m, print "ERROR BAD ORDER".
- Action: ignoring the owners of the houses, initially all houses are "remaining". For i=1, 2, ..., k in order, ai receives the remaining house that comes first in its own preference list (not its effective list), and that house is no longer remaining. If no remaining house is on its preference list, ai receives nothing. An agent that received nothing, and an agent whose received house is not on its own effective list, are called "worse off" agents. Print, in this order, "SERIAL <m> assigned=<number of agents that received a house>", "ALLOCATION <allocation notation>" (same form as for TTC), and "WORSE <the worse-off agents in agent order, separated by single spaces>" ("WORSE -" if there are none).

BOSTON m
- Checks: check two-sided market m.
- Action: run the Boston mechanism, and make the result the current matching (source BOSTON). Print "BOSTON <m> applications=<number of applications>" and "MATCHING <pair notation of the result>".

MANIPULATE m a
- Checks: check two-sided market m, and check proposer a.
- Action: let T be a's preference list and t its length. Let B0 be a's partner when the Boston mechanism is run on m as it is, and G0 a's partner when proposer-proposing deferred acceptance is run on it ("no partner" if a has none). Print "MANIPULATE <m> <a> boston=<B0> gs=<G0> swaps=<s>". Here no partner is written "*", and s is t-1 (0 if t=0).
  Then, for i=1, 2, ..., t-1 in order, take the market in which a's preference list is T with its i-th and (i+1)-th elements swapped and the preference lists and capacities of the other agents are unchanged, run the Boston mechanism and proposer-proposing deferred acceptance on it, and let B and G be a's partners in each, respectively. Comparing by rank in a's true preference list T: if a prefers B to B0, print "IMPROVE BOSTON <i> <i-th element of T> <(i+1)-th element of T> result=<B>"; then, if a prefers G to G0, print "IMPROVE GS <i> <i-th element of T> <(i+1)-th element of T> result=<G>".
  If no IMPROVE line was printed at all, print "NO IMPROVEMENT" at the end. This command does not change the market (preference lists and current matching).

STATS m
- Checks: check two-sided market m. Then, if m has no current matching, print "ERROR NO MATCHING".
- Action: let K be the number of pairs in the current matching, and print "STATS <m> source=<source> pairs=<K>". Then print the following 2 lines.
  "PROPOSERS sum=<S> average=<average> truncated=<truncated> first=<F> unmatched=<U>". S is the sum of "the rank of r for p" over all pairs (p, r), F is the number of those pairs whose rank is 1, and U is the number of proposers that have no partner.
  "RECEIVERS sum=<S> average=<average> truncated=<truncated> first=<F> unmatched=<U>". S is the sum of "the rank of p for r" over all pairs (p, r), F is the number of those pairs whose rank is 1, and U is the number of receivers that have no partner at all.
  In both lines, when K is 1 or more, <average> is S/K written as an irreducible fraction "<numerator>/<denominator>" (with "/1" even when the denominator is 1; examples: 7/3, 2/1), and <truncated> is S/K with the digits from the third decimal place on discarded, written to two decimal places as "<integer part>.<2-digit fractional part>" (examples: 2.33 for 7/3, 2.00 for 2/1). When K is 0, <average> and <truncated> are both "-".

[Output format]
- For each command, print only the lines described in [Processing]. Every command prints at least one line.
- Words and values in a line are separated by single spaces as described. Do not put whitespace at the start or end of a line, and do not print empty lines. Every line ends with a newline.
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Names are written exactly as in the input.
- There are the following 16 error lines (replace each part in < > with the corresponding name).
  ERROR UNKNOWN MARKET <m>
  ERROR DUPLICATE MARKET <m>
  ERROR WRONG TYPE <m>
  ERROR UNKNOWN AGENT <a>
  ERROR DUPLICATE AGENT <a>
  ERROR DUPLICATE HOUSE <h>
  ERROR BAD CHOICE <c>
  ERROR DUPLICATE CHOICE <c>
  ERROR NOT PROPOSER <a>
  ERROR NOT RECEIVER <a>
  ERROR OVER CAPACITY <r>
  ERROR NOT ONE TO ONE
  ERROR TOO MANY MATCHINGS
  ERROR INCOMPLETE <a>
  ERROR BAD ORDER
  ERROR NO MATCHING

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500
- Each line is at most 500 characters long. Each name is between 1 and 8 characters long.
- MARKET: the number k of proposer names and the number l of receiver names are each between 1 and 12 inclusive.
- ROOMS: the number k of names is between 2 and 12 inclusive.
- HOUSES: the number k of words is between 1 and 12 inclusive.
- RANDOM: 1 <= n <= 12, 1 <= seed <= 2147483646
- CAPACITY: 1 <= q <= 12
- The number k of c1 to ck in PREF, of pairs in CHECK, and of names in SERIAL is each between 0 and 24 inclusive.
- 48271 * x in RANDOM may exceed 2^31, but is less than 2^53. The absolute value of every other integer that appears during computation is less than 2^31.

In the GS of this example, x prefers A to B and so rejects B, and B uses up its preference list and ends with no partner. In CHECK, y is not on B's preference list, so (B, y) is an unacceptable pair, and y prefers A and C to its partner B, so (A, y) and (C, y) are blocking pairs. In LATTICE, in M1 the successor of A is C (y prefers A to C) and the successor of C is A (x prefers C to A), so eliminating the rotation A-x C-y gives the second stable matching. STATS uses the result of GS, which was the last command to create the current matching (CHECK and LATTICE do not change the current matching). In TTC, u and v point to each other's houses and form a cycle, and the remaining agent w points to its own house.
