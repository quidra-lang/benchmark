[Overview]
Write a calculator program for chemical formulas and chemical equations. The program reads commands from standard input, processes them in the order they are read, and writes the results to standard output. It reads chemical formulas, counts the atoms of each element, and computes molar masses, mass percentages, empirical formulas, degrees of unsaturation, oxidation states and so on. For a chemical equation, it builds a matrix from the per-element atom counts and the charges of the species, finds the null space of that matrix by Gauss-Jordan elimination carried out exactly with rational numbers (fractions), and determines the coefficients. Using the balanced equation, it computes amounts of substance, masses, gas volumes, the limiting reagent, the yield and the reaction enthalpy. It also balances redox equations and half-reactions in acidic or basic aqueous solution, determines empirical formulas from mass percentages or combustion-analysis results, and assembles the formula of a salt from a cation and an anion.

[Input format]
The first line contains only the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command.
Each command line consists of one or more "words" separated by single spaces. There is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. A word consists of one or more ASCII characters with character codes 33 to 126 (printable characters other than space). The first word is called the "command name", and the words after it are called "arguments".

The commands have the following 24 forms.
ELEMENT sym
PARSE f
MASS f
PERCENT f
EMPIRICAL f
DBE f
OXSTATE f
MOLES f q
GRAMS f q
MOLECULAR f q
SALT f1 f2
DERIVE sym1 q1 sym2 q2 ...
COMBUST q1 q2 q3
BALANCE eq
MATRIX eq
CHECK eq
REDOX medium eq
HALF medium eq
STOICH eq : s q
LIMITING eq : q1 q2 ...
YIELD eq : s1 q1 s2 q2
HEAT s h
DELTAH eq
HEATS
Here sym, sym1, sym2 are words giving element symbols; f, f1, f2 are words giving formulas; q, q1, q2, q3 are words giving quantities; s, s1, s2 are words giving species; and h is a word giving an enthalpy value. medium is the word "acidic" or "basic", eq is a sequence of words representing a chemical equation, and ":" is a word consisting only of the single character ":". DERIVE lists one or more pairs of an element symbol and a quantity, and LIMITING lists one or more quantities after ":". How all of these are written is defined in [Processing]. An input command may have a command name that is none of these, a number of arguments that differs from its form, or arguments that are not written correctly (how these are handled is also defined in [Processing]).

[Processing]
== Overall flow ==
Commands are processed one at a time, from top to bottom. Each command either prints one or more lines as defined in the description of that command, or results in an "error". When a command results in an error, only the single line "error: <MESSAGE>" is printed and nothing else. In that case, the program state (the enthalpy-of-formation table described later) does not change. In the descriptions below, the error conditions are listed in the order in which they are checked, and the MESSAGE of the first condition that applies is used.

Below, strings enclosed in double quotes are strings that appear in the output exactly as written. However, a part enclosed in < and >, such as <f> or <n>, is replaced by its value. When an argument name is enclosed (<f>, <sym>, <q>, etc.), it stands for that argument word itself; anything else stands for a value defined in the description.

== Number notation ==
Integers are written in decimal, with a leading "-" for negative numbers. No extra leading zeros are written (except in the fractional parts described below). floor(x) denotes the largest integer less than or equal to x. In addition, the following notations are used.
- "d-decimal notation of an integer h" (h is an integer >= 0): the quotient of h divided by 10^d, then ".", then the remainder of h divided by 10^d written with d digits (padded with leading zeros if it has fewer than d digits). For example, the 3-decimal notation of the integer 249682 is 249.682, the 2-decimal notation of the integer 7 is 0.07, and the 3-decimal notation of the integer 0 is 0.000. Below, a phrase such as "the 3-decimal notation of floor(A / B)" means the 3-decimal notation of the integer obtained by dividing A by B and rounding down.
- "signed integer": a positive integer is written with a leading "+", 0 is written as "0", and a negative integer is written with a leading "-". For example +2, 0, -1.
- "signed rational": a rational number x is represented as a fraction p/q where p and q are coprime and q >= 1. If x is 0, write "0". Otherwise, write "+" if x is positive or "-" if x is negative, followed by |p| if q is 1, or otherwise by |p|, "/", q. For example +8/3, -2, 0, +5/2.
- "enthalpy notation": for an integer h, if h >= 0, write the 2-decimal notation of h; if h is negative, write "-" followed by the 2-decimal notation of |h|. For example, -28583 is written -285.83, 0 is written 0.00, and 5240 is written 52.40.

All masses are handled as integers in units of 0.001 g, and all molar masses as integers in units of 0.001 g/mol.

== Formulas ==
A formula is a string that follows the rules below.
- formula: a body followed by zero or one charge part.
- body: a sequence followed by zero or more pairs of "." and a hydrate part.
- hydrate part: zero or one number, followed by a sequence.
- sequence: one or more items in a row.
- item: an element symbol, or "(" followed by a sequence and ")", or "[" followed by a sequence and "]", any of which is followed by zero or one number.
- element symbol: one uppercase letter (A-Z), or one uppercase letter followed by one lowercase letter (a-z).
- number: a string of one or more digits (0-9) whose first digit is not 0.
- charge part: "^", zero or one number, and one character "+" or "-", in this order.
The string is read from the beginning; an uppercase letter immediately followed by a lowercase letter is always read as one element symbol made of those two characters. Also, wherever digits follow one another, all the consecutive digits are read together as one digit string. For example, Co is one element symbol, and CO is the element symbols C and O. H12 is the element symbol H and the number 12, and H02 is not a formula because the digit string 02 starts with 0. A string that does not follow these rules (including the empty string) is called an "invalid formula".
For example, H2O, Ca(OH)2, [Cu(NH3)4]^2+, CuSO4.5H2O, Na2CO3.H2O, Fe^3+, NO3^-, Hg2^2+, C60 follow the rules (apart from whether their element symbols are unknown), while h2o, H2O), (H2O, (H2O], (), H0, H02, 2H2O, H2O., .H2O, H2O^2, H^+2, Na+, H^+^-, H2O(l), H_2 are invalid formulas.

The "composition" (the number of atoms of each element) and the "charge" of a formula that follows the rules are determined as follows.
- One element symbol represents one atom of that element. The composition of a bracketed sequence is the sum of the compositions of the items inside it. If an item is followed by a number, the composition of that item is multiplied by that number (by 1 if there is no number). The composition of a sequence is the sum of the compositions of its items.
- The composition of a hydrate part is the composition of its sequence multiplied by the leading number (by 1 if there is no number). The composition of the body is the sum of the composition of the first sequence and the compositions of all hydrate parts.
- The charge is 0 if there is no charge part. If there is a charge part, let k be the number in it (k = 1 if there is no number); the charge is +k for "+" and -k for "-".
For example, the composition of CuSO4.5H2O is Cu 1, S 1, O 9, H 10 with charge 0, and the composition of [Cu(NH3)4]^2+ is Cu 1, N 4, H 12 with charge +2. The elements whose atom count in the composition is 1 or more are called the elements "contained" in that formula.
The "molar mass" of a formula is the integer obtained by summing (number of atoms of E) * W(E) over each contained element E (the mass of electrons is ignored).

When a word w is "read as a formula", the following conditions are checked in order.
1. w is an invalid formula: "invalid formula <w>"
2. w contains an unknown element symbol: examine the element symbols appearing in w from left to right, let x be the first unknown element symbol, and the message is "unknown element <x>"
For example, Xx(OH gives "invalid formula Xx(OH" by 1, and Na2Xx2Hg gives "unknown element Xx" by 2. If there is no error, the composition, charge and molar mass of w are determined.

== Name order and Hill order ==
Strings are compared in the following "name order". Compare the character codes (ASCII) one character at a time from the beginning; the string with the smaller code at the first differing character comes first. If one string matches the beginning of the other, the shorter one comes first. For example, the element symbols B, Be, Br, C, Ca, Cl, H are in this order.
Arranging a set of elements in "Hill order" means: if C is contained, C first, then H (only if contained), then the remaining elements in name order. If C is not contained, all the elements (including H) are arranged in name order.
The "Hill notation" of a composition is obtained by arranging the elements contained in the composition in Hill order and, for each element, writing the element symbol, followed by the atom count if the atom count is 2 or more. In the Hill notation including charge, if the charge is not 0, this is further followed by "^", the absolute value of the charge (only when it is 2 or more), and "+" if the charge is positive or "-" if it is negative. For example, the Hill notation of CuSO4.5H2O is CuH10O9S, the Hill notation of C2H5OH is C2H6O, the Hill notation including charge of [Cu(NH3)4]^2+ is CuH12N4^2+, and the Hill notation including charge of NO3^- is NO3^-.

== Quantities and enthalpy values ==
A word giving a quantity (mass, amount of substance, percentage, etc.) must have the following form.
- It consists of an integer part only, or of an integer part, ".", and a fractional part, in this order.
- The integer part is either "0" or a digit string of 1 to 4 digits whose first digit is not 0. The fractional part is a digit string of 1 to 3 digits.
- The value (read as a decimal number) must be greater than 0.
A word not of this form is called an "invalid quantity". The integer obtained by multiplying the value of a valid quantity word by 1000 (its value in units of 0.001) is called the Q of that word. For example, the Q of 36 is 36000, the Q of 0.5 is 500, the Q of 18.015 is 18015, and the Q of 9999.999 is 9999999. 0, 0.000, .5, 5., 05, 00.5, 1.2345, 10000, -1, +1, 1e3, 1/2 are invalid quantities.

A word giving an enthalpy value must consist of zero or one "-", followed by either an integer part only or an integer part, ".", and a fractional part. The integer part is either "0" or a digit string of 1 to 5 digits whose first digit is not 0. The fractional part is a digit string of 1 or 2 digits. The value may be 0. A word not of this form is called an "invalid enthalpy". The integer obtained by multiplying the value of a valid word by 100 (its value in units of 0.01 kJ/mol, negative if there is a "-") is called the H of that word. For example, the H of -285.83 is -28583, the H of 52.4 is 5240, and the H of 0, -0 and -0.00 is 0. +5, --5, -, 1.234, 012 are invalid enthalpies.

== Species ==
The substances appearing in a chemical equation, and the s of HEAT, are called "species". A word giving a species is a formula followed by zero or one of "(s)", "(l)", "(g)", "(aq)", which denote the state. If the end of the word matches one of these four, that part is the "state" and the remaining part is treated as the formula. Otherwise there is no state, and the whole word is treated as the formula.
In the equation of CHECK, a "coefficient" may additionally be written at the beginning of the word. If the remaining part after removing the state starts with a digit, all the consecutive digits are read together as the coefficient, and the part after them is treated as the formula. The coefficient must be a number (a digit string whose first digit is not 0); if it starts with 0, the word is treated as an invalid formula. If there is no leading digit, the coefficient is 1. Coefficients cannot be written anywhere other than in CHECK (a formula does not start with a digit, so, for example, 2H2O inside BALANCE is an invalid formula).
The "name" of a species is the word with the coefficient removed (the formula and the state). The composition, charge and molar mass of a species are those of its formula. A species whose state is "(g)" is called a "gas".
When a word w is "read as a species", the state and the coefficient are removed as described above, and the same conditions as when reading a formula are checked on the remaining formula part. However, the <w> in "invalid formula <w>" is the whole word, including the coefficient and the state. For example, H2O(l) is the species with name H2O(l) consisting of the formula H2O and the state (l), and 2H2O(l) inside CHECK is that species with coefficient 2. H2O(x), (g), H2O(g)(l), 02H2 are treated as invalid formulas; for example, H2O(x) gives "invalid formula H2O(x)".

== Chemical equations ==
Let w_1, w_2, ..., w_m be the sequence of words in the position of eq. This sequence is well-formed when it satisfies all of the following conditions.
- m is odd.
- Every even-numbered word (w_2, w_4, ...) is "+" or "->", and exactly one of them is "->".
- No odd-numbered word (w_1, w_3, ...) is "+" or "->".
"Reading eq as an equation" means the following. If the sequence is not well-formed, it is an error ("malformed equation"). If it is well-formed, the odd-numbered words are read as species from left to right (if there is an error, it is the error of the first word that causes an error). The species to the left of "->" are called "reactants" and the species to the right are called "products". There is at least one reactant and at least one product. The "listed order" of the species is the order of the words (reactants first, then products). Several species may have the same name (they are treated as separate species).

== Composition matrix and basis solutions ==
From a list of species X_1, ..., X_n and a sign sigma_1, ..., sigma_n (+1 or -1) for each species, the "composition matrix" is built as follows.
- The rows are one row for each element contained in any of X_1, ..., X_n, arranged in Hill order, followed by a "charge" row placed last.
- The columns are n columns in the order X_1, ..., X_n.
- The entry in column j of the row for element E is sigma_j * (number of atoms of E in X_j), and the entry in column j of the charge row is sigma_j * (charge of X_j).
When a composition matrix is built from an equation, unless stated otherwise, the species are arranged in listed order, the sign of each reactant is +1, and the sign of each product is -1.

The following procedure (Gauss-Jordan elimination) is applied to the composition matrix (with m rows and n columns). All computations are done exactly with rational numbers. First let r = 0, and then, for j = 1, 2, ..., n in this order, perform the following steps 1 to 5 on column j.
1. Among rows r+1 to m, let row i be the first row whose entry in column j is not 0. If there is no such row, column j becomes a "free column", and proceed to the next column.
2. If i differs from r+1, swap row i and row r+1.
3. Divide every entry of row r+1 by the entry of row r+1 in column j.
4. For each i' other than r+1 (1 <= i' <= m), if the entry t of row i' in column j is not 0, subtract t * (the entry of row r+1 in column j') from the entry of row i' in each column j' (1 <= j' <= n).
5. Column j becomes the (r+1)-th "pivot column", and r is increased by 1.
The final r is called the "rank", and n - r is called the "nullity". There are n - r free columns.
For each free column, a "basis solution" is built as follows. Let the free column be column f, and define n rational numbers v_1, ..., v_n by: v_f = 1; v_g = 0 for every free column g other than f; and v_{p_k} = -(the entry in row k, column f of the matrix after the procedure) for the k-th pivot column p_k (k = 1, ..., r). Next, multiply every v_j by the least common multiple of all the denominators (positive integers) of v_1, ..., v_n written as irreducible fractions, to make them integers, and then divide all of them by the greatest common divisor of the absolute values of those integers. The resulting sequence of n integers is the basis solution for column f (the integer for column f is positive). For a basis solution v, for each row of the composition matrix, the sum of the products of the entry in column j and v_j is 0.

== Coefficient determination ==
For a list of species X_1, ..., X_n, their signs, and an integer k (1 <= k <= n), "coefficient determination" is performed as follows. Build the composition matrix and find its basis solutions.
- If the nullity is 2 or more, the result is the string "AMBIGUOUS (nullity <d>)" (<d> is the nullity).
- If the nullity is 0, the result is the string "IMPOSSIBLE".
- If the nullity is 1, let v_1, ..., v_n be the only basis solution. If v_1 is negative, change the sign of every v_j. After that, if any of v_1, ..., v_k is 0 or less, the result is the string "IMPOSSIBLE". Otherwise, the result is the "coefficients" v_1, ..., v_n (the coefficient of X_j is v_j).
When determining the coefficients of an equation (BALANCE, STOICH, LIMITING, YIELD, DELTAH), the composition matrix built from the equation is used, with k = n.
The "notation" of a balanced equation is the reactant terms in listed order joined by " + ", then " -> ", then the product terms in listed order joined by " + ". The term for a species with coefficient c is just its name if c is 1, and otherwise c followed by its name. For example, the coefficients of H2 + O2 -> H2O are 2, 1, 2, and its notation is "2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O".

== Ratio report ==
In DERIVE and COMBUST, the following "ratio report" is made from some elements and an amount A(E) for each element E (a positive integer proportional to the mass of E).
Let F be one of the elements for which A(E) / W(E) (a rational number, proportional to the amount of substance of the element) is smallest, and for each element E let rho(E) = (A(E) * W(F)) / (W(E) * A(F)) (a rational number) (rho is the same whichever such element is chosen as F, and rho(E) >= 1).
1. For each element, in Hill order, print "<E> <r>". <r> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((1000 * A(E) * W(F)) / (W(E) * A(F))).
2. Check the following for k = 1, 2, ..., 12 in this order. For each element E, check whether there is an integer i satisfying |k * rho(E) - i| <= 1/20 (if there is one, there is only one; call it i(E)). If such an integer exists for every element, print "<H> (k = <k>)" and stop. <H> is the Hill notation of the composition in which the atom count of each element E is i(E). If none is found after checking up to k = 12, print "no simple ratio".
All comparisons are done exactly with rational numbers (for example, |k * rho(E) - i| <= 1/20 is equivalent to 20 * |k * A(E) * W(F) - i * W(E) * A(F)| <= W(E) * A(F)).

== Enthalpy-of-formation table ==
The program has one table of pairs, each consisting of a species name and its enthalpy of formation (an integer in units of 0.01 kJ/mol). The table never contains two or more pairs with the same name. The table is initially empty, and only HEAT changes it.

== Command checks ==
For each command, the following conditions are first checked in order. <cmd> is the command name.
1. The command name is none of the 24 command names: "unknown command <cmd>" (uppercase and lowercase are distinguished; for example, balance is not a command name)
2. The number of arguments is not as follows: "wrong argument count for <cmd>"
  - ELEMENT, PARSE, MASS, PERCENT, EMPIRICAL, DBE, OXSTATE: exactly 1
  - MOLES, GRAMS, MOLECULAR, SALT, HEAT: exactly 2
  - COMBUST: exactly 3
  - HEATS: 0
  - DERIVE: an even number that is 2 or more
  - BALANCE, MATRIX, CHECK, DELTAH: 1 or more
  - REDOX, HALF: 2 or more
  - STOICH, LIMITING, YIELD: not checked here (checked as described for each command).
Then the command is processed as described for it. Below, "the quantity check of q" means that if q is an invalid quantity, it is an error ("invalid quantity <q>").

== Processing of each command ==
ELEMENT sym
If sym is not an element symbol in the element table, it is an error ("unknown element <sym>"). Otherwise, print "<sym> (Z = <z>) <w> u". <z> is the atomic number, and <w> is the 3-decimal notation of W(sym). For example, the output of ELEMENT Fe is "Fe (Z = 26) 55.845 u".

PARSE f
Read f as a formula. Print "<H> (<list>; charge <c>)". <H> is the Hill notation including charge of f; <list> is the elements contained in f arranged in Hill order, each written as the element symbol, one space, and the atom count, joined by ", " (a comma and one space); and <c> is the charge of f as a signed integer. For example, the output of PARSE [Cu(NH3)4]^2+ is "CuH12N4^2+ (Cu 1, H 12, N 4; charge +2)".

MASS f
Read f as a formula. Print "M(<f>) = <m> g/mol". <m> is the 3-decimal notation of the molar mass of f.

PERCENT f
Read f as a formula. Let M be the molar mass of f, and for each element E contained in f, in Hill order, print "<E> <p>%". <p> is the 2-decimal notation of floor((10000 * n * W(E)) / M) (n is the number of atoms of E).

EMPIRICAL f
Read f as a formula. Let g be the greatest common divisor of all the atom counts of the elements contained in f, and print "<H> (n = <g>)". <H> is the Hill notation (without charge) of the composition in which the atom count of each element is divided by g. The charge of f is not used.

DBE f
Read f as a formula. Then check the following conditions in order.
1. The charge of f is not 0: "charged formula <f>"
2. Examine the elements contained in f in Hill order; if there is an element that is not in the valence table below, let E be the first such element, and the message is "no valence for <E>"
Valence table: H, F, Cl, Br, I have valence 1; O, S, Se have valence 2; B, N, P, As have valence 3; C, Si, Ge have valence 4.
If there is no error, let t be the integer obtained by summing (atom count) * (valence - 2) over each element contained in f and then adding 2 (the degree of unsaturation is t / 2). Print "DBE = <x>". If t >= 0, <x> is floor(t / 2), followed by ".", followed by "5" if t is odd or "0" if t is even. If t is negative, <x> is "-" followed by the same notation written for |t|. For example, C6H6 has t = 8 and gives "DBE = 4.0", C2H3N has t = 4 and gives "DBE = 2.0", CH3 has t = 1 and gives "DBE = 0.5", and H3 has t = -1 and gives "DBE = -0.5".

OXSTATE f
Read f as a formula. Let E_1, ..., E_m be the elements contained in f in Hill order, let n(E) be the number of atoms of element E, and let q be the charge of f. The oxidation state x(E) (a rational number) of each element is determined as follows.
- When m = 1: x(E_1) = q / n(E_1).
- When m >= 2: first, perform the following steps (a) to (e) in this order to "assign" values to some of the elements.
  (a) If f contains F, assign -1 to F.
  (b) Li, Na, K, Rb, Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Al are called "fixed metals". To each fixed metal contained in f, in Hill order, assign +1 if it is Li, Na, K or Rb; +2 if it is Be, Mg, Ca or Sr; and +3 if it is Al.
  (c) If f contains H, assign to H -1 if all the elements of f other than H are fixed metals, and +1 otherwise.
  (d) If f contains O, assign -2 to O.
  (e) If f contains neither O nor F, examine Cl, Br, I in this order and assign -1 to the first one contained in f (if none of them is contained, do nothing).
  The elements to which no value was assigned are called "undetermined elements", and let S be the sum of n(E) * (the value assigned to E) over the elements E to which a value was assigned.
  - If there are 2 or more undetermined elements, print "indeterminate: <list>" and stop. <list> is the element symbols of the undetermined elements in Hill order, separated by single spaces.
  - If there is exactly 1 undetermined element, call it U; the elements other than U take their assigned values as their oxidation states, and x(U) = (q - S) / n(U).
  - If there are no undetermined elements and S = q, the assigned values are used as the oxidation states as they are.
  - If there are no undetermined elements and S != q, let L be the element to which a value was assigned last; the elements other than L take their assigned values as their oxidation states, and, with a the value assigned to L, x(L) = (q - (S - n(L) * a)) / n(L).
Finally, print one line consisting of, for each element, the element symbol, one space, and x(E) as a signed rational, joined by ", " in Hill order.
For example, Fe3O4 gives "Fe +8/3, O -2"; H2O2 gives "H +1, O -1" (assigning +1 to H and -2 to O gives S = -2 != 0, so for O, which was assigned last, x(O) = (0 - 2) / 2); OF2 gives "F -1, O +2"; NaH gives "H -1, Na +1"; ICl gives "Cl -1, I +1"; S4O6^2- gives "O -2, S +5/2"; O3 gives "O 0"; and KCN gives "indeterminate: C N".

MOLES f q
Read f as a formula, and perform the quantity check of q. With M the molar mass of f, print "<a> g <f> = <n> mol". <a> is the 3-decimal notation of Q, and <n> is the 6-decimal notation of floor((Q * 1000000) / M) (q is the mass of f).

GRAMS f q
Read f as a formula, and perform the quantity check of q. With M the molar mass of f, print "<a> mol <f> = <m> g". <a> is the 3-decimal notation of Q, and <m> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q * M) / 1000) (q is the amount of substance of f).

MOLECULAR f q
Read f as a formula; if the charge of f is not 0, it is an error ("charged formula <f>"). Then perform the quantity check of q. f represents an empirical formula, and q represents the approximate molar mass of the molecule. Let M be the molar mass of f, and let k = floor((2 * Q + M) / (2 * M)) (the integer nearest to Q / M). If k = 0 or 10 * |Q - k * M| > M, print "no integer multiple (ratio <r>)". <r> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((1000 * Q) / M). Otherwise, print "<H> (n = <k>, <m> g/mol)". <H> is the Hill notation of the composition in which the atom count of each element of f is multiplied by k, and <m> is the 3-decimal notation of k * M.

SALT f1 f2
Read f1 and then f2 as formulas, and then check the following conditions in order.
1. The charge of f1 is 0 or less: "not a cation <f1>"
2. The charge of f2 is 0 or more: "not an anion <f2>"
3. f1 contains ".": "hydrate ion <f1>"
4. f2 contains ".": "hydrate ion <f2>"
If there is no error, let p be the charge of f1, r the absolute value of the charge of f2, g the greatest common divisor of p and r, a = r / g, and b = p / g (combining a cations f1 and b anions f2 gives charge 0). The part of a formula before "^" is called the "core" of that formula. The notation P(f, k) for the part made of k copies of a formula f is determined as follows.
- If k = 1, it is the core of f itself.
- If k >= 2 and either the core of f consists of a single element symbol only, or the core of f starts with "[" and the "]" matching that "[" is the last character of the core, it is the core followed by k.
- Otherwise, it is "(", the core, ")", and k, written in this order.
Let the composition of the salt be the sum of (the composition of f1 multiplied by a) and (the composition of f2 multiplied by b), and let M be its molar mass. Print "<P1><P2> (<H>, <m> g/mol)". <P1> is P(f1, a), <P2> is P(f2, b), <H> is the Hill notation of the composition of the salt, and <m> is the 3-decimal notation of M. For example, the output of SALT Al^3+ SO4^2- is "Al2(SO4)3 (Al2O12S3, 342.147 g/mol)", and the output of SALT K^+ [Fe(CN)6]^3- is "K3[Fe(CN)6] (C6FeK3N6, 329.247 g/mol)".

DERIVE sym1 q1 sym2 q2 ...
Group the arguments into pairs, two at a time from the front, and for each pair (an element symbol sym and a quantity q), starting from the leftmost pair, check the following conditions in order. q is the mass percentage of element sym.
1. sym is not in the element table: "unknown element <sym>"
2. sym is the same as the element symbol of an earlier pair: "duplicate element <sym>"
3. q is an invalid quantity: "invalid quantity <q>"
4. The Q of q is greater than 100000 (the percentage exceeds 100): "invalid percentage <q>"
After all pairs have been checked, let T be the sum of the Q of all pairs; if |T - 100000| > 1000 (if the sum of the percentages is more than 1 away from 100), it is an error ("percentages sum to <t>", where <t> is the 3-decimal notation of T). If there is no error, make the ratio report, with the amount A(sym) of the element sym of each pair being the Q of that pair.

COMBUST q1 q2 q3
Perform the quantity check on q1, q2, q3 in this order. q1 is the mass of the burned sample (a compound that contains C and H and may contain O), q2 is the mass of the CO2 produced, and q3 is the mass of the H2O produced. With S, P, R their respective Q values, let
C = floor((P * 12011) / 44009), H = floor((R * 2016) / 18015), O = S - C - H
(12011 is W(C), 44009 is the molar mass of CO2, 2016 is twice W(H), and 18015 is the molar mass of H2O; O may be negative). If C = 0, or H = 0, or 100 * O < -S, it is an error ("inconsistent masses"). If there is no error, the elements are the three elements C, H, O if 100 * O > S, and otherwise (if the absolute value of the mass of O is at most 1% of the mass of the sample) the two elements C and H. For each element, in the order C, H, O, print "mass <E> <m> g" (<m> is the 3-decimal notation of the value C, H or O for that element), and then make the ratio report, with the amount A of each element being that value.

BALANCE eq
Read eq as an equation, and perform coefficient determination. If the result is coefficients, print the notation of the balanced equation; otherwise, print the result string ("IMPOSSIBLE" or "AMBIGUOUS (nullity <d>)").

MATRIX eq
Read eq as an equation, build the composition matrix, apply Gauss-Jordan elimination, and find the basis solutions. Print the following lines in order.
1. One line: "columns: " followed by the names of the species in listed order, separated by single spaces
2. For each row of the composition matrix (before Gauss-Jordan elimination is applied), from top to bottom, one line: "<L>: " followed by the n entries (integers) of that row, separated by single spaces. <L> is the element symbol for an element row, and "charge" for the charge row.
3. One line "rank <r>, nullity <d>" (<r> is the rank, and <d> is the nullity)
4. For each basis solution, in increasing order of the number of its free column, one line: "null: " followed by the n integers of the basis solution, separated by single spaces (if the nullity is 0, there are no such lines)

CHECK eq
Read eq as an equation. Here, a coefficient may be written at the beginning of each species word. Let c(X) be the coefficient of species X. For each row of the composition matrix built from the equation (the elements in Hill order, then charge last), the "left-hand amount" is the sum, over each reactant X, of c(X) * (the number of atoms of that element in X; for the charge row, the charge of X), and the "right-hand amount" is the sum computed in the same way over each product.
If there are one or more rows in which the left-hand amount and the right-hand amount differ, print "not balanced", and then, for each differing row, in the row order above, print "<L>: <l> != <r>". <L> is the element symbol or "charge", and <l> and <r> are the left-hand amount and the right-hand amount, written as integers for element rows and as signed integers for the charge row.
If they are equal in every row, let g be the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of all species, and print "balanced" if g = 1, or "balanced, not in lowest terms (gcd <g>)" if g >= 2.

REDOX medium eq
HALF medium eq
If medium is neither "acidic" nor "basic", it is an error ("invalid medium <medium>"). The arguments after medium are taken as eq and read as an equation. Then the species are examined in listed order; if there is a species whose name without the state (that is, its formula) is one of "H^+", "OH^-", "H2O", let x be the name of the first such species, and it is an error ("reserved species <x>").
If there is no error, build the list consisting of the species of the equation (in listed order; sign +1 for reactants and -1 for products) followed by the following "auxiliary species", in this order, with sign +1, and perform coefficient determination with k equal to the number of species in the equation.
- If medium is acidic, the species named "H^+" (composition H 1, charge +1); if basic, the species named "OH^-" (composition O 1, H 1, charge -1)
- The species named "H2O" (composition H 2, O 1, charge 0)
- For HALF only, additionally the species named "e^-" (the electron; it contains no element, and its charge is -1)
If the result is not coefficients, print the result string and stop. If the result is coefficients, print one line written in the same way as the notation of the balanced equation, except that the auxiliary species are examined in this order and, with c the coefficient of each, a term with coefficient c is appended at the end of the reactant terms if c is positive, a term with coefficient -c is appended at the end of the product terms if c is negative, and nothing is appended if c is 0.
For HALF, then print "reduction" if the coefficient of e^- is positive, "oxidation" if it is negative, and "no electron transfer" if it is 0.
For example, the output of REDOX acidic MnO4^- + Fe^2+ -> Mn^2+ + Fe^3+ is "MnO4^- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H^+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O", and the output of HALF basic MnO4^- -> MnO2 is the two lines "MnO4^- + 2H2O + 3e^- -> MnO2 + 4OH^-" and "reduction".

STOICH eq : s q
Check the following conditions in order.
1. The number of arguments that are the word ":" is not exactly 1: "invalid separator"
2. The number of arguments after ":" is not exactly 2: "wrong argument count for STOICH"
Let eq be the arguments before ":", and let s and q be the 2 arguments after ":".
3. Read eq as an equation (its errors).
4. s does not match the name of any species of eq: "species <s> not in equation"
5. The quantity check of q
If there is no error, perform coefficient determination; if the result is not coefficients, print the result string and stop. If the result is coefficients, print the notation of the balanced equation, and then, for each species X in listed order, print "<X> <n> mol <m> g". If X is a gas, append " <V> L" after it. Here, let Y be the first species in listed order whose name matches s (q is the mass of Y), c_Y the coefficient of Y, M_Y the molar mass of Y, D = c_Y * M_Y, c the coefficient of X, and M_X the molar mass of X. <X> is the name of X, <n> is the 6-decimal notation of floor((Q * c * 1000000) / D), <m> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q * c * M_X) / D), and <V> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q * c * 22414) / D) (the volume of a gas is taken to be 22.414 L per 1 mol).

LIMITING eq : q1 q2 ...
Check the following conditions in order.
1. The number of arguments that are the word ":" is not exactly 1: "invalid separator"
2. Take the arguments before ":" as eq and read it as an equation (its errors).
3. The number of arguments after ":" differs from the number of reactants R: "expected <R> masses, got <k>" (<k> is the number of arguments after ":")
4. Perform the quantity check on the arguments after ":", from left to right.
The i-th quantity after ":" is the mass of the i-th reactant in listed order. If there is no error, perform coefficient determination; if the result is not coefficients, print the result string and stop.
If the result is coefficients, let Q_i, c_i, M_i be the Q, the coefficient and the molar mass of reactant i (i = 1, ..., R), and let the "limiting reagent" L be the reactant with the smallest Q_i / (c_i * M_i) (if several are smallest, the first one in listed order). The comparison is done with integers: Q_i / (c_i * M_i) < Q_j / (c_j * M_j) is equivalent to Q_i * c_j * M_j < Q_j * c_i * M_i. Let D = c_L * M_L, and print the following lines in order.
1. The notation of the balanced equation
2. "limiting <X>" (<X> is the name of L)
3. "extent <e> mol" (<e> is the 6-decimal notation of floor((Q_L * 1000000) / D))
4. For each product X, in listed order, "product <X> <m> g". With c the coefficient of X and M_X its molar mass, <m> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q_L * c * M_X) / D). If X is a gas, append " <V> L" after it (<V> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q_L * c * 22414) / D)).
5. For each reactant i other than L, in listed order, "remaining <X> <m> g". <X> is the name of reactant i, and <m> is the 3-decimal notation of floor((Q_i * D - Q_L * c_i * M_i) / D) (the mass left after the reaction).

YIELD eq : s1 q1 s2 q2
Check the following conditions in order.
1. The number of arguments that are the word ":" is not exactly 1: "invalid separator"
2. The number of arguments after ":" is not exactly 4: "wrong argument count for YIELD"
Let eq be the arguments before ":", and let s1, q1, s2, q2 be the 4 arguments after ":".
3. Read eq as an equation (its errors).
4. s1 does not match the name of any reactant: "<s1> is not a reactant"
5. s2 does not match the name of any product: "<s2> is not a product"
6. The quantity check of q1, then the quantity check of q2 (in this order)
Let A be the first reactant in listed order whose name matches s1, and B the first product in listed order whose name matches s2. q1 is the mass of A, and q2 is the mass of B actually obtained. If there is no error, perform coefficient determination; if the result is not coefficients, print the result string and stop.
If the result is coefficients, let c_A and c_B be the coefficients of A and B, M_A and M_B their molar masses, Q1 and Q2 the Q of q1 and q2, and let the theoretical yield be T = floor((Q1 * c_B * M_B) / (c_A * M_A)). Print the following lines in order.
1. The notation of the balanced equation
2. "theoretical <t> g" (<t> is the 3-decimal notation of T)
3. "actual <a> g" (<a> is the 3-decimal notation of Q2)
4. "yield undefined" if T = 0, otherwise "yield <y>%" (<y> is the 2-decimal notation of floor((Q2 * 10000) / T))

HEAT s h
Read s as a species (no coefficient may be written). Then, if h is an invalid enthalpy, it is an error ("invalid enthalpy <h>"). If there is no error, print "dHf(<s>) = <v> kJ/mol". <v> is the enthalpy notation of the H of h. If the enthalpy-of-formation table already has a pair with name s, " (was <w>)" is further appended at the end of this line (<w> is the enthalpy notation of the value of that pair). After that, set the value of the pair with name s in the table to H (if there is no such pair, add a new one).

DELTAH eq
Read eq as an equation. Then examine the species in listed order; if there is a species whose name is not in the enthalpy-of-formation table, let x be the name of the first such species, and it is an error ("no enthalpy for <x>"). If there is no error, perform coefficient determination; if the result is not coefficients, print the result string and stop. If the result is coefficients, print the notation of the balanced equation, and then print "dH = <v> kJ (<kind>)". With c the coefficient of each species X and h(X) its value in the table, let DeltaH = (the sum of c * h(X) over the products) - (the sum of c * h(X) over the reactants); <v> is the enthalpy notation of DeltaH, and <kind> is "exothermic" if DeltaH is negative, "endothermic" if it is positive, and "thermoneutral" if it is 0.

HEATS
If the enthalpy-of-formation table is empty, print "no enthalpies". Otherwise, for each pair in the table, in name order, print "<x> <v> kJ/mol" (<x> is the name, and <v> is the enthalpy notation of the value).

[Output format]
The output consists only of the lines defined in [Processing], printed in command order. When one command prints several lines, they are printed in the order described in [Processing]. A command that does not result in an error prints one or more lines, and a command that results in an error prints only the single line "error: <MESSAGE>".
All output consists of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No whitespace is added at the end of a line, and no empty lines are printed. Unless otherwise specified, items within a line are separated by a single space.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500. Each line from the second line onward is at most 100 characters long.
- Every digit string appearing in a word in a position where a formula or a species is to be written (including invalid words) has at most 3 digits.
- For the formulas and species read without error, the atom count of each element in the composition is at most 1000, and the molar mass is at most 2000000 (at most 2000 g/mol).
- An equation read without error has at most 10 species, and they contain at most 10 distinct elements.
- Every rational number appearing in coefficient determination and in the computation of MATRIX (the matrix entries at every stage of Gauss-Jordan elimination, the intermediate values of that computation, the v_j when building basis solutions, the least common multiple of the denominators, and the values made into integers), when written as a fraction p/q where p and q are coprime and q >= 1, satisfies |p| < 2^26 and q < 2^26.
- In STOICH, LIMITING, YIELD and DELTAH, when the result of coefficient determination is coefficients, every coefficient is at most 100 (in BALANCE and other commands, coefficients may be larger than this).
- The Q of a quantity is at most 9999999, and the absolute value of the H of an enthalpy value is at most 9999999. Therefore, when the formulas in [Processing] (A and B in floor(A / B), the products used in comparisons, DeltaH, etc.) are computed with integers exactly as written, the absolute value of every integer that appears along the way is less than 2^53.
