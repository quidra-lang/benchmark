[Overview]
Write a program that serves as a workbench for building and improving delivery routes. The program reads a sequence of commands from standard input, executes them one at a time from the first, and writes the results to standard output. One depot and several delivery stops are registered at lattice points on a plane, and the distance between locations is measured by the Manhattan distance (moving a distance of 1 takes 1 minute). For a tour in which one vehicle visits all stops, the program covers construction by the nearest-neighbour method, the cheapest-insertion method and an exact solution (10 stops or fewer); improvement by the 2-opt method, the Or-opt method and the swap method; a simulated run that checks whether time windows (ranges of accepted times) can be kept; and comparison with lower bounds such as the minimum spanning tree. For a delivery plan with several vehicles of limited capacity, the program covers construction by the Clarke-Wright savings method (parallel version), by splitting the tour, and by the sweep method; and improvement between routes by moving, by exchanging and by swapping tail parts, and within a route by the 2-opt method. The search order and the choice in every tie are all specified, so the output is uniquely determined.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. Each line consists of a command name and zero or more arguments separated by single spaces; there is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no blank lines. There are 25 commands, with the following 28 forms (STOP, TIMECHECK and VRP each have two forms). The parts other than the command name are argument names.

  DEPOT x y
  START time
  STOP id x y demand service
  STOP id x y demand service open close
  REMOVE id
  LIST
  MATRIX
  ORDER id1 id2 ... idk
  NEAREST
  INSERTION
  EXACT
  TOUR
  TWOOPT
  OROPT
  SWAP
  TIMECHECK
  TIMECHECK r
  BOUND
  VRP q
  VRP q limit
  SPLIT q
  SWEEP q
  ROUTES
  RELOCATE
  EXCHANGE
  CROSS
  ROUTEOPT
  REPORT

The arguments are written as follows.
- id and id1 to idk are stop numbers, integers from 1 to 999 inclusive. The k of ORDER is a symbol for the number of stop numbers on that line; it is not written in the input.
- x and y are coordinates, integers from -500 to 500 inclusive.
- demand is the demand, an integer from 0 to 1000 inclusive. service is the service time (in minutes), an integer from 0 to 240 inclusive.
- time, open and close are times, 5 characters of the form "HH:MM". HH is a 2-digit number from 00 to 23 inclusive, and MM is a 2-digit number from 00 to 59 inclusive.
- r of TIMECHECK is a route number, an integer from 1 to 99 inclusive.
- q is the vehicle capacity, an integer from 1 to 10000 inclusive. limit is the upper limit on the length of one route, an integer from 1 to 100000 inclusive.
All integers are written in decimal; negative numbers are written with a leading "-", and nonzero numbers have no leading zeros. It is guaranteed that every line follows these formats and the [Constraints].

[Processing]
Process the commands one at a time in input order, writing each command's output immediately. Below, a string enclosed in double quotes is a string that appears in the output exactly as written. However, a part enclosed in < and >, such as <id> or <L>, is replaced by its value. When the enclosed name is an argument name of that command (<id> in the description of STOP, <q> in the description of VRP, and so on), it is that argument's value; otherwise it is a value defined in the description. In the headings below, a part enclosed in [ ] denotes arguments that may be absent.

When a command's description has "Checks", test the error conditions listed there in the order listed, output only the line of the first error that applies, and do nothing else for that command (change no state at all). If no condition applies, do what is written under "Action".

## State
The program has the following state.
- The depot. A pair of coordinates; initially "unset".
- The departure time. Initially 08:00.
- The set of stops. Initially empty. A stop has a number, coordinates, a demand, a service time and a time window. A time window is either "none" or a pair of an opening time and a closing time. No two stops with the same number exist at the same time.
- The current tour. Initially "none". A tour is a sequence of stops that contains every stop existing at that time exactly once. The vehicle leaves the depot, visits the stops in sequence order and returns to the depot.
- The current plan. Initially "none". A plan consists of a vehicle capacity Q and one or more routes. A route is a sequence of one or more stops; the vehicle leaves the depot, visits the stops in sequence order and returns to the depot. Every stop existing at that time is contained exactly once in exactly one of the routes.

## Distance, length, and sequence notation
Below, the depot is written D, and the depot and the stops together are called "locations". When two locations have coordinates (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), their distance d is |x1 - x2| + |y1 - y2|. Different locations may have the same coordinates, and the distance between them is 0.
The length of a sequence of stops P1, P2, ..., Pm is the total distance travelled when moving D -> P1 -> P2 -> ... -> Pm -> D, that is, d(D, P1) + d(P1, P2) + ... + d(Pm-1, Pm) + d(Pm, D) (it is 0 when m = 0). The length of a tour and the length of a route mean this. The length of a plan is the sum of the lengths of all its routes.
A subscript written as an expression (e.g. Pi+1, Pj-1) denotes the element whose index is the value of that expression (Pi+1 is the (i+1)-th element of P). When dealing with positions in a sequence, both P0 and Pm+1 are regarded as D. Also, the sum of the demands of the stops in a sequence is called the "load" of that sequence (the load of a sequence that contains no stops is 0).
The notation of a sequence is the string formed by joining D, P1, P2, ..., Pm, D in this order with "-" (e.g. D-3-1-2-D). A stop is written by its number. Below, whenever a location is written as a name, the depot is written "D" and a stop is written by its number.

## Time notation
A time is handled as the number of minutes (an integer) elapsed since 0:00. "HH:MM" in the input represents HH * 60 + MM minutes. To output an elapsed minute count t, take the quotient h and remainder m of t divided by 60, and write h, ":", m in this order. h is written in decimal, and only when it has 1 digit is a single 0 prepended to make it 2 digits. h may be 24 or more, and is then written as is (e.g. 1505 minutes is "25:05", 480 minutes is "08:00"). m is always 2 digits, with a leading 0 when it has 1 digit. Below, "time notation" means this form.

## Simulated run
For a sequence of stops P1, ..., Pm (m >= 1), the run that leaves the depot at the departure time and visits the stops in sequence order is computed as follows. Let the time t be the departure time, and for i = 1, 2, ..., m in this order compute:
- Arrival time a = t + d(Pi-1, Pi).
- Waiting time w. If Pi has a time window and a is before the opening time, it is opening time - a. Otherwise it is 0.
- Start time b = a + w.
- Lateness e. If Pi has a time window and a is after the closing time, it is a - closing time. Otherwise it is 0. A stop with e > 0 is called a "violation".
- The time of leaving that stop f = b + the service time of Pi. For the computation of the next stop, set t to this f.
The return time is the time obtained by adding d(Pm, D) to t after the computation for Pm is finished. Also compute the travel time (equal to the length of the sequence), the total waiting time, the total service time, the number of violations and the total lateness.

The "result lines" of a simulated run consist of the following lines.
- For each stop, in sequence order, "<p> arrive <A> wait <w> begin <B> leave <F> <R>". <p> is the stop number, <A>, <B> and <F> are the time notation of the arrival time, start time and leaving time, and <w> is the waiting time. <R> is "late <e>" (<e> is the lateness) if the stop is a violation, and "ok" otherwise.
- Finally "return <T>, travel <L>, wait <W>, service <S>, violations <V>, lateness <E>". <T> is the time notation of the return time, <L> is the travel time, <W> is the total waiting time, <S> is the total service time, <V> is the number of violations, and <E> is the total lateness.

## Plan normalization and route lines
Normalizing a plan means performing the following operations in this order.
1. Remove every route that contains no stops.
2. For each route, if the number of its first stop is greater than the number of its last stop, reverse the order of that route.
3. Sort the routes in increasing order of the minimum stop number contained in each route.
The routes are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in this order. Below, route r means the route with number r, and the number of routes is written k. A plan is normalized when it is created and when it is changed, at the points stated in each command's description. Therefore the plan before executing a command is always normalized.

The "route lines" of a plan are the lines "route <r>: <S> load <W> length <L>" for r = 1, 2, ..., k in this order. <S> is the notation of the sequence of route r, <W> is its load, and <L> is its length.

## About the improvement procedures
The improvement commands below (TWOOPT, OROPT, SWAP, RELOCATE, EXCHANGE, CROSS, ROUTEOPT) all proceed in the following form. Examining candidate moves one at a time in a specified order is called a "scan". When a candidate with a positive improvement g (g > 0) is found during a scan, perform the move of the first such candidate found, and restart the scan from the beginning. When a scan runs to the end without any candidate with g > 0, finish. In every command g equals the decrease in length caused by the move (for a plan, the length of the plan), and the length decreases with every move, so this repetition always terminates.

## DEPOT x y
Action: Set the depot to (x, y). If it is already set, change its position. The current tour and the current plan are kept as they are, and from then on lengths and so on are computed with the new depot position. Output "depot at (<x>,<y>)".

## START time
Action: Set the departure time to time. Output "start <T>". <T> is the time notation of the new departure time.

## STOP id x y demand service [open close]
Checks:
1. A stop with number id already exists: "error: stop <id> already exists"
2. open and close are given and open is a later time than close: "error: invalid window"
Action: Add a stop with number id, coordinates (x, y), demand demand and service time service. Its time window is the pair of opening time open and closing time close if open and close are given, and "none" if they are not given. open and close may be equal. The current tour and the current plan both become "none". Output "stop <id> added".

## REMOVE id
Checks:
1. No stop with number id exists: "error: unknown stop <id>"
Action: Remove stop id. If no stops remain, the current tour and the current plan both become "none". If stops remain: if there is a current tour, remove id from it (without changing the order of the other stops); if there is a current plan, remove id from the route containing id (without changing the order of the other stops) and normalize the plan. The capacity does not change. Output "stop <id> removed".

## LIST
Action: Output the following lines.
- If the depot is set, "depot (<x>,<y>)" (<x> and <y> are the depot coordinates); if it is unset, "depot none".
- "start <T>". <T> is the time notation of the departure time.
- "stops <n>". <n> is the number of stops.
- For each stop, in increasing order of number, "<p> at (<x>,<y>) demand <d> service <s> window <W>". <p> is the number, <x> and <y> are the coordinates, <d> is the demand and <s> is the service time. <W> is, if there is a time window, the time notation of the opening time, "-", and the time notation of the closing time joined in this order (e.g. 09:00-11:30), and "none" if there is none.

## MATRIX
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
Action: Output a table of distances between locations. Let the "header list" be "D" followed by the stop numbers in increasing order (just "D" if there are no stops). The first line of the table is an empty cell followed by a cell for each name in the header list. Then, for each location p of the header list, in that order, output one line consisting of a cell with the name of p followed by cells with the distances from p to each location in header-list order. Let W be the maximum number of characters over all cells (excluding the empty cell); each cell is padded on the left with spaces to W characters (right-aligned; the empty cell is W spaces). The cells in a line are joined with single spaces between them.

## ORDER id1 id2 ... idk
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
3. Among id1 to idk there is a number of a stop that does not exist: "error: unknown stop <p>". <p> is the leftmost such number.
4. Among id1 to idk the same number appears two or more times: "error: duplicate stop <p>". <p> is, among the numbers for which the same number is written further to the left, the leftmost one.
5. k differs from the number of stops n: "error: order has <k> stops, expected <n>"
Action: Set the current tour to the sequence id1, id2, ..., idk. Output "order: <S> length <L>". <S> is the notation of the tour's sequence and <L> is its length.

## NEAREST
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
Action: Build a tour by the nearest-neighbour method. Let the current location be D and start from an empty sequence. While some stop is not in the sequence, append the one among them with the smallest distance from the current location (if there are several, the one with the smallest number) to the end of the sequence, and make that stop the current location. Make the resulting sequence the current tour and output "nearest: <S> length <L>". <S> is the notation of the tour's sequence and <L> is its length.

## INSERTION
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
Action: Build a tour by the cheapest-insertion method. Start from an empty sequence and repeat the following until all stops are in the sequence. Let the current sequence be P1, ..., Pm; for every pair of a stop c not in the sequence and an insertion position j (0 <= j <= m), compute the increase d(Pj, c) + d(c, Pj+1) - d(Pj, Pj+1) when c is inserted between Pj and Pj+1. Choose the pair with the smallest increase (if there are several, the one with the smallest number of c; if still several, the one with the smallest j), and insert c between Pj and Pj+1. Make the resulting sequence the current tour and output "insertion: <S> length <L>". <S> and <L> are the same as for NEAREST.

## EXACT
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
3. There are 11 or more stops: "error: too many stops for exact (<n>)". <n> is the number of stops.
Action: Find the tour with the smallest length among all tours. If several have the smallest length, choose the one that is smallest in lexicographic order as a sequence of stop numbers (compare the numbers as numbers from the front, and the one with the smaller number at the first differing position is smaller). The method is free (for example, the Held-Karp dynamic programming can be used). Make the resulting sequence the current tour and output "exact: <S> length <L>". <S> and <L> are the same as for NEAREST.

## TOUR
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
Action: Output "tour: <S> length <L>". <S> is the notation of the current tour's sequence and <L> is its length.

## TWOOPT
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
Action: Improve the current tour by the 2-opt method. Let the tour at that time be P1, ..., Pn. A scan examines the pairs (i, j) for i = 1, 2, ..., n - 1 in this order, and for each i, j = i + 1, ..., n in this order. The move of pair (i, j) reverses the order of the part from Pi to Pj, and its improvement is g = d(Pi-1, Pi) + d(Pj, Pj+1) - d(Pi-1, Pj) - d(Pi, Pj+1). When performing a move, first output "reverse <a>..<b> gain <g>". <a> and <b> are the numbers of Pi and Pj before the reversal. When finished, output "twoopt: moves <m>, length <L0> -> <L1>". <m> is the number of moves, and <L0> and <L1> are the lengths of the tour before and after this command.

## OROPT
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
Action: Improve the current tour by the Or-opt method. Let the tour at that time be P1, ..., Pn. A scan considers, for segment lengths s = 1, 2, 3 in this order, and for each s, i = 1, 2, ..., n - s + 1 in this order, the segment Pi, ..., Pi+s-1. Let the sequence remaining after removing this segment from the tour be R1, ..., Rn-s (R0 and Rn-s+1 are D); for j = 0, 1, ..., n - s in this order (excluding j = i - 1, which is the original position), examine the move that inserts the segment, without changing its direction, between Rj and Rj+1. The improvement is
g = (d(Pi-1, Pi) + d(Pi+s-1, Pi+s) - d(Pi-1, Pi+s)) - (d(Rj, Pi) + d(Pi+s-1, Rj+1) - d(Rj, Rj+1))
When n - s + 1 is less than 1, nothing is examined for that s. When performing a move, first output "move <seg> after <a> gain <g>". <seg> is the numbers of the segment's stops joined in order with "-" (e.g. 4-9), and <a> is the name of Rj ("D" if j = 0). When finished, output "oropt: moves <m>, length <L0> -> <L1>". <m>, <L0> and <L1> have the same meaning as for TWOOPT.

## SWAP
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
Action: Improve the current tour by the swap method. Let the tour at that time be P1, ..., Pn and its length L. A scan examines the pairs (i, j) for i = 1, 2, ..., n - 1 in this order, and for each i, j = i + 1, ..., n in this order. The move of pair (i, j) exchanges Pi and Pj; with L' the length of the tour after the exchange, the improvement is g = L - L'. When performing a move, first output "swap <a> and <b> gain <g>". <a> and <b> are the numbers of Pi and Pj before the exchange. When finished, output "swap: moves <m>, length <L0> -> <L1>". <m>, <L0> and <L1> have the same meaning as for TWOOPT.

## TIMECHECK
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
Action: Simulate a run of the current tour. Output "timecheck tour depart <T>" (<T> is the time notation of the departure time), followed by the result lines.

## TIMECHECK r
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
2. r is greater than the number of routes k: "error: no route <r>"
Action: Simulate a run of route r. Output "timecheck route <r> depart <T>" (<T> is the time notation of the departure time), followed by the result lines.

## BOUND
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
Action: Compute lower bounds on the tour length and output the following 3 lines.
(1) Find, by Prim's method, a minimum spanning tree of the complete graph whose vertices are the depot and all stops and whose edge weights are the distances between the two vertices. Start the tree with D only; while some stop is not in the tree, choose, among the pairs of a tree vertex p and a stop c not in the tree, the pair with the smallest d(p, c), and add c to the tree together with the edge p-c. If several pairs have the smallest d(p, c), choose the pair with the smallest number of c; if still several, choose the pair whose p entered the tree earliest (D is the vertex that entered the tree earliest). Let <E> be the added edges, in the order they were added, each written in the form "<p>-<c>" (<p> and <c> are the names of p and c) and joined with single spaces between them, and let M be the sum of the edge weights; output "mst: <E> (length <M>)".
(2) Compute the 1-tree lower bound T as follows. If there is 1 stop, it is twice the distance from the depot to that stop. If there are 2 or more stops, it is the sum of the edge weights of a minimum spanning tree of the complete graph whose vertices are the stops only (this value does not depend on which tree is chosen), plus the two smallest of the distances from the depot to each stop (even if several are equal, each counts as one). With B the larger of M and T, output "bound: mst <M>, one-tree <T>, best <B>".
(3) If there is no current tour, output "gap: no tour". If there is a current tour, let L be its length (L >= B always holds). If B > 0, let u be the quotient of (2000 * (L - B) + B) divided by (2 * B) (this is the value of 100 * (L - B) / B multiplied by 10 and rounded half up at the first decimal place). If B = 0, let u = 0. Output "gap: tour <L>, <G>%". <G> is the quotient of u divided by 10, ".", and the remainder of u divided by 10 (1 digit), in this order (e.g. 12.5 if u = 125, 0.0 if u = 0).

## VRP q [limit]
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
3. Some stop has a demand greater than q: "error: stop <p> demand <d> exceeds capacity <q>". <p> is the smallest-numbered such stop and <d> is its demand.
4. limit is given and there is a stop c with 2 * d(D, c) greater than limit: "error: stop <p> too far for limit <limit>". <p> is the smallest-numbered such stop.
Action: Build a plan by the Clarke-Wright savings method (parallel version).
1. For each stop, create a route consisting of that stop alone.
2. For every pair (i, j) of stops with numbers i < j, compute the saving s(i, j) = d(D, i) + d(D, j) - d(i, j). Take only the pairs with s(i, j) > 0 and order them in decreasing order of s(i, j). Pairs with equal s(i, j) are ordered by increasing i, and if still equal, by increasing j.
3. Examine the pairs (i, j) one at a time in that order. With A the route containing i and B the route containing j, join A and B if and only if all of the following conditions hold.
   - A and B are different routes.
   - i is the first or last stop of A.
   - j is the first or last stop of B.
   - The sum of the load of A and the load of B is q or less.
   - When limit is given, the length of the route after joining is limit or less.
   The joined route is the sequence formed by orienting A so that i is last (if i is the first stop of A, reverse A), orienting B so that j is first (if j is the last stop of B, reverse B), and following A with B. In a route with only one stop, that stop is both first and last, and no change of direction is needed. The joined route becomes a single route in place of A and B.
4. When all pairs have been examined, create a plan from the obtained routes and capacity q, normalize it, and make it the current plan. limit is not recorded in the plan (other commands do not consider an upper limit on route length).
Output "vrp: capacity <q>, merges <m>, routes <k>, length <L>", followed by the route lines. <m> is the number of joins, <k> is the number of routes, and <L> is the length of the plan.

## SPLIT q
Checks:
1. There is no current tour: "error: no tour"
2. Some stop has a demand greater than q: output the same line as check 3 of VRP.
Action: Divide the current tour P1, ..., Pn, keeping its order, into consecutive segments, and create a plan in which each segment is one route. A segment Pi+1, ..., Pj (0 <= i < j <= n) may be used only when its load is q or less, and its cost is its length when the segment is taken as a route. Let V(0) = 0; for j = 1, 2, ..., n in this order, compute V(i) + cost for every usable segment Pi+1, ..., Pj (0 <= i < j), let the minimum be V(j) and the i giving the minimum be pre(j) (if several i give the minimum, the smallest such i). Since every stop's demand is q or less, V(j) is always defined. Starting with j = n, repeat making the segment from the (pre(j)+1)-th to the j-th element of P one route and replacing j with pre(j), until j = 0. Create a plan from the obtained routes and capacity q, normalize it, and make it the current plan. The current tour does not change.
Output "split: capacity <q>, routes <k>, length <L>", followed by the route lines. <k> and <L> have the same meaning as for VRP.

## SWEEP q
Checks:
1. The depot is unset: "error: no depot"
2. There are no stops at all: "error: no stops"
3. Some stop has a demand greater than q: output the same line as check 3 of VRP.
Action: Build a plan by the sweep method. For each stop c, consider its direction (dx, dy) as seen from the depot (dx is the x coordinate of c - the x coordinate of the depot, dy is the y coordinate of c - the y coordinate of the depot), and order the stops as follows.
- The stops with (dx, dy) = (0, 0) come first, in increasing order of number.
- The remaining stops are ordered by increasing angle of the direction (dx, dy) measured counterclockwise from the direction (1, 0) (at least 0 degrees and less than 360 degrees; the direction (0, 1) is 90 degrees, (-1, 0) is 180 degrees, and (0, -1) is 270 degrees). Stops with the same angle are ordered by increasing distance from the depot, and if still equal, by increasing number.
Compare angles exactly. For example, a direction with dy > 0 or (dy = 0 and dx > 0) is at least 0 degrees and less than 180 degrees, and any other direction is at least 180 degrees and less than 360 degrees; for two directions (dx1, dy1) and (dx2, dy2) on the same side, if dx1 * dy2 - dy1 * dx2 is positive the former has the smaller angle, and if it is 0 the angles are equal.
First prepare one empty route; look at the stops c one at a time in this order, and if adding the demand of c to the load of the current route would exceed q, prepare a new empty route and make it the current route; then append c to the end of the current route. Create a plan from the obtained routes and capacity q, normalize it, and make it the current plan.
Output "sweep: capacity <q>, routes <k>, length <L>", followed by the route lines. <k> and <L> have the same meaning as for VRP.

## ROUTES
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
Action: Output "plan: capacity <Q>, routes <k>, length <L>", followed by the route lines. <Q> is the capacity, <k> is the number of routes, and <L> is the length of the plan.

## RELOCATE
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
Action: Improve the current plan by moving stops one at a time to another route. A scan examines moves for r = 1, 2, ..., k in this order; for each r, positions p = 1, 2, ..., (the number of stops of route r) of route r in this order; for each p, t = 1, 2, ..., k (with t != r) in this order; and for each t, j = 0, 1, ..., (the number of stops of route t) in this order. This move removes the p-th stop c of route r from route r and inserts it between the j-th and (j + 1)-th locations of route t (the 0th location of each route and the location after its last stop are D). Moves for which adding the demand of c to the load of route t would exceed Q are not examined. With a and b the locations immediately before and after c in route r, and u and v the j-th and (j + 1)-th locations of route t, the improvement is
g = (d(a, c) + d(c, b) - d(a, b)) - (d(u, c) + d(c, v) - d(u, v))
When performing a move, first output "relocate <c> from route <r> to route <t> after <u> gain <g>" (<c> is the number of c, <u> is the name of u, and <r> and <t> are the route numbers before the move). After the move, normalize the plan (a route that became empty because c left it is removed here). When finished, output "relocate: moves <m>, routes <k0> -> <k1>, length <L0> -> <L1>". <m> is the number of moves, <k0> and <k1> are the numbers of routes before and after this command, and <L0> and <L1> are the lengths of the plan before and after this command.

## EXCHANGE
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
Action: Improve the current plan by exchanging stops of different routes one for one. A scan examines, for r = 1, 2, ..., k in this order; for each r, t = r + 1, ..., k in this order; for each (r, t), positions p = 1, 2, ..., (the number of stops of route r) of route r in this order; and for each p, positions j = 1, 2, ..., (the number of stops of route t) of route t in this order, the move that exchanges the p-th stop c of route r and the j-th stop e of route t (c goes to the position where e was, and e goes to the position where c was). Moves after which the load of route r or route t would exceed Q are not examined. The improvement g is the sum of the lengths of route r and route t before the exchange minus the sum of the lengths of the two routes after the exchange. When performing a move, first output "exchange <c> (route <r>) with <e> (route <t>) gain <g>" (<r> and <t> are the route numbers before the move). After the move, normalize the plan. When finished, output "exchange: moves <m>, length <L0> -> <L1>". <m>, <L0> and <L1> have the same meaning as for RELOCATE.

## CROSS
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
Action: Improve the current plan by swapping the tail parts of two routes (2-opt*). A scan examines moves for r = 1, 2, ..., k in this order; for each r, t = r + 1, ..., k in this order; for each (r, t), i = 0, 1, ..., a in this order; and for each i, j = 0, 1, ..., b in this order. Here route r is A1, ..., Aa and route t is B1, ..., Bb (A0, Aa+1, B0 and Bb+1 are D). Move (i, j) replaces the two routes with the sequences A1, ..., Ai, Bj+1, ..., Bb and B1, ..., Bj, Ai+1, ..., Aa. Moves for which the load of either of the two sequences after the replacement would exceed Q are not examined. The improvement is
g = d(Ai, Ai+1) + d(Bj, Bj+1) - d(Ai, Bj+1) - d(Bj, Ai+1)
When performing a move, first output "cross route <r> after <x> with route <t> after <y> gain <g>" (<x> is the name of Ai and <y> is the name of Bj, "D" for the 0th; <r> and <t> are the route numbers before the move). After the move, normalize the plan (a sequence that no longer contains any stops is removed here). When finished, output "cross: moves <m>, routes <k0> -> <k1>, length <L0> -> <L1>". Each value has the same meaning as for RELOCATE.

## ROUTEOPT
Checks:
1. There is no current plan: "error: no plan"
Action: For r = 1, 2, ..., k in this order, regard route r as a tour and improve it by the same procedure as the action of TWOOPT. However, do not output the "reverse" lines and the "twoopt:" line; instead output "route <r>: moves <m>, length <a> -> <b>". <m> is the number of moves on that route, and <a> and <b> are the lengths of that route before and after the improvement. After all routes have been processed, normalize the plan and output "routeopt: length <L0> -> <L1>". <L0> and <L1> are the lengths of the plan before and after this command.

## REPORT
Action: Output the following 3 lines.
- "report: stops <n>, demand <D>, service <S>, windows <w>". <n> is the number of stops, <D> is the total demand, <S> is the total service time, and <w> is the number of stops with a time window.
- If there is no current tour, "tour: none". If there is one, simulate a run of it and output "tour: length <L>, return <T>, violations <V>, lateness <E>". <L> is the length of the tour, <T> is the time notation of the return time, <V> is the number of violations, and <E> is the total lateness.
- If there is no current plan, "plan: none". If there is one, "plan: capacity <Q>, routes <k>, length <L>, utilization <U>%, return <T>, violations <V>, lateness <E>". <Q> is the capacity, <k> is the number of routes, and <L> is the length of the plan. With W the sum of the loads of all routes, let u be the quotient of (2000 * W + k * Q) divided by (2 * k * Q) (this is the value of 100 * W / (k * Q) multiplied by 10 and rounded half up at the first decimal place); <U> is the quotient of u divided by 10, ".", and the remainder of u divided by 10 (1 digit), in this order. Simulate a run of each route (all leaving at the same departure time); <T> is the time notation of the latest of the return times, <V> is the sum of the numbers of violations, and <E> is the sum of the total latenesses.

[Output format]
The output of each command consists of the lines defined in [Processing], output in command order. When one command outputs several lines, they are output in the order described in [Processing].
The output consists entirely of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No line has trailing whitespace, and no blank lines are output. Leading spaces appear only in the output of MATRIX.
Numbers are written in decimal, with a leading "-" for negative numbers. Except for time notation and the fractional parts of gap and utilization, numbers have no extra leading zeros.
Whitespace inside a string is a single space, except for the spaces used for right alignment in MATRIX.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 1000
- At most 30 stops exist at the same time.
- k of ORDER is from 1 to 30 inclusive.
- Every integer appearing during the computation has an absolute value less than 2 to the 31st power.
