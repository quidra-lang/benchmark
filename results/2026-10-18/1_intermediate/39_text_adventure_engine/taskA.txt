[Overview]
Write the engine of a text adventure (an adventure game played with text only). The program reads from standard input, first, the definition of the "world" in which the adventure takes place, then reads the player's commands one line at a time, executes them, and writes their responses to standard output. The world consists of rooms, exits connecting rooms, items (which have a weight and may have the properties of a treasure, a light, a container or a fixed item), locks opened and closed with keys, and rules for events that happen when an item is used on an item or when a room is entered. Command interpretation handles ignoring articles, synonyms, direction abbreviations, narrowing down items by adjectives, the pronoun it, and repeating the previous command. The player moves between rooms, picks up items, puts them down and puts them into containers, opens and closes containers, locks and unlocks the locks of exits and containers with keys, lights lights to explore dark rooms, and aims to gather all treasures in a designated room. Light fuel, the danger of darkness, score and move count, and saving and restoring the game are also handled.

[Input format]
The input consists of the "world definition part" and the "command part".
- The world definition part starts at line 1 and ends just before the line consisting only of "BEGIN". Each of its lines is a world definition line.
- The command part runs from the line after the "BEGIN" line up to just before the line consisting only of "QUIT". Each of its lines is a player command line. The "QUIT" line is the last line of the input.
A world definition line is one or more words separated by single spaces, with no whitespace at the start or end of the line. The first word (uppercase letters) gives the kind of line; the formats are defined in [Processing] 2.
A command line is a string of 1 to 60 characters consisting of letters (uppercase and lowercase), digits and spaces, whose first and last characters are not spaces. Words are not necessarily separated by exactly one space. A command line never matches "QUIT" exactly.
It is guaranteed that every line follows this format and the [Constraints]. However, the content of a command line is not necessarily correct (how to handle it is defined in [Processing]).

[Processing]
In the following, a string enclosed in double quotes is a string that appears exactly as written in the input or output (such a string may itself contain double quote characters; the outermost pair delimits it). In the descriptions of output, a part such as <x> is replaced by its value. In particular, <name of x> is the name of item x (the name defined by the ITEM line in 2.).

1. Terms
- An identifier is a word consisting only of lowercase letters. Rooms, items and flags (marks that are either on or off) are each named by an identifier. Room identifiers, item identifiers and flag identifiers are handled separately (the same spelling may be used for both a room and an item).
- The directions are the 6 directions north, south, east, west, up, down, and this order is called the direction order. north and south, east and west, and up and down are opposite to each other. Whenever a direction is written in the output, it is always written as one of these 6 words.
- The direction words are the 12 words consisting of the 6 direction words and their 1-letter initials n, s, e, w, u, d. An initial direction word represents the direction that starts with that initial.
- The identifier order is the order obtained by comparing identifiers lexicographically by character code (for example, key comes before keys, and keys comes before lamp). Unless stated otherwise, items are ordered by the identifier order of their item identifiers.

2. World definition lines
Read the world definition lines in input order and build the initial state of the world. In the formats below, a part enclosed in < > is replaced by a word, a part enclosed in [ ] is an optional part, and parts separated by | are replaced by one of them. <room> and <room2> are room identifiers, <item> and <item2> are item identifiers, <flag> is a flag identifier, <direction> is one of the 6 direction words, and <number> is a word consisting only of digits (its value is the integer read as a decimal number). <text> stands for the whole rest of the line from that position to the end of the line (including the spaces in it), and is used as is in the output.

ROOM <room> [DARK] <text>
  Defines a room. When the 3rd word is exactly "DARK", the room is a "dark room" and the 4th and later words are the room's name. Otherwise, the room is not a dark room and the 3rd and later words are the room's name.
DESC <room> <text>
  Adds one line of description to the room. A room has 0 or more description lines, in input order.
EXIT <room> <direction> <room2> [LOCKED <item>]
  Defines an exit such that going <direction> from <room> leads to <room2>. Exits are one-way; moving in the opposite direction requires a separate EXIT line. If "LOCKED <item>" is present, this exit has a lock, its key is <item>, and it is initially locked. Otherwise, it is an exit without a lock.
ITEM <item> <place> <number> [TREASURE <number>] [LIGHT <number>] [CONTAINER <number> [CLOSED] [LOCKED <item2>]] [FIXED] <name>
  Defines an item.
  - <place> is one of: a room identifier (the item is placed on the floor of that room), "PLAYER" (the player holds it directly), or "NOWHERE" (it is nowhere in the world).
  - The <number> after <place> is the weight of the item itself.
  - If "TREASURE <number>" is present, the item is a treasure and <number> is its points.
  - If "LIGHT <number>" is present, the item is a light and <number> is its initial fuel. A light is initially unlit.
  - If "CONTAINER <number>" is present, the item is a container and <number> is its capacity. If it is followed by "CLOSED", the container is initially closed; otherwise it is open. If "LOCKED <item2>" follows further, the container has a lock, its key is <item2>, and it is initially locked (regardless of whether CLOSED is present). Otherwise, it is a container without a lock.
  - If "FIXED" is present, the item is a fixed item and cannot be picked up.
  - The remaining one or more words, joined with single spaces, are the item's name. Each word of the name is called a "name word" of that item.
  Each optional part appears at most once, in the order written here. Name words consist only of lowercase letters, so they can be distinguished from uppercase words.
INSIDE <item> <item2>
  Sets the place of <item> to inside the container <item2>.
ALIAS <item> <word>
  Adds <word> (a word consisting only of lowercase letters) as an alias of the item. The item's identifier and its aliases together are called the "nouns" of that item.
DETAIL <item> <text>
  Adds one line of detailed description to the item. An item has 0 or more detailed description lines, in input order.
RULE <item> <item2> [IF <flag> | UNLESS <flag>] <effect>
  Defines a "rule line" executed when item <item> is used on item <item2> with the command use. When the 4th word is "IF" or "UNLESS", that word and the next word are the condition and the rest is the effect. Otherwise there is no condition and the 4th and later words are the effect.
ENTER <room> [IF <flag> | UNLESS <flag>] <effect>
  Defines a rule line executed when room <room> is entered with the command go. When the 3rd word is "IF" or "UNLESS", that word and the next word are the condition and the rest is the effect. Otherwise there is no condition and the 3rd and later words are the effect.
TROPHY <room>
  Sets the room where treasures are gathered (the trophy room).
START <room>
  Sets the room where the player is at the beginning (the start room).
MAXWEIGHT <number>
  Sets the upper limit of the weight the player can carry.

An effect is one of the following 7 kinds; their meaning is defined in 9.
  MESSAGE <text>
  OPEN <room> <direction> <room2>
  REMOVE <item>
  ADD <item> <room>
  SET <flag>
  CLEAR <flag>
  POINTS <number>

3. Game state
The game state consists of the following.
- The current room: initially the start room.
- The place of each item: one of the floor of some room, the player, inside some container, or nowhere. Initially determined by the ITEM and INSIDE lines. An item whose place is the player is called an item the player "holds directly".
- The state of each container: one of "open" (open), "closed" (closed) or "locked" (locked). A locked container is closed.
- Which rooms have exits in which directions (they can increase through the OPEN effect), and for each exit its destination, whether it has a lock and its key, and whether it is locked.
- Whether each light is lit, and its fuel.
- Whether each flag is on or off: initially every flag is off.
- Bonus points: initially 0.
- The move count: initially 0.
- The set of visited rooms: initially consists only of the start room.
- The darkness count: initially 0.
- The game end status: one of "none", "won" or "dead"; initially "none".
In addition, the program keeps the item the pronoun it refers to (initially none), the previous command line (initially none), verbose mode (on or off, initially off), and the save slot (initially empty). These are not part of the game state (save does not save them, and restore does not change them).

4. Definitions concerning places and lighting
- For a place h (the floor of some room, the player, or inside some container), the "items reachable from h" are the items whose place is h, and the items whose place is inside an open container (a container whose state is open) that is among the items reachable from h (following nested containers). The contents of closed containers are not reachable.
- The "carried items" are the items reachable from the player.
- The "room items" are the items reachable from the floor of the current room.
- The current room is "lit" when the current room is not a dark room, or when there is at least one lit light among the carried items and the room items. Otherwise, the current room is said to be "dark".
- The "visible items" are all carried items and room items when the current room is lit, and only the carried items when it is dark.
- The "total weight" of an item is the sum of the weight of the item itself and the total weights of the items whose place is inside that item (container) (it is the weight of the whole nesting, including the contents of closed containers).
- The "carried weight" is the sum of the total weights of the items the player holds directly.
- The "content weight" of a container is the sum of the total weights of the items whose place is inside that container.
- A treasure is "stored" when, starting from the treasure and repeatedly moving to the container as long as the place of the item currently being looked at is inside a container, the place of the item looked at last is the floor of the trophy room (that is, it is placed directly on the floor of the trophy room, or is nested inside a container that is on the floor of the trophy room; whether the containers along the way are open does not matter).
- The "treasure points" are the sum of the points of the stored treasures, and the "score" is the sum of the treasure points and the bonus points.

5. Descriptions and display of items
- The "display name" of item x is the name of x if it has no annotations; if it has annotations, it is the name of x followed by " (", the annotations joined with ", " (a comma and a space), and ")". The annotations are, in this order: the state of x if x is a container (one of open, closed, locked), and "lit" if x is a light and is lit. For example, "old map", "leather bag (open)", "iron safe (locked)", "oil lamp (lit)".
- The "item tree" of depth d of place h is obtained by ordering the items whose place is h in identifier order and, for each of them, writing one line consisting of 2 * d spaces followed by its display name, and, if the item is an open container, writing immediately after it the lines of the item tree of depth d + 1 inside that container. If no item has place h, it is 0 lines.
- A "description" of the current room is either a full description or a brief description. When the current room is dark, both are just the single line "It is pitch dark.". When it is lit, it consists of the following lines in this order.
  - One line with the room's name.
  - Only for a full description: each line of the room's description (in input order).
  - One line consisting of "Exits: " followed by the exits of the current room in direction order, joined with ", ". Each exit is written as its direction word, and a locked exit is followed by " (locked)". If there are no exits at all, the line is "Exits: none".
  - If at least one item has the floor of the current room as its place, one line "You see:" and the item tree of depth 1 of the floor of the current room.

6. Processing at the start
After reading the world definition part, output the full description of the start room.

7. Processing command lines
Process the command lines one at a time in input order. First output one line consisting of "> " (a greater-than sign and a space) followed by the command line itself (exactly as in the input); then interpret and execute the command line by the procedure below and output its response.

7.1 Splitting into words and repetition
Convert all uppercase letters of the command line to lowercase, split it into a sequence of words at runs of one or more spaces, and remove every word "the", "a" and "an" from it. In the processing and output below, the words after conversion to lowercase are used.
When the first word of the resulting word sequence is "again" or "g", the line is called a "repeat line" and is processed as follows.
- If there are 2 or more words, "I don't understand that.".
- Otherwise, if there is no previous command line, "There is nothing to repeat.".
- Otherwise, process the previous command line by the procedure from 7.2 on, as if it had been entered now (only the "> " line of the current repeat line is output).
A line that is not a repeat line is processed by the procedure from 7.2 on, and that line becomes the previous command line (the same holds when the result is a failure or an interpretation error). A repeat line never becomes the previous command line.

7.2 Interpretation
Build the word sequence by the same method as in 7.1 and perform the following steps in order. As soon as a response is determined at some step, the processing of that command line ends.
(1) If the word sequence is empty, "I beg your pardon?".
(2) If the first word is a direction word, add "go" at the front of the word sequence.
(3) Convert the first word to a verb using the table below. If the word is not in the table, "I don't know the verb "<first word>".". Below, let R be the word sequence of the remaining words without the first word.
  go: go, walk, run
  look: look, l
  examine: examine, x, inspect, read
  take: take, get, grab
  drop: drop, discard
  put: put, insert, place
  inventory: inventory, inv, i
  open: open
  close: close, shut
  unlock: unlock
  lock: lock
  use: use, apply
  light: light, ignite
  extinguish: extinguish, douse, snuff
  wait: wait, z
  score: score
  save: save
  restore: restore, load
  verbose: verbose
  brief: brief
(4) If the game end status is not "none" and the verb is not restore, "The game is over.".
(5) If the verb is look and R is not empty: if the first word of R is "at", change the verb to examine and R to the remaining word sequence without "at". If it is not "at", "I don't understand that.".
(6) If the verb is one of look, inventory, wait, score, save, restore, verbose, brief: if R is not empty, "I don't understand that."; if it is empty, execute that command.
(7) If R is empty: if the verb is go, "Which way do you want to go?"; otherwise "What do you want to <verb>?".
(8) If the verb is go: if R is exactly one word and it is a direction word, execute go in the direction that word represents. Otherwise "I don't understand that.".
(9) In any of the following cases, split R at a preposition into two noun phrases, the first part and the second part.
  - The verb is put (the prepositions are "in" and "into").
  - The verb is use (the prepositions are "on" and "with").
  - The verb is unlock or lock and R contains "with" (the preposition is "with").
  - The verb is take and R contains "from" (the preposition is "from").
  Split at the position where one of the verb's prepositions first appears in R: the word sequence before that position is the first part and the word sequence after it is the second part (the preposition word itself belongs to neither). If R contains no preposition (in the case of put and use), or if the first part or the second part is empty, "I don't understand that.".
(10) When (9) does not apply, the whole of R is one noun phrase, which is the "target" of the command. unlock and lock in this form are called the "form without a key", and in the form split by (9) the "form with a key". take in the form split by (9) is called the "from form".
(11) Execute the command of 8. according to the verb.
When the target of take or drop, the first part of put, or the first part of take in the from form is exactly the single word "all", this is called the "all form" of that command. In the all form, "all" is not resolved as a noun phrase.

7.3 Resolving noun phrases
A noun phrase (a sequence of one or more words) P is resolved to one item by the following procedure. If it fails at some step, the processing of the command ends with that response.
(1) When P is exactly the single word "it": if there is no item it refers to, fail with "I don't know what "it" refers to.". If that item is not a visible item, fail with "You can't see any such thing.". Otherwise, the result of the resolution is that item (the item it refers to does not change).
(2) Examine the words of P from the front; if some word is not in the vocabulary, fail with "I don't know the word "<w>"." for the first such word w. The vocabulary is the set of the nouns and name words of all items in the world (including items that are nowhere).
(3) Collect the visible items such that the last word of P equals one of the item's nouns and every word of P other than the last equals one of the item's name words. If there are none, fail with "You can't see any such thing.". If there are 2 or more, fail with one line consisting of "Which do you mean: " followed by the names of those items in identifier order joined with ", ", followed by "?" (example: "Which do you mean: red apple, green apple?"). If there is exactly one, the result of the resolution is that item, and the item it refers to is set to that item.
Even if the command fails after a noun phrase was resolved successfully and the item it refers to changed, the item it refers to stays changed.

8. Commands
Each command ends in either "success" or "failure". Below, the steps of each command are written in order. A command that stops where "fail" is written is a failure, and one that proceeds to the end is a success. A failed command changes nothing in the state except the item it refers to. Below, "resolve the noun phrase to x" means resolving that noun phrase to an item by the method of 7.3 and letting the result be x (if the resolution fails, the command fails).
Of the commands, the 12 kinds go, take, drop, put, open, close, unlock, lock, use, light, extinguish, wait are called "actions". When an action succeeds, the "passage of a turn" of 10. is performed after its response. The other commands (look, examine, inventory, score, save, restore, verbose, brief) take no time, and no turn passes.

go (let the direction be d)
  (1) If the current room has no exit in direction d, fail with "You can't go that way.".
  (2) If that exit is locked, fail with "The way <d> is locked.".
  (3) Set the current room to the destination room of the exit. Next, if verbose mode is on or the destination room is not in the set of visited rooms, output the full description, otherwise output the brief description, and add the destination room to the set of visited rooms. Finally, take the ENTER lines of the destination room in input order and execute them by the method of 9.
look
  Output the full description.
examine (resolve the target to x)
  Output the following lines in this order.
  - Each line of the detailed description of x (in input order). If there is none, "You see nothing special about the <name of x>.".
  - If x is a light: "It is lit (fuel <fuel>)." if it is lit, "It is unlit (fuel <fuel>)." if it is unlit.
  - If x is a container: "It is locked." if it is locked; "It is closed." if it is closed but not locked; if it is open and some item has its place inside it, one line "It contains:" and the item tree of depth 1 inside x; if it is open and empty, "It is empty.".
  - If x is a treasure, "It is a treasure worth <points of x> points.".
  - "It weighs <total weight of x>.".
take
  (1) If the current room is dark, fail with "It is pitch dark.".
  (2) In the from form, resolve the second part to c. If c is not a container, fail with "The <name of c> is not a container.". If c is not open, fail with "The <name of c> is closed.". Below, the source is inside c. When not in the from form, the source is the floor of the current room.
  (3) In the all form: the candidates are the items whose place is the source and which are not fixed items, in identifier order. If there are no candidates, fail with "There is nothing here to take." when not in the from form, or "There is nothing in the <name of c> to take." in the from form. If there are candidates, process them one at a time in order and output one line per candidate y. If y is not a carried item and the sum of the carried weight and the total weight of y exceeds the upper limit of the weight the player can carry, the line is "<name of y>: You can't carry any more." and y is not moved. Otherwise, set the place of y to the player and the line is "<name of y>: Taken.". The carried weight is checked using its value after the previous candidates have been processed. If no candidate became Taken, the command fails (all these lines are output even when it fails).
  (4) When not in the all form: resolve the target (the first part in the from form) to x. In the from form, if the place of x is not inside c, fail with "The <name of x> is not in the <name of c>.". If the player holds x directly, fail with "You already have the <name of x>.". If x is a fixed item, fail with "The <name of x> is fixed in place.". If x is not a carried item and the sum of the carried weight and the total weight of x exceeds the upper limit of the weight the player can carry, fail with "You can't carry any more.". Otherwise, set the place of x to the player and respond "Taken.".
drop
  drop can be done even if the current room is dark.
  (1) In the all form: if the player holds no item directly, fail with "You are empty-handed.". Otherwise, one at a time in identifier order, set the place of each of those items to the floor of the current room, and output one line "<name of y>: Dropped." for each item y.
  (2) When not in the all form: resolve the target to x. If x is not a carried item, fail with "You aren't carrying the <name of x>.". Otherwise, set the place of x to the floor of the current room and respond "Dropped.".
put
  When not in the all form, resolve the first part to x and the second part to y, in this order, and check the following in order.
  (1) If x is not a carried item, fail with "You aren't carrying the <name of x>.".
  (2) If x and y are the same item, fail with "You can't put the <name of x> inside itself.".
  (3) If y is not a container, fail with "The <name of y> is not a container.".
  (4) If y is not open, fail with "The <name of y> is closed.".
  (5) Put x into y by the "putting procedure" below. If the putting procedure fails, the command fails with its reason text as the response. If it succeeds, "You put the <name of x> in the <name of y>.".
  In the all form, resolve the second part to y and check (3) and (4) above. Next, the candidates are the items the player holds directly other than y, in identifier order. If there are no candidates, fail with "You have nothing to put in the <name of y>.". If there are candidates, put them into y one at a time in order by the putting procedure, and for each candidate z output one line: "<name of z>: Done." if it succeeded, or "<name of z>: " followed by the reason text if it failed. If no candidate succeeded, the command fails (all these lines are output even when it fails).
  The putting procedure (the procedure for putting item x into container y) checks the following in order.
  - If, starting from y and repeatedly moving to the container as long as the place of the item currently being looked at is inside a container, x is moved to at some point (that is, y is nested inside x), fail with the reason "The <name of y> is inside the <name of x>.".
  - If the place of x is already inside y, fail with the reason "The <name of x> is already in the <name of y>.".
  - If the content weight of y, supposing the place of x were changed to inside y, would exceed the capacity of y, fail with the reason "The <name of x> doesn't fit in the <name of y>." (x is not moved).
  - Otherwise, change the place of x to inside y and succeed.
inventory
  If the player holds no item directly, the single line "You are empty-handed.". Otherwise, one line "You are carrying:", the item tree of depth 1 of the player, and one line "Total weight: <carried weight>/<upper limit of the weight the player can carry>".
open (resolve the target to x)
  (1) If x is not a container, fail with "You can't open the <name of x>.".
  (2) If x is open, fail with "The <name of x> is already open.".
  (3) If x is locked, fail with "The <name of x> is locked.".
  (4) Open x (set its state to open); if some item has its place inside x, one line consisting of "Opening the <name of x> reveals " followed by the names of those items in identifier order joined with ", ", followed by "."; otherwise "Opened.".
close (resolve the target to x)
  (1) If x is not a container, fail with "You can't close the <name of x>.".
  (2) If x is not open, fail with "The <name of x> is already closed.".
  (3) Close x (set its state to closed) and respond "Closed.".
unlock, lock
  unlock is the command that opens a lock, and lock is the command that locks it. Below, where the text differs, the text for unlock and the text for lock are written in this order, separated by " / ". The lock target is the target in the form without a key, and the first part in the form with a key.
  When the lock target is exactly one word and it is a direction word, the exit of the current room in the direction d that word represents is handled.
  (1) If the current room has no exit in direction d, fail with "There is no exit <d>.".
  (2) If the exit has no lock, fail with "The way <d> has no lock.".
  (3) For unlock, if the exit is not locked, fail with "The way <d> is not locked." / for lock, if the exit is locked, fail with "The way <d> is already locked.".
  (4) Perform the "key check procedure" below.
  (5) Set the exit to the unlocked state / the locked state. Furthermore, if the destination room of the exit has an exit in the direction opposite to d, the destination of that exit is the current room, and that exit has a lock whose key is the same item as the key of this exit, set that opposite exit to the same state as well. Finally, "Unlocked." / "Locked.".
  When the lock target is anything else, resolve the lock target to x and check the following in order.
  (1) If x is not a container, or is a container without a lock, fail with "The <name of x> has no lock.".
  (2) For unlock, if x is not locked, fail with "The <name of x> is not locked." / for lock, if x is locked, fail with "The <name of x> is already locked.".
  (3) For lock, if x is open, fail with "You must close the <name of x> first.".
  (4) Perform the key check procedure.
  (5) Set the state of x to closed / locked and respond "Unlocked." / "Locked.".
  The key check procedure (let k be the key of the lock):
  - In the form with a key: resolve the second part to y. If y is not a carried item, fail with "You aren't carrying the <name of y>.". If y is not k, fail with "The <name of y> doesn't fit.".
  - In the form without a key: if k is not a carried item, fail with "You don't have the right key.". If it is a carried item, output one line "(with the <name of k>)" before the final "Unlocked." / "Locked." line.
use
  Resolve the first part to x and the second part to y, in this order, and do the following in order.
  (1) If x is not a carried item, fail with "You aren't carrying the <name of x>.".
  (2) Take the RULE lines whose first item is x and whose second item is y, in input order, and execute them by the method of 9. If no rule line was executed, fail with "Nothing happens.".
  (3) If one or more rule lines were executed, the command succeeds. In that case, if no line was output by a MESSAGE effect, output the single line "Done.".
light (resolve the target to x)
  (1) If x is not a light, fail with "You can't light the <name of x>.".
  (2) If x is lit, fail with "The <name of x> is already lit.".
  (3) If the fuel of x is 0, fail with "The <name of x> has no fuel.".
  (4) Light x and respond "The <name of x> is now lit.".
extinguish (resolve the target to x)
  (1) If x is not a light, fail with "You can't extinguish the <name of x>.".
  (2) If x is unlit, fail with "The <name of x> is not lit.".
  (3) Put out x and respond "The <name of x> is now out.".
wait
  "Time passes.".
score
  One line "You have scored <score> points (treasure <treasure points>, bonus <bonus points>) in <move count> moves." and one line "Treasures stored: <number of stored treasures> of <number of treasures in the world>.".
save
  Put a copy of the game state (everything in the list of 3.) into the save slot (discarding its previous content) and respond "Game saved.".
restore
  If the save slot is empty, fail with "There is no saved game.". Otherwise, restore the game state to the content of the save slot (the content of the save slot remains), and output one line "Game restored." and the full description.
verbose
  Turn verbose mode on and respond "Verbose mode.".
brief
  Turn verbose mode off and respond "Brief mode.".

9. Executing rule lines
To execute a sequence of rule lines, examine the rule lines one at a time from the front. Skip a rule line whose condition is "IF <flag>" and that flag is off, and a rule line whose condition is "UNLESS <flag>" and that flag is on. The other rule lines (including rule lines without a condition) are called "executed" rule lines, and their effects are performed as follows. A condition is judged by the flags at the time that rule line is examined (the effects of earlier rule lines in the same sequence are reflected).
  MESSAGE <text>: Output one line <text>.
  OPEN <room> <direction> <room2>: Make the exit of <room> in <direction> an exit without a lock whose destination is <room2> (if there is already an exit, replace it, including whether it has a lock and its locked state).
  REMOVE <item>: Set the place of the item to "nowhere". Items whose place is inside that item stay inside it.
  ADD <item> <room>: Set the place of the item to the floor of <room> (the item is moved wherever it is; its contents move with it).
  SET <flag>: Turn the flag on.
  CLEAR <flag>: Turn the flag off.
  POINTS <number>: Add <number> to the bonus points.
When ENTER lines are executed, nothing is output other than the lines from MESSAGE effects ("Done." and "Nothing happens." are not output).

10. Passage of a turn
When an action succeeds, after its response has been output, perform the following steps in order.
(1) Add 1 to the move count.
(2) Light fuel: examine the lit lights (wherever they are) one at a time in identifier order, and decrease their fuel by 1. A light whose fuel becomes 0 goes out. If a light that went out was a carried item or a room item at the time this step (2) started, output one line "The <name of that light> goes out.".
(3) Change of lighting: the baseline lighting is, for go, whether the current room was lit right after moving (before outputting the description and before executing the ENTER lines), and for any other action, whether the current room was lit before that action started. If the baseline was lit and the current room is now dark, output one line "It is now pitch dark."; if the baseline was dark and the current room is now lit, output the full description.
(4) Darkness: if the current room is now dark, add 1 to the darkness count; if it is lit, set the darkness count to 0. When the darkness count becomes 2, output one line "You hear something moving in the dark.". When the darkness count becomes 3, set the game end status to "dead".
(5) Victory: if the game end status is "none", there is at least one treasure in the world, and all treasures are stored, set the game end status to "won".
(6) If the game end status became "dead" in these steps, output one line "*** You have died ***"; if it became "won", output one line "*** You have won ***"; in either case, then output one line "You scored <score> points in <move count> moves.".
Once the game end status is no longer "none", only restore can be executed afterwards, and any other command becomes "The game is over." by 7.2 (4). When restore restores the game state, the game end status returns to "none" as it was when saved, so the game can continue.

11. Termination
When the "QUIT" line is read, output one line "Final score: <score> points in <move count> moves." and terminate. No "> " line is output for the "QUIT" line.

[Output format]
First output the full description of the start room, then for each command line output one line consisting of "> " followed by the command line, and the lines of the response, and finally output one line starting with "Final score: ". The response to one command line consists of the lines determined by 7. and 8. (the lines of the failure response when it fails), followed by the lines output in 10. when an action succeeds, in this order.
Every line is written exactly as the strings defined in [Processing] and ends with a newline. Numbers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Nothing other than the lines defined in [Processing], such as blank lines, is output. Room names, descriptions, item names, detailed descriptions and MESSAGE texts are output exactly as written in the world definition lines.

[Constraints]
- There are 1 to 200 world definition lines and 1 to 400 command lines. Every line has at most 100 characters and consists only of printable ASCII characters (character codes 32 to 126). Words in a world definition line are separated by exactly one space, and two or more consecutive spaces never occur, even inside <text>.
- Identifiers, aliases and name words are sequences of 1 to 12 lowercase letters. The nouns and name words of items are none of the, a, an, it, all, at, in, into, on, with, from, or the direction words.
- Weights are 0 to 99, treasure points 1 to 100, fuel 0 to 999, capacities 1 to 99, the number of POINTS 1 to 100, and the upper limit of the weight the player can carry 1 to 999.
- The room identifiers of the ROOM lines are all distinct, and the item identifiers of the ITEM lines are all distinct. There is exactly one TROPHY line, one START line and one MAXWEIGHT line. There is at most one EXIT line for the same pair of room and direction.
- The room of a DESC line is defined by a ROOM line before that line. The item of an INSIDE line, ALIAS line or DETAIL line is defined by an ITEM line before that line. The rooms and items referred to by other lines are also defined by some ROOM line or ITEM line somewhere in the world definition part.
- <item2> of an INSIDE line is a container, and INSIDE lines never put an item inside itself through nesting. The <place> of a fixed item is never PLAYER, and a fixed item is never the <item> of an INSIDE line.
- In the initial state, the carried weight is at most the upper limit of the weight the player can carry, and the content weight of every container is at most its capacity.
- Room names, descriptions, detailed descriptions and MESSAGE texts each have at least 1 character.
- All values are small; 32-bit integers are sufficient.
