[Overview]
Write a program that simulates the operation of a bank of several elevators (hereafter each one is called a "car"), using integer times (hereafter "ticks") as the unit. The program reads several scenarios from standard input, validates the configuration of each scenario, and then simulates by the rules: assigning groups of users who arrive at a landing to cars, car movement, door opening and closing, boarding and alighting, parking at home floors, taking cars out of service and back into service, users giving up and walking away, and fire service operation. It outputs a log with one event per line in time order and statistics per group, per car, per floor and per time period, and, when requested, also the results of a comparison in which the same input is simulated again with other assignment policies. Finally, it outputs a ranking of the scenarios and a summary per assignment policy.

[Input format]
The first line is the number of scenarios Q. Q scenario blocks follow, and each block has the following form.

SCENARIO name
configuration line
...
event line
...
END

The first line of a block is "SCENARIO" followed by the name, and the last line is a line consisting of "END" only. Between them there are zero or more configuration lines followed by zero or more event lines (all configuration lines come before all event lines). Every line of the input gets a line number counted over the whole input, with the line of Q as line 1.

A configuration line is a key word followed by zero or more values. The keys and values are specified as follows (configuration lines whose number of values or ranges do not match this specification may also appear; they are treated as errors in item 1 of [Processing]).

Required keys:
- FLOORS F: the number of floors, from 2 to 50. The floors are numbered 1, 2, ..., F from the bottom.
- CARS E: the number of cars, from 1 to 8. The cars are numbered 1 to E.
- CAPACITY C: the capacity of a car (number of people), from 1 to 20.
- DOOR D: the number of ticks from opening the doors until closing them, from 1 to 9.
- START s1 ... sE: the initial floors of cars 1 to E. There are E values, each from 1 to F.

Optional keys (the value used when the key is omitted is in parentheses):
- POLICY p (COST): the assignment policy. One of COST, NEAREST, ROUND, ETA.
- ZONES z1 ... zE (every car "1:F"): the zones of cars 1 to E. There are E values, each of the form "a:b" with 1 <= a < b <= F. A car whose zone is "a:b" serves only the floors from a to b inclusive.
- PARK p h1 ... hE (not used): parking at home floors. There are E+1 values. p is the waiting time (in ticks) before parking starts, from 0 to 100; h1 to hE are the home floors of cars 1 to E, each from 1 to F.
- PATIENCE P (not used): the number of ticks until a user gives up waiting, from 1 to 1000.
- BYPASS b (100): the full-load bypass threshold (a percentage), from 50 to 100.
- PERIOD w (not used): the width (in ticks) of the time periods used for the per-period statistics, from 1 to 1000.
- LIMIT L (1000): the time at which the simulation is cut off, from 1 to 5000.
- COMPARE q1 ... (not used): the assignment policies to simulate again for comparison. There are from 1 to 4 values, each one of COST, NEAREST, ROUND, ETA. The same policy must not appear twice or more.

An event line is a time t and a command name followed by zero or more arguments. The commands are as follows.
- t CALL a b n: a group of n people arrives at the landing on floor a and wants to go to floor b.
- t CANCEL g: the people of group g who are waiting at the landing stop waiting.
- t OUT e: takes car e out of service.
- t IN e: puts car e back into service.
- t HOLD e k: if the doors of car e are open, extends the time until they close by k ticks.
- t FIRE f: starts fire service operation. f is the evacuation floor.
- t RESET: ends fire service operation.
- t WHERE g: outputs the state of group g.
- t STATUS: outputs the state of all cars.

The following is guaranteed about the input.
- Words are separated by a single space, there is no whitespace at the start or end of a line, and there are no empty lines.
- A scenario name is a string of 1 to 16 characters consisting of letters, digits, "_" and "-".
- The first word (the key) of a configuration line is a word that starts with an uppercase letter and consists of uppercase letters, digits and "_", and it is not SCENARIO or END. It may be a word that is not in the list above.
- The values of FLOORS, CARS, CAPACITY, DOOR, START, PARK, PATIENCE, BYPASS, PERIOD and LIMIT are decimal integers of 1 to 9 digits, possibly preceded by "-". The values of ZONES are two such integers joined by ":". The values of POLICY and COMPARE, and the values of keys not in the list, are strings of printable ASCII characters without spaces.
- The first word (the time t) of an event line is an integer from 0 to 10000 written in decimal without extra leading zeros. The second word (the command name) is a word consisting of uppercase letters, and it may be a word that is not in the list above. When the command name is in the list, every argument is a decimal integer of 1 to 9 digits, possibly preceded by "-" (the number of arguments is not necessarily correct). When the command name is not in the list, the arguments are strings of printable ASCII characters without spaces.
- Word comparisons are case-sensitive.

[Processing]
1. Validating the configuration
For each scenario, check the configuration lines in the order (1) to (4) below and report only the first error found. For a scenario with an error, neither the event line checks nor the simulation are performed. Below, K is a key and i is a car number.
(1) Examine the configuration lines from top to bottom, and for each line test (a) and (b) in this order.
  (a) The key is not in the list above: "unknown key K"
  (b) The same key already appears on an earlier line: "duplicate key K"
(2) Examine the required keys in the order FLOORS, CARS, CAPACITY, DOOR, START; if one is missing: "missing key K"
(3) Examine the keys that are specified, in the order FLOORS, CARS, CAPACITY, DOOR, START, POLICY, ZONES, PARK, PATIENCE, BYPASS, PERIOD, LIMIT, COMPARE, and for each key test (a) and (b) in this order (by the time START, ZONES and PARK are examined, the values of FLOORS and CARS have been confirmed to be valid).
  (a) The number of values does not match the specification (E for START and ZONES, E+1 for PARK, 1 to 4 for COMPARE, and 1 for all others, where E is the value of CARS): "wrong number of values for K"
  (b) A value does not match the specification above (range, allowed words, no duplicates): "bad value for K"
(4) For cars 1, 2, ..., E in this order, test the following for each car in this order.
  - The initial floor is not within the car's zone: "car i starts outside its zone"
  - PARK is specified and the home floor is not within the car's zone: "car i home outside its zone"

2. Validating the event lines
If the configuration has no error, check the event lines from top to bottom. A line with an error is reported by outputting "line N: message" (N is the line number of that line) and is ignored; in the remaining checks and in the simulation it is treated as if it did not exist. A line without an error is called an "accepted line". For each line test (1) to (4) below in this order; the first one that applies gives the message.
(1) The command name is not in the list: "unknown command X" (X is the command name)
(2) The number of arguments is wrong (3 for CALL, 2 for HOLD, 1 for CANCEL, OUT, IN, FIRE and WHERE, 0 for RESET and STATUS): "wrong number of arguments for X"
(3) The time t is smaller than the time of an accepted line before this line: "time goes backwards"
(4) For each command, test the following in this order. Below, "fire service is active" means that, among the accepted FIRE and RESET lines before this line, the last one is a FIRE line, and "car e is out of service" means that, among the accepted OUT e and IN e lines (those whose argument is e) before this line, the last one is an OUT e line.
  - CALL a b n: a or b is not between 1 and F inclusive: "bad floor". a = b: "same floor". n is not between 1 and 99 inclusive: "bad count". There is no car whose zone contains both a and b: "no car serves a->b" (a and b are the argument values). Fire service is active: "fire service active"
  - CANCEL g, WHERE g: g is not between 1 and G inclusive (G is the number of accepted CALL lines before this line): "unknown group"
  - OUT e, IN e: e is not between 1 and E inclusive: "bad car". For OUT e, car e is out of service: "car e already out". For IN e, car e is not out of service: "car e already in" (e is the argument value)
  - HOLD e k: e is not between 1 and E inclusive: "bad car". k is not between 1 and 9 inclusive: "bad ticks"
  - FIRE f: f is not between 1 and F inclusive: "bad floor". Fire service is active: "fire already active"
  - RESET: fire service is not active: "fire not active"
  - STATUS: no errors.

3. Terms and state
- The accepted CALL lines get group numbers 1, 2, ... in input order. The group with number g is written "Gg". For the group of CALL a b n, a is called the landing floor, b the destination floor, and the time of the CALL line the call time; the direction is up (+1) if a < b and down (-1) if a > b.
- The number of people of a group who have not yet boarded a car and have not walked away is called the "waiting count". A group whose waiting count is 1 or more is called a "call". A call is either assigned to exactly one car or in the "pending" state, assigned to no car. Each car has a list of the calls assigned to it (in the order they were assigned; hereafter "assignment order"), and globally there is a list of pending calls (in the order they became pending).
- Each car has a current floor, a direction (up +1, down -1, stopped 0), a remaining door time (0 means the doors are closed, 1 or more means they are open), passengers, whether it is in service or out of service, and an idle start time. The passengers are a list (in boarding order) of entries each representing "how many people of which group boarded when" (hereafter "passenger entries"). The total number of people in the passenger entries is called the load.
- The "target floors" of a car are the destination floors of its passenger entries together with the landing floors of the calls assigned to it.
- A car is "bypassing" when load*100 >= C*b (b is the value of BYPASS).
- sign(x) is 1 if x is positive, -1 if x is negative, and 0 if x is 0.
- In the initial state, car i is on floor si, its direction is 0, its doors are closed, it has no passengers and no assigned calls, it is in service, and its idle start time is 0. There are no pending calls and fire service is not active. During the simulation, "fire service is active" refers to the period from executing FIRE until executing RESET.

4. Processing per time
For times t = 0, 1, 2, ... in this order, perform the following (S1) to (S4) at each time.
(S1) If PATIENCE is specified, make each group whose waiting count is 1 or more and for which t = call time+P walk away with the word ABANDON, in increasing order of group number (see "Walking away" in 6).
(S2) Execute the accepted event lines whose time is t, in input order (see 6).
(S3) For cars 1, 2, ..., E in this order, move each car by one tick (see 7).
(S4) If there is no accepted event line with a time greater than t, and every car satisfies the following condition, output "t END" and end the simulation. The condition is: the doors are closed, the car has no passengers and no assigned calls, and furthermore, if fire service is active, it is on the evacuation floor, and if fire service is not active, it is out of service, or PARK is not specified, or it is on its home floor. Remaining pending calls do not affect this test. If the simulation did not end, and t = L, output "t ABORT" and end the simulation (aborted). Otherwise, proceed to the next time.
The time at which the simulation ended is called the end time.

5. Assignment
When assigning a call, the candidates are the cars that are in service and whose zone contains both the landing floor and the destination floor of the group. When a car to exclude is specified, that car is also removed from the candidates. If there are no candidates, the assignment fails. Otherwise, choose one car according to the assignment policy, output "t Ee ASSIGN Gg cost=c" ("t Ee ASSIGN Gg" for ROUND), and append the call to the end of the chosen car's list. If the call was assigned to another car, remove it from that car's list.
- ROUND: among the candidates whose number is greater than the number of the car chosen last time by this policy (if none has been chosen yet in this simulation, it is regarded as car number 0), choose the one with the smallest number. If there is no such candidate, choose the candidate with the smallest number.
- COST, NEAREST, ETA: compute the following cost for each candidate and choose the car with the smallest cost (on a tie, the car with the smaller number). c is the cost of the chosen car.
In the cost computation, let f be the car's current floor, d its direction, x the landing floor of the call, u the direction of the call, and S the set of the car's target floors (the same floor counts once).
- If the policy is NEAREST, or d = 0: the cost is |f-x|.
- Otherwise, if u = d and (x-f)*d >= 0: the distance is |x-f|, and the via count is the number of floors y in S with (y-f)*d > 0 and (x-y)*d > 0.
- If neither applies: let m be the maximum of the floors in S and f if d is +1, or the minimum of the floors in S and f if d is -1; the distance is |f-m|+|m-x|, and the via count is the number of floors y in S with (y-f)*d > 0.
- In the second or third case, the cost for COST is the distance, and the cost for ETA is distance+D*(via count)+(the car's remaining door time).

6. Executing event lines
- CALL a b n: create the group, output "t CALL Gg a->b xn", and assign the call. If the assignment fails, append the call to the end of the pending list and output "t PENDING Gg".
- CANCEL g: if the waiting count of Gg is 1 or more, make it walk away with the word CANCEL. If the waiting count is 0, output "t CANCEL Gg ignored".
- Walking away: remove the call from the list of the car it is assigned to, or from the pending list, output "t WORD Gg xk" (WORD is the word and k is the waiting count), and set the waiting count to 0. These k people are counted as "people who walked away".
- OUT e: set car e out of service and output "t Ee OUT". Then remove all calls assigned to car e from car e's list in assignment order, and assign them one by one in that order. A call whose assignment fails is, as with CALL, appended to the end of the pending list and "t PENDING Gg" is output. A car that is out of service keeps its passengers and goes to let them off as described in 7.
- IN e: set car e in service, set its idle start time to t, and output "t Ee IN". Then take all pending calls out of the list and assign them one by one in that order. A call whose assignment fails is put back into the pending list in the same order, without any output.
- HOLD e k: if the remaining door time of car e is 1 or more, add k to it and output "t Ee HOLD r" (r is the remaining time after the addition). If it is 0, output "t Ee HOLD ignored".
- FIRE f: make fire service active with evacuation floor f, and output "t FIRE f". Then make all groups whose waiting count is 1 or more walk away with the word ABANDON, in increasing order of group number.
- RESET: end fire service, output "t RESET", and set the idle start time of every car to t.
- WHERE g: output "t WHERE Gg waiting=W car=X riding=R delivered=V lost=Z". W is the waiting count. X is "-" if W is 0, "Ee" (e is the number of that car) if the call is assigned, and "pending" if it is pending. R is, for the cars carrying passenger entries of Gg, in increasing order of car number, "Eexk" (k is the total number of people of Gg riding in that car) joined by ",". V is the number of people of Gg who got off at the destination floor, and Z is the number of people of Gg who walked away plus those who were evacuated (see 7 (4)).
- STATUS: for cars 1, 2, ..., E in this order output "t STATUS Ee floor=F dir=X door=K load=L stops=S calls=A service=Y", and finally output "t STATUS pending=P". F is the current floor, X is the direction (up for +1, down for -1, idle for 0), K is the remaining door time, and L is the load. S is the destination floors of the passenger entries, without duplicates, in increasing order, joined by ","; A is the assigned calls in assignment order, each in the form "Gg", joined by ","; P is the pending calls in the order of the pending list, each in the form "Gg", joined by ",". Y is "in" if the car is in service and "out" if it is out of service.
For R, S, A and P, use "-" if there is nothing to join.

7. Car behavior
In step (S3), perform the following (1) to (3) for each car. Below, t is the current time, f is the car's current floor and d is the car's direction.
(1) If the remaining door time is 1 or more, decrease it by 1. If it is still 1 or more after the decrease, this car's action for this tick is over.
(2) When the doors are closed (including when they closed in (1)), determine the next direction n and whether to stop.
  - Fire service is active: n = sign(evacuation floor-f). If n = 0 and the car has passengers, it stops.
  - Fire service is not active: determine n by "Choosing the direction" below. The car stops if one of its passenger entries has destination f, or if the car is not bypassing and one of its assigned calls has landing floor f and direction n.
(3) When the car stops, open the doors (see (4)). When it does not stop: if n != 0, set the car's direction to n and move to floor f+n. If n = 0, first, if d != 0, output "t Ee IDLE f" and set the direction to 0 and the idle start time to t. Then, regardless of whether d was 0, check parking as in (6).

Choosing the direction: if the car is bypassing, "the calls on this floor" are empty; otherwise they are the assigned calls whose landing floor is f (in assignment order). Determine n by the first of (a) to (e) below that applies.
  (a) d != 0, and there is a target floor y with (y-f)*d > 0, or one of the calls on this floor has direction d: n = d
  (b) There is a call on this floor: n = the direction of the first of the calls on this floor
  (c) d != 0, and there is a target floor y with (y-f)*d < 0: n = -d
  (d) d = 0, and there is an assigned call: n = sign(landing floor of the first call in assignment order-f)
  (e) None of the above: n = 0

(4) Opening the doors: output "t Ee OPEN f" and set the remaining door time to D. Then perform the following in this order.
  - Alighting: examine the passenger entries in boarding order, let off those whose destination is f, and output "t Ee ALIGHT Gg xk" (k is the number of people of that entry). While fire service is active, all passenger entries whose destination is not f are let off as well. In that case, again examine them in boarding order, output "t Ee ALIGHT Gg xk" for an entry whose destination is f and "t Ee EVACUATE Gg xk" for an entry whose destination is not f, and count the latter people as "evacuated people". If fire service is active, the door-opening processing ends here.
  - Re-choosing the direction: determine n again by "Choosing the direction", and if n != 0, set the car's direction to n.
  - Boarding: process the calls assigned at this point whose landing floor is f and whose direction is n, one by one in assignment order. Let k be the smaller of the free space (C-load) and the waiting count; if k >= 1, board k people (append a passenger entry boarded at time t to the end, and decrease the waiting count by k) and output "t Ee BOARD Gg xk" (if k = 0, nobody boards and no BOARD line is output). Then, if the waiting count has become 0, remove the call from the car's list. If some waiting count remains (including when k = 0), output "t Ee LEFT Gg xr" (r is the remaining waiting count) and reassign the call excluding this car. If the assignment fails, the call stays at the same position in this car's list.
(5) A car moves one floor per tick. Whether it stops at the floor it moved to is decided in step (S3) of the next tick.
(6) Parking: if fire service is not active, the car is in service, PARK is specified, f is not the home floor, and t-(idle start time) >= p, move one floor toward the home floor (the direction stays 0). If the car arrives at the home floor by this move, output "t Ee PARK h" (h is the home floor).

8. Values used for the statistics
- For each person who got off at the destination floor (a person let off by an ALIGHT line; hereafter a "delivered person"), the waiting time is (boarding time-call time) and the travel time is (alighting time-call time).
- Busy ticks: if, at the point when the car has finished moving in step (S3), its doors are open or it moved during that tick, the tick is counted as a busy tick of that car.
- Energy: add 3+(load just before the move) each time a car moves up one floor, 1 each time it moves down one floor, and 2 each time it opens its doors. Parking moves are included.

[Output format]
For each scenario, in input order, output the following.
- The first line is "== name ==".
- If the configuration has an error, output "error: " followed by the message from 1 on the second line, and end the output for that scenario.
- If there is no error, output the following line.
  "config: floors=F cars=E capacity=C door=D policy=P park=A patience=B bypass=W period=X limit=L compare=Y"
  A is the value of p ("off" if there is no PARK), B is the value of P ("off" if there is no PATIENCE), W is the value of BYPASS, X is the value of w ("off" if there is no PERIOD), and Y is the values of COMPARE joined by "," in the order given ("-" if there is no COMPARE). Omitted keys are shown with the value used when omitted.
- Then, for cars 1 to E in this order, output "Ee: start=S zone=A-B home=H". S is the initial floor, A and B are the ends of the zone, and H is the home floor ("-" if there is no PARK).
- Then output the "line N: message" lines from 2 in input order.
- Then output the log. Each log line starts with the time t, and the lines are in the order in which they arise under the rules of [Processing]. The lines that appear in the log are those described in 4 to 7: "t CALL Gg a->b xn", "t Ee ASSIGN Gg cost=c", "t Ee ASSIGN Gg", "t PENDING Gg", "t ABANDON Gg xk", "t CANCEL Gg xk", "t CANCEL Gg ignored", "t Ee OUT", "t Ee IN", "t Ee HOLD r", "t Ee HOLD ignored", "t FIRE f", "t RESET", "t WHERE ...", "t STATUS ...", "t Ee OPEN f", "t Ee ALIGHT Gg xk", "t Ee EVACUATE Gg xk", "t Ee BOARD Gg xk", "t Ee LEFT Gg xr", "t Ee IDLE f", "t Ee PARK h", "t END", "t ABORT".
- Then, for each group, in order of group number, output the following line.
  "Gg t=T a->b n=N delivered=V lost=Z wait=W travel=R cars=K"
  T is the call time, and a, b and N are the landing floor, the destination floor and the number of people. V and Z are the same as in WHERE. W is (time the last person boarded-call time) if every member of the group boarded a car, and "-" otherwise. R is (time the last person alighted-call time) if every member of the group is a delivered person, and "-" otherwise. K is the numbers of the cars that have ever carried people of the group, without duplicates, in increasing order, joined by "," ("-" if none).
- Then, for cars 1 to E in this order, output the following line.
  "Ee distance=M stops=O boarded=B busy=U util=X% energy=Y"
  M is the total number of floors moved, O is the number of times the doors were opened, B is the total number of people boarded, U is the number of busy ticks, X is U*100/(end time+1), and Y is the energy.
- Then, for each floor f such that at least one group has landing floor f, in increasing order of f, output "Ff: groups=... passengers=... delivered=... avg_wait=...". The values are, in order, the number of such groups, their total number of people, the number of delivered people among those groups, and the average waiting time of those people.
- If PERIOD is specified, then, for each integer k such that at least one group has floor(call time/w) = k, in increasing order of k, output "Pk S-E: groups=... passengers=... delivered=... avg_wait=...". S = k*w and E = k*w+w-1, and each value is the same as in the F lines, computed for the groups with floor(call time/w) = k.
- Then output the following line.
  "summary: groups=... passengers=... delivered=... abandoned=... evacuated=... avg_wait=... avg_travel=... max_wait=... end=..."
  The values are, in order, the number of groups, the total number of people of all groups, the number of delivered people, the number of people who walked away, the number of evacuated people, the average waiting time of the delivered people, the average travel time of the delivered people, the maximum waiting time of the delivered people, and the end time.
- If COMPARE is specified, then, for each policy q in the order given, run the simulation with the value of POLICY changed to q (without outputting the log), and output "compare q: delivered=... avg_wait=... avg_travel=... max_wait=... end=... status=Z". The values are computed in the same way as the values of the same names in the summary line, and Z is "end" if the simulation ended with END and "abort" if it ended with ABORT. The event lines used are exactly the lines accepted in 2.

Averages (avg_wait, avg_travel) are truncated after the second decimal place (the third and further decimal places are discarded) and shown with 2 decimals, and util is truncated after the first decimal place (the second and further decimal places are discarded) and shown with 1 decimal, always writing all decimal digits (e.g. 4.50, 0.00, 47.0). Averages and maximums are "-" if there are 0 people to aggregate.

After all scenarios, output the following.
- One line "== ranking ==".
- The scenarios whose simulation ended with END and that have at least one delivered person, sorted by increasing average waiting time, then by increasing average travel time when those are equal, then by input order when those are also equal, are output one per line, numbered from 1, as "rank. name policy=P avg_wait=... avg_travel=... max_wait=...". P is the value of POLICY of that scenario, and the other values are the same values, in the same format, as in the summary line. Comparisons use the exact values before truncation.
- Then output the other scenarios in input order: "- name invalid" for one whose configuration had an error, "- name aborted" for one that was aborted, and "- name empty" for one that ended with END with 0 delivered people.
- One line "== policies ==".
- For each policy in the order COST, NEAREST, ROUND, ETA such that at least one scenario in the ranking has that policy as its POLICY value, output "P: runs=N avg_wait=... avg_travel=...". N is the number of such scenarios, and avg_wait and avg_travel are, over all those scenarios together, (total waiting time)/(total number of delivered people) and (total travel time)/(total number of delivered people), in the same format as above. The results of the COMPARE simulations are not used for the ranking or for the per-policy summary.

All output consists of ASCII characters. Within a line, separate items with a single space, put no whitespace at the end of any line, and end every line with a newline. Do not output empty lines.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= Q <= 50. The input has at most 1000 lines, and each line has at most 200 characters.
- The simulation always ends by time L.
- Every value, including intermediate values, is an integer whose absolute value is less than 2^53 and can be computed exactly (the ranking comparisons can also be done in integers by cross-multiplying the numerators and denominators of the averages). No real-number computation is needed.
