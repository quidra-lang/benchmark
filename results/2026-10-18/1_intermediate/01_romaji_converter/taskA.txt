[Overview]
Write a program that parses words of Japanese written in romaji (ASCII letters) as sequences of morae and converts them between romanization systems such as Hepburn, Kunrei-shiki, Nihon-shiki and wapuro (keyboard) style. The program also checks spellings, counts morae and syllables, sorts in gojuon order or iroha order, searches by morae, checks the mora counts of haiku and tanka, and judges palindromes and shiritori. The program reads commands from standard input and writes the result of each command as one line to standard output. No kana characters are used; both input and output are handled entirely in ASCII characters.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N, and each of the following N lines contains one command.
A command line consists of the command name, a fixed number of arguments, and, for some commands, a text, in this order, separated by single spaces. The text is the entire part of the line after the single space that follows the command name and the arguments, up to the end of the line; spaces may be consecutive inside it. The command formats are as follows (system, from, to, system1 and system2 are names of romanization systems).
SET option value
TABLE system row
CONVERT from to text
NORMALIZE text
REVERSE system text
VALID system text
DETECT text
DIFF from to text
SAME system1 system2 text
MORAE system text
SYLLABLES system text
ROW system text
STATS system text
SORT system text
INDEX system text
FIND system pattern text
HAIKU system text
KAIBUN system text
SHIRITORI system text

[Processing]
1. Morae
A word is treated as a sequence of morae. There are 108 "syllabic morae" and 3 kinds of "special morae" (the moraic n (hatsuon), the small tsu (sokuon) and the long-vowel mark (chouon)). The syllabic morae are the 72 morae in the following table and the 36 contracted morae described after the table. A syllabic mora is denoted by its identifier (a lowercase name identical to its Nihon-shiki spelling), and each syllabic mora belongs to one row and one dan (vowel column of the table). Each line of the table is written as "row name: identifiers of dan a, dan i, dan u, dan e, dan o" in this order; the ya row and the wa row have only the dans shown in parentheses.
a: a i u e o
ka: ka ki ku ke ko
sa: sa si su se so
ta: ta ti tu te to
na: na ni nu ne no
ha: ha hi hu he ho
ma: ma mi mu me mo
ya: ya (dan a) yu (dan u) yo (dan o)
ra: ra ri ru re ro
wa: wa (dan a) wi (dan i, kana wi) we (dan e, kana we) wo (dan o, kana wo)
ga: ga gi gu ge go
za: za zi zu ze zo
da: da di du de do
ba: ba bi bu be bo
pa: pa pi pu pe po
In addition, each of the 12 rows ka, sa, ta, na, ha, ma, ra, ga, za, da, ba and pa has 3 contracted morae obtained by replacing the final i of the identifier of its dan-i mora with ya, yu and yo (e.g. kya, kyu, kyo from ki; sya, syu, syo from si; dya, dyu, dyo from di). A contracted mora belongs to that row, and its dan is ya, yu or yo respectively. Hereafter, "table order" means the order in which the rows of the table above, from the a row to the pa row, are listed row by row from left to right, followed by the contracted morae in the order of the ka, sa, ta, na, ha, ma, ra, ga, za, da, ba, pa rows, and within each row in the order ya, yu, yo.
The following terms are used.
- Vowel of a syllabic mora: the last letter of its identifier (one of a, i, u, e, o).
- Vowel mora: one of the 5 syllabic morae whose identifiers are a, i, u, e, o.
- Voiced mora and semi-voiced mora: the morae (contracted ones included) of the ga, za, da and ba rows are called voiced morae, and the morae (contracted ones included) of the pa row are called semi-voiced morae.
- Devoicing: when the first letter of an identifier is g, z, d, b or p, replacing it with k, s, t, h or h respectively (e.g. gya becomes kya, and pi becomes hi). Other identifiers do not change.
- Base row: the 10 rows a, ka, sa, ta, na, ha, ma, ya, ra and wa are called base rows. The base row of a row is the row itself for these 10 rows, and the ka, sa, ta, ha and ha rows for the ga, za, da, ba and pa rows respectively.

2. Romanization systems and spellings
There are 5 romanization systems: HEPBURN (Hepburn), TRADITIONAL (traditional Hepburn), KUNREI (Kunrei-shiki), NIHON (Nihon-shiki) and WAPURO (wapuro style). In each system, the spelling of a syllabic mora is the same as its identifier except for the cases listed below.
- HEPBURN: si is spelled shi, ti is spelled chi, tu tsu, hu fu, zi ji, di ji, du zu, wi i, we e, and wo o. A contracted mora is spelled by replacing the first 2 letters of its identifier with sh if they are sy, with ch if they are ty, and with j if they are zy or dy (e.g. sya is sha, tyu is chu, zyo is jo, dya is ja).
- TRADITIONAL: the spellings of the syllabic morae are the same as in HEPBURN (only the way the moraic n is written differs).
- KUNREI: di is spelled zi, du zu, wi i, we e, wo o, dya zya, dyu zyu, and dyo zyo.
- NIHON: every spelling is the same as the identifier.
- WAPURO: si is spelled shi, ti chi, tu tsu, hu fu, and zi ji, and a contracted mora is spelled by replacing the first 2 letters of its identifier with sh if they are sy, with ch if they are ty, and with j if they are zy. di, du, wi, we, wo, dya, dyu and dyo are spelled as their identifiers.
When 2 syllabic morae have the same spelling within one system, that spelling is read as the mora that appears earlier in table order. That is, in HEPBURN and TRADITIONAL the spellings i, e, o, ji, zu, ja, ju, jo are read as the morae with identifiers i, e, o, zi, zu, zya, zyu, zyo respectively, and in KUNREI the spellings i, e, o, zi, zu, zya, zyu, zyo are read as the morae with identifiers i, e, o, zi, zu, zya, zyu, zyo respectively.

3. Text and words
The characters allowed in a text are letters (A-Z, a-z), "'" (apostrophe), "-" (hyphen), and separators. The separators are the space, the digits 0-9, and the symbols ".", ",", "!", "?", ";", ":", "(", ")" and the double quote ("); in the texts of HAIKU and SAME, "/" is also a separator. A longest part of a text consisting only of consecutive letters, "'" and "-" is called a "word". A "column", which denotes a position in a text, is counted with the first character of the text as column 1.
The use of uppercase and lowercase letters in a word must follow one of the following 3 patterns. The patterns are tested in the order lowercase pattern, capitalized pattern, uppercase pattern, and the first pattern that applies is the pattern of the word.
- Lowercase pattern: all letters are lowercase (a word that contains no letter at all also has this pattern).
- Capitalized pattern: the first character of the word is an uppercase letter and all other letters are lowercase (a word consisting of a single uppercase letter only also has this pattern).
- Uppercase pattern: there are 2 or more letters and all of them are uppercase.
When a word is parsed, all of its letters are converted to lowercase before parsing.

4. Parsing a word
A word is read in order from the beginning and divided into a sequence of morae. For each mora, the column of the character at which reading of that mora began is recorded. "A syllable spelling matches at position p" means that the part starting at position p begins with one of the spellings of the syllabic morae of the system; when several spellings match, the longest one is used. Let c be the character at the current position and c2 the character after it (a character beyond the end of the word is "none"). Check the following rules from top to bottom and apply the single first rule that applies; repeat this until the end of the word. The column of an error is the column of c.
(1) If c is "-": if the preceding mora in the same word is a syllabic mora, read a long-vowel mark and advance 1 character. If c is the first character of the word, or the preceding mora is a special mora, it is a "misplaced long mark" error.
(2) If c is "'": it is a "misplaced apostrophe" error (an apostrophe in a correct position is read together with n by rule (5)).
(3) If a syllable spelling matches at the current position: read the syllabic mora with that spelling and advance by the length of the spelling.
(4) If the system is WAPURO and "xtu" follows from the current position: read a small tsu and advance 3 characters.
(5) If c is n: read a moraic n. In WAPURO, advance 2 characters if c2 is n, and 1 character otherwise. In the other systems, advance 2 characters if c2 is "'", and 1 character otherwise.
(6) If the system is TRADITIONAL, c is m, and c2 is one of b, m, p: read a moraic n and advance 1 character.
(7) If c is a letter other than a, i, u, e, o, n, c2 is the same character as c, and a syllable spelling matches at the position of c2: read a small tsu and advance 1 character.
(8) If the system is HEPBURN or TRADITIONAL, c is t, c2 and the character after it are "ch", and a syllable spelling matches at the position of c2: read a small tsu and advance 1 character.
(9) If none of these applies: it is an "unknown syllable" error.
Example: in HEPBURN, "Kin'en" is read as ki, moraic n, e, moraic n; "matcha" is read as ma, small tsu, tya; and "ko-hi-" is read as ko, long-vowel mark, hi, long-vowel mark. In WAPURO, "kanna" is read as ka, moraic n, a.

5. Writing a word
This section defines how a sequence of morae is written as a string in a system T.
First, the "long-vowel pairs" are determined. Look at the morae of the word in order from the beginning: if the mora being looked at is a syllabic mora, the next mora is a vowel mora, and the combination of the vowel of the mora being looked at and the identifier of the next mora is one of "the same letter", "o and u" or "e and i", these 2 morae form a long-vowel pair, and looking continues from the mora after the pair. Otherwise, move on to the next mora (e.g. in o, o, u the first 2 morae form a pair, and the last u does not form a pair).
A "long element" is a long-vowel mark, or the latter mora of a long-vowel pair whose combination is "o and o", "o and u" or "u and u". The "vowel" of a long element is the vowel of the mora immediately before it (the mora immediately before a long-vowel mark is always a syllabic mora).
Next, write each mora in order from the beginning and concatenate the results. Below, the "next spelling" means the spelling in T of the next mora when the next mora is a syllabic mora; when there is no next mora or the next mora is a special mora, the next spelling is "none".
(a) A long element is written as follows, depending on the value of the setting LONG.
  KEEP: write "-" for a long-vowel mark, and the spelling for a vowel mora.
  DROP: write nothing.
  PASSPORT: write "h" if the vowel of the long element is o and the next spelling does not begin with a vowel letter (a, i, u, e, o) (this includes none); otherwise write nothing.
  HYPHEN: write "-".
  DOUBLE: write the letter of the vowel of the long element.
(b) A syllabic mora that is not a long element is written as its spelling in T.
(c) A moraic n is written as follows.
  WAPURO: write "nn" if the next mora is a moraic n or the next spelling begins with a vowel letter, y or n; otherwise write "n".
  TRADITIONAL: write "m" if the next spelling begins with one of b, m, p, "n'" if it begins with a vowel letter or y, and "n" otherwise.
  HEPBURN, KUNREI, NIHON: write "n'" if the next spelling begins with a vowel letter or y, and "n" otherwise.
(d) For a small tsu, if the next spelling begins with a character other than a, i, u, e, o, n, write the first character of the next spelling. However, when T is HEPBURN or TRADITIONAL and the next spelling begins with "ch", write "t". When the next spelling is none or begins with one of a, i, u, e, o, n, write "xtu" in WAPURO; in the other systems it is a "small tsu cannot be written" error at the column of that small tsu.
Finally, the written string is adjusted to the pattern of the word: for the lowercase pattern it is left as is, for the capitalized pattern its first character is made uppercase, and for the uppercase pattern all of its letters are made uppercase (the first character of the written string is always a letter). Which pattern it is adjusted to is determined by the setting CASE: KEEP means the pattern of the original word, and LOWER, TITLE and UPPER mean the lowercase pattern, the capitalized pattern and the uppercase pattern respectively.
The "canonical form" of a word is the sequence of morae of the word written in the system in which the word was parsed, with LONG taken as KEEP, and adjusted to the pattern of the original word.
Example: HEPBURN "Shinbun" is written "Sinbun" in KUNREI, "kin'en" is written "kinnen" in WAPURO, and "shinbun" is written "shimbun" in TRADITIONAL. Writing HEPBURN "Toukyou" in HEPBURN gives "Toukyou" if LONG is KEEP, "Tokyo" if DROP, "Tohkyoh" if PASSPORT, "To-kyo-" if HYPHEN, and "Tookyoo" if DOUBLE.

6. Settings
There are 3 settings, LONG, CASE and ORDER, initially KEEP, KEEP and GOJUON respectively. Settings change only through the SET command, and are used by all subsequent commands.

7. Order of checks and errors
Each command is first checked in the following order; as soon as a problem is found, that error is printed and the command ends (settings do not change).
(1) If the command name (the part of the line before the first space, or the whole line if there is no space) is none of the 19 names: "unknown command NAME" (NAME is the command name).
(2) Number of arguments: for SET and TABLE, if the line split at spaces has fewer than 3 parts: "missing argument"; if it has more: "too many arguments". For the other commands, with k the number of arguments shown in the format, if the line contains fewer than k+1 spaces (that is, if there is no text): "missing argument".
(3) The arguments that are names of systems (system, from, to, system1, system2) are checked from left to right; if one is none of the 5 names: "unknown system NAME" (NAME is that argument).
(4) For commands that have a text, the following checks are then made in this order. For FIND, the pattern check (see FIND) is made first. Next, if the text contains a character that is not allowed, "invalid character" at the column of the leftmost such character. Next, for HAIKU and SAME, the shape check of the text (see each command) is made. Next, if the text contains no word at all, "no words". Next, if there is a word that fits none of the 3 patterns, "mixed case" for the leftmost such word, at the column of the first character of that word. Next, the words are parsed from left to right, and the first parse error that occurs is reported (the system and the way of reading used for parsing are given under each command; DETECT does not parse here and is processed as described under DETECT). Finally, the processing of each command is performed, and among the writing errors (small tsu cannot be written) that occur in it, the first one in processing order is reported.
The output format of errors is given in [Output format].

8. Commands
The processing of each command and its output in the normal case are given below. Every number in the output is a decimal integer with no sign and no extra zeros.
SET option value
If option is none of LONG, CASE, ORDER, it is an "unknown option OPTION" error; if value is not a value of that setting, it is an "unknown value VALUE" error (OPTION and VALUE are those arguments). The value of LONG is one of KEEP, DROP, PASSPORT, HYPHEN, DOUBLE; the value of CASE is one of KEEP, LOWER, UPPER, TITLE; and the value of ORDER is either GOJUON or IROHA. Change the setting to value and print "option=value" (e.g. LONG=DROP).
TABLE system row
If row is none of the 15 row names, it is an "unknown row ROW" error (ROW is that argument). Print the spellings in system of the syllabic morae of that row in table order, separated by single spaces (e.g. TABLE HEPBURN sa gives sa shi su se so sha shu sho).
CONVERT from to text
Parse the words with from, and write each word in to. Print the text in which each word is replaced by its written string and the separators are kept as they are.
NORMALIZE text
Converts a text in which various systems are mixed into HEPBURN. Words are parsed with a special way of reading that differs only in the following points. As syllable spellings, all spellings of any of HEPBURN, KUNREI, NIHON and WAPURO are used (the same spelling becomes the same mora whichever system it is read in). Rules (4), (6) and (8) are applied regardless of the system. In rule (5), advance 2 characters if c2 is "'"; advance 2 characters if c2 is n and the character after it is none of a vowel letter, y, "'" (this includes none) (nn as 1 moraic n); otherwise advance 1 character. Write the sequence of morae obtained in this way in HEPBURN, and print in the same form as CONVERT.
REVERSE system text
Parse the words with system; for each word, first replace each long-vowel mark with the vowel mora whose identifier is the same as the vowel of that long element (its column stays the column of the long-vowel mark), then reverse the sequence of morae, and write it in system. Print in the same form as CONVERT (e.g. HEPBURN "kitsune" becomes "netsuki").
VALID system text
Parse the words with system; if every word matches its canonical form, print "VALID". Otherwise, for the leftmost word that does not match, compare the original word and its canonical form character by character from the beginning, and let the first differing character be the k-th character (when one is a prefix of the other, k is the length of the shorter one plus 1); let c be the column of the first character of the original word plus k-1, and print "INVALID col c: expected W" (W is the canonical form). Example: HEPBURN "kan'ji" gives "INVALID col 4: expected kanji".
DETECT text
For the 5 systems, in the order HEPBURN, TRADITIONAL, KUNREI, NIHON, WAPURO, parse the words with that system. The verdict is error if some word causes a parse error, canonical if every word matches its canonical form, and readable otherwise (parse errors here are not printed and are used only for the verdict). Print "HEPBURN=verdict TRADITIONAL=verdict KUNREI=verdict NIHON=verdict WAPURO=verdict".
DIFF from to text
Parse the words with from, and for each word from left to right make the string A written in from and the string B written in to. With k the number of words for which A and B differ and n the number of words, print "0 of n" if k is 0; otherwise print "k of n: " followed by "A>B" of those words in their original order, separated by single spaces.
SAME system1 system2 text
Shape check of the text: unless the text contains exactly 1 "/" and both its left side and its right side contain 1 or more words, it is an "expected two texts" error. Parse the words on the left side with system1 and the words on the right side with system2 (starting with the words on the left side). For each word, its sequence of morae with the long elements removed is the "loose form" of that word. Compare the left and right words one to one from the beginning; if the first pair whose loose forms differ is the i-th pair (counting from 1), print "DIFFERENT at word i". If the loose forms of all pairs are the same but the numbers of words on the left and right differ, print "DIFFERENT at word i" with i the smaller of the two numbers plus 1. Otherwise, print "EXACT" if the sequences of morae of the left and right words are all exactly the same, and "LOOSE" otherwise.
MORAE system text
Parse the words with system, and print "M: " followed by, for each word, its morae joined with ".", the words separated by single spaces. M is the total number of morae. A syllabic mora is shown as its spelling in system, a moraic n as "N", a small tsu as "Q", and a long-vowel mark as "-" (e.g. HEPBURN "Nippon ko-hi-" gives 8: ni.Q.po.N ko.-.hi.-).
SYLLABLES system text
Parse the words with system and divide each word into syllables. Among the second and later morae of a word, the special morae and the latter mora of a long-vowel pair (whatever its combination) join the preceding syllable, and every other mora starts a new syllable. Each syllable is the concatenation of the parts of the canonical form in which the morae of that syllable were each written, with all letters converted to lowercase. Print "S: " followed by, for each word, its syllables joined with ".", the words separated by single spaces. S is the total number of syllables (e.g. HEPBURN "Toukyou kin'en" gives 4: tou.kyou kin'.en).
ROW system text
Parse the words with system; for every mora, make "spelling: row dan" for a syllabic mora and "display: special" for a special mora, and print them in text order separated by ", " (a comma and a space). The spelling is the spelling in system, row and dan are the row name and the dan of the table (the dan of a contracted mora is ya, yu or yo), and display is N, Q or - as in MORAE (e.g. HEPBURN "jisho" gives ji: za i, sho: sa yo).
STATS system text
Parse the words with system, and print the following items on one line, separated by single spaces. First "morae=M" (M is the total number of morae); next, for the base rows a, ka, sa, ta, na, ha, ma, ya, ra, wa in this order, with c the number of syllabic morae whose row has that row as its base row (for example, for ka these are the morae of the ka row and the ga row, contracted morae included), "name=c" (name is the row name) only for the rows whose c is 1 or more; then "voiced=", "semivoiced=", "contracted=", "N=", "Q=" and "L=", each followed by the number of voiced morae, semi-voiced morae, contracted morae, moraic n, small tsu and long-vowel marks respectively (these last 6 items are printed even when the number is 0).
SORT system text
Parse the words with system, sort them by the collation order below, and print the words in their original spelling, separated by single spaces.
Collation order: from each mora, a sequence of collation elements (triples of base kana, voicing and size) is made as follows. A long-vowel mark has no collation element. A moraic n is (N, 0, 0), and a small tsu is (tu, 0, 1). A syllabic mora that is not a contracted mora is 1 element whose base kana is its devoiced identifier. A contracted mora is 2 elements: an element whose base kana is the devoiced identifier of the dan-i mora of its row (e.g. ki for gya, si for zyo, ti for dyu, hi for pya), followed by (ya, 0, 1) (likewise for yu and yo). The voicing is 1 for the first element made from a voiced mora, 2 for the first element made from a semi-voiced mora, and 0 otherwise. The size is as written above: only the small tsu and the second element of a contracted mora have 1. The rank of the base kana follows, if ORDER is GOJUON, the order
a i u e o ka ki ku ke ko sa si su se so ta ti tu te to na ni nu ne no ha hi hu he ho ma mi mu me mo ya yu yo ra ri ru re ro wa wi we wo N
and, if ORDER is IROHA, the order
i ro ha ni ho he to ti ri nu ru wo wa ka yo ta re so tu ne na ra mu u wi no o ku ya ma ke hu ko e te a sa ki yu me mi si we hi mo se su N
Two words are compared by (i) the sequence of the ranks of the base kana of their collation elements, (ii) the sequence of voicings, (iii) the sequence of sizes, (iv) the sequence that has, for each mora, 1 for a long-vowel mark and 0 otherwise, and (v) their order in the text, in this order, and the first item that differs decides the order. Two sequences are compared from the beginning, and the one with the smaller value at the first differing position comes first; when one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first.
INDEX system text
Print the words, sorted in the same way as SORT, grouped by heading. The heading of a word is determined from the base kana of its first collation element: if ORDER is GOJUON, it is "N" if the base kana is N, and otherwise the name of the row to which the base kana belongs (e.g. ka for ki); if ORDER is IROHA, it is the base kana itself ("N" for N). In sorted order, each run of consecutive words with the same heading forms one group; each group is written as "heading: word word ..." (the words in their original spelling, separated by single spaces), and the groups are joined with "; " (a semicolon and a space) and printed (e.g. HEPBURN "sakura kaki gakkou ame" gives a: ame; ka: kaki gakkou; sa: sakura).
FIND system pattern text
pattern is checked as follows before the checks of the text; if there is a problem, it is an "invalid pattern" error. pattern must be a string consisting only of lowercase letters, "'", "-", "?" and "*". Each part between the positions of "?" and "*" when pattern is split at them (a part may be empty) is parsed with system as an independent word (an empty part is a sequence of 0 morae), and no part may cause a parse error. In this way, pattern is regarded as a sequence of morae, "?" and "*". "?" matches any 1 mora, "*" matches any sequence of 0 or more morae, and a mora matches only the same mora. Parse the words of the text with system; with k the number of words whose entire sequence of morae matches the entire pattern and n the number of words, print "0 of n" if k is 0; otherwise print "k of n: " followed by the matching words in their original spelling, in their original order, separated by single spaces.
HAIKU system text
Shape check of the text: each part of the text split at "/" is a line of the poem; if the number of lines is neither 3 nor 5, or some line contains no word at all, it is an "expected 3 or 5 lines" error. Parse the words with system, and count the total number of morae of the words contained in each line. With 3 lines, compare the counts with 5, 7, 5 as a haiku; with 5 lines, compare them with 5, 7, 5, 7, 7 as a tanka. The verdict is "OK" if all of them match and "NG" otherwise; print, as in "OK haiku 5-7-5", the verdict, a space, haiku or tanka, a space, and the mora counts of the lines joined with "-".
KAIBUN system text
Parse the words with system, and make the sequence of the base kana of the collation elements of all morae of the whole text, in order. Print "YES " if this sequence reads the same backwards, and "NO " otherwise, followed by the sequence joined with "." (e.g. HEPBURN "takeyabu yaketa" gives YES ta.ke.ya.hu.ya.ke.ta).
SHIRITORI system text
Parse the words with system, and check whether they are a correct shiritori. The "head" of a word is the base kana of the first collation element of its first mora, and the "tail" of a word is the base kana of the last collation element of its last mora other than long-vowel marks (e.g. the head of "gakkou" is ka and its tail is u, the tail of "jisho" is yo, and the tail of "ko-hi-" is hi). For the words from left to right, for the i-th word W counting from 1, check the following 3 conditions in this order, and stop at the first one that holds.
- i is 2 or more and the head of W differs from the tail of the preceding word: "NG i: W must start with X" (X is the spelling in system of the syllabic mora whose identifier is the tail of the preceding word; for example, in HEPBURN, if the tail is si, X is shi).
- The sequence of morae of W is the same as the sequence of morae of some earlier word: "NG i: W is repeated".
- The tail of W is N: "NG i: W ends with N".
If none of them holds up to and including the last word, print "OK n" with n the number of words. W is the word in its original spelling.

[Output format]
Exactly 1 line is printed per command. The output in the normal case is as given under each command in [Processing]. On an error, print "ERROR " followed by the message. The message is one of the following, where NAME, OPTION, VALUE and ROW are the corresponding argument and c is a column.
unknown command NAME
missing argument
too many arguments
unknown system NAME
unknown option OPTION
unknown value VALUE
unknown row ROW
invalid pattern
expected two texts
expected 3 or 5 lines
no words
col c: invalid character
col c: mixed case
col c: misplaced long mark
col c: misplaced apostrophe
col c: unknown syllable
col c: small tsu cannot be written
(Example: ERROR col 3: unknown syllable)
Put exactly 1 space after ":" and between words and items, as shown. The output consists only of ASCII characters, no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline. The output consists only of the N lines of command results.
