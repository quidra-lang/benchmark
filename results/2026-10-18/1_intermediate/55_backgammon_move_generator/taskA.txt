[Overview]
Write a single program that acts as a backgammon referee, a legal move generator and a simple playing engine. Following commands read from standard input, it sets up positions, enumerates legal plays, checks and applies plays, shows pip counts and a board diagram, makes automatic plays with an evaluation function, handles the doubling cube, undoes and redoes plays, plays self-play games and matches with random dice, and so on, and writes the results to standard output.

[Input format]
The first line is the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. Each line consists of a command name and zero or more arguments, separated by single spaces, with no whitespace at the beginning or end of the line. The input consists of these N+1 lines only.

[Processing]
Process the commands one at a time in input order, writing each command's output immediately. In what follows, a string enclosed in double quotes appears verbatim in the input or output. In output descriptions, a part such as <v> is replaced by its value.

1. Board and numbering
- The board has points 1 to 24. Points are numbered as seen from White; these numbers are called board numbers. White (W) and Black (B) have 15 checkers each, and a point can hold checkers of only one color.
- White moves in the direction of decreasing board numbers (from 24 toward 1), and the points with board numbers 1-6 are White's home. Black moves in the direction of increasing board numbers (from 1 toward 24), and the points with board numbers 19-24 are Black's home.
- Each color has a bar, where its hit checkers are placed, and an off area, where its checkers removed from the board by bearing off are placed.
- The numbering as seen from a color is called that color's "own numbers". White's own number of a point equals its board number, and Black's own number is 25 minus the board number. In own numbers, both colors move in the direction of decreasing numbers, and points 1-6 are the home. A color's own bar is treated as 25 and its own off area as 0.
- When the location of an opposing checker is expressed in a color's own numbers, the opponent's bar is treated as 0 and the opponent's off area as 25.
- Sections 3 and 4 are written in the own numbers of the side to move. The point numbers in play notation and in plays given to PLAY are also the own numbers of the side making the play. For example, a Black play uses Black's own numbers, not board numbers.

2. State
The program keeps the following state.
- Position: for both colors, the number of checkers on each point, on the bar and in the off area
- Side to move ("W" or "B")
- Cube value (starts at 1, doubles each time, maximum 64) and cube owner (one of "center", "W", "B")
- Whether a double offer is pending
- Whether the game is over
- Score (the cumulative points of White and of Black)
- History (the sequence of plays made) and redo list (the sequence of undone plays)
In the initial state, the position is the standard starting position, the side to move is White, the cube value is 1 with owner "center", no double is pending and the game is not over, the score is 0 for both White and Black, and the history and redo list are empty. In the standard starting position, each color has, in its own numbers, 2 checkers on point 24, 5 on point 13, 3 on point 8 and 5 on point 6, and 0 checkers on the bar and in the off area (in board numbers: White has 2 on point 24, 5 on point 13, 3 on point 8 and 5 on point 6; Black has 2 on point 1, 5 on point 12, 3 on point 17 and 5 on point 19).

3. Sub-moves and legal plays
- Moving one checker of the side to move using one die number d is called a sub-move. A sub-move follows these rules.
  (1) If the side to move has checkers on its bar, only a checker on the bar (25) can be moved. Otherwise, a checker can be moved from any of points 1-24 that holds checkers of the side to move. Let s be the starting point.
  (2) If s - d is 1 or more, the destination is t = s - d. The checker cannot be moved if t holds two or more opposing checkers. If t holds exactly one opposing checker, that checker is hit and moved to the opponent's bar, and the moving checker is placed on t.
  (3) If s - d is 0 or less, the sub-move is a bear-off. Bearing off is allowed only when all checkers of the side to move that have not yet been borne off are on points 1-6 (none on the bar or on points 7-24). Furthermore, when s - d is negative, it is allowed only when the side to move has no checker on any of points s + 1 through 6. A borne-off checker moves to the off area, and the destination of this sub-move is 0.
- A play is a sequence of zero or more sub-moves made in succession using the two dice of the turn. When the two dice are different, each die may be used at most once, in either order. When the two dice are the same (doubles), that number may be used up to four times. Each sub-move must satisfy (1)-(3) in the position after all earlier sub-moves have been made.
- Let M be the maximum number of sub-moves among the possible plays. The legal plays are the plays with exactly M sub-moves. However, when M = 1, the two dice are different, and a sub-move using the larger die is possible, only the plays that use the larger die are legal. When M = 0, the only legal play is the play that does nothing.
- Plays that reach the same final position (the numbers of checkers on each point, on the bar and in the off area, for both colors) are regarded as one and the same play, even if the order or combination of their sub-moves differs.

4. Play notation and canonical order
- A play is written by the following procedure. Represent each sub-move of the play as a pair (starting point, destination), and let H be the set of points where a hit occurred.
  (1) Arrange the pairs into a list in descending order of starting point, and for equal starting points in descending order of destination.
  (2) Repeat the following merge until it can no longer be applied. Scanning the list from the front, let a be the first pair satisfying the condition: its destination t is 1 or more, t is not in H, and the list contains a pair whose starting point is t. Let b be the frontmost pair in the list among the pairs whose starting point is t. Remove a and b from the list, add the pair (starting point of a, destination of b), and re-sort the list in the order of (1).
  (3) Merge adjacent identical pairs in the list into one, and let k be the number of pairs merged.
  (4) For each merged pair, write the starting point, "/", and the destination in succession. A starting point of 25 is written "bar", a destination of 0 is written "off", and all other values are written in decimal. If the destination is in H and the pair is the frontmost in the list among the pairs having that point as destination, append "*" immediately after it. If k is 2 or more, further append "(k)" (for example "(2)" when k = 2).
  (5) Write the merged pairs in list order, separated by single spaces. If there are no pairs (the play that does nothing), write "none".
- Any sequence of sub-moves that reaches the same final position yields the same notation, whichever one it is computed from. Examples of notation: "24/18 13/11", "8/4(2)", "bar/22*", "6/off", "13/10*(2) 10/7 6/3", "24/18* 18/13".
- The list at the end of (2) (before merging, with each pair repeated as many times as it occurs) is the key of the play. Two plays are ordered by comparing their keys pair by pair from the front: at the first pair that differs, the play whose pair has the larger starting point comes first, or, if the starting points are equal, the play whose pair has the larger destination. If one key equals the beginning of the other key, the longer one comes first. This order is called the canonical order.

5. End of game and results
- The game ends as soon as some color X bears off its 15th checker, and X wins. If the opponent has borne off one or more checkers, the result is "single" (multiplier 1). If the opponent has borne off no checker and has at least one checker on the opponent's bar or in X's home, the result is "backgammon" (multiplier 3); otherwise it is "gammon" (multiplier 2). The winner's points are the product of the multiplier and the cube value.
- Game endings by drop and by resignation are as described under DROP and RESIGN.
- When the game ends, the winner's points are added to the winner's side of the score. The side to move does not change when the game ends.

6. Evaluation function
The integer value E, the evaluation of a position from the standpoint of color X, is defined as follows. Y is the opposing color, and all locations are expressed in X's own numbers.
- The pip count of a color is the sum, over points 1-24 in that color's own numbers, of (point number) * (number of checkers on that point), plus (number of that color's checkers on its bar) * 25.
- P = (pip count of Y) - (pip count of X)
- S: for each point p among 1-24 that holds exactly one X checker (a blot), compute the value s(p) below; S is the sum of all of them. Let D be the set of the distances p - q over the points q with 0 <= q <= p - 1 that hold Y checkers (q = 0 is Y's bar). s(p) is the number of the 36 ordered dice pairs (a, b) (a and b each from 1 to 6) that satisfy at least one of: a is in D; b is in D; a + b is in D; a = b and 3*a or 4*a is in D. Whether intermediate points are blocked is not considered.
- H: the number of points among 1-6 that hold two or more X checkers
- R: the maximum number of consecutively numbered points among 1-24 that each hold two or more X checkers (0 if there is no such point)
- A: the number of points among 19-24 that hold two or more X checkers
- G = (number of checkers on Y's bar) - (number of checkers on X's bar)
- E = 2*P - S + 4*H + 3*R + 3*A + 6*G

7. Automatic play
For each legal play of the side to move X, evaluate from X's standpoint the position right after that play (before the side to move changes; for a play that ends the game, the position at the end of the game). Choose the play with the largest evaluation; if several plays share the largest evaluation, choose the one that comes first in canonical order. This is called the automatic play.

8. Cube decision
The cube decision of the side to move X (let Y be the opponent), made before rolling the dice, is determined as one of "unavailable", "no double", "double take" and "double drop" by the following procedure.
  (1) If the cube owner is Y, if the cube value is 64, or if the double-prohibition condition defined under MATCH applies, the decision is "unavailable".
  (2) If there is contact, use the value E of the current position evaluated from X's standpoint. If E < 60, the decision is "no double"; if 60 <= E <= 100, "double take"; if E > 100, "double drop".
  (3) If there is no contact, let V = 8*(Keith count of X) - 7*(Keith count of Y), and let L = 28 if the cube owner is "center" or L = 21 if the owner is X. If V > L, the decision is "no double"; if V <= L and V >= 14, "double take"; if V < 14, "double drop".
- Contact: there is contact when the largest board number holding a White checker (25 if White has checkers on its bar) is greater than the smallest board number holding a Black checker (0 if Black has checkers on its bar).
- Keith count: the pip count of a color plus all of the following four values, counted in that color's own numbers: 2*max(c1 - 1, 0), max(c2 - 1, 0), max(c3 - 3, 0), and the number of points among points 4, 5 and 6 that hold no checker of that color. Here ck is the number of that color's checkers on point k.

9. Random numbers and self-play
- Random numbers are generated by MINSTD: x <- (48271*x) mod 2147483647. The initial value of x is the seed given by the command. Each time a random number is needed, x is updated, and the updated x is the value of the random number. When one die is rolled, its number is the random value mod 6 plus 1. When two dice are rolled, the first die is rolled first, then the second. Within one command, all random numbers are taken in order from a single random sequence.
- In one game of self-play (GAME, MATCH, STATS), turns are numbered t starting from 1, and on each turn the following steps (1)-(4) are performed for the side to move X.
  (1) Determine the cube decision. If it is "double take", X doubles and Y takes: the cube value is doubled and the owner becomes Y. If it is "double drop", X doubles, Y drops, and the game ends. In this case X wins, the result is "drop", and the points are the cube value before doubling.
  (2) Roll two dice; let d1 be the number of the first die and d2 the number of the second.
  (3) Choose one legal play according to X's strategy and make it. Strategy "BEST" chooses the automatic play, and "FIRST" chooses the first play in canonical order. "RANDOM" takes one random number (even when there is only one legal play), lets i be that value mod the number of legal plays, and chooses the i-th play in canonical order, counting from 0.
  (4) If X has borne off its 15th checker, the game ends. Otherwise the side to move changes and t is increased by 1.
- Each game in MATCH and STATS starts from the standard starting position with cube value 1 and owner "center". First, as the opening roll, one die is rolled for White and then one for Black; if the two numbers are equal, they are rolled again in the same way until they differ. The color with the larger number moves first. On turn 1 of the first player, (1) and (2) are skipped, and (3) is performed with White's number as d1 and Black's number as d2.

10. Commands
The arguments, behavior and output of each command are as follows. The arguments d1 and d2 are dice numbers and must be integers from 1 to 6. The meaning of "not allowed after game over" and "not allowed while pending" is given in section 11.

NEW
Resets the state to the initial state, except that the score is not changed. Outputs "OK".

POS <points> <wbar> <bbar> <side>
Sets the position. <points> is 24 integers separated by commas, and the i-th value represents the point with board number i. A positive value means that many White checkers, a negative value means as many Black checkers as its absolute value, and 0 means empty. <wbar> and <bbar> are the numbers of checkers on White's and Black's bars, and <side> is the side to move, "W" or "B". Each color's number of borne-off checkers is 15 minus the total number of that color's checkers on the board and on the bar. The command is valid when there are exactly 4 arguments, <points> has exactly 24 values, <wbar> and <bbar> are between 0 and 15 inclusive, <side> is "W" or "B", and each color's number of borne-off checkers is between 0 and 14 inclusive. If valid, sets the position and the side to move as given, sets the cube value to 1, the owner to "center", no double pending and the game not over, empties the history and the redo list (the score is not changed), and outputs "OK". If invalid, outputs "ERROR bad position" without changing the state.

SHOW
Outputs the current position and side to move in the same format as POS, as "POS <points> <wbar> <bbar> <side>". The values of <points> are written in decimal, with "-" for negative values.

BOARD
Outputs a board diagram of 13 lines. Each line of the diagram (except line 7) consists of 12 fields of width 3: 6 fields written in succession, then " |" (a space and a vertical bar), then the remaining 6 fields written in succession. The content of each field is right-aligned and padded on the left with spaces.
- Line 1: the fields for board numbers 13, 14, ..., 24. The content of each field is that number.
- Lines 2-6: the point fields for board numbers 13, 14, ..., 24, where the row of line r is r = 1-5 (line 2 is r = 1, line 6 is r = 5).
- Line 7: "bar W<a> B<b> | off W<c> B<e> | cube <v> <owner> | <status>". a and b are the numbers of checkers on White's and Black's bars, c and e are the numbers of White's and Black's borne-off checkers, v is the cube value, and <owner> is the cube owner. <status> is "game over" if the game is over; otherwise "<side> doubles" if a double is pending; otherwise "<side> to move", where <side> is the side to move.
- Lines 8-12: the point fields for board numbers 12, 11, ..., 1, where the row is r = 5 on line 8, r = 4 on line 9, ..., r = 1 on line 12.
- Line 13: the fields for board numbers 12, 11, ..., 1. The content of each field is that number.
With c the number of checkers on the point, a point field is the color letter ("W" for White, "B" for Black) if c >= r, and "." if c < r. However, when r = 5 and c > 5, the count c is written instead of the color letter.

PIPS
Outputs "pips W <White's pip count> B <Black's pip count>".

RACE
Not allowed after game over. Outputs "contact" if there is contact; otherwise outputs "race W <White's Keith count> B <Black's Keith count>".

MOVES d1 d2
Not allowed after game over. Outputs the legal plays of the side to move with dice d1, d2 in canonical order, one play per line in notation, and finally outputs "total <number of legal plays>".

PLAY d1 d2 <play>
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. <play> is all arguments from the third one on, and at least one is required. The side to move makes the play <play> with dice d1, d2. <play> must follow the syntax below; if it does not, outputs "ILLEGAL syntax".
- If <play> is the single argument "none", it represents the play with no sub-moves.
- Otherwise, each argument is one element. An element has the form "P1/P2/.../Pm" (m >= 2), optionally followed by one "(k)" (k is 2, 3 or 4). P1 is "bar" or an integer from 1 to 24, and P2 to Pm are integers from 1 to 24 or "off". Each integer among P2 to Pm may optionally be immediately followed by one "*". Integers are in decimal notation without leading zeros. Treating "bar" as 25 and "off" as 0, it must hold that P1 > P2 > ... > Pm.
- An element represents the sub-moves (P1, P2), (P2, P3), ..., (P(m-1), Pm). If "(k)" is present, each of them is made k times. "*" is ignored. Let U be all the sub-moves represented by all the elements together.
If the syntax is correct, build the position F obtained from the current position by U as follows. For each sub-move (s, t) of U, decrease the number of checkers of the side to move at s by 1 and increase the number at t by 1 (25 is the bar, 0 is the off area). In addition, for each point of 1 or more that is the destination of some sub-move of U and that holds exactly one opposing checker in the current position, move that checker to the opponent's bar. Then check (1)-(7) below in order and perform the first one that applies.
  (1) If F has a point (including the bar) where the number of checkers of the side to move is negative, outputs "ILLEGAL no checker".
  (2) If in F the side to move has checkers on its bar and U contains a sub-move whose starting point is not the bar, outputs "ILLEGAL bar".
  (3) If U contains a sub-move whose destination is 1 or more and is a point holding two or more opposing checkers in the current position, outputs "ILLEGAL blocked".
  (4) If U contains a sub-move whose destination is 0 and in F the side to move has a checker on its bar or on points 7-24, outputs "ILLEGAL bear-off".
  (5) If F is the same as the final position of some legal play, makes that legal play and outputs "OK <notation of that legal play>".
  (6) If F is the same as the final position of a possible play as defined in section 3 (not necessarily a legal play), outputs "ILLEGAL too few dice" if among such plays there is one with fewer than M sub-moves, and "ILLEGAL larger die required" otherwise.
  (7) Otherwise, outputs "ILLEGAL impossible".
On ILLEGAL, the state is not changed.
When a play is made (PLAY and AUTO), the final position of the play becomes the new position. If the side to move has borne off its 15th checker, the game ends as in section 5, and one more line "result <winner> <result> <points>" is output. If the game did not end, the side to move changes. Furthermore, the three items, namely the record "<color that played> <d1>-<d2> <notation>" (d1 and d2 in argument order), the state before the play and the state after the play, are appended to the end of the history as one entry, and the redo list is emptied.

AUTO d1 d2
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. Makes the automatic play with dice d1, d2 and outputs "OK <notation> eval <evaluation>". The evaluation is that of the play chosen in section 7. The processing after the play is the same as for PLAY.

HINT d1 d2 k
Not allowed after game over. k is between 1 and 1000 inclusive. Sorts the legal plays with dice d1, d2 in descending order of the evaluation of section 7, with equal evaluations in canonical order, and outputs the first k plays (all of them if there are fewer than k legal plays), one per line, in the form "<evaluation> <notation>". The state is not changed.

EVAL
Not allowed after game over. Evaluates the current position from the standpoint of the side to move and outputs "eval <E> pips <P> blots <S> home <H> prime <R> anchors <A> bar <G>". The values are as in section 6.

SHOTS
Not allowed after game over. Let X be the side to move and Y the opponent; counts the rolls with which X's blots (points 1-24 holding exactly one X checker) are hit. The 21 rolls (a, b) are examined with a decreasing from 6 to 1 and, for each a, b decreasing from a to 1. For each roll, determine Y's legal plays assuming that Y is the side to move in the current position; an X blot that is hit by any of Y's legal plays is a blot hit by that roll. The weight of a roll is 1 if a = b and 2 if a != b. Line 1 is "shots <c>/36" followed, in the order examined, by " <a>-<b>" for each roll that hits at least one blot. c is the total weight of those rolls. From line 2 on, for each X blot in descending order of X's own number, output "blot <p> <c>/36", where p is the point number in X's own numbers and c is the total weight of the rolls that hit that blot.

CUBE
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. Determines the cube decision of the side to move by section 8 (without using the double-prohibition condition of MATCH) and outputs "cube <decision>".

DOUBLE
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. If the cube owner is the side not to move, or the cube value is 64, outputs "ERROR cube unavailable". Otherwise, the side to move offers a double, which becomes pending, and outputs "OK double <twice the cube value>".

TAKE
Not allowed after game over. If no double is pending, outputs "ERROR no double". Otherwise, clears the pending double, doubles the cube value, makes the side not to move the owner, and outputs "OK take <new cube value> <owner>". The side to move does not change.

DROP
Not allowed after game over. If no double is pending, outputs "ERROR no double". Otherwise, clears the pending double and ends the game, with the side to move as the winner. The result is "drop", and the points are the current cube value. Outputs the two lines "OK drop" and "result <side to move> drop <points>".

RESIGN <kind>
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. <kind> is one of "single", "gammon" and "backgammon". The side to move resigns and the game ends, with the side not to move as the winner. The result is <kind>, and the points are the product of the multiplier of <kind> and the cube value. Outputs the two lines "OK resign" and "result <winner> <kind> <points>".

SCORE
Outputs "score W <White's score> B <Black's score>".

HISTORY
Outputs "history <k>" (k is the number of entries in the history), followed by the entries of the history from oldest to newest, in the form "<i> <record>" for i = 1, 2, ..., k.

UNDO n
n is between 1 and 1000 inclusive. If n is greater than the number of entries in the history, outputs "ERROR cannot undo". Otherwise, restores the state (position, side to move, cube value and owner, pending double, game over, score) to the before-play state of the n-th entry from the end of the history, removes the last n entries from the history and adds them, keeping their original order, to the front of the redo list, and outputs "OK".

REDO n
n is between 1 and 1000 inclusive. If n is greater than the number of entries in the redo list, outputs "ERROR cannot redo". Otherwise, removes the first n entries of the redo list and appends them, keeping their original order, to the end of the history, sets the state to the after-play state of the last of those n entries, and outputs "OK".
The redo list is emptied whenever NEW, POS, PLAY, AUTO, DOUBLE, TAKE, DROP or RESIGN succeeds.

GAME <seed> <ws> <bs>
Not allowed after game over, not allowed while pending. <seed> is between 1 and 2147483646 inclusive, and <ws> and <bs> are the strategies of White and Black, each one of "BEST", "FIRST" and "RANDOM". Starting from the current position, side to move, cube value and cube owner, plays a self-play game to its end by the procedure of section 9. The output is as follows.
- When X doubles in (1): outputs "<t> <X> doubles <value after doubling>", followed by "<t> <Y> takes" for a take or "<t> <Y> drops" for a drop.
- When a play is made in (3): outputs "<t> <X> <d1>-<d2> <notation>".
- When the game ends: finally outputs "result <winner> <result> <points>".
GAME does not change the program's state (the score and history do not change either).

MATCH <n> <seed>
<n> is between 1 and 15 inclusive, and <seed> is between 1 and 2147483646 inclusive. It can be executed in any state and does not change the program's state. Starting with White's and Black's match points at 0, games are repeated until either side's match points reach n or more. Before starting each game, if no Crawford game has been played yet and the larger of White's and Black's match points is exactly n - 1, that game is the Crawford game. Each game is played from the opening roll as in section 9, with strategy "BEST" for both sides. The double-prohibition condition of section 8 (1) is that the game is the Crawford game, or that (X's match points) + (cube value) >= n. After each game, the points won are added to the winner's match points, and "game <k> start <first player> turns <T> result <winner> <result> <points> score <White's match points>-<Black's match points>" is output. k is the game number counting from 1, and T is the number of plays made in that game (including the play made with the opening roll and plays that do nothing, and not including the turn on which a drop occurred). In the Crawford game, "crawford" is inserted between "game <k>" and "start", as in "game <k> crawford start ...". Finally, outputs "match <winner> <White's match points>-<Black's match points>". The winner is the color whose match points reached n or more.

STATS <k> <seed>
<k> is between 1 and 50 inclusive, and <seed> is between 1 and 2147483646 inclusive. It can be executed in any state and does not change the program's state. Plays k games in succession, each starting from the opening roll as in section 9. Both strategies are "BEST", and the double-prohibition condition defined under MATCH is not used. Counts the games won for each result, and outputs six lines in this order: the four lines "single W <a> B <b>", "gammon W <a> B <b>", "backgammon W <a> B <b>" and "drop W <a> B <b>", the line "points W <a> B <b>" with the total points, and the line "turns <T>" with the total number of plays made over all games (counted in the same way as T in MATCH).

11. Errors
Each command is checked in the following order; for the first case that applies, one line is output, and processing moves on to the next command without changing the state.
  (1) If the command name is none of those in section 10, "ERROR unknown command". Command names are case-sensitive.
  (2) If the number or values of the arguments do not match the description in section 10, "ERROR bad arguments". The same applies when a command that takes no arguments is given arguments. However, for POS, "ERROR bad position" is output as described in section 10, including in this case. Also, the syntax of PLAY's <play> is not checked here but in (5).
  (3) For a command marked "not allowed after game over", if the game is over, "ERROR game over".
  (4) For a command marked "not allowed while pending", if a double is pending, "ERROR double pending".
  (5) The errors and ILLEGAL outputs given in each command's description in section 10.
Commands not marked "not allowed after game over" or "not allowed while pending" can be executed even when the game is over or a double is pending.

[Output format]
For each command, output the lines defined in sections 10 and 11 in that order. The output consists of ASCII characters only; no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline. No blank lines are output. All numbers are written as decimal integers without extra leading zeros, with "-" for negative numbers. A color (such as <winner>, <side>, <X>, <Y> or <first player>) is written "W" for White and "B" for Black.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 500
- Each line has at most 200 characters and consists only of printable ASCII characters and spaces.
- The numeric arguments (d1, d2, k, n, <seed>, <wbar>, <bbar>) are unsigned decimal integers without extra leading zeros, with values at most 2147483647. Values out of range can occur.
- The <points> of POS is one or more integers separated by commas; each integer is in decimal notation without extra leading zeros (a negative number has a single leading "-", and zero is "0"), with absolute value at most 15. The number of values and their totals are not necessarily correct.
- Each self-play game ends within 1000 turns.
- For any position and dice, the number of sub-move sequences considered when enumerating legal plays (including partial ones) is at most 10000.
