[Overview]
Write a program that keeps double-entry bookkeeping books. The program reads commands from standard input one line at a time and writes, for each command, a result in the prescribed format to standard output.
It covers a chart of accounts with a hierarchy of account codes, entry of multi-line journal entries with verification that debits equal credits, cancellation by reversing entries, repeated posting of standard entries (templates), depreciation, interest accrual based on daily balances, per-partner settlement and aging analysis for accounts such as accounts receivable, bad-debt write-offs, locking of periods by closing entries (closing) and undoing it, and reports such as the ledger, monthly activity, trial balance, income statement, balance sheet and account roll-ups.
All money amounts are handled as integers counted in cents (units of 1/100); floating-point numbers are not used. Dates are given in the form "year-month-day". Do not use the language's date library for date calculations; instead, convert dates to the day numbers defined in [Processing] and calculate with those.

[Input format]
The first line contains N, the number of command lines that follow. Lines 2 through N+1 each contain one command. Each line is a sequence of tokens separated by single ASCII spaces, with no whitespace at the start or end of the line. The program ends after all N commands have been processed.

The token formats are as follows.
- Account code: 1 to 6 characters consisting only of digits (0-9), whose first character is not 0 (e.g. 1, 112, 1219).
- Prefix: the same format as an account code.
- Type: one of ASSET, LIABILITY, EQUITY, REVENUE, EXPENSE, CONTRA_ASSET. They represent assets, liabilities, equity (net assets), revenue, expenses, and contra-asset accounts (such as accumulated depreciation), respectively.
- Account name, memo: 1 to 24 characters that start with a lowercase letter (a-z) and consist only of lowercase letters, digits and the underscore "_" (e.g. cash, owner_investment).
- Entry number: 1 to 8 characters that start with an uppercase letter (A-Z) and consist only of uppercase letters and digits (e.g. J1, INV2026).
- Entry ID: 1 to 20 characters consisting only of letters (A-Z, a-z), digits and the hyphen "-". Besides entry numbers, IDs assigned automatically by the program (e.g. rent-1, CLOSE-2-R) may also be written.
- Template name: 1 to 8 characters consisting only of lowercase letters (e.g. rent).
- Partner name: 1 to 12 characters that start with a lowercase letter and consist only of lowercase letters and digits (e.g. acme).
- Date: 10 characters of the form "YYYY-MM-DD". YYYY is 4 digits, MM and DD are 2 digits each, and they are separated by ASCII hyphens "-" (e.g. 2026-03-01). The characters always have this form, but a nonexistent date (e.g. 2026-02-30) may be written.
- Amount: an integer part and a fractional part joined by ".". The integer part is a decimal number from 0 to 999999 with no extra leading zeros. The fractional part has exactly 2 digits (e.g. 0.07, 85.50, 1500.00). The amount "a.bc" represents the integer a*100+bc counted in cents.
- Year: a 4-digit decimal number.
- Count, useful life in months, rate, number of days: decimal non-negative integers with no extra leading zeros. The rate is the annual interest rate in units of 1/10000; for example, 600 means 6.00% per year.

There are the following 28 commands. Each <...> part is a token of the format above.

[Processing]
== Dates ==
- A date "YYYY-MM-DD" is "valid" only if, with Y, M and D the values of YYYY, MM and DD read as decimal numbers (the year, month and day), all of 2000 <= Y <= 2099, 1 <= M <= 12 and 1 <= D <= (the number of days in month M of year Y) hold. The numbers of days in the months January to December are 31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31 in order, except that February of a leap year has 29 days. A year Y is a leap year if Y is divisible by 4 and either "not divisible by 100" or "divisible by 400".
- Each valid date is associated with a "day number". The day number of 2000-01-01 is 0, and it increases by 1 for each following day. From here on, dates are ordered (<, <=, etc.) by comparing their day numbers, and the difference of two dates (-) is the difference of their day numbers.
- When a date is output, it is written in the form "YYYY-MM-DD" (the year with 4 digits, and the month and the day with 2 digits each, with a leading 0 when they have 1 digit).
- The date "k months after" a date (year Y, month M, day D) (k is an integer >= 0) is obtained as follows. Let t = (M-1)+k; the year is Y+floor(t/12), the month is (t mod 12)+1, and the day is the smaller of D and the number of days in that year and month. For example, 1 month after 2026-01-31 is 2026-02-28, and 2 months after it is 2026-03-31. 0 months after a date is the original date.

== State and initial state ==
- Accounts: initially there are none. They are registered by ACCOUNT. Each account has an account code, a type, an account name and a status (active or inactive). An account is active when it is registered and becomes inactive by DEACTIVATE. There is no way to make an inactive account active again.
- Entries: the sequence of posted journal entries, initially empty. Each entry has an entry ID, a date, a category, a memo, a sequence of lines, a sequence number, a reversed-by ID and a reverses ID.
  A line has a side (DR is debit, CR is credit), an account code, an amount and a partner name (which may be absent).
  Sequence numbers 1, 2, 3, ... are assigned in the order the entries are posted, serially across all entries.
  The category is one of journal, recurring, reversal, closing, reopen, depreciation, interest, writeoff, and is determined by the command that posted the entry.
  The reversed-by ID is the entry ID of the entry that reversed this entry, and the reverses ID is the entry ID of the entry that this entry reversed; both are initially "none".
- Templates: initially there are none. Each template has a template name, a memo, a sequence of lines and a use count (initially 0).
- Draft block: a container for collecting lines, opened by JOURNAL or TEMPLATE and closed by POST. At most one block is open at a time; initially no block is open. A block has a kind (journal or template), a key (the entry number of JOURNAL or the template name of TEMPLATE), a date token (only when the kind is journal), a memo, and a sequence of lines (empty when the block is opened).
- Close records: a stack onto which one record (close date, entry ID of the closing entry or "none") is pushed for each successful CLOSE; initially empty. When the close records are not empty, the close date of the most recently pushed record is called the "closed-through date"; when they are empty, the closed-through date is "none". When the closed-through date is d, a date is said to "fall in the closed period" if it is on or before d (d included). If the closed-through date is none, no date falls in the closed period.
- Counters: there are four counters, the close counter, the depreciation counter, the interest counter and the write-off counter, all initially 0.
A command that results in an error changes no state at all, except in the case of POST (described below); it only outputs one error line.

== Terms and orderings ==
- "Ascending order" of account codes, partner names and template names is the lexicographic order by character code (ASCII). When one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first (e.g. the order 1, 11, 112, 1219, 122, 2).
- For two accounts a and b, when the account code of a is a prefix of the account code of b and the two codes differ, a is called an "ancestor account" of b, and b a "descendant account" of a. Among the ancestor accounts of an account, the one with the longest account code is called its "parent". Also, among the ancestor accounts of an account, the one with the shortest account code (or the account itself if it has no ancestor accounts) is called its "top-level account". These relations are determined over all accounts registered at that time (including inactive accounts).
- "Class" of types: ASSET and CONTRA_ASSET belong to the same class, and LIABILITY, EQUITY, REVENUE and EXPENSE each form a class on their own.
- Accounts of type ASSET and EXPENSE are called "debit-normal", and accounts of type LIABILITY, EQUITY, REVENUE and CONTRA_ASSET are called "credit-normal".
- For a set S of entries, the "debit total" and the "credit total" of account a are, among the lines of the entries in S, the sum of the amounts of the lines whose account is a and whose side is DR, and the sum of the amounts of the lines whose account is a and whose side is CR, respectively. The "difference" of a is debit total - credit total. The "balance" of a is the difference if a is debit-normal, and -difference if a is credit-normal. Differences and balances may be negative.
- S is chosen as follows. A difference, balance, etc. "as of date d" uses as S all entries whose date is on or before d. "For the period a to b" uses as S all entries whose date is >= a and <= b. "Current" uses as S all posted entries (regardless of date).
- The "posting order" of entries is ascending order of date, and for equal dates ascending order of sequence number. When the lines of several entries are listed, they are ordered by the posting order of their entries, and within one entry in the order of its sequence of lines.
- The "amount" of a sequence of lines (such as the lines of an entry or a template) is the sum of the amounts of its lines whose side is DR. The amount of an entry is the amount of its sequence of lines.
- trunc(x/y) (y > 0) is the quotient of the absolute value of x divided by y with the fractional part discarded, with "-" prefixed if x is negative. That is, it truncates toward zero (e.g. trunc(7/2) = 3, trunc(-7/2) = -3).

== Posting and reversing entries ==
- To "post" an entry means to append to the end of the sequence of entries a new entry that has the given entry ID, date, category, memo and sequence of lines, with reversed-by ID and reverses ID "none", assigning it the next sequence number. Posting itself does not check whether the date falls in the closed period or whether the accounts are active (the necessary checks are performed as described for each command).
- To "reverse" entry e with entry ID x, date d, category k and memo m means to post an entry with entry ID x, date d, category k and memo m whose sequence of lines consists of the lines of e with their sides swapped (DR and CR swapped; account code, amount and partner name unchanged), in the same order as in e, and then to set the reversed-by ID of e to x and the reverses ID of the new entry to the entry ID of e.

== Sum of daily balances ==
For account c and dates a, b with a <= b, the sum of "the balance of c as of t" over every date t that is >= a and <= b is called the "sum of daily balances" of c over the period a to b. The number of days in the period is b-a+1.

== Open items per partner ==
For account c and partner p, the "open items" and the "unapplied amount" as of date d are obtained as follows.
Among the lines of the entries whose date is on or before d, the lines whose account is c and whose partner name is p are processed one at a time in line order (posting order). During processing, a list of open items (initially empty) and an unapplied amount u (initially 0) are kept. The "signed amount" v of each line is as follows: if c is debit-normal, +amount for a DR line and -amount for a CR line; if c is credit-normal, -amount for a DR line and +amount for a CR line.
  If v > 0: let x = min(v, u), and decrease u by x. If v-x > 0, append to the end of the list an open item based on that line's entry, with original amount v and remaining amount v-x.
  If v < 0: let w = -v. While w > 0 and the list is not empty, repeat the following: for the first item of the list, let t = min(w, its remaining amount), decrease its remaining amount and w each by t, and if its remaining amount becomes 0, remove the item from the list. Finally, add w to u.
The items remaining in the list after all lines have been processed are the open items, and u is the unapplied amount. The "date" and "entry ID" of an open item are the date and entry ID of the entry it is based on, and its "age" is d - (the date of the open item).
Also, the partners that appear at least once as the partner name of a line whose account is c, among the lines of the entries whose date is on or before d, are called "the partners of c as of d".

== Processing of each command ==
Errors are checked in the order written, and only the first error that applies is output. The text of the error lines is given in [Output format]. The following common procedures are used in the checks.
- "Date check of x": if x is not a valid date, invalid date.
- "Closed-period check of date x": if x falls in the closed period, period closed.
- "Existence check of account c": if c is not registered, unknown account.
- "Type check of account c (type T)": first perform the existence check of c; then, if the type of c is not T, wrong type.
- "Active check": examine the given sequence of accounts in order; for the first inactive account, inactive account.
- "Range check of period a to b": perform the date check of a and the date check of b in this order; then, if a > b, invalid range.

1. ACCOUNT c T name: (a) c is already registered: duplicate account, (b) among the registered accounts, there is one whose account code is a prefix of c or of whose account code c is a prefix, and whose type class differs from the class of T: class mismatch (when there are several such accounts, the one whose account code comes first in ascending order is written in the error line). If there is no error, register c as an active account with type T and account name name.
2. DEACTIVATE c: (a) existence check of c, (b) c is already inactive: already inactive, (c) the current balance of c is not 0: nonzero balance. If there is no error, make c inactive.
3. JOURNAL id date memo: there are no errors. If a block is open, first discard that block (together with the lines it has collected) and output a DISCARD line. Then open a new block with kind journal, key id, date token date and memo memo. id and date are checked at POST.
4. TEMPLATE name memo: there are no errors. As with JOURNAL, if a block is open, discard it and output a DISCARD line, then open a new block with kind template, key name and memo memo.
5. DR c x, DR c x p, CR c x, CR c x p: if no block is open, it is a no open entry error. Otherwise, append a line (side DR or CR according to the command name, account code c, amount x, partner name p ("none" if p is absent)) to the end of the block's sequence of lines. Whether c is registered, whether it is active and whether the amount is non-zero are not checked here; they are checked at POST.
6. POST: if no block is open, it is a no open entry error (in this case the state does not change). If a block is open, first close that block, regardless of the result of the checks. Even when an error occurs, the closed block and its lines are discarded and are not restored. Then process according to the kind of the block as follows.
  Kind journal (key id, date token date): check in the following order. (a) id equals the entry ID of some posted entry: duplicate entry, (b) date check of date, (c) closed-period check of date, (d) fewer than 2 lines: too few lines, (e) examine the lines one at a time in order: if the line's account is not registered, unknown account; if it is inactive, inactive account; if the amount is 0, zero amount (decided in this order for the first line where a problem is found), (f) the sum of the amounts of the DR lines differs from the sum of the amounts of the CR lines: unbalanced. If there is no error, post an entry with entry ID id, date date, category journal, the block's memo and the block's sequence of lines.
  Kind template (key name): check in the following order. (a) template name already exists: duplicate template, (b) fewer than 2 lines: too few lines, (c) examine the lines one at a time in order: if the line's account is not registered, unknown account; if the amount is 0, zero amount (whether the account is inactive is not checked), (d) the sum of the amounts of the DR lines differs from the sum of the amounts of the CR lines: unbalanced. If there is no error, register template name with the block's memo, the block's sequence of lines and use count 0.
7. DRAFT: if no block is open, it is a no open entry error. The state is not changed.
8. REVERSE id date: (a) there is no entry with entry ID id: unknown entry, (b) the category of that entry is one of reversal, closing, reopen: cannot reverse, (c) the reversed-by ID of that entry is not none: already reversed, (d) date check of date, (e) date is before the date of that entry: reverse before entry, (f) closed-period check of date, (g) active check of the accounts of that entry's lines, listed in the order of its lines. If there is no error, reverse that entry with entry ID "id-R" (id followed by a hyphen and the uppercase letter R), date date, category reversal and the memo of that entry unchanged.
9. RECUR name date k: (a) template name does not exist: unknown template, (b) date check of date, (c) k is 0: invalid count, (d) closed-period check of date, (e) active check of the accounts of the template's lines, listed in the order of its lines. If there is no error, do the following for j = 0, 1, ..., k-1 in this order: add 1 to the template's use count, and post an entry with entry ID "name-<use count>" (e.g. rent-4 when the use count is 4), date j months after date, category recurring, the template's memo and the template's sequence of lines.
10. DEPRECIATE date a c x L: (a) date check of date, (b) closed-period check of date, (c) type check of a (ASSET), (d) type check of c (CONTRA_ASSET), (e) type check of x (EXPENSE), (f) active check of c, x in this order, (g) with the balance of a as of date as the acquisition cost P and the balance of c as of date as the accumulated depreciation A, let the depreciation amount be q = min(floor(P/L), P-A) if P > 0, and q = 0 if P <= 0; if q <= 0, nothing to depreciate. If there is no error, add 1 to the depreciation counter and post an entry with entry ID "DEP-<depreciation counter>", date date, category depreciation, memo depreciation, and the sequence of lines (DR x q), (CR c q) in this order (no partner names).
11. INTEREST c t a b r: (a) existence check of c, (b) the type of c is neither ASSET nor LIABILITY: not interest bearing, (c) type check of t (REVENUE if the type of c is ASSET, EXPENSE if it is LIABILITY), (d) range check of period a to b, (e) closed-period check of date b, (f) active check of c, t in this order, (g) with S the sum of daily balances of c over the period a to b, compute the interest q = trunc(S*r/3650000); if q <= 0, no interest. If there is no error, add 1 to the interest counter and post an entry with entry ID "INT-<interest counter>", date b, category interest and memo interest. Its lines are (DR c q), (CR t q) in this order if the type of c is ASSET, and (DR t q), (CR c q) in this order if it is LIABILITY (no partner names).
12. WRITEOFF date c x n: (a) date check of date, (b) closed-period check of date, (c) type check of c (ASSET), (d) type check of x (EXPENSE), (e) active check of c, x in this order, (f) examine the partners of c as of date in ascending order; for each of them, the "write-off amount" is the sum of the remaining amounts of those open items of c and that partner as of date whose age is greater than n. The partners whose write-off amount is 1 or more are the "target partners"; if the grand total W of the write-off amounts is 0, nothing to write off. If there is no error, add 1 to the write-off counter and post an entry with entry ID "WO-<write-off counter>", date date, category writeoff and memo writeoff. Its lines are (DR x W) (no partner name) followed by (CR c <write-off amount> <partner name>) for each target partner in ascending order.
13. CLOSE date c: (a) date check of date, (b) closed-period check of date, (c) type check of c (EQUITY), (d) active check of c, (e) with the "accounts to close" being the accounts of type REVENUE or EXPENSE whose difference as of date is not 0, listed in ascending order of account code, active check of the accounts to close.
  If there is no error, list, for each account to close in ascending order, the line (CR <that account> v) if its difference v is positive, or (DR <that account> -v) if it is negative. Then compute the net income I = -(the sum of the differences of the accounts to close), and append (CR c I) if I > 0, or (DR c -I) if I < 0, at the end of the list (no partner names on any of the lines). Add 1 to the close counter. If the list has 1 or more lines, post an entry with entry ID "CLOSE-<close counter>", date date, category closing and memo closing, and push (date, that entry ID) onto the close records. If the list has no lines, post no entry and push (date, none) onto the close records. In either case, the closed-through date becomes date.
14. REOPEN: (a) the close records are empty: nothing to reopen, (b) if the most recently pushed close record has a closing entry, active check of the accounts of its lines, listed in the order of its lines. If there is no error, and if there is a closing entry, reverse it with entry ID "<entry ID of the closing entry>-R", date the close date of that record, category reopen and memo reopen. Then remove the most recently pushed record from the close records. The closed-through date returns to the close date of the record before it (none if there is no such record).
15. LEDGER c a b: (a) existence check of c, (b) range check of period a to b. The state is not changed.
16. ACTIVITY c y: perform the existence check of c. The state is not changed.
17. TRIAL d, BALANCE d, ROLLUP p d: perform the date check of d. The state is not changed.
18. INCOME a b, DAYBOOK a b: perform the range check of period a to b. The state is not changed.
19. AGING c d, ITEMS c p d: perform the existence check of c and the date check of d in this order. The state is not changed.
20. ENTRY id: if there is no entry with entry ID id, it is an unknown entry error. The state is not changed.
21. ACCOUNTS, TEMPLATES, STATUS: there are no errors. The state is not changed.

[Output format]
For each command, output zero or more lines in the formats below. DR and CR output nothing when they succeed. JOURNAL and TEMPLATE output nothing except one DISCARD line when they discard a block. Text inside double quotes is written literally as shown (ASCII characters), and each <...> part is replaced by a value. Apart from indentation, every space between tokens is a single ASCII space. "(indent k)" means that k ASCII spaces are placed at the start of the line. The output consists of ASCII characters only, no line has trailing whitespace, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No blank lines are output.

== Number notation ==
- Amount notation: if the number of cents c is 0 or more, it is the integer part of c/100 (a decimal number with no extra leading zeros), ".", and c mod 100 as 2 digits (with a leading 0 when it has 1 digit), written in this order (e.g. 0.07, 12.50, 1000.29). If c is negative, it is "-" followed by the amount notation of -c (e.g. -49.50).
From here on, all values counted in cents, such as amounts, differences, balances and totals, are written in amount notation. Integers such as numbers of lines, numbers of items, numbers of days and counts are written as decimal numbers with no extra leading zeros, and dates are written in the form given in [Processing].

== Output of each command (on success) ==
- ACCOUNT: "ACCOUNT <account code> <type> <account name> parent <account code of the parent after registration>". If there is no parent, the <account code of the parent after registration> part is written as "none".
- DEACTIVATE: "DEACTIVATE <account code> <account name>"
- DISCARD line (when JOURNAL or TEMPLATE discards a block): "DISCARD <key of the discarded block> lines <number of lines of the discarded block>"
- POST: for kind journal, "POST <entry number> <date> lines <number of lines> amount <amount of the entry>"; for kind template, "TEMPLATE <template name> lines <number of lines> amount <amount of the sequence of lines>".
- DRAFT: "DRAFT <kind of the block> <key of the block> lines <number of lines> debit <sum X of the amounts of the DR lines> credit <sum Y of the amounts of the CR lines> difference <X-Y>"
- REVERSE: "REVERSE <id> entry <entry ID of the posted entry> <date> amount <amount of the posted entry>"
- RECUR: the first line is "RECUR <template name> count <k>". Then, for each posted entry in the order they were posted, (indent 2) "POST <entry ID> <date> lines <number of lines> amount <amount of the entry>" is output.
- DEPRECIATE: "DEPRECIATE <asset account code> entry <entry ID> <date> amount <q> accumulated <A+q> book <P-A-q>"
- INTEREST: "INTEREST <account code> entry <entry ID> <end date> days <number of days in the period b-a+1> average <trunc(S/(b-a+1))> amount <q>". S is the sum of daily balances computed in (g).
- WRITEOFF: the first line is "WRITEOFF <receivable account code> entry <entry ID> <date> partners <number of target partners> amount <W>". Then, for each target partner in ascending order, (indent 2) "<partner name> <write-off amount>" is output.
- CLOSE: if an entry was posted, "CLOSE <date> entry <entry ID> lines <number of lines> net income <I>"; if no entry was posted, "CLOSE <date> no entry".
- REOPEN: "REOPEN <close date of the removed record> entry <entry ID of the entry posted by the reversal> closed <closed-through date after the removal>". If the removed record had no closing entry, "no entry" is written instead of the two tokens "entry <...>". If the closed-through date after the removal is none, that part is written as "none".
- LEDGER: the first line is "LEDGER <account code> <account name> <a> <b>". The second line is (indent 2) "opening <balance with S being all entries whose date is before a>". Then, for each line of the entries whose date is >= a and <= b whose account is c, one at a time in posting order, (indent 2) "<date of the entry> <entry ID> <DR or CR> <amount> balance <running balance> <memo of the entry>" is output. The running balance starts from the opening value; at each line, if c is debit-normal, the amount is added for a DR line and subtracted for a CR line, and if c is credit-normal, the amount is subtracted for a DR line and added for a CR line. Finally, (indent 2) "ending <last running balance (the opening value if there are no lines)> debits <sum of the amounts of the DR lines output> credits <sum of the amounts of the CR lines output> days <b-a+1> average <trunc(S/(b-a+1))>" is output. S is the sum of daily balances of c over the period a to b.
- ACTIVITY: the first line is "ACTIVITY <account code> <account name> <year>". Then, for each month from January to December of that year, in month order, (indent 2) "<year>-<month as 2 digits (with a leading 0 when it has 1 digit)> debit <debit total for the period from the 1st to the last day of that month> credit <credit total for the same period> balance <balance as of the last day of that month>" is output. Finally, (indent 2) "total debit <sum of the 12 monthly debit totals> credit <sum of the 12 monthly credit totals>" is output.
- TRIAL: the first line is "TRIAL <date>". Then, for each account whose difference v as of d is not 0, one at a time in ascending order, (indent 2) "<account code> <account name> debit <v>" is output if v > 0, and (indent 2) "<account code> <account name> credit <-v>" if v < 0. Finally, (indent 2) "total debit <sum of the positive differences> credit <sum of the absolute values of the negative differences>" is output.
- INCOME and BALANCE are output by combining the following "section tables". A section table is determined by a heading H, a set of types K, a sign sigma (+1 or -1) and a set of entries S; the registered accounts whose type is in K are the "target accounts", and the difference of a target account in S multiplied by sigma is that account's "displayed amount".
  First, (indent 2) "<H> <sum of the displayed amounts of the target accounts>" is output (only for EQUITY of BALANCE, the value written is this sum plus the current earnings).
  Next, the "groups", formed by putting together target accounts that have the same top-level account, are examined in ascending order of the account code of the top-level account. For each group that contains at least one account whose displayed amount is not 0, (indent 4) "<account code of the top-level account> <account name of the top-level account> <sum of the displayed amounts of all target accounts of the group>" is output, followed by the target accounts of that group whose displayed amount is not 0, one at a time in ascending order, as (indent 6) "<account code> <account name> <displayed amount>".
  Only for the EQUITY section table of BALANCE, (indent 4) "current earnings <current earnings>" is output at the end (it is output even when the current earnings are 0).
- INCOME: the first line is "INCOME <a> <b>". With S being the entries for the period a to b whose category is neither closing nor reopen, the section table with heading REVENUE, K = {REVENUE}, sigma = -1 and the section table with heading EXPENSE, K = {EXPENSE}, sigma = +1 are output in this order. Finally, (indent 2) "NET INCOME <REVENUE total - EXPENSE total>" is output (each total is the one written on the first line of its section table).
- BALANCE: the first line is "BALANCE <date>". With S being all entries as of date d (regardless of category), and the current earnings being -(the sum of the differences in S of all accounts of type REVENUE or EXPENSE), three section tables are output in this order: heading ASSETS, K = {ASSET, CONTRA_ASSET}, sigma = +1; heading LIABILITIES, K = {LIABILITY}, sigma = -1; heading EQUITY, K = {EQUITY}, sigma = -1. Finally, with X the ASSETS total and Y the sum of the LIABILITIES total and the EQUITY total (including the current earnings), (indent 2) "check ok" is output if X = Y, and otherwise (indent 2) "check mismatch <X-Y>".
- ROLLUP: the first line is "ROLLUP <p> <date>". The registered accounts of whose account code p is a prefix (including the case where the account code equals p) are the "target accounts". If there are no target accounts, one line (indent 2) "(none)" is output and the output ends. If there are target accounts, for each of them one at a time in ascending order, "<account code> <account name> own <balance as of d> total <subtotal>" is output with 2*(1+depth) ASCII spaces at the start of the line. The depth is the number of ancestor accounts of that account that are target accounts. The subtotal is the sum of the differences as of d of the account itself and all of its descendant accounts, multiplied by +1 if the account is debit-normal and by -1 if it is credit-normal. Finally, (indent 2) "postings debit <sum of the debit totals as of d of the target accounts> credit <sum of the credit totals as of d of the target accounts>" is output.
- AGING: the first line is "AGING <account code> <account name> <date>". Then the partners of c as of d are examined in ascending order, and for each partner that has at least one open item as of d or whose unapplied amount is 1 or more, (indent 2) "<partner name> 0-30 <x0> 31-60 <x1> 61-90 <x2> 91+ <x3> unapplied <unapplied amount u> balance <x0+x1+x2+x3-u>" is output. x0, x1, x2 and x3 are the sums of the remaining amounts of the open items whose age is from 0 to 30, from 31 to 60, from 61 to 90, and 91 or more, respectively. Finally, the sums of each column over the partners output are output as (indent 2) "total 0-30 <sum of x0> 31-60 <sum of x1> 61-90 <sum of x2> 91+ <sum of x3> unapplied <sum of u> balance <sum of balance>".
- ITEMS: the first line is "ITEMS <account code> <account name> <partner name> <date>". Then, for each open item of c and p as of d, one at a time in list order, (indent 2) "<date of the item> <entry ID of the item> amount <original amount> open <remaining amount> age <age>" is output. Finally, (indent 2) "unapplied <unapplied amount u> balance <sum of the remaining amounts - u>" is output.
- ENTRY: the first line is "ENTRY <entry ID> <date> <category> <memo>". Then, for each line of the entry, one at a time in the order of its lines, (indent 2) "<DR or CR> <account code> <account name> <amount>" is output. For a line that has a partner name, a single ASCII space and the partner name follow. Next, (indent 2) "total <amount of the entry>" is output. Finally, if the reverses ID is not none, (indent 2) "reverses <reverses ID>" is output, and then, if the reversed-by ID is not none, (indent 2) "reversed by <reversed-by ID>".
- DAYBOOK: the first line is "DAYBOOK <a> <b>". Then, for each entry whose date is >= a and <= b, one at a time in posting order, (indent 2) "<date> <entry ID> <category> <amount of the entry> <memo>" is output. Finally, (indent 2) "total entries <number of entries output> amount <sum of the amounts of the entries output>" is output.
- ACCOUNTS: the first line is "ACCOUNTS". Then, for each registered account, one at a time in ascending order, (indent 2) "<account code> <type> <account name> parent <account code of the parent (none if there is none)> balance <current balance> <active if active, inactive if inactive>" is output.
- TEMPLATES: the first line is "TEMPLATES". Then, for each template, one at a time in ascending order of template name, (indent 2) "<template name> <memo> lines <number of lines> amount <amount of the sequence of lines> used <use count>" is output.
- STATUS: "STATUS accounts <number of registered accounts> inactive <number of inactive accounts> entries <number of posted entries> templates <number of templates> closed <closed-through date> open <key of the open block>". If the closed-through date is none, "none" is written, and if no block is open, "none" is written.
For ACCOUNTS and TEMPLATES, if there are no lines to output after the first line, one line (indent 2) "(none)" is output instead. LEDGER, ACTIVITY, TRIAL, INCOME, BALANCE, AGING, ITEMS and DAYBOOK always output their total lines and the like even when there are no matching lines (for example, if there are no entries, the last line of DAYBOOK is "  total entries 0 amount 0.00").

== Error lines ==
When an error occurs, exactly one of the following lines is output. In a <...> part for an account code, date, entry number, entry ID or template name, insert the one that caused the problem in the check, exactly as the token written in the input. For an account that is not written directly in the input, such as the inactive account found by an active check or the other account of a class mismatch, insert that account's account code. Amounts are written in amount notation, and "the date of that entry" is written in the date form defined in [Processing].

[Constraints]
- 1 <= N <= 1000.
- The input correctly follows the formats in [Input format] (there are no unknown commands, wrong numbers of tokens, or tokens that do not match their formats). The only errors to detect are those listed in [Processing].
- The year of ACTIVITY is from 2000 to 2099. The count of RECUR is from 0 to 60, the useful life in months of DEPRECIATE is from 1 to 600, the rate of INTEREST is from 0 to 10000, and the number of days of WRITEOFF is from 0 to 36600.
- The dates of the entries posted by RECUR are all on or before 2099-12-31.
- At most 200 accounts are registered, at most 50 templates are registered, and at most 100 lines are added to one block.
- A block may still be open when the last command has been processed. Nothing is output for that block.
- When computed according to the rules above, the absolute values of all integers, including intermediate values (including the sum of daily balances S and S*r), are less than 2 to the 53rd power (computing with 64-bit signed integers does not overflow).

  ASSETS 950.50

      11 cash 700.00

  LIABILITIES 0.00

The first A3 is an error because its debits and credits do not match, and its block is discarded. Therefore it can be entered again with the same entry number A3. The average of LEDGER is the sum of daily balances, 26700.00, obtained by adding up the balance 1000.00 of each day from April 1 to April 19 (19 days) and the balance 700.00 of each day from April 20 to April 30 (11 days), divided by the number of days, 30. CLOSE closes out the revenue sales of 250.50 and the expense rent of 300.00, and posts the net loss of 49.50 to the debit side of retained_earnings. The asset-side accounts 11 and 12 are put together in the group of the top-level account 1, but 31 and 32 have no ancestor accounts, so each of them forms a group with itself as the top-level account.
