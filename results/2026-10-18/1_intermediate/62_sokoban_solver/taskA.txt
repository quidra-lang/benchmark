[Overview]
Write a program that plays and analyzes Sokoban. Sokoban is a puzzle in which the player moves up, down, left and right, pushing boxes, so as to carry every box onto a goal. The program reads an initial level and a sequence of commands from standard input, executes the commands in order, and writes the results to standard output. The commands cover loading and validating levels, moving the player and pushing boxes, undoing and redoing moves, displaying, rotating and mirroring the level, detecting deadlocks, shortest player paths, searching for a solution with the fewest pushes, and more.

[Input format]
The input consists of the following lines, in this order.
- The first line contains the number of rows H and the number of columns W of the initial level, separated by a single space.
- The next H lines are the rows of the initial level, from top to bottom.
- The next line contains the number of commands N.
- Then follow N commands, one per line. However, each LOAD command line is immediately followed by the h level rows that the command reads (these lines are not counted in N).
A level row represents its cells from left to right, each by one of the following 7 characters (called the level characters below).
  #: wall
  -: floor (a cell that is not a goal and has nothing on it)
  .: goal
  $: box on a non-goal cell
  *: box on a goal
  @: player on a non-goal cell
  +: player on a goal
Empty space outside the walls is also written as "-", so no blank appears in a level row.
A command line consists of one or more words, each separated by a single space, with no blank at the start or end of the line. The first word is the command name and the remaining words are the arguments. The command formats are as follows (everything other than the command name is the name of an argument).
LOAD h w
IMPORT text
VALID
INFO
SHOW
EXPORT
MOVE s
PUSH r c d
BRING r1 c1 r2 c2
PATH r c
UNDO n
REDO n
RESET
STATUS
HISTORY
BOXES
REACH
DEADSQUARES
DEADLOCK
BOUND
SAVE name
RESTORE name
CHECK s
SOLVE limit
HINT limit
PLAY
TRANSFORM t
Arguments are written as follows.
- r, c, r1, c1, r2, c2, n and limit are number words. A number word is a string of 1 to 6 characters consisting only of digits (0 to 9) that, when it has 2 or more characters, does not start with 0. Its value is the value read as a decimal number. For example, 0, 7 and 120 are number words, while 07, -1, +3, 1234567 and x are not.
- s is a step word (explained in 3. of [Processing]).
- d is a single character, one of u, d, l, r.
- name is a string of 1 to 8 characters consisting only of lowercase English letters (a to z).
- t is one of ROT90, ROT180, ROT270, FLIPH, FLIPV.
- text is an arbitrary word (its content is examined when IMPORT is processed).
A LOAD line is always well formed (see [Constraints]).

[Processing]
Process the commands one at a time in input order, writing each command's output as you go. Below, level characters, symbols such as "|", and strings made of them are sometimes shown in double quotes (the double quotes themselves appear in neither input nor output). In the output descriptions, a part such as <n> is replaced by its value.

1. Level and terminology
- A level consists of cells in H rows and W columns. The cell in the r-th row from the top and the c-th column from the left is written (r, c) (1 <= r <= H, 1 <= c <= W). Where the output shows a cell, written <cell>, print r and c separated by a single space (for example, 3 5 for (3, 5)).
- Each cell is either a wall or a non-wall cell. Some of the non-wall cells are goals. A non-wall cell may hold a box (at most one box per cell), and the player stands on a non-wall cell without a box. The meanings of the level characters are as given in [Input format]; for example, "*" represents a non-wall cell that is a goal and holds a box.
- There are 4 directions: u (up: r decreases by 1), d (down: r increases by 1), l (left: c decreases by 1) and r (right: c increases by 1). Whenever directions are examined in turn, they are always examined in the order u, d, l, r. The cell one step from cell p in direction x is written p+x, and the cell one step from p in the direction opposite to x is written p-x.
- Row-major order is the ordering of cells by increasing r, and for equal r by increasing c.
- A position is the pair of the cell where the player stands and the set of cells holding boxes. Two positions whose player cells and sets of box cells are both equal are called the same position.
- The interior is the set of all cells that can be reached from the cell where the player stood when the level was loaded, by repeatedly moving to an orthogonally adjacent (up, down, left or right) non-wall cell (including the starting cell). Boxes are ignored for this, and the movement never leaves the level. The interior is determined when the level is loaded and does not change when the player or boxes move (in TRANSFORM, its cells are relocated just like the other cells).
- The reachable cells of a position are all the cells that can be reached from the position's player cell by repeatedly moving up, down, left or right to an interior cell without a box (including the player cell).
- The representative cell of a position is the first of its reachable cells in row-major order.

2. State and loading a level
The program keeps the following state.
- Current level: H, W, and the layout of walls and goals
- Initial position and current position
- Move history: the list of successful steps (3.), from oldest to newest. Each step consists of a direction and whether a box was pushed.
- Redo list: the list of directions of steps that were taken back by UNDO.
- Snapshots: a collection of entries, each pairing a name with a position and a move history.
- Stored solution: the pair of the notation of a solution found by SOLVE and the position for which that solution was computed. It may be absent.
When a level is loaded (the initial level, LOAD, IMPORT), it becomes the current level, and the position drawn in that level becomes both the initial position and the current position. The move history, the redo list and the snapshots become empty, and the stored solution becomes absent.
For a loaded level, check the following (1) to (5) in this order; the string on the right of the first one that applies is the reason for invalidity. A level to which none of them applies is valid.
(1) The number of player characters ("@" and "+") is not 1: players <that number>
(2) There is no box character ("$" and "*"): no boxes
(3) The number of box characters differs from the number of goal characters (".", "*" and "+"): boxes <number of box characters> goals <number of goal characters>
(4) The interior contains a cell on the border of the level (row 1, row H, column 1, column W): open <the first such cell in row-major order>
(5) Some cell holding a box or a goal is not in the interior: outside <the first such cell in row-major order>
In a valid level, for every interior cell p and every direction x, p+x and p-x lie inside the level, and boxes are always on interior cells.

3. Steps, step sequences, walking paths
A step of the player in direction x is performed as follows. Let p be the player's cell and t = p+x.
- If t is a wall, the step fails, and its reason is wall.
- If t holds a box and t+x is a wall or a cell holding a box, the step fails, and its reason is blocked.
- If t holds a box and t+x is neither a wall nor a cell holding a box, move the box from t to t+x and move the player to t (a push step).
- If t holds no box, move the player to t (a walk step).
A failed step changes nothing. A successful step appends its direction and whether it pushed to the end of the move history. The notation of a step is the direction letter (u, d, l, r) for a walk step and its uppercase (U, D, L, R) for a push step. The notation of a list of steps is the concatenation of the notations of its steps in order.

Run-length notation is as follows.
- Encoding a string: split the string into maximal runs of the same character; represent a run of length 1 by that single character and a run of length k (k >= 2) by the decimal representation of k followed by that character, and concatenate these in order. For example, "###--@$" is encoded as "3#2-@$".
- Decoding a string (done with a specified set of allowed characters): split the string, from the beginning, into "units". A unit is either a count (1 or 2 digits whose first digit is not 0) followed by one allowed character, or a single allowed character alone. Each unit represents that character repeated count times (1 time if there is no count), and the concatenation of these in order is the result of decoding. If the whole string cannot be split into such units (a character that is not allowed appears, a count starts with 0, 3 or more digits appear in a row, the string ends with a digit, etc.), decoding fails. Decoding the empty string gives the empty string.
A step word is a word whose decoding succeeds with u, d, l, r, U, D, L, R as the allowed characters. The list of directions represented by the characters of its decoded result, in order (an uppercase letter denotes the same direction as its lowercase), is called the step sequence of the word. For example, the step sequence of "3r2Ul" is r, r, r, u, u, l.

To execute a list of directions means to take a step in each direction from the first one in order, and if a step fails, to stop there without taking the rest (steps that succeeded before it remain in effect). If at least one step succeeded in the execution, clear the redo list.

The walking path from the player's cell s to a target cell g is determined by the following breadth-first search. Start with a queue containing only s; until the queue is empty, remove the cell q at its front and examine the directions x in the order u, d, l, r: if q+x is an interior cell without a box that has never been put into the queue (and is not s), record on q+x "came from q by x" and append q+x to the end of the queue. Then follow the records from g back to s; the list of directions followed, ordered from the s side, is the walking path (if g = s it is the empty list). Executing a walking path always succeeds, with every step being a walk step.

4. Push distance, dead squares, freezing
- For a goal g, the number of pushes needed to push a single box to g, disregarding the other boxes and the player's position, is computed by the following breadth-first search. Set the value of g to 0, start with a queue containing only g, and until the queue is empty, remove the cell q at its front and examine the directions x in the order u, d, l, r: if q-x and (q-x)-x are both interior cells and the value of q-x is not yet determined, set the value of q-x to the value of q plus 1 and append q-x to the end of the queue (this corresponds to a box at q-x moving to q when pushed in direction x by the player standing at (q-x)-x). When the value of a cell p is determined, that value is called the push distance from p to g. For a cell whose value is not determined, the push distance to g is undefined.
- A dead square is an interior cell whose push distance to every goal is undefined (a goal is never a dead square).
- Freezing: given a placement of boxes and a set F of cells regarded as walls, a cell b holding a box is "frozen under F" if both the horizontal condition and the vertical condition hold. The horizontal condition concerns the two cells b+l and b+r adjacent to b on the left and right, the vertical condition concerns the two cells b+u and b+d adjacent to b above and below, and each condition holds if at least one of the following (a) to (c) holds.
(a) One of the two cells is a wall or is contained in F.
(b) Both of the two cells are dead squares.
(c) One of the two cells holds a box, and that box is frozen under the set obtained by adding b to F.
Saying simply "frozen" means the case where F is the empty set. Since F grows in the check of (c), the check always terminates.

5. Command checks and errors
For each command, check the following (1) to (7) in order and handle the first one that applies. A command that printed an error changes no state (when some steps of MOVE succeeded, follow the description of MOVE in 6.).
(1) If it is LOAD, process it as described in 6.
(2) The command name is not among the formats (uppercase and lowercase letters are distinguished): error: unknown command <command name>
(3) The number of arguments differs from the format, or an argument does not follow the rules in [Input format]: error: bad arguments
(4) If it is IMPORT or VALID, process it as described in 6.
(5) The current level is not valid: error: invalid level
(6) An argument value is out of range: error: bad value. The ranges are: every cell specified in PATH, PUSH and BRING satisfies 1 <= r <= H and 1 <= c <= W; n of UNDO and REDO is at least 1; limit of SOLVE and HINT is at least 1 and at most 100000.
(7) Process it as described in 6. (some errors are defined there as well).

6. Processing of each command
- LOAD h w: read the next h lines as level rows. Whether or not there is an error, move on to the next command only after reading the h lines. If some row does not have length w or contains a character that is not a level character, let the topmost such row be row i (1 <= i <= h), print error: bad row <i>, and change nothing. Otherwise, load that level and print loaded <h>x<w>. The x between <h> and <w> is a single lowercase letter x with no blank before or after it (the same holds for the form <H>x<W> below).
- IMPORT text: split text at "|" into parts (an empty part at either end or between two "|" also counts as one part), decode each part with the level characters as the allowed characters, and use the results as the level rows from top to bottom. If decoding fails for some part, the number of rows is greater than 10, the rows do not all have the same length, or the row length is 0 or greater than 10, print error: bad level and change nothing. Otherwise, load that level and print loaded <number of rows>x<row length>.
- VALID: print valid if the current level is valid, and otherwise invalid <reason for invalidity> (example: invalid boxes 3 goals 2).
- INFO: print size <H>x<W> walls <number of wall cells> cells <number of interior cells> boxes <number of boxes> dead <number of dead squares>.
- SHOW: print the current position as H rows of level characters. The character of each cell is "#" for a wall, "+" (goal) or "@" (not a goal) for the player's cell, "*" or "$" for a box cell, and "." or "-" for any other cell (a non-wall cell that is not in the interior is also "-").
- EXPORT: encode each of the H rows that SHOW would print, join them from the top row down with "|", and print the resulting string after level and a single space (example: level 5#|#@$.#|5#).
- MOVE s: execute the step sequence of s. If at least one step succeeded, print the notation of the list of successful steps in the form moved <notation>. If a step failed, then print error: step <i> <reason>, where i is the position of that step in the step sequence (counting from 1) and the reason is wall or blocked. If the first step failed, print only the error line.
- PUSH r c d: let b = (r, c) and push the box at b once in direction d. If b holds no box, print error: no box; if b+d is not an interior cell or holds a box, print error: blocked; if b-d is not an interior cell, holds a box, or is not a reachable cell of the player, print error: unreachable (check in this order). Otherwise, execute the walking path from the player's cell to b-d followed by one direction d (it always succeeds entirely), and print pushed <notation of the list of executed steps>.
- BRING r1 c1 r2 c2: carry the box at (r1, c1) to (r2, c2) without moving any other box. If (r1, c1) holds no box, print error: no box. If (r1, c1) and (r2, c2) are the same cell, print brought 0 (the state does not change). Otherwise, perform the push search (7.) with the boxes allowed to be pushed being only the box that was at (r1, c1) (the other boxes stay at their original positions in every state), the goal condition being "that box is at (r2, c2)", no pruning, and no expansion limit. If found, execute the step sequence of the notation of the solution obtained (it always succeeds entirely) and print brought <number of push steps of the solution> <notation of the solution>. If there is no solution, print error: no route.
- PATH r c: if (r, c) is not an interior cell, holds a box, or is not a reachable cell of the player, print error: unreachable. Otherwise, execute the walking path from the player's cell to (r, c), and print path <k> <notation> if its length k is at least 1, or path 0 if it is 0.
- UNDO n: if the move history is empty, print error: nothing to undo. Otherwise, let k = min(n, length of the move history), repeat k times removing the last step of the move history and taking it back, and print undone <k>. To take back a step in direction x, let p be the player's cell; if it was a push step, move the box from p+x to p; then move the player to p-x. The direction of the step taken back is appended to the end of the redo list.
- REDO n: if the redo list is empty, print error: nothing to redo. Otherwise, let k = min(n, length of the redo list), repeat k times removing the direction at the end of the redo list and taking a step in that direction (it always succeeds and is added to the move history), and print redone <k>. A step taken by REDO does not clear the redo list.
- RESET: make the current position the same as the initial position, clear the move history and the redo list, and print reset.
- STATUS: print moves <length of the move history> pushes <number of push steps in the move history> goals <number of boxes on goals>/<number of boxes>. When every box is on a goal, follow this with a single space and solved (example: moves 8 pushes 4 goals 2/2 solved).
- HISTORY: print history 0 if the move history is empty, and otherwise history <length of the move history> <notation of the move history>.
- BOXES: print boxes <number of boxes> on the first line, then one line box <cell> <distance> <directions> per box, in row-major order of the box cells. <distance> is the minimum of the defined push distances from that box's cell to the goals; if none is defined, write dead. <directions>, with b being that box's cell, is the concatenation, in the order u, d, l, r, of the letters of the directions x for which b-x is a reachable cell of the player and b+x is an interior cell without a box. If there is no such direction, write -.
- REACH: print reach <number of reachable cells> first <representative cell>.
- DEADSQUARES: print dead <number of dead squares> on the first line, then the level as H rows. The character of each cell is "#" for a wall, "." for a goal, "x" for a dead square, and "-" otherwise (boxes and the player are not drawn).
- DEADLOCK: if some box is on a dead square, print deadlock dead <cell> for the first such box in row-major order. Otherwise, if some box not on a goal is frozen, print deadlock frozen <cell> for the first such box in row-major order. If neither applies, print no deadlock.
- BOUND: let b1, b2, ..., bm be the box cells in row-major order and g1, g2, ..., gm the goals in row-major order. Among the ways of assigning a distinct goal to each box, consider only those in which the push distance from every box to its assigned goal is defined, and choose one whose total of those push distances is minimum. If several have the minimum total, choose the one whose list of the indices of the goals assigned to b1, b2, ..., bm, in order, is lexicographically smallest. If there is no such assignment at all, print bound none. Otherwise, print bound <total> on the first line, then, for i = 1, 2, ..., m in order, one line box <bi> goal <goal assigned to bi> pushes <that push distance> (parts such as <bi> are in the form of <cell>).
- SAVE name: make a copy of the current position and move history the snapshot named name (if a snapshot with the same name exists, replace it). Print saved <name> moves <length of the move history>.
- RESTORE name: if there is no snapshot named name, print error: no snapshot <name>. Otherwise, set the current position and move history to copies of that snapshot's position and move history, clear the redo list, and print restored <name> moves <length of the move history>. The snapshot itself does not change.
- CHECK s: print what would happen if the step sequence of s were executed from the current position. No state (position, move history, redo list, etc.) changes. If some step would fail along the way, print check fail step <i> <reason> (i and the reason are as in MOVE). If all steps would succeed, print check ok moves <length of the step sequence> pushes <number of push steps among them> goals <number of boxes on goals after the execution>/<number of boxes>, and if every box is on a goal after the execution, follow this with a single space and solved.
- SOLVE limit: if every box is on a goal, print already solved and make the stored solution absent. Otherwise, perform the push search (7.) with the boxes allowed to be pushed being all boxes, the goal condition being "every box is on a goal", pruning enabled, and the expansion limit being limit. Let e be the expansion count and k the number of known states when the search ends. If found, print solution pushes <number of push steps of the solution> moves <length of the notation of the solution> expanded <e> states <k>, print the notation of the solution on the next line, and make the pair of the notation of the solution and the current position the stored solution. If there is no solution, print no solution expanded <e> states <k>; if the limit was hit, print limit expanded <e> states <k>; in both cases make the stored solution absent. SOLVE does not move the position.
- HINT limit: perform the same search as SOLVE. When every box is on a goal, when there is no solution, and when the limit is hit, print the same line as SOLVE. If found, execute the step sequence of the part of the notation of the solution from its beginning up to and including the first uppercase letter (the first push step and the walking path before it), and print hint <that part>. HINT does not change the stored solution.
- PLAY: if there is no stored solution, or the position of the stored solution is not the same as the current position, print error: no solution. Otherwise, execute the step sequence of the notation of the stored solution (it always succeeds entirely) and print played pushes <number of push steps> moves <length of the notation>. The stored solution remains as it is.
- TRANSFORM t: rotate or mirror the whole level. With H, W being the size before the transformation, move cell (r, c) to the following cell and replace directions as follows.
  ROT90 (rotate 90 degrees clockwise): move to (c, H + 1 - r). The new size is W rows by H columns. Replace u with r, r with d, d with l, and l with u.
  ROT180 (rotate 180 degrees): move to (H + 1 - r, W + 1 - c). The size does not change. Swap u and d, and swap l and r.
  ROT270 (rotate 270 degrees clockwise): move to (W + 1 - c, r). The new size is W rows by H columns. Replace u with l, l with d, d with r, and r with u.
  FLIPH (mirror left-right): move to (r, W + 1 - c). The size does not change. Swap l and r (u and d do not change).
  FLIPV (mirror top-bottom): move to (H + 1 - r, c). The size does not change. Swap u and d (l and r do not change).
  Move all the cells of the walls, the goals, the interior, the initial position, the current position, the positions of the snapshots, and the position of the stored solution, and replace the direction of each step of the move history, the redo list and the move histories of the snapshots, as well as the direction of each character of the notation of the stored solution (whether a step pushed, and whether a letter is uppercase or lowercase, do not change). Then print transformed <new H>x<new W>.

7. Push search
The push search is a breadth-first search that examines positions in order of increasing number of pushes; it is used by BRING, SOLVE and HINT. It is performed given how the boxes allowed to be pushed are determined, the goal condition, whether pruning is enabled, and the expansion limit L (no limit for BRING).
A search state is the pair of the set of box cells and the representative cell of the position in which the player stands with that box placement (positions with the same box placement and the same reachable cells of the player become the same state). For each state, record the parent state that produced it and the push (the pair of a box cell and a direction).
(1) Let S0 be the pair of the set of box cells of the current position and the representative cell of that position. Let the set of known states be the set containing only S0, let the queue be the list containing only S0, and set the expansion count to 0 (no parent is recorded for S0).
(2) If the queue is empty, the search ends with "no solution".
(3) If the expansion count equals L, the search ends with "limit".
(4) Remove the state S at the front of the queue and add 1 to the expansion count. Consider the position with the box placement of S in which the player stands on the representative cell of S, and compute its reachable cells.
(5) Pick the boxes of S that are allowed to be pushed one at a time, in row-major order of their cells. With b being the picked box's cell, examine the directions x in the order u, d, l, r, and consider "pushing the box at b in direction x" when all of the following conditions hold.
  - b-x is one of the reachable cells computed in (4).
  - b+x is an interior cell with no box in the box placement of S.
  - When pruning is enabled, b+x is not a dead square, and, in the box placement after moving the box from b to b+x, no box that is not on a goal is frozen.
  For each push considered, let T be the pair of the set of box cells with the box moved from b to b+x and the representative cell of the position with that box placement in which the player stands on b. If T is a known state, do nothing. Otherwise, add T to the known states and record its parent S and push (b, x). If T satisfies the goal condition, the search ends with "found", with T as the result. If not, append T to the end of the queue.
(6) When all pushes of S have been examined, return to (2).
When found, the notation of the solution is built as follows. Follow the parent records from the result state back to S0, and list the pushes in order from the S0 side as (b1, x1), ..., (bk, xk). Starting from the current position, for i = 1, ..., k in order, take the notation of the walking path from the player's cell to bi-xi (computed with the box placement at that moment) followed by the uppercase of xi, and move the box from bi to bi+xi and the player to bi. The concatenation of the strings obtained this way, in order, is the notation of the solution. The push search itself changes no state.

[Output format]
- For each command, print the lines described in [Processing], in that order. Unless specified otherwise, each command's output is one line.
- Words in a line are separated by a single space, no line has trailing blanks, and every line ends with a newline.
- Numbers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- A <cell> part is <r> <c>, that is, the row number and the column number separated by a single space.
- In the descriptions in [Processing], the period that ends each item or sentence is punctuation and is not part of the output.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= H <= 10 and 1 <= W <= 10. Each row of the initial level consists of exactly W level characters (the initial level is not necessarily valid).
- 1 <= N <= 500, and the whole input has at most 2000 lines.
- Each word of a command line is a string of 1 to 100 characters consisting of printable ASCII characters other than space (! through ~).
- A LOAD line is always LOAD, h and w separated by single spaces, where h and w are decimal numbers from 1 to 10 without leading zeros. Each of the h lines that follow it is a string of 1 to 20 characters consisting of printable ASCII characters other than space.
- In every level that is loaded successfully (the initial level, and the levels of LOAD and IMPORT commands that did not cause an error), there are at most 4 box characters and at most 4 goal characters.
