[Overview]
Write a calculator program that handles polynomials in the variable x with rational coefficients, using exact fraction arithmetic. It reads a sequence of commands from standard input; performs definition of polynomials by expressions, sum, difference, product and power, division giving the quotient and the remainder, the greatest common divisor polynomial, the extended Euclidean algorithm and the least common multiple polynomial, inverse, Chinese remainder theorem and power modulo a polynomial, derivative, indefinite integral, definite integral, tangent line, Taylor expansion, composition and evaluation, rational roots and factorization, square-free factorization, Descartes' rule of signs, counting real roots and isolating real roots in intervals with Sturm's theorem, resultant and discriminant, power sums of roots, interpolating polynomials, and generation of well-known orthogonal polynomials; and writes the results to standard output.

[Input format]
The first line contains the number of commands N. Each of the following N lines contains one command. Each command consists of the command name and zero or more arguments, separated by single spaces; there are no spaces at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. However, the expression of DEF may contain spaces. There are 34 command formats:

  DEF R e
  PRINT A
  DELETE A
  LIST
  DEGREE A
  ADD R A B
  SUB R A B
  MUL R A B
  POW R A n
  DIV R1 R2 A B
  GCD R A B
  XGCD R1 R2 R3 A B
  LCM R A B
  INVMOD R A M
  CRT R A1 M1 A2 M2
  MODPOW R A n M
  DERIV R A n
  INTEG R A
  AREA A u v
  TANGENT R A c
  TAYLOR A c
  COMPOSE R A B
  EVAL A c1 c2 ... cm
  ROOTS A
  FACTOR A
  SQFREE A
  DESCARTES A
  STURM A u v
  ISOLATE A n
  RESULTANT A B
  DISCRIM A
  POWERSUM A n
  INTERP R x1 y1 x2 y2 ... xm ym
  SPECIAL R k n

The arguments mean the following.
- R, R1, R2, R3, A, B, A1, A2, M, M1, M2 are polynomial names. A name is a string consisting of one uppercase letter (A to Z) followed by zero or more uppercase letters or digits (0 to 9). R, R1, R2, R3 are names under which results are stored; the others are names of polynomials used in the computation.
- e is an expression (defined in the section "Expressions"). On a DEF line, e is everything after "DEF", one space, R and one space, up to the end of the line. e neither starts nor ends with a space.
- n is an integer >= 0.
- c, c1 to cm, x1 to xm, y1 to ym, u, v are "rational literals". A rational literal consists either of an integer p alone, or of an integer p and an integer q >= 0 joined by "/" in the form p/q; p may be preceded by "-" (examples: 3, -1/2, 4/6, -0, 0/7, 5/0). The form p alone means the same as p/1, and p/q denotes the fraction p / q (the handling of q=0 is defined in the section "Error checking"). For u and v of STURM, "-inf" and "inf" may also be written in addition to rational literals.
- k is a string of lowercase letters and digits.
- Except inside expressions, every integer is written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- The m in c1 ... cm and x1 y1 ... xm ym is a symbol for the number of values and is not written in the input.
It is guaranteed that every line follows this format and the ranges in [Constraints].

[Processing]
Process the commands in order from the first line, and output the result of each command as described in [Output format].

== Polynomial storage ==
- The program holds any number of named polynomials. Initially it holds none.
- A command that stores a result keeps the result under the name R (R1, R2, R3). If a polynomial with the same name already exists, it is replaced with the new polynomial. All computations use the values as they were before the command starts storing (example: ADD P P Q makes the sum of the previous P and Q the new P).
- Each time a result is stored, output one line "<name> = <polynomial notation>". This is called the "store output".
- All polynomial coefficients are handled exactly as fractions; floating-point numbers are not used.

== Fractions ==
- A fraction is always kept as an irreducible fraction (with a positive denominator).
- Fraction operations are done as "multiply, then reduce": compute a/b+c/d as (a*d+c*b)/(b*d), a/b-c/d as (a*d-c*b)/(b*d), (a/b)*(c/d) as (a*c)/(b*d), and (a/b)/(c/d) as (a*d)/(b*c), then reduce and make the denominator positive. A comparison is done by comparing a*d with c*b.
- The input is constructed so that, when the steps of [Processing] are performed exactly in the order written and fraction operations and comparisons are done exactly as above, every integer that appears along the way (including products such as a*d, numerators and denominators before reduction, and the product of the two numbers when computing a least common multiple) has an absolute value less than 2^53.

== Terms and notation ==
- deg A denotes the degree of a polynomial A. The polynomial whose coefficients are all 0 is called the "zero polynomial" and is written 0. The degree of 0 is -inf (a value smaller than every integer). A polynomial of degree 0 is called a "nonzero constant".
- A[i] denotes the coefficient of x^i in A. For a nonzero A, the highest-degree coefficient A[deg A] is written lc(A).
- The "monic form" of A is A with each coefficient divided by lc(A) if A is nonzero, and 0 if A is 0.
- The value A(c) of A at a fraction c is the final v obtained by starting from v=0 and, for i=deg A, deg A-1, ..., 0 in this order, replacing v with v*c+A[i] (it is 0 if A is 0).
- The sum A+B and the difference A-B are the sums and differences of the coefficients of the same degree; the product c*A of a fraction c and A is A with each coefficient multiplied by c.
- The product A*B is 0 if either A or B is 0. Otherwise, it is the final C obtained by starting from the polynomial C whose coefficients are all 0 and, for i=0, 1, ..., deg A in this order, and for each i, for j=0, 1, ..., deg B in this order, adding A[i]*B[j] to C[i+j].
- The power A^n is the result of multiplying by A n times starting from 1 (1 if n=0). 0^0 is also 1.
- Division: when B is nonzero, the quotient S and the remainder T of dividing A by B are computed as follows. Starting from S=0, T=A, while T is nonzero and deg T>=deg B, repeat: let t=lc(T)/lc(B) and d=deg T-deg B, set S[d] to t, and for j=0, 1, ..., deg B in this order replace T[d+j] with T[d+j]-t*B[j] (each time, the coefficient of degree deg T of T becomes 0 and the degree of T decreases). The final S is written "the quotient of A / B" and the final T is written "A mod B". deg(A mod B)<deg B.
- The derivative A' of A is the polynomial whose coefficient of x^(i-1) is i*A[i] for each i>=1. The n-th derivative of A is the result of computing A' n times repeatedly (A if n=0).
- The indefinite integral of A is the polynomial whose coefficient of x^(i+1) is A[i]/(i+1) for each i, and whose constant term is 0.
- The composition A(B) is the final S obtained by starting from S=0 and, for i=deg A, deg A-1, ..., 0 in this order, replacing S with S*B+A[i] (it is 0 if A is 0).
- The greatest common divisor polynomial gcd(A,B) is obtained by repeatedly replacing (A, B) with (B, A mod B) while B is nonzero, and taking the monic form of the final A. gcd(0,0)=0.
- The "content" cont(A) and the "primitive part" prim(A) of a nonzero A: let L be the least common multiple of the denominators of all coefficients of A (including the coefficients that are 0), and let G be the greatest common divisor of the absolute values of all numerators (both are computed in order starting from the lowest-degree coefficient). cont(A)=G/L if lc(A)>0 and cont(A)=-G/L if lc(A)<0, and prim(A) is A with each coefficient divided by cont(A). The coefficients of prim(A) are integers, their greatest common divisor is 1, and the highest-degree coefficient is positive.
- The "square-free part" sqf(A) of a nonzero A is the quotient of A / gcd(A,A') if deg A>=1, and A if deg A=0.
- Polynomial degrees are limited to 30. The error for exceeding the degree limit (ERROR DEGREE LIMIT) is checked only where it is stated in the section "Expressions" and in the descriptions of the commands. As a result, every stored polynomial always has degree 30 or less. In other intermediate computations the degree may exceed 30.

== Notation ==
- The notation of a fraction writes the irreducible fraction p/q (q>0) as "<p>" if q=1, and as "<p>/<q>" otherwise. A negative number has "-" before p (examples: 3, -7/4, 0).
- The notation of a polynomial lists the terms with nonzero coefficients in descending order of degree. Each term is the "coefficient part" immediately followed by the "variable part". The coefficient part is the fraction notation of the absolute value of the coefficient, except that the coefficient part is not written for a term whose coefficient has absolute value 1 and whose degree is 1 or more. The variable part is empty for degree 0, "x" for degree 1, and "x^<i>" for degree i>=2. Immediately before the first term, put "-" if the coefficient is negative, and nothing if it is positive. Immediately before the second and later terms, put " + " if the coefficient is positive and " - " if it is negative (each with one space before and after the sign). 0 is written "0" (examples: -x^3 + 2/3x - 1, x^2 + 1, 1/2x^2 - x, -7/4, x, 0).

== Error checking ==
For every command, if there is an error, output exactly one error line and do nothing else for that command (no polynomial is created, changed or deleted). The errors of commands other than DEF are checked in the following order, and the first one that applies is output.
(1) For DIV when R1 and R2 are the same name, and for XGCD when two or more of R1, R2, R3 are the same name: "ERROR SAME NAME".
(2) Check the names of the polynomials used in the computation (including A of PRINT, DELETE and DEGREE) in the order of the arguments; if no polynomial with that name exists, "ERROR UNKNOWN <name>" for the first such name.
(3) Check the rational-literal arguments in the order of the arguments; if any of them has q=0, "ERROR ZERO DENOMINATOR". The "-inf" and "inf" of STURM are not checked.
(4) Check the errors listed under "Check" in the description of each command, in the order listed.
The errors of DEF are defined in the section "Expressions".

== Expressions ==
An expression is a sequence of "tokens". Read the expression from left to right, skipping spaces, and split it into tokens as follows.
  Number: a sequence of digits (including all digits that follow). It may have leading zeros; its value is its decimal value (example: 007 is 7).
  Name: one uppercase letter followed by zero or more uppercase letters or digits (including all uppercase letters and digits that follow).
  x, +, -, *, /, ^, (, ), ': each is a single character forming one token.
Spaces only separate tokens and have no other meaning (examples: "1 2" is two numbers, "P Q" is two names, "Px" is the name P and x). The "position" of a token is the index in the expression of its first character (the first character of the expression is character 1).

A valid expression is defined as follows.
  expression: zero or one "-" first, then one term, followed by zero or more occurrences of "+ term" or "- term".
  term: one factor, followed by zero or more occurrences of "* factor", "/ factor", or "factor" without an operator. A factor without an operator (an implicit product) is restricted to a factor that starts with x, a name, or "(".
  factor: a primary, or "primary ^ number".
  primary: one of a number, x, a name reference, a function call, or "( expression )". A name reference is a name followed by zero or more "'", and a function call is "name reference ( expression )". When a name reference is immediately followed by "(", it is always read as a function call.

The value of an expression is defined as follows.
- A number is the constant with that value, and x is the polynomial x.
- A name reference is the polynomial with that name differentiated as many times as the number of "'". A function call is the composition F(G), where F is the polynomial of the name reference and G is the value of the expression inside the parentheses.
- "primary ^ number" is the value of the primary raised to the power number. "^" applies only to the immediately preceding primary.
- Within a term, "*" and the implicit product are products and "/" is division, computed from left to right. The right side of a division must be a nonzero constant, and each coefficient of the left side is divided by that constant.
- The "-" at the beginning of an expression negates the value of the first term. "+" and "-" are computed from left to right.
- Examples: "x/2x" is (x/2)*x, which is 1/2x^2; "6/2(x+1)" is 3*(x+1); "-x^2" is -(x^2); "2P'(x+1)" is 2*(P'(x+1)); "P^2(x)" is (P^2)*x; "(Q)(x)" is Q*x; "P (x)" is P(x).

The expression of DEF is processed in the following order.
(1) Syntax check: look at the tokens from left to right; if there is a smallest i such that the sequence from the first token to the i-th token is not a prefix of any valid expression, output "ERROR SYNTAX AT <p>", where p is the position of the i-th token. If there is no such i and the whole token sequence is not a valid expression (the case where the expression ends prematurely), output "ERROR SYNTAX AT END". Examples: "x^2 3" gives 5, "--x" gives 2, "x^-2" gives 3, "(x)'" gives 4, "P'2" gives 3, and "x +" and "(x+1" give END.
(2) Evaluation: if there is no syntax error, compute the value of the expression. Each operation computes the left operand, then the right operand, then the operation itself, in this order. A name reference looks up the polynomial of the name and then differentiates it. A function call computes the name reference, then computes the expression inside the parentheses, then composes, in this order. Output the line of the first of the following errors that occurs when computing in this order.
  No polynomial with the name exists: "ERROR UNKNOWN <name>"
  The right side of a division is 0: "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO"
  The right side of a division has degree 1 or more: "ERROR NONCONSTANT DIVISOR"
  In a product, both sides are nonzero and the sum of their degrees exceeds 30: "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT"
  In "primary ^ number", the value of the primary is nonzero and the product of its degree and the number exceeds 30: "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT" (checked before computing the power)
  In a function call, the degrees of F and G are both 1 or more and the product of the degrees exceeds 30: "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT" (checked before computing the composition)
Examples: "UNK + 1/0" gives ERROR UNKNOWN UNK, and "1/0 + UNK" gives ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO (UNK is a name that does not exist).

== Managing polynomials ==

DEF R e
Processing: store the value of the expression e in R. An R inside e denotes the value of R before storing.

PRINT A
Processing: output "<A> = <notation of A>".

DELETE A
Processing: delete A and output "deleted <A>".

LIST
Processing: for all stored polynomials, in ascending order of name, output "<name> = <polynomial notation>". Names are compared character by character from the beginning by character code (ASCII); if one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one is smaller (example: the order E1, E10, E3, P). If there are no polynomials, output "no polynomials".

DEGREE A
Processing: output "deg(<A>) = <deg A>". If A is 0, deg A is written "-inf".

== Arithmetic and the Euclidean algorithm ==

ADD R A B
Processing: store A+B in R.

SUB R A B
Processing: store A-B in R.

MUL R A B
Check: if A and B are both nonzero and deg A+deg B>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: store A*B in R.

POW R A n
Check: if A is nonzero and deg A*n>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: store A^n in R.

DIV R1 R2 A B
Check: if B is 0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO".
Processing: store the quotient of A / B in R1 and A mod B in R2, in this order.

GCD R A B
Processing: store gcd(A,B) in R.

XGCD R1 R2 R3 A B
Processing: compute G, S and T by the following steps, and store G in R1, S in R2 and T in R3, in this order. Starting from r0=A, r1=B, s0=1, s1=0, t0=0, t1=1, while r1 is nonzero, repeatedly let q be the quotient of r0 / r1 and replace (r0, r1) with (r1, r0 mod r1), (s0, s1) with (s1, s0-q*s1), and (t0, t1) with (t1, t0-q*t1). When this ends, if r0 is nonzero, G is the monic form of r0, and S and T are s0 and t0 with each coefficient divided by lc(r0). If r0 is 0, G=r0, S=s0, T=t0. Then G=gcd(A,B) and S*A+T*B=G.

LCM R A B
Check: if A and B are both nonzero and deg A+deg B-deg gcd(A,B)>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: store in R 0 if either A or B is 0, and otherwise the monic form of (the quotient of A / gcd(A,B))*B.

INVMOD R A M
Check: if M is 0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". If deg M=0, "ERROR DEGREE TOO LOW". Compute G, S and T from A and M by the same steps as XGCD; if G is not the constant 1, "ERROR NOT COPRIME".
Processing: store S mod M in R. This is the polynomial of degree less than deg M whose product with A leaves the remainder 1 when divided by M.

CRT R A1 M1 A2 M2
Check: if M1 or M2 is 0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". If deg M1+deg M2>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT". Compute G, S and T from M1 and M2 by the same steps as XGCD; if G is not the constant 1, "ERROR NOT COPRIME".
Processing: with r1=A1 mod M1, r2=A2 mod M2 and U=((r2-r1)*S) mod M2, store r1+M1*U in R. This is the polynomial of degree less than deg M1+deg M2 whose remainder is r1 when divided by M1 and r2 when divided by M2.

MODPOW R A n M
Check: if M is 0, "ERROR DIVISION BY ZERO". If 2*deg M-2>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: starting from X=1 mod M, Y=A mod M, e=n, repeat the following while e>0: if e is odd, replace X with (X*Y) mod M; replace e with the integer part of e/2; and if that e is nonzero, replace Y with (Y*Y) mod M. Store the final X in R.

== Calculus and values ==

DERIV R A n
Processing: store the n-th derivative of A in R.

INTEG R A
Check: if A is nonzero and deg A+1>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: store the indefinite integral of A in R.

AREA A u v
Processing: let I be the indefinite integral of A (the degree limit is not checked), and output "integral(<A>, <u>, <v>) = <fraction notation of I(v)-I(u)>". u and v are written in the fraction notation of their values.

TANGENT R A c
Processing: with y=A(c) and s=A'(c), store s*x+(y-s*c) (the polynomial whose x coefficient is s and whose constant term is y-s*c) in R.

TAYLOR A c
Processing: let B be the composition A(x+c) of A and the polynomial x+c, and output "taylor(<A>, <c>) = <notation of B>". However, in the notation of B, the string V is used instead of the "x" of the variable part ("V" for degree 1, "V^<i>" for degree i>=2). V is "x" if c=0, "(x - <c>)" if c>0, and "(x + <-c>)" if c<0. c and -c are written in the fraction notation of their values (examples: if H=1/2x^2 - 1/3x, then taylor(H, 1) = 1/2(x - 1)^2 + 2/3(x - 1) + 1/6; if K=-x^3 - 6x^2 - 7x + 2, then taylor(K, -2) = -(x + 2)^3 + 5(x + 2)).

COMPOSE R A B
Check: if deg A>=1, deg B>=1 and deg A*deg B>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: store the composition A(B) in R.

EVAL A c1 c2 ... cm
Processing: for i=1, ..., m in this order, output "<A>(<ci>) = <fraction notation of A(ci)>". ci is written in the fraction notation of its value.

== Roots and factorization ==

Rational root procedure: for a nonzero A, compute the "list of rational roots" and the "remaining factor" as follows.
(1) Let P=prim(A). While P[0]=0, repeatedly divide P by x (lower the degree of each coefficient by 1); let z be the number of times this was done.
(2) Let a=|P[0]| and b=lc(P). For all positive divisors s of a and positive divisors t of b such that the greatest common divisor of s and t is 1, collect s/t and -s/t, and sort them in ascending order; these are the "candidates".
(3) Examine the candidates r in ascending order. For r=s'/t (irreducible fraction, t>0), while deg P>=1 and P(r)=0, repeatedly replace P with "the quotient of P / (t*x-s')"; let m be the number of times this was done. If m>=1, r is a root of multiplicity m.
(4) If z>=1, 0 is also a root of multiplicity z. All roots sorted in ascending order form the "list of rational roots", and the final P is the "remaining factor". The remaining factor has integer coefficients and a positive highest-degree coefficient, and has no rational roots.

ROOTS A
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: perform the rational root procedure. If the list of rational roots is empty, output "roots(<A>) = none"; otherwise output "roots(<A>) = <r1> (<m1>), <r2> (<m2>), ..." (the fraction notation of each root ri and its multiplicity mi, in the order of the list, separated by ", ").

FACTOR A
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: perform the rational root procedure, and output a line consisting of "factor(<A>) = <fraction notation of cont(A)>" followed by the following factors concatenated in order. Each factor is immediately preceded by " * ".
- For each root r=s/t (irreducible fraction, t>0) in the list of rational roots, in the order of the list: "x" if r=0, and otherwise "(<notation of the polynomial t*x-s>)". If the multiplicity m is 2 or more, append "^<m>" immediately after it.
- If the degree of the remaining factor is 1 or more: "(<notation of the remaining factor>)".
Examples: if F=-1/2x^3 + 1/2x, then factor(F) = -1/2 * (x + 1) * x * (x - 1); if G=3x^5 - 2x^4 - 12x^3 + 8x^2 + 12x - 8, then factor(G) = 1 * (3x - 2) * (x^4 - 4x^2 + 4); if K=-7/4, then factor(K) = -7/4.

SQFREE A
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: if deg A>=1, compute the square-free factorization by the following steps. Let W0 be the monic form of A, and let C=gcd(W0,W0'), W=the quotient of W0 / C, and i=1. While deg W>=1, repeat the following: let Y=gcd(W, C) and F=the quotient of W / Y, and if deg F>=1, record (prim(F), i). Replace C with the quotient of C / Y and W with Y, and increase i by 1. If deg A=0, nothing is recorded. Output a line consisting of "sqfree(<A>) = <fraction notation of cont(A)>" followed, in the order recorded, for each recorded pair (H, i), by " * " and then "x" if H is the polynomial x and "(<notation of H>)" otherwise, and further "^<i>" if i is 2 or more (example: if F=x^6 - x^5 - 5x^4 + x^3 + 8x^2 + 4x, then sqfree(F) = 1 * x * (x - 2)^2 * (x + 1)^3).

DESCARTES A
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: list the nonzero coefficients of A in descending order of degree, and let p be the number of places where two adjacent coefficients differ in sign. Let q be the number counted the same way using (-1)^i*A[i] instead of A[i]. Output "descartes(<A>) = positive <p>, negative <q>".

Sturm sequence: for a nonzero A, let S0=sqf(A) and S1=S0', and for i=1, 2, ... in this order, while Si is nonzero, let S(i+1)=-(S(i-1) mod Si). The Sturm sequence is S0, S1, S2, ... in this order, up to just before the first one that is 0 (if S0 is a constant, then S1=0, so the sequence is S0 alone). For t that is a fraction, -inf or inf, the sign at t of each polynomial Si of the Sturm sequence is the sign of Si(t) if t is a fraction, the sign of lc(Si) if t=inf, and, if t=-inf, the sign of lc(Si) when deg Si is even and its opposite when deg Si is odd. Arrange these signs in the order of the sequence, remove the zeros, and let V(t) be the number of places where two adjacent signs differ. N(u,v)=V(u)-V(v) is the number of distinct real roots of A in the range u<x<=v.

STURM A u v
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL". If u>=v, "ERROR BAD INTERVAL". Here -inf is smaller than every fraction, inf is larger than every fraction, -inf equals -inf, and inf equals inf.
Processing: output "sturm(<A>, <u>, <v>) = <N(u,v)>". u and v are written in the fraction notation of their values if they are fractions; -inf and inf are written as they are.

ISOLATE A n
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: using the Sturm sequence, find intervals of width at most 1/2^n, each containing one distinct real root of A, as follows. Let S0=sqf(A) and beta=1+(the maximum of |S0[i]/lc(S0)| over i=0, 1, ..., deg S0-1) (beta=1 if deg S0=0). Define the procedure "examine" for an interval (l, h] (the range l<x<=h) as follows, and examine (-beta, beta].
(a) If N(l,h) is 2 or more, let g=(l+h)/2, examine (l, g], and then examine (g, h].
(b) If N(l,h) is 1, while h-l>1/2^n, repeatedly let g=(l+h)/2 and replace h with g if N(l,g)>=1, and otherwise replace l with g; then record the final interval (l, h].
(c) If N(l,h) is 0, do nothing.
If no interval was recorded, output "isolate(<A>, <n>) = none"; otherwise output "isolate(<A>, <n>) = (<l1>, <h1>], (<l2>, <h2>], ..." (the recorded intervals, in the order recorded, with both ends written in fraction notation, separated by ", ").

== Resultant and properties of roots ==

RESULTANT A B
Check: if A or B is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL".
Processing: compute the resultant res(A,B) by the following steps, and output "res(<A>, <B>) = <fraction notation of res(A,B)>". Starting from r=1, P=A, Q=B, repeat the following while deg Q>=1: let T=P mod Q; if T is 0, then res(A,B)=0 and the steps end. If T is nonzero, multiply r by lc(Q)^(deg P-deg T), replace r with -r if deg P*deg Q is odd, and replace (P, Q) with (Q, T). When the repetition ends (Q is then a nonzero constant), res(A,B) is r*Q[0]^(deg P). Here, the e-th power w^e of a fraction w is computed by multiplying 1 by w e times.

DISCRIM A
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL". If deg A=0, "ERROR DEGREE TOO LOW".
Processing: with d=deg A, compute D=res(A,A')/lc(A), and replace D with -D if d*(d-1)/2 is odd. Output "disc(<A>) = <fraction notation of D>".

POWERSUM A n
Check: if A is 0, "ERROR ZERO POLYNOMIAL". If deg A=0, "ERROR DEGREE TOO LOW".
Processing: with d=deg A and C the monic form of A, compute the sum pj of the j-th powers of the roots of A (complex roots, each counted as many times as its multiplicity) for j=1, 2, ..., n in this order, as follows. Start from w=j*C[d-j] if j<=d and from w=0 if j>d, add C[d-i]*p(j-i) to w for i=1, 2, ..., (the smaller of j-1 and d) in this order, and let pj=-w. Output "powersum(<A>) = <p1>, <p2>, ..., <pn>" (the fraction notations separated by ", ").

== Interpolation and special polynomials ==

INTERP R x1 y1 x2 y2 ... xm ym
Check: examine j=2, ..., m in this order; if the value of xj equals the value of some earlier xi, "ERROR DUPLICATE X <fraction notation of the value of xj>" for the first such xj.
Processing: compute the polynomial of degree at most m-1 that passes through the points (x1, y1), ..., (xm, ym), using Newton's divided differences. Let di=yi (i=1, ..., m), and for j=1, 2, ..., m-1 in this order, and for each j, for i=m, m-1, ..., j+1 in this order, replace di with (di-d(i-1))/(xi-x(i-j)). Then, starting from P=0, for i=m, m-1, ..., 1 in this order, replace P with P*(x-xi)+di (x-xi is the polynomial whose x coefficient is 1 and whose constant term is -xi), and store the final P in R.

SPECIAL R k n
Check: if k is none of chebyshev, chebyshev2, legendre, hermite, laguerre, "ERROR UNKNOWN KIND <k>". If n>30, "ERROR DEGREE LIMIT".
Processing: determine F0 and F1 according to k, compute F(j+1) for j=1, 2, ..., n-1 in this order, and store Fn in R.
  chebyshev (Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind): F0=1, F1=x, F(j+1) is the product of the polynomial 2x and Fj minus F(j-1)
  chebyshev2 (Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind): F0=1, F1=2x, F(j+1) is given by the same formula as chebyshev
  legendre (Legendre polynomials): F0=1, F1=x, F(j+1) is the product of the polynomial (2j+1)x and Fj minus j*F(j-1), with each coefficient multiplied by 1/(j+1)
  hermite (Hermite polynomials): F0=1, F1=2x, F(j+1) is the product of the polynomial 2x and Fj minus 2j*F(j-1)
  laguerre (Laguerre polynomials): F0=1, F1=1-x (-x+1), F(j+1) is the product of the polynomial -x+(2j+1) and Fj minus j*F(j-1), with each coefficient multiplied by 1/(j+1)

[Output format]

- Do not put spaces at the beginning or end of a line, and do not output empty lines. Every line ends with a newline.
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Fractions are written in fraction notation, and polynomials in polynomial notation.
- The error lines are of the following 14 kinds (the parts in < > are replaced with the relevant name, position, value or string).
