Write a program that converts the 8 integers 9, 14, 49, 94, 444, 1994, 2768, 3999 (each between 1 and 3999 inclusive), in the given order, into Roman numerals, and then converts each Roman numeral back into an integer. To convert to a Roman numeral, start with the original integer as the remaining value and examine the value-symbol pairs 1000 (M), 900 (CM), 500 (D), 400 (CD), 100 (C), 90 (XC), 50 (L), 40 (XL), 10 (X), 9 (IX), 5 (V), 4 (IV), 1 (I) in this order; while the remaining value is greater than or equal to the value of the pair, append its symbol on the right and subtract the value of the pair from the remaining value. To convert back to an integer, let the symbols I, V, X, L, C, D, M have the values 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1000 respectively, and examine the symbols from left to right: a symbol whose value is smaller than the value of the symbol immediately to its right has its value subtracted, and every other symbol (including the rightmost symbol) has its value added.

Output format:
Print one line per integer, in the given order, in the format "n -> R -> m". n is the original integer, R is the converted Roman numeral (ASCII uppercase letters), and m is the value obtained by converting R back into an integer; n and m are decimal integers without extra leading zeros. All other parts are ASCII characters exactly as written, with one ASCII space before and after each "->" (a hyphen and a greater-than sign). Print only these 8 lines, with no trailing whitespace at the end of any line, and end every line with a newline.

Example output (the numbers are illustrative and differ from the actual answer):
27 -> XXVII -> 27
1490 -> MCDXC -> 1490
