For the undirected graph with the 9 vertices 0 to 8 and the 9 edges (5, 0), (3, 8), (7, 2), (1, 6), (0, 2), (5, 3), (7, 5), (6, 4), (8, 7) (in this order), find the order in which the vertices are visited by a depth-first search (DFS) and by a breadth-first search (BFS), both starting from vertex 0, and also divide the graph into connected components.
An edge (a, b) is an undirected edge connecting vertex a and vertex b. No edge appears more than once, and no vertex is connected to itself.
In both searches, the neighbors of a vertex (the vertices directly connected to it by an edge) are examined in increasing order of their numbers, not in the order in which the edges are given.
DFS is a recursive preorder traversal. When vertex v is visited, mark v as visited and append it to the end of the order, then examine the neighbors of v in increasing order of number, and whenever an examined neighbor is not yet visited, recursively visit that vertex.
BFS uses a queue. First mark vertex 0 as visited and put it into the queue; then, until the queue is empty, repeat the following: take out the vertex v at the front of the queue, append it to the end of the order, examine the neighbors of v in increasing order of number, and mark each neighbor that is not yet visited as visited and put it at the end of the queue.
Both searches visit only the vertices that can be reached from vertex 0 by following edges.
A connected component is a group of vertices that can all reach one another by following edges; every vertex belongs to exactly one connected component (a vertex that is not connected to any other vertex forms a connected component by itself).

Output format:
Print the following lines in this order.
Line 1 is "DFS:" followed by the numbers of the vertices visited by DFS, in the order in which they were visited.
Line 2 is "BFS:" followed by the numbers of the vertices visited by BFS, in the order in which they were visited.
Line 3 is "components: C", where C is the number of connected components.
From line 4 on, print one line per connected component: "component i:" followed by the numbers of the vertices in that connected component in increasing order. i is a sequential number starting from 1. Print the connected components in increasing order of the smallest vertex number they contain.
"DFS", "BFS", "components" and "component" are written in ASCII letters. Put one ASCII space after each ":" and between consecutive numbers, and also one ASCII space between "component" and i. Write every number as a decimal integer without extra leading zeros. Print only these 3 + C lines, with no trailing whitespace on any line, and end every line with a newline.

Example output (the numbers are illustrative and differ from the actual answer):
DFS: 0 1 6 3
BFS: 0 1 3 6
components: 3
component 1: 0 1 3 6
component 2: 2 5 8
component 3: 4 7
