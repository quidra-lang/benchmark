For an integer array (11 elements) whose values are 72, 85, 64, 90, 85, 58, 77, 85, 69, 93, 71, in that order, compute the mean, median, mode, range and standard deviation. The mean is the real number (double-precision floating point) obtained by dividing the sum of the 11 values by 11. The median is the 6th value when the values are sorted in increasing order. The mode is the value that appears most often (if several values appear most often, it is the smallest of them, but with this data it is unique). The range is the maximum value minus the minimum value. The standard deviation is the population standard deviation: going through the array from the first element, add up one by one the squares of the differences obtained by subtracting the mean from each value to get the sum S, and take the square root of S divided by 11. Compute the standard deviation in double-precision floating point, using the mean above.

Output format:
Output the following 5 lines in this order.
Line 1: the ASCII "mean:" and one ASCII space, followed by the mean.
Line 2: "median:" and one ASCII space, followed by the median.
Line 3: "mode:" and one ASCII space, followed by the mode.
Line 4: "range:" and one ASCII space, followed by the range.
Line 5: "stdev:" and one ASCII space, followed by the standard deviation.
Round the mean and the standard deviation half up at the 4th decimal place to 3 decimal places, and always write exactly 3 digits after the decimal point. Write the median, mode and range as decimal integers without extra leading zeros. Output only these 5 lines, with no trailing whitespace on any line, and end every line with a newline.

Example output (the numbers are illustrative and differ from the actual answer):
mean: 52.364
median: 51
mode: 48
range: 27
stdev: 8.105
