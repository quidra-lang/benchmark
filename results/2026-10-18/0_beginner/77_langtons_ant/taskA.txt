On a board of 11 rows by 11 columns, move Langton's ant for 300 steps. Rows are numbered 0 to 10 from top to bottom, and columns are numbered 0 to 10 from left to right. Initially every cell is white, and the ant is on the cell at row 5, column 5, facing up (the direction in which the row number decreases). In one step, do the following in this order. First, if the cell the ant is on is white, the ant turns 90 degrees to the right (clockwise); if it is black, the ant turns 90 degrees to the left (counterclockwise). For example, an ant facing up that turns 90 degrees to the right faces right (the direction in which the column number increases). Next, invert the color of the cell the ant is on (white becomes black, black becomes white). Finally, the ant moves one cell forward in the direction it is facing. The edges of the board wrap around to the opposite edges; for example, moving up from row 0 goes to row 10, and moving right from column 10 goes to column 0.

Output format:
Print the board after the 300 steps have finished as 11 lines, starting from row 0. Each line represents the cells of that row, starting from column 0, with one character per cell: a black cell is "#", a white cell is ".", and the cell the ant is on is "A" regardless of its color. Finally, as the 12th line, print the number N of black cells (including the ant's cell if it is black) in the form "black: N". Put one ASCII space after ":", and write everything else in ASCII lowercase exactly as shown. Write every number as a decimal integer with no extra leading zeros. Print only these 12 lines, with no trailing whitespace on any line, and end every line with a newline. The example output below is for moving the ant only 12 steps on the same board.
