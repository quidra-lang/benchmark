There are 3 pegs A, B and C, and peg A holds 4 disks of different sizes, stacked from the bottom in decreasing order of size. The disks are numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 from the smallest. A disk is moved one at a time, from the top of one peg to the top of another peg, and a larger disk must never be placed on a smaller disk. Using recursion, find the sequence of moves that transfers all 4 disks to peg C, using peg B as the auxiliary peg. To move the n disks numbered 1 to n from the source peg to the target peg, first move the top n-1 disks from the source peg to the remaining peg (the auxiliary peg), then move disk n from the source peg to the target peg, and finally move the n-1 disks from the auxiliary peg to the target peg. Moving the n-1 disks uses the same method, with the auxiliary peg being the one peg other than that move's source and target pegs.

Output format:
Each time a disk is moved, output one line in the format "move disk d from X to Y", in the order of the moves. d is the number of the disk moved, X is the source peg and Y is the destination peg; pegs are written as the ASCII uppercase letters A, B, C. After all moves, output as the last line the total number of moves N in the format "total moves: N". All other parts are ASCII lowercase exactly as written; separate words, numbers and peg letters from each other by exactly one ASCII space, and also put exactly one ASCII space after ":". Write every number as a decimal integer without extra leading zeros. Output only the move lines and the final line, with no trailing whitespace on any line, and end every line with a newline. The example output below is for moving 2 disks from peg A to peg B.

Example output (the numbers are illustrative and differ from the actual answer):
move disk 1 from A to C
move disk 2 from A to B
move disk 1 from C to B
total moves: 3
