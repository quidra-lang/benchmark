There are 4 kinds of coins, 1 yen, 5 yen, 6 yen and 9 yen, and any number of coins of each kind may be used. For each of the 6 amounts 8 yen, 11 yen, 15 yen, 22 yen, 33 yen and 40 yen, compute the following 3 values.
- Minimum count: the minimum number of coins needed to pay exactly that amount. Compute it by dynamic programming that records the minimum count in a table for each amount in order, from 0 yen up to the target amount.
- Greedy count: the number of coins used when you repeatedly use one coin of the largest denomination that does not exceed the remaining amount, until the remaining amount becomes 0 yen.
- Number of combinations: the number of combinations of coins that pay exactly that amount. The order in which coins are used is not distinguished; combinations whose tuples of counts of the 4 kinds of coins differ are counted as different combinations. For example, for 6 yen there are 3 combinations: one 6-yen coin; one 5-yen coin and one 1-yen coin; six 1-yen coins. Compute this too by dynamic programming that records the number of combinations for each amount in a table.

Output format:
Print 6 lines in total, one line per amount, in the order of the amounts above. Each line consists of, in this order: the amount, ":", one space, "min=", the minimum count, one space, "greedy=", the greedy count, one space, "ways=", and the number of combinations. Put no whitespace before or after "=". Do not append "yen" to the amount, and write every number as a decimal integer. Do not put trailing whitespace at the end of any line, and end every line, including the last one, with a newline.

Example output (the numbers are illustrative and differ from the actual answer):
3: min=3 greedy=3 ways=1
13: min=3 greedy=5 ways=7
30: min=4 greedy=6 ways=41
