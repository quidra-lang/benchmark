[Overview]
Write a program that decodes and verifies aviation weather reports (METAR, SPECI, TAF). It reads the three files in the input/ folder (the list of airports, the sequence of reports, and the settings), decodes the reports one by one according to a fixed grammar, and computes derived values such as the ceiling and the flight category. It also expands each TAF (aerodrome forecast) into a sequence of hourly forecasts and verifies the forecasts by comparing them with the observations (METAR and SPECI). The results are written to five files in the output/ folder, and nothing is printed to standard output.
All computation uses only integers (and fractions of integers); no floating-point numbers are used.

[Input files]
Every input file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF). There are no empty lines.

* input/stations.txt (list of airports)
One airport per line. Each line consists of the four items "station code", "elevation", "runway list" and "airport name", in this order, separated by single spaces.
- The station code is 4 uppercase letters and is unique within the file.
- The elevation is an integer giving the elevation of the airport in feet; a negative number is prefixed with "-".
- The runway list consists of "name/heading" entries separated by commas (,). A name is either a 2-digit number (01 to 36) or such a number followed by one of the letters L, C, R; names are unique within an airport. The heading is a 3-digit number giving the direction of the runway in degrees; its value is a multiple of 10 between 10 and 360 inclusive. The order of the runways is significant (below it is called "list order").
- The airport name is the whole rest of the line and consists of uppercase letters and spaces.
Example:
RJTT 21 34L/340,22/220 TOKYO HANEDA

* input/reports.txt (sequence of reports)
The reports in time order. A line that does not begin with a space starts a new report, and a line that begins with a space (a continuation line) continues the same report as the line before it. Continuation lines appear only in TAFs, and the first line of the file is not a continuation line.
Each line consists of words separated by single spaces. A continuation line has the same form once its leading one or more spaces are removed. There are no spaces at the end of a line.
The words of a report are the words of all of its lines concatenated in order. The reports are numbered consecutively from 1 starting at the top (the report number), and the words within a report are numbered from 1 starting at the beginning (the word number). Word numbers continue across lines.
Example (two reports; the second line is a continuation line):
TAF RJTT 311900Z 3120/0102 34012KT 9999 FEW030
  BECMG 3121/3123 02010KT 6000 -RA BKN025
METAR RJTT 312000Z 33013G25KT 300V010 9999 FEW030 08/M01 Q1022 NOSIG
The first report consists of 13 words, and "BECMG" is word number 8.

* input/params.txt (settings)
It consists of the following 5 lines; each line is an item name and its value, separated by single spaces.
- Line 1: YEAR and the year (a 4-digit integer)
- Line 2: MONTH and the month (an integer from 1 to 12)
- Line 3: CROSSWIND_LIMIT and the crosswind limit (knots, an integer >= 0)
- Line 4: TAILWIND_LIMIT and the tailwind limit (knots, an integer >= 0)
- Line 5: VERIFY and one or more station codes of the airports to verify (separated by single spaces)
The year and month give the month to which the report times (the observation time of METAR and SPECI, the issue time of TAF) belong (below, the "base month"). Days written in the validity period and the change groups of a TAF may belong to the following month (see "Month of a day"). Every station code in VERIFY is in stations.txt, and they are all distinct.
Example:
YEAR 2027
MONTH 12
CROSSWIND_LIMIT 20
TAILWIND_LIMIT 5
VERIFY RJTT

[Processing]
* Common notation
- floor(x) is the largest integer less than or equal to x. Negative numbers are rounded away from zero (floor(-4.2) = -5). Below, every division written inside floor(...) has this meaning.
- To "write a fraction p/q (q > 0) in lowest terms" means to divide p and q by the greatest common divisor of |p| and q and then write it in the form "p/q" (with a leading "-" if p is negative). If p = 0 it is "0/1".
- The number of days in a month is 31 for months 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12 and 30 for months 4, 6, 9, 11; February has 29 days if the year is divisible by 4 and not by 100, or divisible by 400, and 28 days otherwise. Let N be the number of days of the base month.
- A time is represented as the number of minutes (an integer) elapsed since day 1, 00:00 of the base month. Day D, hour H, minute M of the base month is ((D - 1) * 24 + H) * 60 + M, and day D, hour H, minute M of the month after the base month (January of the next year if the base month is December) is N * 1440 + ((D - 1) * 24 + H) * 60 + M. The same formula is used when H = 24 (it represents 00:00 of the next day).
- How to write a time m (below, STAMP): if m < N * 1440 it is a date in the base month; otherwise it is a date in the following month and N * 1440 is subtracted from m. Then, with day = floor(m / 1440) + 1, hour = floor((m mod 1440) / 60) and minute = m mod 60, write it in the form "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MMZ". The year has 4 digits; the month, day, hour and minute have 2 digits, with a leading 0 if they have only one digit. Example: 2028-01-01T00:30Z
- The "hour mark" of a time t is t - (t mod 60).

* Report header
The leading words of each report (the header) are examined in the following order.
1. If word 1 is none of METAR, SPECI, TAF, the report is "rejected" (the reason is invalid type).
2. For a TAF, if word 2 is AMD or COR, it is read as a modifier (AMD means amended, COR means corrected).
3. The following words are read one word at a time: "station" and "time" in this order for METAR and SPECI, and "station", "time" and "validity" in this order for TAF.
  - Station: it must be a station code listed in stations.txt.
  - Time: it must be 7 characters, a 6-digit number DDHHMM followed by "Z", with DD between 1 and N inclusive, HH <= 23 and MM <= 59. Its value is day DD, hour HH, minute MM of the base month (the observation time for METAR and SPECI, the issue time for TAF).
  - Validity: it must be 9 characters: a 4-digit number D1H1 (a 2-digit day and a 2-digit hour), "/", and a 4-digit number D2H2, in this order. H1 must be <= 23, and H2 must be between 1 and 24 inclusive. The months of D1 and D2 are determined by "Month of a day" below, and each of D1 and D2 must be at least 1 and at most the number of days of its month. The start s (day D1, H1:00) and the end e (day D2, H2:00) must satisfy s < e <= s + 1800 (30 hours).
  If there is no word at a required position (the words of the report end there), the report is rejected, the reason is the one of missing station, missing time, missing validity for that item, and the position is "the number of words in the report + 1". If a word does not satisfy the conditions, the report is also rejected, the reason is the one of invalid station, invalid time, invalid validity for that item, and the position is the word number of that word.
A rejected report only writes one line to errors.txt and does not appear in any other output at all (it still consumes its report number). The words after the header (the body) first go through "Group joining" below and are then processed according to "Body of METAR and SPECI" for METAR and SPECI, and according to "Body of TAF" for TAF.
Month of a day: a day D written in the validity period or a change group of a TAF is a day of the base month if D is greater than or equal to the day of the issue time of that TAF, and a day of the following month otherwise.

* Group joining
The words of the body are collected into "groups" in order from the beginning. Normally one word is one group, but when a word consisting of exactly one digit character is immediately followed by a word consisting of "one digit, /, a 1- or 2-digit number, SM" in this order, the string formed by joining the two words with a single space (e.g. 1 1/2SM) is one group. The position of a group is the word number of its first word. Below, the string of a group itself is also called the "group".

* Group formats and value conditions
For each kind of group, a "format" by which a group is recognized as that kind and "value conditions" for its value to be correct are defined. A group that matches a format but does not satisfy the value conditions is an "invalid group". Some value conditions depend on the groups already accepted in the same "section" (each of the METAR main part, a trend section, the TAF base forecast and a change group, defined below).

(1) Modifier: AUTO or COR. There are no value conditions. AUTO means automated, COR means corrected.

(2) Wind: "a 3-digit number (direction) or VRB", "a 2- to 3-digit number (speed)", "G and a 2- to 3-digit number (gust, optional)", "KT or MPS (unit)", in this order. Value conditions:
  - A 3-digit direction is a multiple of 10 not greater than 360.
  - The direction being 000 is equivalent to the speed being 0. A wind with direction 000 and speed 0 is called "calm", and a calm wind cannot have a gust. The speed of a VRB (variable direction) wind is at least 1.
  - If there is a gust, gust > speed.
  If the unit is MPS, then after the value conditions are checked, the speed and the gust are each converted to floor((v * 1944 + 500) / 1000) knots. Below, speeds and gusts are always values in knots.

(3) Wind variation: 7 characters, "a 3-digit number", "V", "a 3-digit number" in this order. Value conditions: both directions are multiples of 10 between 10 and 360 inclusive and differ from each other, and a wind that is neither variable nor calm has already been accepted in the same section.

(4) Visibility: one of the following formats. Only the mile format has value conditions.
  - CAVOK: represents the visibility "10 km or more (CAVOK)"; its value is 10000 meters and its qualifier is P.
  - A 4-digit number: a visibility in meters. 9999 represents "10 km or more"; its value is 10000 meters and its qualifier is P. Otherwise the value is the written value (meters) and there is no qualifier.
  - Mile format: a single word consisting of "M or P (qualifier, optional)", "a 1- or 2-digit number (whole part) or one digit, /, a 1- or 2-digit number (fraction part)", "SM" in this order, or a group created by "Group joining" of the form "one digit (whole part), space, one digit, /, a 1- or 2-digit number, SM" (the latter has no qualifier). It is a visibility in statute miles; the qualifier M means "less than" and P means "more than". Value conditions: in the form with only a whole part, the whole part is at least 1; in a form with a fraction part, the denominator is one of 2, 4, 8, 16 and the numerator is at least 1 and less than the denominator; furthermore, in a two-word group, the whole part is at least 1.
  When visibility values are compared (flight category and lowest visibility), the qualifier is ignored and the values are compared as fractions in statute miles. The mile format is "whole part + numerator/denominator", and a value v in meters (including CAVOK) is 1000v/1609344 miles.

(5) Runway visual range (RVR): "R", "a 2-digit number", "one of the letters L, C, R (optional)", "/", "a reading", "V and a reading (optional)", "FT (optional)", "one of the letters U, D, N (optional)", in this order. A reading is "M or P (qualifier, optional)" followed by "a 4-digit number". Value conditions: the string after R and before / (the runway name) is equal to one of the runway names of the airport, and if there are two readings, the number of the first < the number of the second. If FT is present the unit is feet, otherwise meters. U, D, N mean an upward, downward and no-change trend, respectively.

(6) Weather: "one of -, +, VC (intensity, optional)" followed by one or more concatenated two-letter abbreviations of the descriptors or phenomena in the table below. A group whose whole word is NSW also has the weather format (it represents "no significant weather").
  Descriptors: MI shallow, BC patches, PR partial, DR low drifting, BL blowing, SH showers, TS thunderstorm, FZ freezing
  Phenomena: DZ drizzle, RA rain, SN snow, SG snow grains, IC ice crystals, PL ice pellets, GR hail, GS small hail, UP unknown precipitation, BR mist, FG fog, FU smoke, VA volcanic ash, DU dust, SA sand, HZ haze, PY spray, PO dust whirls, SQ squalls, FC funnel cloud, SS sandstorm, DS duststorm
  The 9 phenomena DZ, RA, SN, SG, IC, PL, GR, GS, UP are called "precipitation". The intensity - is light, + is heavy and VC is nearby; an omitted intensity is moderate (no word).
  Value conditions for weather other than NSW:
  - A descriptor may appear only at the beginning of the sequence of abbreviations (so there is at most one).
  - The same phenomenon does not appear twice or more.
  - If there is no phenomenon, the descriptor is TS or SH.
  - If the intensity is - or +, at least one precipitation is included.
  - If there are two or more phenomena, all of them are precipitation.
  Furthermore, for all weather (including NSW): it is invalid if three weather groups have already been accepted in the same section. It is invalid if NSW has already been accepted in the same section. NSW itself is valid only in a TAF change group or a METAR trend section, and only when no weather has been accepted yet in that section; elsewhere (the METAR main part, the TAF base forecast) it is invalid.

(7) Cloud: one of the following formats.
  - One of FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC (amount), a 3-digit number (height of the cloud base), CB or TCU (optional), in this order. The amounts are few, scattered, broken and overcast respectively; CB is cumulonimbus and TCU is towering cumulus.
  - VV followed by a 3-digit number (height of the vertical visibility).
  - SKC (sky clear), CLR (clear below 12000 ft), NSC (no significant cloud).
  The height is the value of the 3-digit number * 100 feet. Value conditions: if a cloud group has already been accepted in the same section, the new group must have one of the FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC formats, the last accepted group must also have one of the FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC formats, and the height of the new group must be greater than the height of the last accepted group (so VV, SKC, CLR, NSC can be used only as the only cloud group of a section).

(8) Temperature: "M (optional) and a 2-digit number", "/", "M (optional) and a 2-digit number", in this order. The first is the temperature and the second is the dew point (integers in degrees Celsius); M indicates a negative number (M00 is 0). Value condition: dew point <= temperature.

(9) Pressure: "A or Q" followed by a 4-digit number. A is in hundredths of an inch of mercury (inHg), Q is in hectopascals (hPa). Value conditions: between 2500 and 3150 inclusive for A, between 850 and 1070 inclusive for Q.
  Conversion: from an hPa value q, the value in hundredths of inHg is floor((q * 2953 + 500) / 1000); from a value a in hundredths of inHg, the hPa value is floor((a * 33864 + 50000) / 100000).

(10) No trend: NOSIG (no significant change). There are no value conditions.

* Matching groups to slots
The groups of a section are matched in order against the sequence of "slots" of that section. Each slot corresponds to one kind of group (one of (1) to (10) above), and some slots are "repeatable". With the "current slot" initially set to the first slot, the groups are processed one by one from the beginning as follows.
1. Look at the slots in order from the current slot to the last slot, and find the first slot whose kind's format the group matches.
2. If one is found and the value conditions are satisfied, accept the group and record its value. If that slot is repeatable, the current slot stays that slot; otherwise the current slot becomes the next slot. However, if the accepted group is CAVOK, set both the weather and the cloud of that section to the "empty sequence", and set the current slot to the slot after the cloud slot (if the cloud slot is the last slot, no later group matches any slot).
3. If one is found but the value conditions are not satisfied, the group is skipped as an invalid group (invalid group). The current slot does not change.
4. If none is found, the group is skipped as an out-of-order group (out of order group) if it matches the format of some slot before the current slot, or as an unknown group (unknown group) if it matches the format of no slot. The current slot does not change.
Every skipped group is recorded in errors.txt. The value of a slot with no accepted group is "none".

* Body of METAR and SPECI
The groups of the body are divided as follows.
- The groups after the first RMK group are the "remarks" (RMK itself is not included anywhere). If there is no RMK, there are no remarks.
- Among the groups before RMK, the groups from the first BECMG or TEMPO group up to the group just before RMK (the last group if there is no RMK) are the "trend part".
- The groups before that are the "main part".
The main part is matched against the following 10 slots. RVR, weather and cloud are repeatable.
  1 modifier, 2 wind, 3 wind variation, 4 visibility, 5 RVR, 6 weather, 7 cloud, 8 temperature, 9 pressure, 10 no trend
The trend part is divided into "trend sections". A trend section starts with a BECMG (becoming) or TEMPO (temporarily) group and extends up to just before the next BECMG or TEMPO group (up to the end of the trend part if there is none). Immediately after the first group of a section, time groups may be placed in one of the combinations "none", "FM", "TL", "FM, TL (in this order)", "AT". A time group is 6 characters, "one of FM, TL, AT" followed by a 4-digit number HHMM; FM means from, TL means until and AT means at. A time group that is not at this position or does not fit a combination is treated like any other group. A time group must have HH <= 23 and MM <= 59; otherwise it is recorded in errors.txt as invalid period, and the whole trend section (the time groups before it and all groups after it) is discarded without recording anything further. The groups after the time groups are matched against the following 4 slots. Weather and cloud are repeatable.
  1 wind, 2 visibility, 3 weather, 4 cloud
In the remarks, only the following two kinds of groups are read; all other groups are ignored without being recorded.
- 6 characters, SLP followed by a 3-digit number ppp: the sea level pressure. Its value in units of 0.1 hPa is 9000 + ppp if ppp >= 500, and 10000 + ppp otherwise.
- 9 characters, T, 0 or 1, a 3-digit number, 0 or 1, a 3-digit number, in this order: the precise temperature and dew point. Each is a value in units of 0.1 degree, positive if the character before it is 0 and negative if it is 1.

* Body of TAF
Among the groups of the body, BECMG, TEMPO, PROB30, PROB40, and an 8-character group consisting of FM followed by a 6-digit number DDHHMM, are "change indicators". The groups before the first change indicator are the "base forecast". The change indicators are read in order from the beginning as follows.
- If the group right after PROB30 or PROB40 is TEMPO, the two together form one indicator (PROB30 TEMPO, PROB40 TEMPO).
- An indicator other than FM must be immediately followed by a group of the form "a 4-digit number, /, a 4-digit number" (the period). If there is none, missing period is recorded in errors.txt (the position is the first group of the indicator) and that change group is discarded. The period must satisfy the same rules as the validity period (H1 <= 23, 1 <= H2 <= 24, month of a day, range of the day, start < end), and furthermore, with the validity period of the TAF written as [vs, ve), it must satisfy vs <= start and end <= ve (there is no 30-hour limit). If it does not, invalid period is recorded (the position is the period group) and that change group is discarded.
- For an FM indicator, day DD, hour HH, minute MM (HH <= 23, MM <= 59, month of a day, range of the day) is the time t, and vs <= t < ve must hold. If it does not, invalid period is recorded (the position is the FM group) and that change group is discarded.
A change group holds the groups from after the indicator (and the period) up to just before the next change indicator (up to the end of the body if there is none). The groups of a discarded change group are ignored without being recorded. The base forecast and each change group that was not discarded are each matched against the 4 slots above (1 wind, 2 visibility, 3 weather, 4 cloud).
The kind of a change group is one of FM, BECMG, TEMPO, PROB30, PROB40, PROB30 TEMPO, PROB40 TEMPO. Below, TEMPO, PROB30, PROB40, PROB30 TEMPO and PROB40 TEMPO are called "temporary change groups".

* Weather state and flight category
The METAR main part, each trend section, the base forecast and each change group each have the four elements "wind", "visibility", "weather (a sequence of groups)" and "cloud (a sequence of groups)", whose values are the values recorded during matching. If no group was accepted, the element is "none". The "empty sequence" created by CAVOK is distinguished from "none".
Ceiling: the height of the first group in the cloud sequence that is a group with amount BKN or OVC or a VV group. If there is no such group (including when the cloud is none or the empty sequence), there is no ceiling.
Flight category: with c the ceiling (infinity if there is none) and v the visibility as a fraction in miles (infinity if the visibility is none), it is LIFR if c < 500 or v < 1; otherwise IFR if c < 1000 or v < 3; otherwise MVFR if c <= 3000 or v <= 5; otherwise VFR.

* Derived values for METAR and SPECI
- inHg value a of the pressure: the written value if the pressure of the main part is A, or the value obtained by the conversion above if it is Q (in hundredths).
- Pressure altitude = elevation + (2992 - a) * 10 (feet). It is not computed if the pressure is none.
- Density altitude = pressure altitude + floor(120 * (1000 * temperature - 15000 + 2 * pressure altitude) / 1000) (feet). It is not computed if the pressure or the temperature is none.
- Runway wind components: computed only when the wind of the main part is not none and is neither variable nor calm. The following integer sine table S is used.
  S(0) = 0, S(10) = 174, S(20) = 342, S(30) = 500, S(40) = 643, S(50) = 766, S(60) = 866, S(70) = 940, S(80) = 985, S(90) = 1000
  For a multiple of 10 x, let y = x mod 360 (0 <= y < 360); sin(x) is S(y) if y <= 90, S(180 - y) if 90 < y <= 180, -S(y - 180) if 180 < y <= 270, and -S(360 - y) otherwise. cos(x) = sin(x + 90).
  With d the wind direction, h the runway heading, w the wind speed and x = d - h, the headwind component = floor(w * cos(x) / 1000) and the crosswind component = floor(w * sin(x) / 1000). If the headwind component is negative, its absolute value is a tailwind. A positive crosswind component is a crosswind from the right, and a negative one is a crosswind from the left. If there is a gust, the components with w replaced by the gust (the gust components) are also computed.
  A runway is "usable" if, for the deciding components (the gust components if there is a gust, otherwise the components of the mean wind), the absolute value of the crosswind component is at most the crosswind limit and the headwind component with its sign reversed is at most the tailwind limit.
  The "preferred runway" is the usable runway whose mean-wind headwind component is the largest. On a tie, the one that comes first in list order is chosen. If no runway is usable, there is no preferred runway.
- Wind change test (with reference angle alpha): performed only when the earlier wind and the later wind are both not none.
  - If neither wind is variable or calm, the larger of the two speeds is at least 10, and the difference of the two directions (with r = |d1 - d2| mod 360, the smaller of r and 360 - r) is at least alpha, the result is "wind shift".
  - If the absolute difference of the two speeds is at least 10, the result is "wind speed" (the speed of a calm wind is 0; the speed of a variable wind is the written value).
  - Gusts are not used. If both apply, both are listed in the order wind shift, wind speed.
- SPECI check: for a SPECI, it is compared with the previous report of the same airport (the last non-rejected METAR or SPECI before it), and the following reasons are listed in this order.
  1. If the flight categories differ, "category <previous category> to <current category>".
  2. The result of the "wind change test" with alpha = 45, with the main-part wind of the previous report as the earlier wind and the current main-part wind as the later wind.
  3. If the presence of a group with descriptor TS in the main-part weather differs, "thunderstorm began" if it is present now, or "thunderstorm ended" if it is absent now.
  4. If the presence of "a group whose descriptor is FZ and whose phenomena include DZ or RA" in the main-part weather differs, "freezing precipitation began" if it is present now, or "freezing precipitation ended" if it is absent now.
  If there is no previous report, the result is "no previous report"; if there is no reason at all, it is "no criteria met".

* Hourly expansion of a TAF
For each hour mark h = vs, vs + 60, ..., ve - 60 of the validity period [vs, ve) of a TAF, the prevailing state and the temporary states are determined.
- Prevailing state: start from the four elements of the base forecast and examine the FM and BECMG change groups in the order in which they appear in the body. An FM with t <= h replaces all four elements with the elements of that change group (an element that is none in that change group becomes none); a BECMG whose period end <= h replaces only the elements that are not none in that change group.
- Temporary states: the temporary change groups with period start <= h < end, listed in the order in which they appear in the body. For each of them, the temporary state is the prevailing state of that hour mark with the elements that are not none in that change group replaced.
Even when a weather sequence contains an NSW group, NSW is not output as a weather group in the TOKENS of taf_timeline.txt (replacing the weather with a weather that contains NSW amounts to clearing the weather). In the SUMMARY of decoded.txt, NSW is written too, as no significant weather.

* Verification
For each airport listed in VERIFY, the non-rejected METARs and SPECIs of that airport (below, the "observations", in report order) are compared with the non-rejected TAFs of the same airport.
- Let t be the observation time and H its hour mark. A TAF that satisfies issue time <= t and vs <= H < ve is called "a TAF covering t".
- For each observation, among the TAFs covering t, choose the one with the latest issue time (on a tie, the later one in report order), and call the flight category of the prevailing state of that TAF at hour mark H the "forecast category". If no TAF covers t, the observation has "no forecast".
- For the observations that do not have no forecast, build a 4x4 contingency table of the forecast category (row) against the flight category of the observation (column). The order of the categories is VFR, MVFR, IFR, LIFR. Let n be the total of the table and k the sum of its diagonal entries.
- The hit rate is k/n.
- The hit rate with temporary groups is the number of observations whose flight category is equal to the forecast category or to the flight category of any of the temporary states of the chosen TAF at hour mark H, divided by n.
- The Heidke skill score is (n * k - E) / (n * n - E), where E is the sum over the categories of "row total * column total".
- For each category, the probability of detection (pod) is "the diagonal entry of that category / the column total of that category", and the false alarm ratio (far) is "(the row total of that category - the diagonal entry) / the row total of that category".
- Hit rate by forecast hour: among all pairs of a non-rejected TAF and an observation of that airport, count those in which the TAF covers the observation time t. With j = (H - vs) / 60, the pair is put into bucket number floor(j / 6) (0 to 4), and a pair is a hit if the flight category of the prevailing state of that TAF at hour mark H is equal to the flight category of the observation. The hit rate of each bucket is "the number of hits / the number of pairs".
- Amendment indicators: for each observation that does not have no forecast, the following reasons are checked. There is a "category reason" if the flight category of the observation is equal neither to the forecast category nor to any of the flight categories of the temporary states of the chosen TAF at hour mark H. There is a "wind reason" if the "wind change test" with alpha = 60, with the wind of the prevailing state of the chosen TAF at hour mark H as the earlier wind and the main-part wind of the observation as the later wind, lists anything.
Every fraction is written in lowest terms, and undefined is written when the denominator is 0.

[Output files]
Every line of every file ends with LF and has no trailing spaces. In a file made of "blocks", one empty line is placed between consecutive blocks (none before the first block or after the last block). A file with no block or line to write is empty (0 bytes). Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, with "-" for negative numbers (except where a number of digits is specified, such as 3-digit directions).

* Building phrases
- Wind phrase W: calm if calm, "variable at <speed> KT" if variable, otherwise "<direction> degrees at <speed> KT" (the direction in 3 digits, e.g. 040 degrees at 15 KT). If there is a gust, " gusting <gust> KT" is appended.
- Visibility phrase V: "10 km or more (CAVOK)" for CAVOK, "10 km or more" for 9999, and "<value> m" for other values in meters (e.g. 800 m). For the mile format, "less than " is prefixed for the qualifier M and "more than " for P, and the number is written as "<whole part> <numerator>/<denominator>" (e.g. 1 1/2); however, it is "<numerator>/<denominator>" if the whole part is 0, and only "<whole part>" if there is no fraction part. Finally " SM" is appended.
- Weather phrase X: of the three parts "the intensity word", "the descriptor word" and "the phenomenon words joined with ' and '", those that exist, in this order, separated by single spaces (e.g. -SHRASN is light showers rain and snow, VCSH is nearby showers). NSW is no significant weather.
- Cloud phrase C: FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC give "<amount word> at <height> ft", followed by " cumulonimbus" or " towering cumulus" if CB or TCU is present (e.g. broken at 1200 ft cumulonimbus). VV gives "vertical visibility <height> ft", and SKC, CLR, NSC give sky clear, clear below 12000 ft and no significant cloud, respectively.
- State summary SUMMARY: "wind W" if the wind is not none, "visibility V" if the visibility is not none, "weather " followed by the X of each group joined with ", " if the weather sequence is neither none nor empty, and "cloud " followed by the C of each group joined with ", " if the cloud sequence is neither none nor empty, joined in this order with "; ". If there is none of them, it is no conditions.
- Wind code: 00000KT if calm, otherwise the concatenation of "the direction in 3 digits (VRB if variable)", "the speed", "G and the gust" (if there is a gust) and "KT". The speed and the gust are padded with a leading 0 if they have fewer than 2 digits (e.g. VRB03KT, 02016G26KT).
- State token list TOKENS: the wind code (if the wind is not none), the visibility code (if the visibility is not none), each weather group (excluding NSW, as written) and each cloud group, in this order, separated by single spaces, and finally the flight category is appended. The visibility code is CAVOK for CAVOK; for meters, a 4-digit number (padded with leading 0s if it has fewer than 4 digits; 9999 is 9999); for the mile format, the concatenation without spaces of "the qualifier", "the number written in the same way as in V" and "SM" (e.g. M1/4SM, 1 1/2SM, P6SM). Each cloud group is the concatenation of "the amount (or VV), the 3-digit height number, CB or TCU (if present)", or SKC, CLR, NSC.
- Value in units of 0.1 (sea level pressure, precise temperature and dew point): write "-" if negative, the quotient of the absolute value divided by 10, ".", and the remainder of the absolute value divided by 10 (1 digit), in this order (e.g. 1021.5, -1.9, 0.0).
- inHg value: a value a in hundredths is written as "floor(a / 100)", ".", "a mod 100 in 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit)" in this order (e.g. 30.18).
- Component phrase COMP: "headwind <headwind component> KT" ("tailwind <its absolute value> KT" if the headwind component is negative), followed by ", crosswind <crosswind component> KT from right" if the crosswind component is positive, ", crosswind <its absolute value> KT from left" if it is negative, or ", crosswind 0 KT" if it is 0.

* output/decoded.txt
One block per non-rejected report, in report order. The first line of a block is "REPORT <report number>: <kind> <station code> (<airport name>)" (the kind is METAR, SPECI or TAF), and from the second line on, the following lines are written in the following order, each prefixed with two spaces. A line whose condition in parentheses does not hold is not written.
Blocks of METAR and SPECI:
- time: STAMP of the observation time
- modifier: automated or corrected (if a modifier was accepted in the main part)
- wind: W. If a wind variation was accepted, ", varying between <direction 1> and <direction 2> degrees" is appended (directions in 3 digits). If the wind is none: wind: not reported
- visibility: V. If the visibility is none: visibility: not reported
- runway visual range: one line per accepted RVR. After "runway <runway name> ", write the reading if there is one reading, or "between <reading 1> and <reading 2>" if there are two, then " ft" or " m", then, if there is a trend, one of ", rising", ", falling", ", no change". A reading is the number prefixed with "less than " if the qualifier is M, or with "more than " if it is P (the number is written as an integer). Example: runway visual range: runway 27L between 450 and 700 m, rising
- weather: one line per weather group of the main part, X
- cloud: one line no significant cloud if the visibility of the main part is CAVOK, one line not reported if the cloud is none, otherwise one line per cloud group, C
- temperature: "<temperature> C, dew point <dew point> C, spread <temperature minus dew point> C". If the temperature is none: temperature: not reported (e.g. temperature: 8 C, dew point -1 C, spread 9 C)
- altimeter: "<inHg value> inHg (<hPa value> hPa)" if the pressure is A, "<hPa value> hPa (<inHg value> inHg)" if it is Q (e.g. altimeter: 1022 hPa (30.18 inHg)). If the pressure is none: altimeter: not reported
- trend: no significant change (if a no-trend group was accepted in the main part)
- trend: one line per trend section that was not discarded. becoming or temporarily, then for each time group one of " from HH:MMZ", " until HH:MMZ", " at HH:MMZ" (in group order), then ": " and the SUMMARY of that section (e.g. trend: becoming from 21:00Z: visibility 6000 m; weather light rain).
- sea level pressure: <0.1-unit value of the sea level pressure> hPa (for each SLP group of the remarks)
- precise temperature: <0.1-unit value of the temperature> C, dew point <0.1-unit value of the dew point> C (for each T group of the remarks)
- ceiling: <ceiling> ft. If there is no ceiling: ceiling: none
- flight category: <flight category>
- pressure altitude: <pressure altitude> ft. If it is not computed: pressure altitude: not available
- density altitude: <density altitude> ft. If it is not computed: density altitude: not available
- runway <name>: COMP (if the wind components are computed; one line per runway, in list order). If there is a gust, "; gusts " and the COMP of the gust components are appended.
- usable runways: the names of the usable runways in list order, separated by single spaces; none if there is none (if the wind components are computed)
- preferred runway: the name of the preferred runway; none if there is none (if the wind components are computed)
- runway wind: not computed (if the wind components are not computed; written instead of the three kinds of lines above)
- speci check: the reasons of the SPECI check joined with ", " (for a SPECI)
Blocks of TAF:
- issued: STAMP of the issue time
- modifier: amended or corrected (if there is a modifier)
- valid: <STAMP of vs> to <STAMP of ve>
- base: SUMMARY of the base forecast
- One line per change group that was not discarded (in body order): "FM <STAMP of t>: SUMMARY" for FM, otherwise "<kind> <STAMP of the start> to <STAMP of the end>: SUMMARY"

* output/categories.csv
The first line is station,time,ceiling,visibility,category. Then one line per non-rejected METAR and SPECI follows, in report order. Each line consists of "station code", "STAMP of the observation time", "ceiling (NONE if there is none)", "visibility" and "flight category", separated by commas. The visibility is the qualifier (M or P, if any) followed by an integer in meters: for meters it is the value (P10000 for 9999 and CAVOK), and for the mile format it is floor(1609344 * numerator / (1000 * denominator)) (computed with the improper fraction whose numerator includes the whole part; if there is no fraction part, the denominator is 1). If the visibility is none, it is NONE.
Example: RJTT,2027-12-31T21:00Z,2000,5000,MVFR

* output/taf_timeline.txt
One block per non-rejected TAF, in report order. The first line is "TAF <station code> issued <STAMP of the issue time> valid <STAMP of vs> to <STAMP of ve>". Then, for each hour mark h, one line consisting of "<STAMP of h>", " " and "<TOKENS of the prevailing state>", followed by " | <kind> <TOKENS of the temporary state>" for each temporary state. The last line is "summary: VFR a, MVFR b, IFR c, LIFR d", where a to d are the numbers of hour marks whose prevailing state has that flight category.

* output/verification.txt
One block per airport, in the order listed in VERIFY. A block consists of the following lines in this order (with no leading spaces).
- station <station code> (<airport name>)
- reports: METAR <count>, SPECI <count> (the number of observations of each kind)
- observed categories: VFR a, MVFR b, IFR c, LIFR d (the number of observations of each flight category)
- lowest temperature: <temperature> C at STAMP
- highest temperature: <temperature> C at STAMP
- strongest wind: W at STAMP
- lowest ceiling: <ceiling> ft at STAMP
- lowest visibility: V at STAMP
- verified: n
- no forecast: <the number of observations with no forecast>
- The 6 lines of the contingency table
- hit rate: <hit rate>
- hit rate with temporary groups: <hit rate with temporary groups>
- heidke skill score: <Heidke skill score>
- VFR: pod <probability of detection>, far <false alarm ratio> (likewise for MVFR, IFR, LIFR, one line each, in this order)
- hours 0-5: <hit rate of bucket 0> (likewise hours 6-11, hours 12-17, hours 18-23, hours 24-29, one line each, with the hit rates of buckets 1 to 4)
- amendment <STAMP of the observation time>: <reasons> (one line per observation that has amendment-indicator reasons, in observation order)
For the extreme-value lines (lowest temperature to lowest visibility), among the observations for which that element is not none (for the ceiling, the observations that have a ceiling), the observation with the minimum (or maximum) temperature, the maximum wind strength (the gust if there is a gust, otherwise the speed; calm is 0), the minimum ceiling, or the minimum visibility (compared as fractions in miles) is chosen. On a tie, the observation that comes first in report order is chosen. The wind is written as W (without the wind variation), the visibility is written as V, and STAMP is the observation time of that observation. If there is no eligible observation, not available is written without the part from "at" on, as in "lowest temperature: not available".
The 6 lines of the contingency table: line 1 is a header consisting of "forecast" left-aligned in a width of 8 characters (padded with trailing spaces), followed by VFR, MVFR, IFR, LIFR and total, each right-aligned in a width of 6 characters (padded with leading spaces). Lines 2 to 5 have the category name left-aligned in a width of 8 characters, followed by the 4 counts of the row whose forecast category is that category and the row total, right-aligned in a width of 6 characters. Line 6 has "total" left-aligned in a width of 8 characters, followed by the 4 column totals and n, right-aligned in a width of 6 characters.
The reasons of the amendment indicators are "category <flight category of the observation>, forecast <forecast category>" for the category reason and "wind <wind code of the observation>, forecast <wind code of the forecast prevailing state>" for the wind reason; if both exist, they are joined with "; " in the order category, wind.

* output/errors.txt
The recorded errors, one per line, in increasing order of report number and, within the same report, in increasing order of position (word number). Each line is "report <report number> word <position>: <content>", where the content is one of the following. WORD is the string of the group or word concerned, enclosed in ASCII double quotes (").
- rejected report, invalid type "WORD"
- rejected report, invalid station "WORD" (likewise for time and validity)
- rejected report, missing station (likewise for time and validity)
- unknown group "WORD"
- out of order group "WORD"
- invalid group "WORD"
- missing period "WORD" (WORD is the change indicator; for a two-word indicator such as PROB30 TEMPO, the two words are joined with a single space)
- invalid period "WORD"

[Source files]
There are the following 6 source files (with the extension appropriate to the language).
- main: holds the overall flow: reading the input files and splitting them into reports, parsing headers and rejecting reports, group joining, the slot-matching procedure and its application to the METAR main part, the trend sections and each TAF section, computing the derived values (ceiling, flight category, pressure altitude, density altitude, runway wind components and usable and preferred runways, the wind change test, the SPECI check), building all phrases, token lists and output lines, assembling the verification (extreme values, output of the contingency table, each metric, hit rate by forecast hour, amendment indicators), and writing the five output files. Most of the processing is placed in this file.
- groups: the tables of descriptors, phenomena, cloud amounts and so on, the format test and the value-condition check for each kind of group (including the section-dependent conditions for weather and cloud), and recording accepted groups.
- units: the integer sine table and sin, cos, the wind speed and pressure conversions, the calendar (days in a month, the following month, computing times with the month-of-a-day rule) and building STAMPs, converting visibility to a mile fraction and to a meter value, and fractions in lowest terms.
- metar: the definition of the METAR main-part slots and the test for each slot, splitting the body into the main part, the trend part and the remarks, reading the time groups of trend sections, and reading the SLP and T groups of the remarks.
- taf: the definition of the TAF slots, splitting into the base forecast and change groups by reading change indicators and checking periods, and the hourly expansion (prevailing state and temporary states).
- verification: selecting the TAF covering an observation, the contingency table, the numerators and denominators of the hit counts and the skill score, and the numerators and denominators of pod and far.

[Constraints]
- stations.txt has 1 to 20 lines and reports.txt has 1 to 2000 lines; there are at most 1000 reports, a report has at most 200 words, and each line has at most 200 characters.
- The report times (observation times of METAR and SPECI, issue times of TAF), when correctly written, lie within the base month and appear in time order in the file (the same time may occur consecutively).
- Words are non-empty and consist only of printable ASCII characters other than space. The words and groups of the reports are not guaranteed to be correct.
- The absolute value of every intermediate integer is less than 2^53. However, cross-multiplying fractions in the comparison for the lowest visibility reaches about 1.7 * 10^13, so 32-bit integers are not enough.
