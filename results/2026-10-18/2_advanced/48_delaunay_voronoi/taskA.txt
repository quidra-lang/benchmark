[Overview]
Write a program that, for a set of points with integer coordinates in the plane (called sites below), computes the Delaunay triangulation and the Voronoi diagram and analyzes various structures derived from them.
The Delaunay triangulation is built by incremental insertion, adding the sites one at a time in input order. If a new site lies inside an existing triangle, that triangle is split into three; if it lies outside the convex hull, the site is joined to the hull edges visible from it. After that, Lawson edge flips (the operation that replaces an edge that violates the circumcircle condition by the other diagonal of its quadrilateral) are repeated. The decisions use the orientation predicate orient and the circumcircle predicate incircle, which can be computed exactly using integers only. Since no three sites are collinear and no four sites are cocircular, the Delaunay triangulation is uniquely determined.
For the resulting triangulation, the program computes the triangles, the edges, the convex hull, the convex layers and statistics of the triangle angles. For the Voronoi diagram, it computes the Voronoi vertices (the circumcenters of the triangles, with exact fractional coordinates), the Voronoi edges (segments or rays), the Voronoi cell of each site with its area, and the area clipped to a square. In addition, it computes the nearest-neighbor graph, the minimum spanning tree (Kruskal's algorithm), the Gabriel graph, the relative neighborhood graph, the largest empty circle inside the convex hull, the diameter, the width, the minimum enclosing circle, shortest paths along the edges of the Delaunay triangulation and stretch factors, and statistics of alpha complexes, and it answers queries about point location and other things. Finally, it makes character drawings of the triangulation and of the Voronoi diagram.
The program reads points.txt (the site coordinates) and params.txt (settings and queries) in the input/ folder, and writes four files to the output/ folder: triangulation.txt (the triangulation), voronoi.txt (the Voronoi diagram), derived.txt (the derived structures and the answers to the queries) and plot.txt (the plots). Nothing is written to standard output. No random numbers are used.

[Input files]
There are two input files: input/points.txt and input/params.txt. Both consist of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There is no whitespace at the start or end of a line, and there are no tab characters. The words in a line are separated by a single space. In both files, blank lines (lines of 0 characters) and comment lines (lines whose first character is "#") are skipped.
Below, an "integer word" is a sequence of one or more digits (0-9), optionally preceded by a single "-" (examples: 17, -4, 007). Its value is the value read as a base-10 number.

* input/points.txt (site coordinates)
Line numbers are counted 1, 2, ... from the start of the file, including blank lines and comment lines. A line that is neither blank nor a comment line is called a data line. A data line represents one point in the form "x y". The data lines are examined one at a time from the top, each against the following checks (1) to (3) in this order, and a line is discarded as an error line of the first kind that applies.
(1) If the number of words is not exactly 2, or either of the two words is not an integer word, it is format (examples: "412 x7", "305", "12 44 9", "3.5 700", "+15 200").
(2) If either of the values x, y of the two words is not between 0 and 1000 inclusive, it is range.
(3) If the coordinates (x, y) are equal to the coordinates of a point accepted earlier, it is duplicate. In this case, the site number k of the point with the same coordinates is also recorded.
A data line to which none of these applies is accepted, and its point becomes a site. The sites are numbered 1, 2, ... (site numbers) in the order of acceptance, and the coordinates of site s are written P_s = (x_s, y_s). Let N be the number of sites.

* input/params.txt (settings and queries)
Each line that is neither blank nor a comment line consists of a first word (the name) followed by zero or more values, and is one of the following. Each name other than query appears at most once (the order of the lines is not fixed); for a name that does not appear, the default value given in parentheses is used.
- plot W H (61 31): the width W and the height H (in characters) of the plots. W is an integer from 10 to 120, and H is an integer from 10 to 60.
- plot_layers name1 name2 ... (edges hull sites): the names of the layers to draw in the triangulation plot, in drawing order (Part 12). Each name is one of edges, mst, hull, sites and circle, and no name appears twice or more. There may be zero names (a line consisting of plot_layers only).
- sections name1 name2 ... (summary): the names of the sections to write, in writing order. Each name is one of the 24 names listed in [Output files], and no name appears twice or more. There may be zero names.
- alpha a_1 a_2 ... (no values): the sequence of values alpha for which alpha complexes (Part 10) are computed. Each value is an integer from 1 to 10000000, and there are at most 10 values.
- query: a query (Part 11). Any number of query lines may appear, and they are answered in the order of appearance. There are the following 7 forms. x, y, i and j are all integer words without extra zeros (there are no words such as 007 or -0).
  query locate x y
  query walk x y
  query natural x y
  query route i j
  query path i j
  query neighbors i
  query edge i j
  The x, y of locate, walk and natural are between 0 and 1000 inclusive. The i, j of route, path, neighbors and edge denote site numbers, but they are not necessarily between 1 and N inclusive.

[Processing]
* Notation
- For points p = (p_x, p_y), q and r, let orient(p, q, r) = (q_x - p_x) * (r_y - p_y) - (q_y - p_y) * (r_x - p_x). When orient(p, q, r) > 0, r lies to the left of the line directed from p to q, and the three points p, q, r are said to be in counterclockwise order in this order.
- Let d2(p, q) = (p_x - q_x)^2 + (p_y - q_y)^2 (the squared distance), and let dot(o; p, q) = (p_x - o_x) * (q_x - o_x) + (p_y - o_y) * (q_y - o_y).
- For three points a, b, c in counterclockwise order and a point d, with a' = a - d, b' = b - d, c' = c - d (componentwise differences) and |v|^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2 for a point v, let
  incircle(a, b, c, d) = a'_x * (b'_y * |c'|^2 - |b'|^2 * c'_y) - a'_y * (b'_x * |c'|^2 - |b'|^2 * c'_x) + |a'|^2 * (b'_x * c'_y - b'_y * c'_x)
  This value is positive if d lies inside the circle through a, b, c (the circumcircle), 0 if it lies on the circle, and negative if it lies outside.
- The values above, and the values defined as integers below, are all computed exactly with integers.
- Real numbers are computed in double-precision floating point. When an integer is used in a real computation, it is converted to the real number with the same value. sqrt is the square root, atan2(y, x) is the two-argument arctangent (in radians), and pi is the circle constant. "Add in order" means starting from 0.0 and adding the values one at a time in the specified order.
- Fraction notation: a value n / d given by an integer n and a positive integer d is reduced to lowest terms and written as "n/d" (examples: -7/2, 1047/2). If the reduced denominator is 1, only "n" is written (examples: 12, -3).
- Real number notation: a real number is rounded to exactly 3 decimal places (examples: 12.300, 0.000).
- Edge order: the order of the pairs (i, j) of site numbers with i < j in increasing order of i, and for equal i in increasing order of j.
- floor(a / b) (a is a nonnegative integer, b is a positive integer) is the integer obtained by truncating the fractional part of a / b.
- The point sequence joining two points (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) (called the Bresenham point sequence below) is the sequence of points obtained by the following procedure. Let dx = |x1 - x0| and dy = -|y1 - y0|; let sx be 1 if x0 < x1 and -1 otherwise, and sy be 1 if y0 < y1 and -1 otherwise. Starting from e = dx + dy and (x, y) = (x0, y0), repeat the following (i) to (v).
  (i) Append the point (x, y) to the end of the sequence.
  (ii) If x = x1 and y = y1, stop.
  (iii) Let e2 = 2 * e.
  (iv) If e2 >= dy, add dy to e and add sx to x.
  (v) If e2 <= dx, add dx to e and add sy to y (the e2 here is the value computed in (iii)).

* Part 1. Overall flow
(1) Read the two input files ([Input files]).
(2) Build the Delaunay triangulation (Part 3), and number and order the triangles, the edges and the convex hull (Part 4).
(3) Compute the triangle properties (Part 5), the Voronoi diagram (Part 6), the proximity graphs (Part 7), the empty circle, the extent and the convex layers (Part 8), the shortest paths and stretch factors (Part 9) and the alpha complexes (Part 10), answer the queries (Part 11), and draw the plots (Part 12).
(4) Write the sections listed in sections to the four output files ([Output files]).

* Part 2. Representation of the triangulation
A triangulation is a set of triangles, and each triangle is represented by three sites (a, b, c) in counterclockwise order (writing the same triangle as (b, c, a) or (c, a, b) means the same). The triangle (a, b, c) is said to have the directed edges a->b, b->c and c->a. For every directed edge of a triangulation, at most one triangle has it. When some triangle has the directed edge u->v and no triangle has the directed edge v->u, u->v is called a hull edge (the interior of the convex hull lies to the left of the hull edges).

* Part 3. Delaunay triangulation (incremental insertion)
(1) The initial triangulation is the single triangle consisting of sites 1, 2 and 3. It is the triangle (1, 2, 3) if orient(P_1, P_2, P_3) > 0, and the triangle (1, 3, 2) otherwise.
(2) For s = 4, 5, ..., N in this order, insert site s as in the following (a) to (d).
  (a) If P_s lies inside some triangle (a, b, c) (orient(P_a, P_b, P_s), orient(P_b, P_c, P_s) and orient(P_c, P_a, P_s) are all positive), remove that triangle and add the triangles (a, b, s), (b, c, s) and (c, a, s). The kind of this insertion is inside, the removed triangle is called the "containing triangle", and m_0 = 3.
  (b) Otherwise, P_s lies outside the convex hull (since no three sites are collinear, P_s never lies on an edge). For every hull edge u->v with orient(P_u, P_v, P_s) < 0 (an edge visible from s), add the triangle (v, u, s). Let e be the number of visible edges; the kind of this insertion is outside, and m_0 = e + 1.
  (c) For each triangle added in (a) or (b), put the edge opposite s (the edge that does not have s as an endpoint) into the set of edges to check. Until the set becomes empty, take one edge out of it and do the following. Let the edge taken out be the directed edge a->b of the triangle (a, b, s) containing s. If there is a triangle (b, a, q) having the directed edge b->a and incircle(P_a, P_b, P_s, P_q) > 0, remove the two triangles (a, b, s) and (b, a, q), add the triangles (a, q, s) and (q, b, s) (this is called a flip), and put the edges a->q and q->b into the set.
  (d) Let f be the number of flips performed in this insertion. f equals the degree of s after the insertion is finished (the number of sites joined to s by an edge) minus m_0, and it is determined independently of the order in which edges are taken out of the set.
(3) The triangulation after the insertion of site s is finished is the Delaunay triangulation of sites 1..s (the triangulation in which no site among 1..s lies inside the circumcircle of any triangle). By the conditions on the sites, it is uniquely determined. Therefore the containing triangle, e, f, and the number of triangles and the number of hull vertices after the insertion do not depend on implementation details (such as how triangles are searched for or the order in which edges are taken out). The triangulation after the insertion of site N is finished is simply called "the triangulation" below.

* Part 4. Numbering and orders
(1) Canonical form and numbers of the triangles: the canonical form of a triangle is the triangle written, keeping the counterclockwise order, so that the smallest site number comes first (for example, (5, 2, 9) becomes (2, 9, 5)). The triangles of the triangulation are sorted in lexicographic order of their canonical forms (comparing the first number, then the second, then the third), numbered 1, 2, ..., T, and called triangle k. The containing triangle of Part 3 (2) (a) is also represented in canonical form.
(2) Edges: a pair of two sites that form an edge of some triangle of the triangulation is written (i, j) (i < j) and simply called an edge. Edges are handled sorted in edge order. Let E be the number of edges. For an edge (i, j), the triangle having the directed edge i->j is called the left triangle, and the triangle having the directed edge j->i is called the right triangle. If both exist, the edge is an inner edge (inner); if only one exists, it is a hull edge (hull).
(3) Adjacent triangles: for a triangle with canonical form (a, b, c), the adjacent triangles across the edges (b, c), (c, a) and (a, b) are the triangles having the directed edges c->b, a->c and b->a, respectively (they may not exist).
(4) Convex hull: following the hull edges u->v one after another goes around the hull vertices (the sites that are endpoints of hull edges) exactly once. Let h_1 be the hull vertex with the smallest number, and let h_(t+1) be the end point of the hull edge leaving h_t; h_1, h_2, ..., h_H is the hull order. H is the number of hull vertices, and h_(H+1) is read as h_1. For a hull vertex u, next(u) is the end point of the hull edge leaving u, and prev(u) is the start point of the hull edge entering u.
(5) Neighbors and degree: the sites joined to site s by an edge are called the neighbors of s, and their number is called the degree of s. The counterclockwise order n_0, n_1, ..., n_(m-1) of the neighbors of s (m is the degree of s) is defined as follows. If s is not a hull vertex, n_0 is the neighbor with the smallest number; if it is a hull vertex, n_0 = next(s). n_(k+1) is the vertex other than s and n_k of the triangle having the directed edge s->n_k. If s is not a hull vertex, following this returns to n_0 at n_m. If s is a hull vertex, there is no triangle having the directed edge s->n_(m-1), and n_(m-1) = prev(s).

* Part 5. Triangle properties
For triangle k (canonical form (a, b, c)), compute the following.
- Twice the area: A_k = orient(P_a, P_b, P_c) (a positive integer).
- Kind: among the three values dot(P_a; P_b, P_c), dot(P_b; P_c, P_a) and dot(P_c; P_a, P_b), if there is a 0 it is right (right triangle); otherwise, if there is a negative value it is obtuse (obtuse triangle); if neither, it is acute (acute triangle).
- Angles: the angle at vertex v (let u and w be the two vertices other than v) is theta = atan2(|orient(P_v, P_u, P_w)|, dot(P_v; P_u, P_w)) * 180 / pi (in degrees). The angle at the vertex opposite the shortest edge, compared by d2, is the minimum angle, and the angle at the vertex opposite the longest edge is the maximum angle (when there are two shortest or two longest edges, the angles opposite either of them have the same value). Since the coordinates are integers, there are no equilateral triangles, and the minimum angle is less than 60 degrees.

* Part 6. Voronoi diagram
(1) Voronoi vertices: the circumcenter of triangle k (canonical form (a, b, c)) is Voronoi vertex k. With b' = P_b - P_a and c' = P_c - P_a, compute as integers
  D = 2 * (b'_x * c'_y - b'_y * c'_x),
  X = x_a * D + c'_y * |b'|^2 - b'_y * |c'|^2,
  Y = y_a * D + b'_x * |c'|^2 - c'_x * |b'|^2
  Then D > 0, and the coordinates of Voronoi vertex k are (X / D, Y / D).
(2) Circumradius: with L_1 = d2(P_a, P_b), L_2 = d2(P_b, P_c) and L_3 = d2(P_c, P_a), the squared radius is computed as a real number R2_k = ((L_1 * L_2) * L_3) / ((4 * A_k) * A_k), and the radius is sqrt(R2_k).
(3) Position relative to the convex hull: for each edge h_t->h_(t+1) (t = 1..H) in hull order, compute as an integer
  sigma_t = (x_(h_(t+1)) - x_(h_t)) * (Y - y_(h_t) * D) - (y_(h_(t+1)) - y_(h_t)) * (X - x_(h_t) * D)
  (with V the Voronoi vertex, this is D times orient(P_(h_t), P_(h_(t+1)), V)). If all sigma_t are positive, the position is inside (inside the convex hull); if any of them is negative, it is outside (outside the convex hull); otherwise it is on (on a hull edge).
(4) Outward normal: for a hull edge u->v, with (d_x, d_y) = P_v - P_u and g the greatest common divisor of |d_x| and |d_y|, the integer pair (d_y / g, -d_x / g) is written out(u, v) (it is perpendicular to the edge and points out of the convex hull).
(5) Voronoi edges: for each edge (i, j) there is one corresponding Voronoi edge. For an inner edge, it is the segment (segment) joining the Voronoi vertex of the left triangle and the Voronoi vertex of the right triangle. For a hull edge, it is the ray (ray) leaving the Voronoi vertex of the only triangle having that edge, and its direction is out(i, j) if i->j is a hull edge, and out(j, i) if j->i is a hull edge.
(6) Voronoi cells: let n_0, ..., n_(m-1) be the counterclockwise order of the neighbors of site s, and let t_k be the number of the triangle having the directed edge s->n_k (m values k = 0..m-1 if s is not a hull vertex, and m - 1 values k = 0..m-2 if it is a hull vertex). The vertex sequence of the Voronoi cell of site s (the set of points whose distance to s is less than or equal to their distance to any other site) is the sequence of Voronoi vertices t_0, t_1, ..., in this order.
  - If s is not a hull vertex, the cell is bounded (bounded), and the vertex sequence is the vertices of a convex polygon in counterclockwise order in this order.
  - If s is a hull vertex, the cell is not bounded (unbounded). Traversing the boundary of the cell counterclockwise, one comes from infinity, traveling backward along the ray that leaves Voronoi vertex t_0 in direction out(s, next(s)), arrives at t_0, proceeds in the order of the vertex sequence, and goes away to infinity along the ray that leaves the last Voronoi vertex in direction out(prev(s), s). The former direction is called the "incoming direction" and the latter the "outgoing direction".
(7) Area of a bounded cell: with (X_k / D_k, Y_k / D_k) the coordinates of the k-th (k = 0..m-1) Voronoi vertex of the vertex sequence, compute the real numbers u_k = X_k / D_k - x_s and w_k = Y_k / D_k - y_s (X_k / D_k is a real division; dividing the values before or after reduction gives the same result). Add u_k * w_(k+1) - u_(k+1) * w_k (index m is read as 0) in order for k = 0, 1, ..., m-1, and the sum divided by 2 is the area.
(8) Clipped area: the area of the intersection of the Voronoi cell of site s and the square 0 <= x <= 1000, 0 <= y <= 1000 is computed as a real number by the following procedure (the Sutherland-Hodgman method).
  - The initial polygon is the sequence of the four points (0, 0), (1000, 0), (1000, 1000), (0, 1000) in this order.
  - For each neighbor t, in the counterclockwise order n_0, n_1, ..., n_(m-1) of the neighbors, do the following. Compute the integers c_1 = 2 * (x_t - x_s), c_2 = 2 * (y_t - y_s) and c_3 = x_t^2 + y_t^2 - x_s^2 - y_s^2, and for a point p let f(p) = c_1 * p_x + c_2 * p_y - c_3 (a real number, computed from left to right) (f(p) <= 0 means that p is closer to s than to t, or equally distant). Let p_1, ..., p_r be the point sequence of the current polygon, prepare an empty new sequence, and for i = 1..r in order, with p = p_i and q = p_(i+1) (p_(r+1) is read as p_1), do the following. If f(p) <= 0, append p to the end of the new sequence. Then, if f(p) < 0 < f(q) or f(q) < 0 < f(p), with lambda = f(p) / (f(p) - f(q)), append the point (p_x + (q_x - p_x) * lambda, p_y + (q_y - p_y) * lambda) to the end of the new sequence. When finished, the new sequence becomes the current polygon.
  - For the point sequence p_1, ..., p_r of the final polygon, add (p_i)_x * (p_(i+1))_y - (p_(i+1))_x * (p_i)_y in order for i = 1..r, and the sum divided by 2 is the clipped area.
(9) Totals: compute the total of the areas of the bounded cells added in order of site number, the total of the clipped areas added in order of site number (the exact value is 1000000), and the bounded cell with the largest area (for equal areas, the one with the smaller site number).

* Part 7. Proximity graphs
(1) Nearest neighbor: the nearest neighbor nn(s) of site s is the site t other than s with the smallest d2(P_s, P_t) (for equal values, the one with the smaller site number). nn(s) is always a neighbor of s, so it may be chosen among the neighbors. When nn(nn(s)) = s, s and nn(s) are a mutual nearest-neighbor pair.
(2) Closest pair: the pair (i, j) with i < j that has the smallest d2(P_i, P_j) (for equal values, the pair that comes first in edge order). It is always an edge.
(3) Components of the nearest-neighbor graph: the connected components of the (undirected) graph obtained by joining each site s and nn(s) with a line. Each component contains exactly one mutual nearest-neighbor pair. The components are sorted in increasing order of the smallest site number they contain and numbered 1, 2, ... in this order.
(4) Number of nearest-neighbor edges: the number of distinct (undirected) pairs of the form {s, nn(s)}; it equals N minus the number of mutual nearest-neighbor pairs.
(5) Minimum spanning tree: examine the edges (i, j) in increasing order of (d2(P_i, P_j), i, j), and adopt an edge if i and j are not connected by the edges adopted so far (Kruskal's algorithm). The N - 1 adopted edges, in the order of adoption, form the edge sequence of the minimum spanning tree. The edge lengths sqrt(d2(P_i, P_j)) added in order of adoption give the total length.
(6) Gabriel edges: an edge (i, j) is a Gabriel edge when dot(P_q; P_i, P_j) > 0 holds for every vertex q other than i and j of each triangle having that edge (whichever of the left triangle and the right triangle exist) (this is equivalent to no other site lying on or inside the circle with P_i and P_j as the endpoints of a diameter).
(7) Relative neighborhood edges: an edge (i, j) is a relative neighborhood edge when there is no site k with d2(P_i, P_k) < d2(P_i, P_j) and d2(P_j, P_k) < d2(P_i, P_j) (k = i and k = j do not satisfy this condition, so all sites may be examined).

* Part 8. Empty circle, extent, convex layers
(1) Largest empty circle: the Voronoi vertices whose position relative to the convex hull is inside are the candidates. Among the candidates, select the one with the largest R2_k (for equal values, the one with the smaller number). No site lies inside the circle centered at that Voronoi vertex with radius sqrt(R2_k).
(2) Diameter: the pair (i, j) with i < j that has the largest d2(P_i, P_j) (for equal values, the pair that comes first in edge order).
(3) Width: for each edge h_t->h_(t+1) (t = 1..H) in hull order, let M_t (an integer) be the maximum of orient(P_(h_t), P_(h_(t+1)), P_q) over all hull vertices q, and let omega_t = M_t / sqrt(d2(P_(h_t), P_(h_(t+1)))). For the t with the smallest omega_t (for equal values, the smaller t), omega_t is the width.
(4) Minimum enclosing circle: the circle with the smallest radius among the circles that contain all sites on or inside them. This circle is uniquely determined, and there are 2 or 3 sites on it. Whether site p lies on or inside the circle with sites a and b as the endpoints of a diameter can be decided exactly by dot(P_p; P_a, P_b) <= 0 (= 0 if on the circle), and whether it lies on or inside the circle through sites a, b, c in counterclockwise order by incircle(P_a, P_b, P_c, P_p) >= 0 (= 0 if on the circle). Any method may be used, but for example it can be found by the following procedure (Welzl's method). Let q_1, ..., q_H be the vertices in hull order, and let circle C be "none". For i = 1..H: if C is none or does not contain q_i, set C to the circle consisting of q_i only (radius 0), and for j = 1..i-1: if C does not contain q_j, set C to the circle with q_i and q_j as the endpoints of a diameter, and for k = 1..j-1: if C does not contain q_k, set C to the circle through q_i, q_j and q_k.
  The center and the radius are computed as follows. When some two sites a and b on the circle are the endpoints of a diameter (including the case where there are 3 sites on the circle and two of them are the endpoints of a diameter), the center is ((x_a + x_b) / 2, (y_a + y_b) / 2) and the squared radius is R2 = d2(P_a, P_b) / 4 (a real number). Otherwise, with (a, b, c) the three sites on the circle in counterclockwise order, the center is (X / D, Y / D) by the formulas of Part 6 (1), and R2 is computed by the formula of Part 6 (2) (using orient(P_a, P_b, P_c) in place of A_k). The radius is sqrt(R2).
(5) Convex layers: with the set S of remaining sites initially being all sites, repeat the following until S is empty. If S has 3 or more sites, the next layer is the vertices of the convex hull of S (the sites that are corners of the convex hull of S) in counterclockwise order, starting from the one with the smallest number. If S has 2 or fewer sites, the next layer is those sites in increasing order of number. The sites of the layer are removed from S. The layers are numbered 1, 2, ..., and the number of the layer containing a site is the depth of that site. The first layer is the same as the hull order of Part 4 (4).

* Part 9. Shortest paths and stretch factors
Consider, in the graph where the length of edge (i, j) is sqrt(d2(P_i, P_j)), the shortest path from site i to site j (the path whose total length of traversed edges is smallest). In the given data, the shortest path between any two sites is unique. The length L(i, j) of the shortest path is the edge lengths added in order along the shortest path from i to j (this is the same as the value obtained by Dijkstra's algorithm when the distance from i is computed as "distance to the previous site + edge length").
For each pair (i, j) with i < j, the stretch factor is rho(i, j) = L(i, j) / sqrt(d2(P_i, P_j)). The number of pairs is N * (N - 1) / 2. Compute the pair with the largest stretch factor (for equal values, the pair that comes first in edge order) and the mean stretch factor (the stretch factors of all pairs added in order in edge order, divided by the number of pairs).

* Part 10. Alpha complexes
For each value alpha of the alpha setting, compute the following.
- Selected triangles: the triangles k with R2_k <= alpha (R2_k is the real number of Part 6 (2)). Let F be their number.
- Selected edges: the union of the edges of the selected triangles and the Gabriel edges (i, j) with d2(P_i, P_j) <= 4 * alpha (the same edge is counted once). Let E_alpha be their number.
- Number of boundary edges: the number of selected edges that are an edge of exactly one selected triangle.
- Number of dangling edges: the number of selected edges that are an edge of no selected triangle.
- Number of isolated sites: the number of sites that are not an endpoint of any selected edge.
- Number of components: the number c of connected components of the graph whose vertices are all sites, joined by the selected edges (an isolated site is also one component).
- Number of holes: c - (N - E_alpha + F).
- Twice the area: the sum of A_k over the selected triangles.

* Part 11. Queries
For locate, walk and natural, let Q = (x, y) be the query point.
- locate x y: compute the position of Q and the site closest to Q. The position is: if Q equals the coordinates of some site, that site (vertex); otherwise, if it lies on some edge (i, j) (orient(P_i, P_j, Q) = 0 and dot(Q; P_i, P_j) < 0), that edge (edge); otherwise, if it lies inside some triangle, that triangle (triangle); if none of these, outside the convex hull (outside). The site closest to Q is the site s with the smallest d2(P_s, Q) (for equal values, the one with the smaller number).
- walk x y: starting from triangle 1, follow triangles as follows (a visibility walk). Let (a, b, c) be the canonical form of the current triangle; examine the directed edges a->b, b->c, c->a in this order and look for the first directed edge u->v with orient(P_u, P_v, Q) < 0. If there is no such directed edge, Q lies on or inside the current triangle, so stop. If there is, move to the triangle having the directed edge v->u. If that triangle does not exist (u->v is a hull edge), Q lies outside the convex hull, so stop without moving. In a Delaunay triangulation, this walk always stops.
- natural x y: find all triangles whose circumcircle contains Q inside (triangles with canonical form (a, b, c) such that incircle(P_a, P_b, P_c, Q) > 0), and compute their number and the sites that are vertices of those triangles (if Q coincides with no site and lies inside the convex hull, these are the sites that would become adjacent to Q if Q were added as a new site).
- route i j: the greedy route from site i to site j. Starting from site c = i, while c != j, move c to the neighbor t of c with the smallest d2(P_t, P_j) (for equal values, the one with the smaller number). In a Delaunay triangulation, each move gets strictly closer to j, so the route always reaches j. The length of the route is the lengths of the traversed edges added in order from the i side.
- path i j: the shortest path from site i to site j of Part 9. The length of the path is L(i, j) (also when i > j, it is the value added in order from the i side). If i != j, the stretch factor is L(i, j) / sqrt(d2(P_i, P_j)).
- neighbors i: the degree of site i and the counterclockwise order of its neighbors.
- edge i j: if the pair {i, j} is an edge, report the properties of that edge. If it is not an edge, find all edges that cross the segment joining P_i and P_j. An edge (u, v) crosses the segment when orient(P_i, P_j, P_u) * orient(P_i, P_j, P_v) < 0 and orient(P_u, P_v, P_i) * orient(P_u, P_v, P_j) < 0.
For route, path, neighbors and edge, if any of the given site numbers is not between 1 and N inclusive, it is an error and the answer is unknown_site. For edge, if that is not the case and i = j, it is an error and the answer is same_site. For route and path, i = j is allowed.

* Part 12. Plots
A plot is drawn on a character canvas of W columns and H rows. Columns are numbered 0..W-1 from the left and rows 0..H-1 from the top; initially every position is a space.
- Position mapping: a point with coordinates (x, y) (x and y are integers between 0 and 1000 inclusive) is mapped to column floor((2 * x * (W - 1) + 1000) / 2000) and row (H - 1) - floor((2 * y * (H - 1) + 1000) / 2000). A point with coordinates (X / D, Y / D) (D > 0, X >= 0, Y >= 0) is mapped to column floor((2 * X * (W - 1) + 1000 * D) / (2000 * D)) and row (H - 1) - floor((2 * Y * (H - 1) + 1000 * D) / (2000 * D)) (using the values before or after reduction gives the same result).
- Drawing the line joining two points with character ch means: with (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) the (column, row) of the positions of the start point and the end point, write ch at the position of each point of the Bresenham point sequence. At every position, a character written later overwrites the earlier one.
(1) Triangulation plot: draw the layers of plot_layers in the order listed.
  - edges: for all edges (i, j), in edge order, draw the line from P_i to P_j with ".".
  - mst: for the edges (i, j) (i < j) of the minimum spanning tree, in order of adoption, draw the line from P_i to P_j with "*".
  - hull: for t = 1..H in order, draw the line from P_(h_t) to P_(h_(t+1)) with "#".
  - sites: for the sites in order of number, write "o" at the position of P_s.
  - circle: if there is a largest empty circle, write "@" at the position of its center. If there is none, do nothing.
(2) Voronoi plot: for each position (column c, row r), the site closest to the representative point G = (c * 1000 / (W - 1), (H - 1 - r) * 1000 / (H - 1)) is the owner of that position. The comparison uses, for site s,
  Delta_s = ((c * 1000 - x_s * (W - 1)) * (H - 1))^2 + (((H - 1 - r) * 1000 - y_s * (H - 1)) * (W - 1))^2
  (((W - 1) * (H - 1))^2 times d2(G, P_s), an integer), and the site with the smallest Delta_s (for equal values, the one with the smaller number) is the owner. Next, write "#" at each position with c < W - 1 whose owner differs from the owner of its right neighbor (column c + 1, row r), and at each position with r < H - 1 whose owner differs from the owner of its lower neighbor (column c, row r + 1). Finally, for the sites in order of number, write "o" at the position of P_s.
(3) Writing the canvas: the first and the last line are frame lines made by concatenating "+", W characters "-" and "+"; between them, for each row in order starting from row 0, write "|", the W characters of that row and "|" concatenated. The canvas becomes H + 2 lines.

[Output files]
Write all four files output/triangulation.txt, output/voronoi.txt, output/derived.txt and output/plot.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, every line ends with a newline (LF), and there is no whitespace at the end of a line.
There are 24 kinds of sections, and each belongs to the following file.
- triangulation.txt: input, summary, insertions, triangles, edges, hull, convex_layers, quality
- voronoi.txt: vertices, voronoi_edges, cells
- derived.txt: nearest, components, mst, proximity, degrees, empty_circle, extent, spanner, alpha, sites, queries
- plot.txt: delaunay_plot, voronoi_plot
The sections listed in sections are written, in the listed order, to the file each section belongs to (the order of the sections within each file is their order in sections). Each section consists of a heading line "[section name]" (example: [summary]) followed by its content lines. Sections not listed in sections are not written. A file with no section to write is made an empty file (0 bytes).
Below, < > marks a part to be replaced by its value. The words in a line are separated by a single space, and sequences of numbers are also written separated by a single space. Real numbers and fractions are written in the notation of the Notation part.

[Source files]
Split the source into files with the following six roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow and the largest share of the processing. Reading points.txt and params.txt (deciding error lines, default values), recording and tallying the insertions, organizing the triangles, edges, convex hull and neighbor orders, triangle properties and angle statistics, tallying Voronoi vertices and cells, components of the nearest-neighbor graph, tallying the minimum spanning tree and proximity graphs, the largest empty circle, the diameter, width and minimum enclosing circle, convex layers, stretch factor statistics, alpha complexes, the site table, the 7 kinds of queries, assembling each section and writing the four files.
- predicates: integer predicates and computations (orient, incircle, d2, dot, X, Y, D of Voronoi vertices), fraction notation, reduction of directions.
- triangulation: the triangulation data structure (a map from a directed edge to the remaining vertex of its triangle), the initial triangle, site insertion (search for the containing triangle, visible hull edges, Lawson flips and their count), hull edges, the visibility walk.
- voronoi: squared circumradius, position relative to the convex hull, counterclockwise order of neighbors, outward normals, area of bounded cells, area clipped to the square.
- proximity: nearest neighbors, disjoint sets (union-find) and components of the nearest-neighbor graph, Kruskal's algorithm, Gabriel edge and relative neighborhood edge tests, greedy routes, Dijkstra's algorithm.
- plot: the character canvas, mapping coordinates to positions, lines by the Bresenham point sequence, owners and borders of the Voronoi plot, writing with a frame.

[Constraints]
- points.txt has at most 3000 lines, and each line has at most 40 characters. An integer word has at most 9 digits. The number of accepted sites is at least 3 and at most 400.
- No three sites are collinear, and no four sites are cocircular.
- The values in params.txt are within the ranges described in [Input files]. There are at most 50 query lines, and the absolute values of the site numbers in route, path, neighbors and edge are at most 1000000.
- When computed with the formulas of this statement, the absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values, is less than 2^53 (for example, incircle values are less than 1.3 * 10^13, the absolute values of X and Y of Voronoi vertices are at most 6 * 10^9, the absolute values of sigma_t are at most 1.6 * 10^13, and Delta_s is less than 10^14). Some values reach 2^31 or more, so compute with 64-bit integers or similar.
- In the given data, for every real number output, its exact value (the value of the formulas of this statement computed without error) is at least 10^-7 away from a rounding boundary at 3 decimal places. Also, wherever a result is decided by comparing real numbers (selecting the triangles with the minimum angle and the maximum angle, the minimum angle bins, the largest bounded cell, the largest empty circle, the edge giving the width, shortest paths, the pair with the largest stretch factor, comparing R2_k with alpha, the sign of f(p) in clipping), the difference between the compared values is always at least 10^-6 in relative terms (f(p) is never exactly 0 in clipping either). Therefore differences in the order of additions, differences in the last digit of square root or atan2 results, fused multiply-add and the like do not change the output. Nevertheless, compute with the formulas and orders defined in this statement.
