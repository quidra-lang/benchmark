[Overview]
Write a program that uses the finite volume method to numerically simulate how heat spreads through a thin plate made of a combination of several materials (for example, a heat sink of an electronic device). The plate is divided into square cells, and the temperature of each cell is an unknown. How easily heat passes between two adjacent cells is determined by the harmonic mean of the thermal conductivities of the two cells. The outer boundary of the plate is divided into segments, each of which is one of the following: a boundary with a fixed temperature, an insulated boundary that lets no heat through, or a convective boundary that exchanges heat with the surroundings. Inside the plate there are heat sources that operate only during specified periods.
The change of temperature over time (the transient computation) is computed by three methods: the explicit FTCS method, the backward Euler method and the Crank-Nicolson method. The backward Euler method and the Crank-Nicolson method proceed by the alternating direction implicit method (ADI method), which alternates row-by-row computations and column-by-column computations, each time solving tridiagonal systems of linear equations by the Thomas algorithm. For the temperature distribution after a sufficiently long time (the steady state), the solution obtained directly by Gaussian elimination for a band matrix serves as the reference, and the results of three iterative methods, the Jacobi method, the Gauss-Seidel method and the SOR method, are compared with it. The optimal relaxation factor of the SOR method is estimated from the convergence speed of the Jacobi method. In addition, the program computes an energy balance check, a record of the temperatures at probe points, the location of the highest temperature, the time when the alarm temperature is exceeded, the influence coefficients and heat escape paths of each heat source, the largest heat output that keeps the temperature within the upper limit, isotherm maps, and so on.
The program reads the four files in the input/ folder (plate, materials, boundary conditions and heat sources, computation settings) and writes five files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output. The only operations used in the computation are the four arithmetic operations and the square root, and no random numbers are used.

[Input files]
The input files are the four files input/plate.txt, input/materials.txt, input/boundary.txt and input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There is no space at the beginning or at the end of a line, and the values within a line are separated by single spaces. There are no empty lines. The descriptions below use the following terms.
- Integer: a decimal integer that is 0 or greater, without superfluous leading zeros.
- Real number: a decimal number consisting of, in this order, "-" (present only for a negative number), a sequence of one or more digits, and "." followed by a sequence of one or more digits (this part may be absent) (examples: 0.004, 22.0, 60, -3.5).
- Name: a string of 1 to 12 characters consisting of letters and digits.
- All times and durations written in the input files are integers in milliseconds.

== input/plate.txt (plate) ==
The first line has the form "W H dx d": the number of cells in the horizontal direction W (integer), the number of cells in the vertical direction H (integer), the side length of a cell dx (real number, in m) and the thickness of the plate d (real number, in m).
Of the following H lines, line r + 1 (r = 0, 1, ..., H - 1) consists of exactly W characters. Its character c + 1 (c = 0, 1, ..., W - 1) is the material code (one uppercase letter) of the cell that is r-th from the top and c-th from the left (both counted from 0). This cell is written (r, c); r is called its row number and c its column number.

== input/materials.txt (materials) ==
Each line represents one material and has the form "code name k rho cp". The code is one uppercase letter, and the name is a string of 1 to 16 characters consisting of lowercase letters only. k is the thermal conductivity (in W/(m*K)), rho is the density (in kg/m^3) and cp is the specific heat (in J/(kg*K)); all of them are positive real numbers. The order of the lines is called "the material order".

== input/boundary.txt (boundary conditions and heat sources) ==
One or more segment lines, which describe segments of the outer boundary, come first, followed by one or more source lines, which describe heat sources.
A segment line has one of the following three forms.
  segment name edge start end fixed Tf
  segment name edge start end convective h Ta
  segment name edge start end insulated
edge is one of the words top (the top edge), bottom (the bottom edge), left (the left edge) and right (the right edge). start and end are integers; when edge is top or bottom they give a range of column numbers, and when edge is left or right they give a range of row numbers (both ends inclusive). fixed is a boundary whose temperature is fixed at Tf (real number, in degrees Celsius), convective is a boundary that exchanges heat with surroundings at temperature Ta (real number, in degrees Celsius) with the heat transfer coefficient h (positive real number, in W/(m^2*K)), and insulated is an insulated boundary. The order of the segment lines is called "the segment order".
A source line has the following form.
  source name r1 c1 r2 c2 P on_1 off_1 on_2 off_2 ... on_m off_m
r1, c1, r2 and c2 are integers, and the heat source occupies all cells (r, c) that satisfy r1 <= r <= r2 and c1 <= c <= c2 (below called the cells of the heat source). P (positive real number, in W) is the amount of heat generated by the whole heat source while it is operating (the heat output). It is followed by m pairs (m >= 1) of a time on_i at which the source starts operating and a time off_i at which it stops (integers). The order of the source lines is called "the source order".

== input/params.txt (computation settings) ==
The following lines appear in this order. There are one or more probe lines and one or more run lines, and lines of the same kind appear consecutively.
- initial T0: the initial temperature T0 of all cells (real number, in degrees Celsius).
- end_time t_end: the end time t_end of the transient computation (positive integer). The start time is 0.
- snapshots ts_1 ts_2 ... ts_k: the times ts_1 to ts_k at which the temperature distribution is written (integers, k >= 1).
- probe_every pe: the interval pe at which the probe temperatures are recorded (positive integer).
- probe name r c: the name of a probe and the cell (r, c) at its position (r and c are integers). The order of these lines is called "the probe order".
- run name method D: the settings for performing one transient computation; below, one such transient computation is called a "run". method is one of the words ftcs (the FTCS method), backward_euler (the backward Euler method) and crank_nicolson (the Crank-Nicolson method), and D is the time step (positive integer). The order of these lines is called "the run order".
- reference name: the name of the reference run with which the other runs are compared.
- tolerance eps: the value eps used for the convergence test of the iterative methods (positive real number, in degrees Celsius).
- max_iterations Kmax: the upper limit Kmax on the number of iterations of the iterative methods (integer).
- omega omega: the relaxation factor omega of the SOR method (real number).
- bands b_1 b_2 ... b_L: the boundary temperatures b_1 to b_L of the isotherm maps (real numbers, L >= 1).
- alarm Tal: the alarm temperature Tal (real number, in degrees Celsius).
- limit Tlim: the upper limit temperature Tlim (real number, in degrees Celsius).

[Processing]
Process as described in sections 0 to 14 below.

== 0. Computation rules ==
- Evaluate every formula exactly as written. * and / are evaluated before + and -, and operations of the same precedence are evaluated from left to right. For example, 2 * k1 * k2 / (k1 + k2) * d is computed by first obtaining ((2 * k1) * k2) / (k1 + k2) and then multiplying it by d.
- Unless stated otherwise, a "sum" is computed by adding the terms one at a time from the left, in the order listed. That is, the second term is added to the first term, the third term is added to that result, and so on. A sum with no terms is 0. A "sum over the cells" is a sum that adds the cells arranged in row-major order (section 1).
- min and max are the minimum and the maximum, sqrt is the square root, |x| is the absolute value of x, and floor(x) is the largest integer not greater than x.
- Temperature field: an assignment of one temperature to every cell is called a temperature field, and the value of cell p in a temperature field X is written X[p].
- Hottest cell: the hottest cell of a temperature field X is the cell p for which X[p] is largest. If several cells have the largest value, it is the one among them with the smallest number p.

== 1. Cells and faces ==
- Cell (r, c) is given the number p = r * W + c, and increasing order of the number is called "row-major order". The total number of cells is nc = W * H. The k, rho and cp of the material of cell p are written k[p], rho[p] and cp[p].
- The heat capacity of cell p is C[p] = rho[p] * cp[p] * dx * dx * d.
- Each cell has four faces, the left face, the right face, the top face and the bottom face, and this order is called "the face order". The left and right faces are called the horizontal faces, and the top and bottom faces are called the vertical faces. Beyond the left face of cell (r, c) is cell (r, c - 1), beyond the right face is (r, c + 1), beyond the top face is (r - 1, c), and beyond the bottom face is (r + 1, c). A face whose cell beyond it is inside the plate is called an interior face, and that cell is called the neighbor cell of the face. All other faces are outer faces.
- Faces assigned to a segment: a segment whose edge is top is assigned the top faces of the cells (0, start), (0, start + 1), ..., (0, end); a segment whose edge is bottom is assigned the bottom faces of the cells (H - 1, start) to (H - 1, end); a segment whose edge is left is assigned the left faces of the cells (start, 0) to (end, 0); and a segment whose edge is right is assigned the right faces of the cells (start, W - 1) to (end, W - 1); in each case in this order. This order is called "the segment face order". An outer face that is assigned to no segment is insulated.
- The conductance g and the outside temperature theta of each face are determined, as seen from cell p, as follows.
  - Interior face (let q be the neighbor cell): g = 2 * k[p] * k[q] / (k[p] + k[q]) * d (the harmonic mean of the two thermal conductivities multiplied by the thickness).
  - Face assigned to a fixed segment: g = 2 * k[p] * d, theta = Tf.
  - Face assigned to a convective segment: g = 1 / (1 / (h * dx * d) + 1 / (2 * k[p] * d)), theta = Ta.
  - Insulated face (a face assigned to an insulated segment, or an outer face assigned to no segment): g = 0, and there is no theta.
  A face assigned to a fixed or convective segment is called a "boundary face".
- a[p] is the sum of the g of the four faces of cell p, in the face order (the g = 0 of insulated faces is also included as terms).
- Heat flow into cell p through a face: for a temperature field X, it is g * (X[q] - X[p]) for an interior face (neighbor cell q) and g * (theta - X[p]) for a boundary face. Insulated faces have no heat flow and are not terms of the heat flow sums below.
- Hx_p(X) is the sum of the heat flows through the horizontal faces of cell p (the left face, then the right face), Hy_p(X) is the sum of the heat flows through the vertical faces (the top face, then the bottom face), and F_p(X) is the sum of the heat flows through the four faces (in the face order).

== 2. Heat sources ==
- The number of cells of a heat source is n_s = (r2 - r1 + 1) * (c2 - c1 + 1), and the heat output per cell is w = P / n_s.
- A heat source is operating at a time t for which there exists an i with on_i <= t < off_i.
- S_t[p]: the sum, in the source order, of the w of the heat sources that are operating at time t and whose cells include cell p.
- Sall[p]: the sum, in the source order, of the w of all heat sources whose cells include cell p, regardless of whether they are operating.
- P(t): the sum, in the source order, of the P of the heat sources that are operating at time t.
- The duty ratio of a heat source is (min(off_1, t_end) - min(on_1, t_end)) + ... + (min(off_m, t_end) - min(on_m, t_end)) divided by t_end (the numerator is computed as an integer before dividing).

== 3. Accepting runs ==
For each run in the run order, let dt = D / 1000 (in seconds) and decide as follows.
(1) If any of t_end, pe, ts_1 to ts_k, and all on_i and off_i of all heat sources is not divisible by D, the run is not performed. Such a run is called a "step-mismatched run".
(2) If (1) does not apply, the method is ftcs and dt > dt_max, the run is not performed. Such a run is called an "unstable run". Here dt_max is the minimum of C[p] / a[p] over all cells p (the largest step with which the FTCS method can update the temperatures without oscillation).
(3) A run to which neither applies is called an "accepted run" and is performed as described in sections 4 to 9.

== 4. Proceeding with the transient computation ==
An accepted run consists of N = t_end / D time steps. For n = 0, 1, ..., N the time is t_n = n * D, and the temperature field at time t_n is written T_n. The initial field is T_0[p] = T0 for all p.
In step n (n = 0, 1, ..., N - 1), using the state of the heat sources at the start time of the step, S = S_(t_n), compute T_(n+1) from T_n by the formulas of section 5 or section 7, depending on the method. Perform the observation of section 8 for T_0 and for each T_(n+1) computed in a step.

== 5. The FTCS method ==
For all p, compute T_(n+1)[p] by the following formula.
  T_(n+1)[p] = T_n[p] + dt * (F_p(T_n) + S[p]) / C[p]

== 6. Horizontal sweep and vertical sweep ==
Given a positive real number tau and a value R[p] for each cell, solving the following system of linear equations to obtain a temperature field Y is called "the horizontal sweep with tau and R".
  For all p, C[p] / tau * Y[p] - Hx_p(Y) = R[p]
This system consists of tridiagonal systems of linear equations that are independent for each row. For each row r, let len = W, write y_i for the value Y[p] of cell p = (r, i) for i = 0, 1, ..., len - 1, and determine the coefficients as follows (g_left, theta_left and so on are the g and theta of the left face and so on of cell p).
  lambda_i = -g_left (when the left face is an interior face), 0 (otherwise)
  mu_i = C[p] / tau + g_left + g_right
  nu_i = -g_right (when the right face is an interior face), 0 (otherwise)
  phi_i = R[p] + g_left * theta_left + g_right * theta_right (where the term g_left * theta_left is added only when the left face is a boundary face, and the term g_right * theta_right only when the right face is a boundary face)
Solve the equations lambda_i * y_(i-1) + mu_i * y_i + nu_i * y_(i+1) = phi_i (i = 0, 1, ..., len - 1; lambda_0 = 0 and nu_(len-1) = 0) by the following Thomas algorithm.
  nu'_0 = nu_0 / mu_0, phi'_0 = phi_0 / mu_0
  For i = 1, 2, ..., len - 1 in this order: kappa = mu_i - lambda_i * nu'_(i-1), nu'_i = nu_i / kappa, phi'_i = (phi_i - lambda_i * phi'_(i-1)) / kappa
  y_(len-1) = phi'_(len-1)
  For i = len - 2, len - 3, ..., 0 in this order: y_i = phi'_i - nu'_i * y_(i+1)
"The vertical sweep with tau and R" is the same, except that Hy_p is used instead of Hx_p, and that for each column c, with len = H and cell p = (i, c) (i = 0, 1, ..., len - 1), the system is solved reading the left face in the coefficients above as the top face and the right face as the bottom face.

== 7. The backward Euler method and the Crank-Nicolson method ==
In the backward Euler method (backward_euler), T_(n+1) is obtained in the following two stages (an ADI method of the Douglas-Rachford type).
  (1) Let R[p] = C[p] / dt * T_n[p] + Hy_p(T_n) + S[p], and let Y be the field obtained by the horizontal sweep with dt and R.
  (2) Let R[p] = C[p] / dt * Y[p] - Hy_p(T_n), and let T_(n+1) be the field obtained by the vertical sweep with dt and R.
In the Crank-Nicolson method (crank_nicolson), with tau = dt / 2, T_(n+1) is obtained in the following two stages (an ADI method of the Peaceman-Rachford type).
  (1) Let R[p] = C[p] / tau * T_n[p] + Hy_p(T_n) + S[p], and let Y be the field obtained by the horizontal sweep with tau and R.
  (2) Let R[p] = C[p] / tau * Y[p] + Hx_p(Y) + S[p], and let T_(n+1) be the field obtained by the vertical sweep with tau and R.
In both methods, the Y obtained in (1) is called the "intermediate field" of the step.

== 8. Observation ==
For the field T_n at each time t_n (n = 0, 1, ..., N) of an accepted run, let hs_n be its hottest cell, and do the following.
- Alarm: let n_al be the smallest n that satisfies T_n[hs_n] >= Tal; the time t_(n_al) is the alarm time and the cell hs_(n_al) is the alarm cell. If there is no such n, there is no alarm. Also, the number of n that satisfy 1 <= n <= N and T_n[hs_n] >= Tal, multiplied by D, is called "the time above the alarm temperature".
- Peak: let n_pk be the n for which T_n[hs_n] is largest (if several n have the same value, the smallest n); T_(n_pk)[hs_(n_pk)] is the peak temperature, t_(n_pk) is the peak time, and hs_(n_pk) is the peak cell.
- Probes: when t_n is divisible by pe, record the temperature T_n[p] of the cell p of each probe, in the probe order.
- Temperature distribution: when t_n equals one of ts_1 to ts_k, record T_n and the cumulative values U(n), B(n) and Q(n) of section 9.

== 9. Energy balance ==
The amount of heat E(j, n) that enters the plate from segment j in step n of an accepted run is determined as follows. For an insulated segment, E(j, n) = 0. For any other segment, compute the sum of g * (theta - X[p]) (p is the cell of the face) over the faces assigned to segment j, added in the segment face order, and let E(j, n) be that sum multiplied by dt (computed as dt * sum). X is chosen according to the method and the edge of the segment as follows.
  - ftcs: X = T_n.
  - backward_euler: X = the intermediate field Y for a segment whose edge is left or right, and X = T_(n+1) for a segment whose edge is top or bottom.
  - crank_nicolson: X = the intermediate field Y for a segment whose edge is left or right, and X[p] = (T_n[p] + T_(n+1)[p]) / 2 for a segment whose edge is top or bottom.
Also, the amount of heat generated by the heat sources in step n is Q_n = dt * P(t_n).
For n = 0, 1, ..., N, the cumulative values up to time t_n are defined as follows.
  - Heat that entered from segment j, B_j(n): the sum of E(j, 0), E(j, 1), ..., E(j, n - 1).
  - Heat that entered through the boundary, B(n): the sum of B_1(n), B_2(n), ... in the segment order.
  - Heat generated by the heat sources, Q(n): the sum of Q_0, Q_1, ..., Q_(n-1).
  - Change of the stored heat, U(n): the sum over the cells of C[p] * (T_n[p] - T0). The change per material, U_material(n), is the same sum taken only over the cells of that material.
Finally, the balance verdict is ok if |U(N) - B(N) - Q(N)| <= 0.000001 * (|U(N)| + |B(N)| + |Q(N)|), and failed otherwise. (With the formulas of sections 5 to 7, choosing X in this way makes U(N) equal to B(N) + Q(N) apart from rounding errors.)

== 10. Steady state and the direct solver ==
The temperature field T that no longer changes over time when all heat sources keep operating (the steady state) satisfies the following equation for all p.
  a[p] * T[p] - (the sum of g * T[q] over the interior faces of cell p) = b[p]
  b[p] = beta[p] + Sall[p], where beta[p] = the sum of g * theta over the boundary faces of cell p, in the face order
This system of linear equations is represented by an nc x nc matrix M and a right-hand-side vector. The row and column indices are the cell numbers p (0 to nc - 1). M[p][p] = a[p], and when q is the neighbor cell of an interior face of cell p, M[p][q] = -g (g is the g of that face as seen from cell p). All other elements are 0.
Solve it by the following Gaussian elimination for a band matrix (without pivoting). When there are several right-hand-side vectors, apply the same elimination to all of them simultaneously (solving them one at a time gives the same result).
  (1) For k = 0, 1, ..., nc - 1 in this order, and for i = k + 1, k + 2, ..., min(nc - 1, k + W) in this order, do the following.
     Let f = M[i][k] / M[k][k], and set M[i][k] to 0. For j = k + 1, k + 2, ..., min(nc - 1, k + W) in this order, replace M[i][j] by M[i][j] - f * M[k][j]. Also, for each right-hand-side vector y, replace y[i] by y[i] - f * y[k].
  (2) For each right-hand-side vector y, for i = nc - 1, nc - 2, ..., 0 in this order, subtract M[i][j] * x[j] from y[i] one at a time for j = i + 1, i + 2, ..., min(nc - 1, i + W) in this order, and let x[i] be the result divided by M[i][i]. x is the solution for that right-hand-side vector.
Solutions are obtained for the following right-hand-side vectors.
  (a) b: the solution is the steady solution Tst. It is the reference for comparisons with the iterative methods and the transient computations.
  (b) beta: the solution is Tbase (the steady state when all heat sources are stopped).
  (c) For each heat source, the vector whose value is 1 / n_s at the cells of that heat source and 0 at the other cells: the solution is x_s and is called the unit response of that heat source (the steady state when the theta of every boundary face is set to 0 and only that heat source generates 1 W in total). In the given data, all x_s[p] are positive.

== 11. Iterative methods ==
The steady state is obtained by the following four iterative methods. This order is called "the solver order".
  jacobi, gauss_seidel, sor, sor_estimated
- G_p(X): the value obtained by adding Sall[p] to the sum, over the faces of cell p in the face order, of g * X[q] for an interior face and g * theta for a boundary face (insulated faces are not terms).
- Initially T[p] = T0 for all p, and the following update (the k-th iteration) is repeated for k = 1, 2, ... in this order.
  - jacobi (the Jacobi method): for all p, compute G_p(T) / a[p] using only the values before the update, and make them the new T[p].
  - gauss_seidel (the Gauss-Seidel method): for each p in row-major order, replace T[p] by G_p(T) / a[p]. G_p(T) uses the values at that moment (for cells already replaced in the same iteration, the values after replacement).
  - sor (the SOR method): with the same order and the same use of values as gauss_seidel, for each p compute y = G_p(T) / a[p] and replace T[p] by T[p] + omega * (y - T[p]).
  - sor_estimated: the same as sor, but omega_opt obtained below is used instead of omega.
- Let delta_k be the maximum over the cells of the size of the change |value after the iteration - value before the iteration| in the k-th iteration. When delta_k < eps, the iteration ends; even if not, the iteration ends when k = Kmax. The k at which the iteration ends is the number of iterations K; if delta_K < eps the method "converged", and otherwise it "did not converge".
- Estimate of omega_opt: with K the number of iterations of jacobi, rhoJ = sqrt(delta_K / delta_(K-2)) and omega_opt = 2 / (1 + sqrt(1 - rhoJ * rhoJ)). rhoJ is an estimate of the spectral radius of the iteration matrix of the Jacobi method (for the equations of this problem, the eigenvalues of the iteration matrix of the Jacobi method appear in pairs of opposite sign, and the size of the change may go up and down from one iteration to the next, so the ratio over two iterations is used).
- For each iterative method, with K the number of iterations of that iterative method, compute the following values.
  - Convergence rate: sqrt(delta_K / delta_(K-2)).
  - Milestones: for each of the factors 1000, 100, 10 and 1, the smallest k (1 <= k <= K) that satisfies delta_k < eps * factor. If there is no such k, the milestone is "not reached".
  - Maximum error: the maximum of the difference |T[p] - Tst[p]| between T at the end of the iteration and Tst.
  - Maximum residual: the residual of cell p is the value obtained by adding the g * T[q] of each interior face of cell p to b[p] one at a time in the face order and then subtracting a[p] * T[p] from the result; the maximum residual is the maximum of its absolute value. For the steady solution Tst as well, the maximum residual is computed in the same way with T taken to be Tst.

== 12. Analysis of the steady solution ==
- The hottest cell of the steady solution Tst, the minimum temperature (the minimum of Tst[p]) and the mean temperature (the sum over the cells of Tst[p] divided by nc).
- For each material, the number of cells of that material, and the minimum, the maximum and the mean (the sum taken only over the cells of that material divided by the number of cells) of Tst[p] over those cells.
- For each heat source, its duty ratio, and the mean (the sum taken only over the cells of the heat source divided by n_s) and the maximum of Tst[p] over the cells of the heat source.
- Influence coefficient: for a pair of heat sources s and s', the sum of x_s'[p] taken only over the cells of heat source s, divided by n_s (the average temperature rise of the cells of heat source s when heat source s' generates 1 W, in K/W).
- Superposition check: for each p, compute the value obtained by adding P * x_s[p] (P is the heat output of heat source s) to Tbase[p] in the source order. If the maximum of the absolute value of the difference between that value and Tst[p] is 0.000001 or less, the result is ok; otherwise it is failed.
- Heat escape path: for a pair of heat source s and segment j, 0 if segment j is insulated, and otherwise the sum of g * x_s[p] over the faces assigned to segment j, added in the segment face order (the amount of heat, out of the 1 W generated by heat source s, that leaves through segment j).
- Steady state with duty ratios, Tduty: for each p, Tduty[p] is the value obtained by adding (duty ratio * P) * x_s[p] to Tbase[p] in the source order. Its hottest cell, minimum temperature and mean temperature are computed in the same way as for the steady solution.
- Maximum heat output: for each heat source s, compute u[p] = (Tlim - (Tst[p] - P * x_s[p])) / x_s[p] for each p, and find its minimum and the cell that attains the minimum (if there are several, the cell with the smallest number). This minimum is the largest heat output with which, in the steady state, the temperature of no cell exceeds Tlim when only the heat output of heat source s is changed and the other heat sources stay as they are.
- Heat flow across material interfaces: the sequence of interior faces obtained by taking the cells p in row-major order and examining, for each cell, its right face and then its bottom face is called "the interior face order". Examine each face in the interior face order, and when the materials of the face's cell p and its neighbor cell q differ, let phi = g * (Tst[p] - Tst[q]) (g is the g of that face as seen from cell p). If the material of p comes before the material of q in the material order, record phi for the material pair (material of p, material of q); otherwise record -phi for the pair (material of q, material of p) (in both cases, this is the heat flow from the first material of the pair to the second material). For each pair, compute the number of recorded values (the number of faces), the sum of the positive recorded values (the heat flow from the first to the second), and the sum of the absolute values of the negative recorded values (the heat flow from the second to the first). Both sums are added in the order of recording.
- Segment heat flow: for each segment, 0 if it is insulated, and otherwise the sum of g * (theta - Tst[p]) over the faces assigned to the segment, added in the segment face order (the direction into the plate is positive).
- Total of the heat sources: the sum Ptotal of the P of all heat sources, in the source order.
- Steady-state balance: ok if the absolute value of the sum of the segment heat flows in the segment order plus Ptotal is at most 0.000001 * (the sum of the absolute values of the segment heat flows in the segment order + Ptotal), and failed otherwise.
- Maximum temperature gradient: compute |Tst[p] - Tst[q]| / dx for each face in the interior face order, and find its maximum (in K/m) and the cell p and the neighbor cell q of the face that attains the maximum (if there are several, the first such face in the interior face order).

== 13. Comparison of the transient computations ==
For each accepted run, compute the following values.
- Probe summary: for each probe, the largest of the temperatures recorded in section 8 (if several are equal, the one with the earliest time) and its time, and the smallest of them (if several are equal, the one with the earliest time) and its time.
- Comparison with the reference: for each run other than the reference run, at each of the times ts_1 to ts_k, the maximum of the difference e[p] = |X[p] - Z[p]| between the field X of that run and the field Z of the reference run, and the root mean square sqrt((the sum over the cells of e[p] * e[p]) / nc).
- Difference from the steady solution: the maximum of the difference |T_N[p] - Tst[p]| between the last field T_N and Tst.

== 14. Isotherm maps ==
The isotherm map of a temperature field X consists of H strings. Character c + 1 of string r + 1 is, for cell p = (r, c), one digit (0 to L) representing the number of i (1 <= i <= L) that satisfy b_i <= X[p]. However, only the hottest cell of X is written as *. Also, for each of the digits 0, 1, ..., L, count the cells that correspond to that digit (the hottest cell is also counted, by its digit before it is replaced by *).
Isotherm maps are made for the steady solution Tst and for each of the fields of the reference run at the times ts_1 to ts_k.

[Output files]
The program writes five files: output/snapshots.txt, output/probes.csv, output/steady_state.txt, output/solver_comparison.txt and output/isotherms.txt.

== Common rules ==
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). Do not put spaces at the beginning or at the end of a line, and do not write empty lines.
- In the formats below, each part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and all other characters are written as they are. Values are separated by single spaces (only probes.csv uses commas).
- Integers are written in decimal without superfluous leading zeros.
- A real number is rounded to the number of digits (the number of decimal places) determined for its kind, and written in fixed-point notation with exactly that number of decimal places. Exponent notation is not used, and 0 is written when the integer part is 0. A negative number is written with a leading -, but a value whose rounded result is 0 is written without - (example: with 2 digits, -0.004 is written as 0.00). The numbers of digits are as follows.
  - Temperature (degrees Celsius): 2 digits. However, the temperatures in probes.csv and in the probe summary use 3 digits.
  - Amount of heat (J) and temperature gradient (K/m): 2 digits.
  - Heat output and heat flow (W), duty ratio, influence coefficient, heat escape path, relaxation factor (omega and omega_opt), dt_max (seconds): 4 digits.
  - Maximum error, maximum residual, the maximum and the root mean square of the comparison with the reference, difference from the steady solution, convergence rate, rhoJ and rhoJ * rhoJ: 6 digits.
- <time> is an integer t in milliseconds expressed in seconds: the decimal representation of floor(t / 1000), ".", and the remainder of t divided by 1000 as a 3-digit decimal number (padded with leading zeros to 3 digits if it has fewer), in this order (examples: t = 46000 gives 46.000, and t = 200 gives 0.200). The time step D and the time above the alarm temperature are written in the same way.
- <cell> is the r and c of cell (r, c), separated by a single space (example: 9 11).
- <method> is one of the words ftcs, backward_euler and crank_nicolson.
- "The H lines of a temperature field" are the H lines in which line r + 1 (r = 0, 1, ..., H - 1) lists the temperatures of the cells (r, 0), (r, 1), ..., (r, W - 1) in this order.

== output/snapshots.txt (temperature distributions) ==
For each run in the run order, write the following lines.
- Step-mismatched run:
  run <name> <method> dt <D> rejected step
- Unstable run:
  run <name> ftcs dt <D> rejected unstable max_dt <dt_max>
- Accepted run: for each of the times ts_1, ts_2, ..., ts_k, the following line and the H lines of the temperature field at that time.
  run <name> <method> dt <D> time <time> max <temperature of the hottest cell> at <hottest cell> min <minimum temperature> mean <mean temperature>
  The minimum temperature is the minimum of the values of the field, and the mean temperature is the sum over the cells of the values of the field divided by nc.

== output/probes.csv (probe temperatures) ==

  <run name>,<time>,<probe name>,<temperature>

[Source files]
Divide the source code into the following six files. The names are language-independent role names; follow the conventions of each language for file names and extensions.
- main: handles the overall flow and contains most of the processing. It reads params.txt and boundary.txt, builds the conductances and outside temperatures of the faces (section 1), determines the operation of the heat sources and computes S_t, Sall, P(t) and the duty ratios (section 2), accepts runs (section 3), runs the loop over time steps and the observation (sections 4 and 8), computes the energy balance (section 9), builds the right-hand-side vectors, runs each iterative method, estimates omega_opt, and computes convergence rates and milestones (the parts of sections 10 and 11 that are not contained in steady below), analyzes the steady solution (section 12), compares the transient computations (section 13), and builds the lines of all output files.
- mesh: reads plate.txt, numbers the cells, determines the cell beyond each face, and creates the lists of the cells of the faces assigned to segments and the lists of the cells of heat sources.
- materials: reads materials.txt and holds the formulas for the heat capacity C[p] and for the g of interior faces, faces of fixed segments and faces of convective segments.
- transient: holds the heat flow sums Hx_p, Hy_p and F_p, the Thomas algorithm, the horizontal sweep and the vertical sweep, and one step of the FTCS method, the backward Euler method and the Crank-Nicolson method (sections 5 to 7).
- steady: holds G_p, the iterations and convergence tests of the Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel and SOR methods, Gaussian elimination for a band matrix (including the case of several right-hand-side vectors), and the maximum residual.
- report: holds the formatting of real numbers and times (including the handling of - for values whose rounded result is 0), the building of the H lines of temperature fields and of the lines of isotherm maps, and the writing to files.

[Constraints]
- 2 <= W <= 40, 2 <= H <= 25, 0 < dx <= 1, 0 < d <= 1.
- There are 1 to 9 materials, and their codes are all different from each other. Every material code in the plate appears in materials.txt, and every material in materials.txt is used by at least one cell of the plate.
- There are 1 to 20 segments and 1 to 5 heat sources. The names of the segments are all different from each other, and so are the names of the heat sources.
- The start and end of a segment satisfy 0 <= start <= end, and end is at most W - 1 if the edge is top or bottom and at most H - 1 if the edge is left or right. No face is assigned to two or more segments. There is at least one fixed or convective segment.
- For each heat source, 0 <= r1 <= r2 <= H - 1, 0 <= c1 <= c2 <= W - 1, 1 <= m <= 10, and 0 <= on_1 < off_1 < on_2 < off_2 < ... < on_m < off_m <= 10000000. The cells of heat sources may overlap (an overlapping cell receives the w of each of those heat sources).
- 1 <= t_end <= 10000000, 1 <= k <= 5, 0 <= ts_1 < ts_2 < ... < ts_k <= t_end, 1 <= pe <= t_end.
- There are 1 to 8 probes and 1 to 8 runs. The names of the probes are all different from each other, and so are the names of the runs. The cells of the probes are inside the plate. 1 <= D <= t_end, and N of every accepted run is at most 500. The reference line names an accepted run.
- 3 <= Kmax <= 2000, 0 < omega < 2, 1 <= L <= 9, b_1 < b_2 < ... < b_L.
- For every iterative method, the number of iterations K is at least 3 and delta_(K-2) > 0. Also, 0 < rhoJ < 1.
- The input files correctly follow the formats above. There is no need to check for input errors.
- The absolute value of every integer that appears during the computation is less than 2^31.
- In the given input data, for every comparison that affects the content of the output (dt and dt_max, delta_k and eps * factor, the selection of hottest cells, maximum values and minimum values and of their cells and times, the alarm temperature, the band boundaries of the isotherm maps, the maximum temperature gradient, the maximum heat output), the difference between the two compared values is at least 10^-7 times the larger of their absolute values (except when values computed by the same formula are exactly equal, as in the field at time 0). Also, for every real number that is output, the difference between the value and a rounding boundary (the value exactly halfway between two adjacent numbers at the number of digits written) is at least max(1, v) * 10^-9, where v is the absolute value of the value. Therefore, differences in the order of additions and last-digit differences caused by fused multiply-add do not change the results. Nevertheless, perform the computation with the formulas and in the order defined in this statement.
