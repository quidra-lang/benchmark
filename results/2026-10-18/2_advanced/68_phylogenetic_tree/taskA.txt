[Overview]
Write a program that estimates a molecular phylogenetic tree from a multiple alignment of DNA sequences. The program reads three files in the input/ folder (alignment, parameters, clades to test) and writes 10 files to the output/ folder. Nothing is written to standard output.
The processing consists of the following nine main parts.
1. Input checking: read the alignment in FASTA format and exclude the sequences that do not satisfy the conditions. Compute the tallies of each sequence and each column, the base composition and the site patterns.
2. Distances: for each pair of sequences, excluding the columns that contain gaps and the like (pairwise deletion), compute the p-distance, the Jukes-Cantor (JC) distance and the Kimura two-parameter (K2P) distance. Pairs that are "saturated", so that the correction cannot be applied, are reported and replaced by a fixed value.
3. Distance methods: build a tree by the neighbor-joining (NJ) method from each of the three distance matrices. In addition, find the minimum evolution (ME) tree by nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI) hill climbing under the balanced minimum evolution (BME) criterion.
4. Maximum parsimony: compute the parsimony score of a tree with Fitch's algorithm, and search for the maximum parsimony (MP) tree by stepwise addition and NNI hill climbing. Reconstruct the ancestral sequences of the MP tree and the number of changes on each branch. For the specified taxa (at most 7), examine all trees and list the most parsimonious trees.
5. Maximum likelihood: under the JC model, compute the log-likelihood of the NJ trees with Felsenstein's pruning algorithm, list the sites with low log-likelihood, and compare the trees with the Kishino-Hasegawa (KH) test.
6. Resampling: make bootstrap replicates by resampling the columns with replacement using random numbers (MINSTD), build an NJ tree for each replicate, and compute the clade support counts and the majority-rule consensus tree. Also examine the stability of the NJ tree when the taxa are removed one at a time (jackknife).
7. Molecular clock: compute the relative rate test using the outgroup, and the root-to-tip distances.
8. Comparison: for all trees, compute the Robinson-Foulds (RF) distances, the quartet distances and the list of clades.
9. Hypothesis tests: check whether each specified clade is monophyletic in each tree.
Trees are rooted with the outgroup and written in Newick format and as cladograms (tree diagrams) made of ASCII characters.

[Input files]
There are three input files: input/alignment.fasta, input/params.txt and input/clades.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There are no blank lines. In params.txt and clades.txt, the tokens on a line are separated by a single space, and there is no whitespace at the start or end of a line.

* input/alignment.fasta (alignment)
FASTA format. The file is a sequence of records; one record consists of one header line followed by one or more sequence lines. The file starts with a header line.
- Header line: starts with ">", immediately followed by a name. The name may be followed by a single space and a description (an arbitrary string that may contain whitespace). The name is the string from just after ">" up to just before the first space (up to the end of the line if there is no space). A name is a string of 1 to 20 characters consisting of English letters, digits and "_". The description is not used.
- Sequence line: a line of 1 to 60 characters that does not start with ">" and contains no whitespace. The string obtained by concatenating all sequence lines of a record in order and converting lowercase English letters to uppercase is called the "sequence" of that record. Lowercase letters are parts that were written in lowercase in the original data as marks of repeats and the like, and they are treated as the same as the corresponding uppercase letters.
In a valid record, each character of the sequence (after conversion to uppercase) is one of the following.
- Bases: the 4 characters A, C, G, T. This order is called the "base order".
- Ambiguity characters: the 10 characters R, Y, S, W, K, M, B, D, H, V.
- Unknown: N.
- Gap: "-".
The set of bases that each character represents (called the "base set" of the character) is as follows. A = {A}, C = {C}, G = {G}, T = {T}, R = {A,G}, Y = {C,T}, S = {C,G}, W = {A,T}, K = {G,T}, M = {A,C}, B = {C,G,T}, D = {A,G,T}, H = {A,C,T}, V = {A,C,G}, N = {A,C,G,T}, - = {A,C,G,T}.
Some records contain other characters (for example X, U, *, ?) (they are excluded in section 2 of [Processing]).

* input/params.txt (parameters)
The following 8 lines appear in this order. Each line has the form "key value"; the exhaustive_taxa line has 4 to 7 values, and every other line has one value.
- model: the main model. One of P, JC, K2P.
- outgroup: the name of the outgroup.
- replicates: the number of bootstrap replicates B. An integer from 1 to 50.
- seed: the random seed. An integer from 1 to 2147483646.
- max_missing_percent: the upper limit of the proportion of missing characters (as a percentage). An integer from 0 to 100.
- saturation_distance: the value D used as the distance of saturated pairs. A positive decimal number, written either as a sequence of digits, or as a sequence of digits, ".", and a sequence of digits concatenated in this order (examples: 3, 2.5). It is less than 10.
- exhaustive_taxa: the list of names of the taxa for the exhaustive search. The names are distinct from each other.
- low_sites: the number k of low-likelihood sites to list. An integer from 1 to 20.

* input/clades.txt (clade hypotheses)
One hypothesis per line, in the form "hypothesis_name taxon_name taxon_name ...", 1 to 10 lines. Hypothesis names and taxon names are strings of 1 to 20 characters consisting of English letters, digits and "_". The hypothesis names are distinct from each other, and the taxon names on a line are 2 or more and distinct from each other. A taxon name may be the name of a record that was not accepted, or a name that does not appear in the alignment (handled in section 15 of [Processing]).

[Processing]
* 1. Terms and common rules
- Column: a character of a sequence of the alignment is specified by its column number, counted 1, 2, 3, ... from the start. The collection of the characters at column number c is called "column c".
- Taxa: the records accepted in section 2 of [Processing], arranged in file order. They are numbered 0, 1, 2, ... from the start; this number is called the "taxon number". Let N be the number of taxa. "Taxon order" means increasing order of taxon number.
- Outgroup: the taxon given by outgroup in params.txt is the outgroup o, and the other taxa are called "ingroup" taxa.
- Missing: the characters "-" and "N" are called missing.
- Name comparison: names are compared in lexicographic order by ASCII character code (example: "Beta" < "alpha" < "beta").
- Order of sums: unless stated otherwise, a sum is computed by adding the terms one at a time, starting from 0, in increasing order of index (column number order for columns, taxon order for taxa). log is the natural logarithm, exp is the exponential function, sqrt is the square root, and x^2 is x * x.
- Mask: the mask of a set S of taxa is the integer obtained by adding 2 to the power k for the taxon number k of each taxon contained in S.
- Symbol string: the symbol string of a set S of taxa is the string of length N whose k-th character (k = 0, 1, ..., N-1) is "*" if taxon k is contained in S and "." otherwise. Symbol strings are compared by ASCII character code ("*" < ".").
- Tree: a tree in this task is an unrooted binary tree whose leaves are taxa and whose internal nodes have exactly 3 branches each (however, the majority-rule consensus tree of section 12 and the strict consensus tree of section 10 of [Processing] may have internal nodes with 4 or more branches). Trees contain the outgroup.
- Outgroup rooting: the internal node joined by a branch to the outgroup o of the tree is called the "root" R, and the tree is treated as a rooted tree with root R. The children of R are all nodes adjacent to R (including o); the children of an internal node other than R are its adjacent nodes other than its parent.
- Clade: the clade of a node v other than the root is the set of the taxa of the leaves that are v or descendants of v (for a leaf, just its own taxon). The clade of a branch is the set of taxa on the side that does not contain the outgroup when the tree is split into two by that branch. The clade of the branch joining a parent u and a child v is the same as the clade of v, and the clade of the branch joining o and R is all ingroup taxa contained in the tree.
- Split set: the collection of the clades of the internal nodes other than the root R is called the "split set" of the tree. It is the same as the collection of the clades of the branches whose both ends are internal nodes.
- Child order: the children of a node are arranged in increasing order of the minimum of the names of the taxa contained in each child's clade (for o, that single taxon).
- real(k): fixed-point notation rounded to k decimal places (examples: 0.04160 with real(5), -12.5000 with real(4)). A negative value is preceded by "-". However, when all digits of the rounded result are 0, no "-" is written (for example, -0.000001 is 0.00000 with real(5)).

* 2. Input checking
Examine the records of alignment.fasta in file order. Let L be the length of the sequence of the first record of the file. For each record, check the following (a) to (d) in the order written, and exclude the record with the first one that applies as the reason.
(a) The name is the same as the name of an earlier record (including excluded records): the reason is "duplicate name".
(b) The sequence contains a character that is none of a base, an ambiguity character, unknown and a gap: the reason is "invalid character <character> at column <column number>". <character> and <column number> are the earliest such character (after conversion to uppercase) and its position in that sequence (the first position is 1).
(c) The length of the sequence differs from L: the reason is "length mismatch <length of the sequence>".
(d) With m the number of missing characters, 100 * m > max_missing_percent * L: the reason is "too many missing <m>".
A record to which none of these applies is accepted.

* 3. Alignment tallies
- Per-taxon tallies: count the number of bases (A, C, G, T), the number of ambiguity characters and the number of missing characters. The "unique" count is the number of columns that satisfy all three of the following: the character of that taxon is a base; at least one other taxon has a character that is a base; none of the other taxa has a character that is the same base. The GC content is (number of C + number of G) / (number of bases).
- Column classification: for each column, look only at the taxa whose character is a base, and count the number of kinds of bases that appear. A column with 0 kinds is "empty", one with 1 kind is "constant", and one with 2 or more kinds is "variable". Also, a column in which there are 2 or more kinds of bases that each appear in 2 or more taxa is "informative" (these are a subset of the variable columns). A column that contains at least one gap "-" is "gapped".
- Base composition: among the characters of all columns of all taxa, for the characters that are bases, the count of each of A, C, G, T divided by their total.
- Site patterns: the string obtained by concatenating the characters of a column in taxon order is called the pattern of that column. The number of distinct patterns is called the "pattern count". (Columns with the same pattern give the same result in every computation below, so it is allowed to compute each pattern only once.)

* 4. Distances
From two taxa i, j and a weight w(c) (an integer >= 0) for each column c, compute the following three integers. For ordinary distances every column has weight 1; in the bootstrap the weights determined in section 12 of [Processing] are used.
- n: the sum of the weights of the columns where both characters are bases (the number of compared columns).
- s: among them, the sum of the weights of the columns where the two bases differ and are either the pair A and G or the pair C and T (transitions).
- v: among them, the sum of the weights of the columns where the two bases differ and are not a transition (transversions).
Columns that contain an ambiguity character, unknown or a gap are not used for that pair (pairwise deletion). From n, s, v, the distances of the three models are computed as follows.
- P (p-distance): d = (s + v) / n.
- JC: d = -(3 / 4) * log(1 - (4 * (s + v)) / (3 * n)).
- K2P: d = -(1 / 2) * log(1 - (2 * s) / n - v / n) - (1 / 4) * log(1 - (2 * v) / n).
In the following cases the pair is "saturated" under that model, and the value D of saturation_distance is used as the distance instead of d. Every test is done in integers.
- For every model, when n = 0.
- For JC, when 4 * (s + v) >= 3 * n.
- For K2P, when 2 * s + v >= n or 2 * v >= n.
The distance between taxon i and itself is 0. The N x N distance matrices computed with the three models with all column weights 1 are called the P matrix, the JC matrix and the K2P matrix, respectively. The matrix of the model given by model in params.txt is called the "main matrix".

* 5. Neighbor joining (NJ method)
From a distance matrix and a list of taxa (3 or more; assumed to be arranged in taxon order), build a tree by the following procedure.
1. The taxa of the list, as they are, form the "active list". Internal nodes are distinguished by numbers assigned in the order in which they are created. A distance d(x,y) = d(y,x) is kept for each pair of nodes of the active list (initially the values of the distance matrix).
2. While the number r of nodes in the active list is 4 or more, repeat the following.
 (a) For each node x of the active list, compute T(x) = (the sum of d(x,y) over each node y of the active list other than x).
 (b) For each pair (x,y) in which x comes before y in the active list, compute Q(x,y) = (r-2) * d(x,y) - T(x) - T(y), and choose the pair with the minimum Q. If several pairs attain the minimum, choose the one whose x is earlier in the active list, and if that is also the same, the one whose y is earlier. Let (i,j) be the chosen pair.
 (c) Create a new internal node u; the length of branch i-u is Li = d(i,j) / 2 + (T(i) - T(j)) / (2 * (r-2)), and the length of branch j-u is Lj = d(i,j) - Li (negative lengths are handled in step 4).
 (d) For each node k of the active list other than i and j, set d(u,k) = (d(i,k) + d(j,k) - d(i,j)) / 2.
 (e) Remove i and j from the active list and append u to the end of the list.
3. When r = 3, let a, b, c be the three nodes of the active list from the front; create a new internal node z, and set the lengths of the branches a-z, b-z, c-z to (d(a,b) + d(a,c) - d(b,c)) / 2, (d(a,b) + d(b,c) - d(a,c)) / 2, (d(a,c) + d(b,c) - d(a,b)) / 2, respectively.
4. Among the branch lengths computed in steps 2 and 3, replace those less than 0 by 0. The number of replaced branches is called the "number of negative branches". From here on, the branch lengths of an NJ tree mean the values after this replacement.
When r = 4, Q(x,y) and the Q of the pair of the remaining two nodes are always mathematically equal. Whichever is chosen, the resulting tree (including branch lengths) is the same, so it does not matter which one is chosen because of rounding errors.
The trees built from the P matrix, the JC matrix and the K2P matrix with the list of all taxa are called NJ_P, NJ_JC and NJ_K2P (these are method names). NJ_<model> (for example NJ_K2P if model is K2P) is called the "main NJ tree".

* 6. Fitch parsimony score
It is convenient to represent a set of bases as the sum of the values of the bases it contains, with A, C, G, T having the values 1, 2, 4, 8, respectively. The "merge" of two sets X and Y is their intersection if X and Y have a common base, and their union otherwise; the latter case is counted as a "change".
For one column of a tree, in the outgroup-rooted tree, assign sets from the children upward: to each leaf the base set of the taxon's character in that column, and to each internal node v other than the root R the merge of the sets of its two children. To R assign the merge of the sets of its two children other than o (call it S(R)), and finally merge S(R) with the set of o. The number of changes counted during this process is the "number of changes" of the column. The set assigned to each node is called its "provisional set" S(v). The parsimony score of the tree is the sum of the numbers of changes of all columns. The number of changes is the minimum number of substitutions in the column, and it does not depend on where the tree is rooted.
Compute two integers that give the range of the parsimony score.
- Minimum M: for each column, compute the minimum number of elements of "a set of bases that has a nonempty intersection with the base set of the character of every taxon in that column", minus 1 (this is the minimum number of changes of that column), and add these over all columns (for example, a column of A, C, R gives {A,C}, i.e. 2, and minus 1 gives 1).
- Maximum G: for each column, compute for each base x "the number of taxa whose character's base set does not contain x", take the minimum of these, and add these minima over all columns.
For a tree with parsimony score S, the consistency index CI is M / S and the retention index RI is (G - S) / (G - M).

* 7. Common operations of tree search
The following operations are used on trees that contain the outgroup (they may contain only some of the taxa). The mask of a branch is the mask of the clade of that branch.
- Insertion: inserting a taxon x into a branch e = (u,v) means creating a new internal node w, replacing branch e by the two branches u-w and w-v, and adding branch w-x. The branches of a tree (including leaf branches) are handled in increasing order of mask.
- Stepwise addition: builds a tree from a list of taxa (whose first element is the outgroup). Start from the tree in which the first three taxa of the list are joined to one internal node, and add each taxon x from the 4th on, in list order, as follows. For each branch of the current tree, compute the parsimony score of the tree with x inserted into that branch, and insert x into the branch with the minimum score (if several branches attain the minimum, the branch with the smallest mask). The total number of trees whose score was computed is called the "evaluation count". Taxa not yet added are ignored in the score computation.
- NNI: for a branch e = (u,v) whose both ends are internal nodes (u is the side closer to the outgroup), among the two neighbors of v other than u, let c be the one whose side's clade has the smaller mask and d the one whose side's clade has the larger mask. Among the two neighbors of u other than v, let s be the one on the side that does not contain the outgroup (the other one is the node toward the outgroup, and may be the outgroup itself). Swap 1 exchanges s and c (removes the branches u-s and v-c and adds the branches u-c and v-s), and swap 2 exchanges s and d. The mask of e is the mask of the clade on v's side before the exchange.
- NNI hill climbing: start from a tree and an objective function (smaller values are better), and repeat the following. Arrange the branches of the current tree whose both ends are internal nodes in increasing order of mask, and for each branch evaluate the objective function of the swapped trees in the order swap 1, swap 2. As soon as a tree with a value strictly smaller than that of the current tree is found, make it the current tree at that point (this is one "move"), re-sort the branches and start again from the first branch. If no such tree is found after examining all swaps of all branches, stop. The total number of trees whose objective function was evaluated is called the "evaluation count".

* 8. Balanced minimum evolution
The BME length of a tree consisting of a set T of taxa is the sum, over all pairs (i,j) (i < j) of taxa of T, of d(i,j) of the main matrix divided by 2 to the power (tau(i,j) - 1). tau(i,j) is the number of branches on the path joining i and j in the tree (since this only divides by powers of 2, rounding errors arise only in the sum). The BME length is determined only by the shape of the tree and does not use branch lengths.
Starting from the main NJ tree, the tree obtained by NNI hill climbing with the BME length as the objective function is called the "ME tree" (method name ME). The branch lengths of the ME tree are not computed.

* 9. MP tree search and ancestral sequences
Perform stepwise addition with the list consisting of the outgroup followed by the ingroup taxa in taxon order, and then NNI hill climbing from the resulting tree with the parsimony score as the objective function; the tree obtained is called the "MP tree" (method name MP).
For each column of the MP tree, assign one base (the "reconstructed state") to each node as follows. First, let x0 be the first base, in base order, of the merge of S(R) and the set of o. The reconstructed state of R is x0 if x0 is contained in S(R), and otherwise the first base of S(R) in base order. Likewise, the reconstructed state of o is x0 if x0 is contained in the set of o, and otherwise the first base of the set of o. Then, from the root toward the children, the reconstructed state of a child c of a parent v is the reconstructed state of v if it is contained in S(c), and otherwise the first base of S(c).
The number of changes on a branch is the number of columns in which the reconstructed states of the two end nodes of the branch differ (for the branch joining o and R, the number of columns in which the reconstructed states of R and o differ). The total of the numbers of changes over all branches equals the parsimony score of the MP tree. The length of a branch of the MP tree is its number of changes / L.

* 10. Exhaustive search
The taxa of exhaustive_taxa (which include the outgroup) are called the "exhaustive taxa", and their number is n. There are (2n-5) * (2n-7) * ... * 3 * 1 unrooted binary trees (containing the outgroup) consisting of the exhaustive taxa in total, and all of them are examined (for example, if the outgroup is followed by the rest in taxon order and, starting from the tree of the first three taxa, each following taxon is inserted into every branch of each tree at that point, every tree is obtained exactly once).
- Compute the parsimony score of each tree (taxa other than the exhaustive taxa are ignored), and count the number of trees for each score. The minimum score is called the "best score", and the trees with the best score are called the "optimal trees".
- Strict consensus tree: the tree consisting of the exhaustive taxa that has only the clades common to the split sets of all optimal trees (it is built in the same way as the majority-rule consensus tree of section 12 of [Processing], with "the ingroup taxa" being the ingroup taxa of the exhaustive taxa).
- Heuristic search: perform stepwise addition with the list of the exhaustive taxa consisting of the outgroup followed by the rest in taxon order, then NNI hill climbing on the parsimony score; compute the score of the resulting tree and compare it with the best score.
- Exhaustive BME search: compute the BME length of each tree. The tree with the minimum (if several trees attain the minimum, the one whose Newick string (the common format of [Output files]) comes first in ASCII order) is called the "BME-best tree". Compute the minimum and the second smallest value (the minimum of the BME lengths of the trees other than the minimum tree).

* 11. Maximum likelihood (JC model)
For each of NJ_P, NJ_JC and NJ_K2P, compute the log-likelihood using the branch lengths of that tree (after replacing negative branches by 0).
- Transition probability: the probability P(x,y,t) that base x changes to y on a branch of length t is, with e = exp(-(4 * t) / 3), 1 / 4 + (3 / 4) * e if x = y, and 1 / 4 - (1 / 4) * e if x != y.
- Partial likelihoods: for each column, assign to each node v of the outgroup-rooted tree the four values Lv(A), Lv(C), Lv(G), Lv(T) in base order, from the children upward. At a leaf, the value is 1 for a base contained in the base set of its character and 0 otherwise. For a node v other than the root, with t the length of the branch to its parent, Uv(x) = SUM_y P(x,y,t) * Lv(y) (SUM_y is the sum over the bases y), and for an internal node v (including R), Lv(x) = (the product of Uc(x) over all children c of v). The children of R include o.
- Site log-likelihood: log(SUM_x (1 / 4) * LR(x)). The log-likelihood of the tree is the sum of the site log-likelihoods of all columns.
- KH test: for a pair of methods (A,B), compute for each column c delta_c = (site log-likelihood of A) - (site log-likelihood of B), and let the difference Delta = SUM delta_c, the mean m = Delta / L, the standard error se = sqrt((L / (L-1)) * SUM (delta_c - m)^2), and z = Delta / se.
- Low-likelihood sites: sort all columns in increasing order of the site log-likelihood under the main NJ tree rounded to real(4) (the value that is output), ties by increasing column number, and select the first k columns. For each of these columns, also compute the Fitch number of changes of that column on the main NJ tree.

* 12. Resampling
- Random numbers: the generator has a state x initialized with seed. Each time a value is drawn, x is updated to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647, and the updated x is returned.
- Bootstrap: for b = 1, 2, ..., B in this order, make the following replicate. Set all column weights to 0; then, L times, draw one random number and, with x the drawn value, add 1 to the weight of the ((x mod L) + 1)-th column (the same generator is used continuously over all replicates). With these weights, build the distance matrix of the main model of section 4 of [Processing] (saturation is handled in the same way), build a tree by the NJ method with the list of all taxa, and compute its split set. The number of columns with weight 1 or more is called the "number of distinct columns".
- Support count: for each clade (mask), count the number of replicates whose split set contains it. The support count of a clade that never appears is 0.
- Majority-rule consensus tree: collect all clades that satisfy 2 * support count > B (they are mutually compatible: for any two of them, one is contained in the other or they have no common element). The majority-rule consensus tree is built as an outgroup-rooted tree as follows. The children of the root are o, the maximal collected clades that are not contained in any other collected clade, and the ingroup taxa that are not contained in any collected clade. The children of the internal node corresponding to a clade C are the collected clades properly contained in C that are maximal, i.e. not contained in another such clade, and the taxa of C that are not contained in any of them. When no clade is collected, all taxa are children of the root. The majority-rule consensus tree is given the method name CONSENSUS.
- Jackknife: for each ingroup taxon t, in taxon order, build a tree by the NJ method from the main matrix with the list of taxa without t, compare its split set with the set of those clades, obtained by removing t from each clade of the split set of the main NJ tree, that have at least 2 and at most N-3 elements, and compute the number of clades contained in only one of the two.

* 13. Molecular clock
- Relative rate test: for every pair (i,j) of ingroup taxa (i before j in taxon order), among the columns where the three characters of i, j and o are all bases, count the number m1 of columns where the characters of i and j differ and the characters of j and o are the same, and the number m2 of columns where the characters of i and j differ and the characters of i and o are the same. If m1 + m2 > 0, chi2 = (m1 - m2)^2 / (m1 + m2). When 1000 * (m1 - m2)^2 > 3841 * (m1 + m2) (chi2 > 3.841, tested in integers), rate constancy is "rejected" for that pair.
- Root-to-tip distance: compute the sum of the branch lengths on the path from the root R of the main NJ tree to each ingroup taxon. Also compute their mean mu, the standard deviation sigma = sqrt(SUM (x - mu)^2 / (number of ingroup taxa - 1)), and the coefficient of variation sigma / mu.

* 14. Tree comparison
The method order is NJ_P, NJ_JC, NJ_K2P, ME, MP, CONSENSUS.
- RF distance: the number of elements of the symmetric difference of the split sets of two trees.
- Quartets: for every set of four taxa with taxon numbers a < b < c < d, the topology is 1 if the split set of the tree contains a clade that separates {a,b} from {c,d} (one that contains both a and b and neither c nor d, or the reverse), 2 if it contains a clade that separates {a,c} from {b,d}, 3 if it contains a clade that separates {a,d} from {b,c}, and 0 if none of these. The quartet distance of two trees is the number of quartets whose topologies differ.
- Clade list: all clades contained in the split set of any of the six trees.
- Goodness of fit: for an NJ tree, let the "patristic distance" p(i,j) be the sum of the branch lengths (after replacing negative branches by 0) on the path between taxa i and j in the tree; the residual sum of squares is the sum, over all pairs i < j, of the squared difference (d(i,j) - p(i,j))^2 from d(i,j) of the distance matrix from which the tree was built.

* 15. Clade hypotheses
Examine the lines of clades.txt from top to bottom.
- If the taxon names include names that are not taxa (names of accepted records), the hypothesis is invalid, with the reason "unknown taxon <name>" using the earliest such name on the line.
- Otherwise, if the outgroup is included, it is invalid with the reason "contains outgroup".
- Otherwise, compute the following for the set H of the taxa of the hypothesis. The support count is the support count of section 12 of [Processing] with H as the clade. For each tree in method order, find the smallest set that contains H among the split set of that tree with "the set of all ingroup taxa" added (this is called the "smallest common clade"); if it is equal to H, H is "monophyletic" in that tree. The taxa contained in the smallest common clade but not in H are called "intruders".
- Comparison with the exhaustive search: let H' be the intersection of H with the ingroup taxa of the exhaustive taxa. When the number of elements of H' is 2 or more and less than the number of ingroup taxa of the exhaustive taxa, compute, separately for the trees of the exhaustive search whose split set contains H' and for those whose split set does not contain H', the minimum of the parsimony scores and the minimum of the BME lengths.

[Output files]
The output files are the following 10 files in the output/ folder.

* Common format
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). No whitespace is written at the end of a line. Blank lines appear only where specified in the description of each file. The separator within a line is a single space (except for the spaces used to left-align names in the tree matrix format).
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- <symbol string> is the symbol string of section 1 of [Processing].
- Newick: the string that writes the tree as an outgroup-rooted tree. The string of a node is the name of its taxon for a leaf, and for an internal node the concatenation of "(", the strings of its children in child order separated by ",", and ")". In a tree written with branch lengths, ":" and the length of the branch to the parent as real(5) are appended after the string of each node other than the root. In a tree written with support counts, the support count of the clade is appended immediately after the ")" of each internal node other than the root. The string of the whole tree is the string of the root followed by ";".
- Tree matrix format: let w be the maximum length of the taxon names. For each taxon in taxon order, write one line consisting of the taxon name left-aligned in width w (padded with spaces on the right), followed by "a single space and the value as real(5)" for each taxon in taxon order.

* output/distances.txt

- Minimum evolution block: consists of the following lines.

* output/cladograms.txt

The cladogram of one tree has "TREE <method name>" as its first line and "*", representing the root, as its second line, followed by one line for each node other than the root, in the order of a depth-first traversal from the root following child order (preorder). The line of a node is the concatenation of <prefix>, <connector> and <label>.

[Source files]
Split the source into files with the following six roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: handles the overall flow and the largest share of the processing: interpreting the parameters, checking and excluding records, tallying taxa and columns, controlling the construction of the distance matrices of the three models and of the NJ trees and writing the matrices, controlling stepwise addition and NNI hill climbing (used for both the parsimony score and the BME length), the BME length, the range of the parsimony score and CI/RI, writing the ancestral sequences and the numbers of changes of the MP tree, the exhaustive search (tallying scores, optimal trees, the strict consensus tree, the comparison with the heuristic search, the exhaustive BME search), assembling consensus trees, maximum likelihood (transition probabilities, partial likelihoods, site log-likelihoods, the KH test, low-likelihood sites), bootstrap replicates and support counts, the jackknife, the relative rate test and the root-to-tip distances, RF distances, quartet distances and the clade list, testing the clade hypotheses, and assembling the lines of all output files.
- alignment: the table of the base sets of the characters, reading the FASTA file, the numeric representation of base sets, compressing site patterns, classifying columns.
- distances: tallying n, s, v of a pair, the distances of the three models and the saturation tests, tallying over all pairs, building distance matrices.
- tree_building: the tree representation (enumerating leaves, masks, enumerating internal nodes, split sets), the NJ method, conversion to an outgroup-rooted tree and mapping of branch lengths, patristic distances.
- parsimony: merge, provisional sets and per-column numbers of changes, parsimony score, reconstruction of ancestral states and per-branch numbers of changes, the list of branches, insertion, generating NNI candidates.
- tree_io: formatting real(k), symbol strings, child order, formatting Newick and cladograms, writing a sequence of lines to a file.

[Constraints]
- alignment.fasta has at most 20 records, and the first record of the file matches none of the checks of section 2 of [Processing]. 5 <= N <= 16 and 10 <= L <= 300.
- Every taxon has at least one character that is a base.
- The names in outgroup and exhaustive_taxa are names of accepted records, and exhaustive_taxa includes the outgroup. k <= L.
- No hypothesis in clades.txt contains all ingroup taxa.
- The input files correctly follow the formats of [Input files]. The only errors to detect are those listed in sections 2 and 15 of [Processing].
- Every integer that appears in the computation (including intermediate values) is less than 2^31. However, the MINSTD product 48271 * x may be 2^31 or more, and it is less than 2^53.
- In the given data, the choices of the minimum Q in the NJ method (in all of the three trees including the main NJ tree, all bootstrap replicates and all jackknife trees, except for the equal pairs at r = 4), the tests for negative branches, the comparisons of BME lengths in NNI hill climbing and the choice of the BME-best tree all involve differences sufficiently larger than 1e-9, and every real number that is output is at least 1e-9 away from a rounding boundary. Therefore, the output does not change even if the order in which sums are added or the last digits of the values of log and exp differ slightly between runtime environments.
- The parsimony scores are positive, and G > M. The se of the KH test and the mean of the root-to-tip distances are positive.
- The exhaustive search has at most 945 trees.

In this example, the 4th record Lu is excluded because its name is the same as that of the 2nd record, and the taxa are the five taxa Ka, Lu, Mo, Ne, Po (taxon numbers 0 to 4). The lowercase letters ttt of Mo are treated as TTT, and R represents {A,G}. Ne has 10 gap columns, and its proportion of missing characters is 10 / 24, which is at most 50%, so it is accepted. The pair Ka and Po has 22 compared columns, 11 transitions and 1 transversion; since 2 * 11 + 1 >= 22 it is saturated under K2P, and its distance in the K2P matrix is the saturation_distance value 2.5. In NJ_K2P the lengths of two branches were negative and were replaced by 0, so negative is 2. In the bootstrap, the support counts of the two clades of the main NJ tree are both 2, half of 4, which does not satisfy 2 * 2 > 4, so in the majority-rule consensus tree all taxa are children of the root. Hypothesis km is invalid because Qu is not a taxon.
