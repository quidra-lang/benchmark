[Overview]
Write a program that simulates the signal interlocking of a railway station and the running of trains on the station's track layout, in steps of 1 second.
The track is divided into "sections", each with a track circuit. A point (turnout) switches the destination of trains between its normal and reverse positions, a signal stands at the entrance of a route, and a buffer stop marks the end of a line. The route table lists, for each route, its start signal and its finish, the required point positions, the sections of the route, the overlap sections (a margin for a train that overruns the signal), the flank-protection points, the conflicting routes and the approach-locking time.
The interlocking receives the route requests issued for each train at fixed times and the dispatcher's instructions (route requests and cancellations, individual point throws), checks conflicts and locking, then sets routes, throws points and decides the signal aspects (stop RED, caution YELLOW, proceed GREEN). The sections a train has passed are unlocked in order (sectional release), and when a route is cancelled while a train is approaching it, approach locking keeps the route for a fixed time.
Train positions and lengths are integers in decimetres (dm), speeds are integers in dm/s, and accelerations and decelerations are integers in dm/s^2. A train chooses its speed while computing braking distances in integers, so that it can stop before a signal showing stop, before its stopping position and before the end of the line. Events such as track circuit failures, point failures and route cancellations are also given as input. In addition, every second the program checks that no section holds two or more trains, that no point moves under a train or in a route in use, that no conflicting routes are set at the same time, and that no train passes a stop signal.
As results, write an event log, track diagrams at specified times (diagrams drawn with ASCII characters), the arrival and departure times of the trains with their delays, the safety check results and a summary to five files in the output/ folder. Print nothing to standard output. Do every calculation with integers; use neither real numbers nor random numbers. Use only the standard library.

[Input files]
There are five input files: input/layout.txt, input/routes.txt, input/trains.txt, input/events.txt and input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last one) ends with a line feed (LF). There are no empty lines, no spaces at the beginning or end of a line, and the items in a line are separated by a single space. All integers are 0 or greater and are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. A "name" is a string of 1 to 12 characters consisting of uppercase letters, digits and underscores (_). All names that appear in layout.txt (names of sections, points, signals, buffer stops and boundaries) are distinct from one another. Route names are distinct from one another, and so are train names. In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes; the angle brackets that mark such a part appear in neither the input nor the output.

== input/layout.txt (track layout) ==
It consists of the following seven kinds of lines. All SECTION lines come first, followed by the lines of the other kinds in any order.
- SECTION <name> <length> <row> <col1> <col2>: a section. <length> is its length in dm. A section is laid out in the left-right direction of the track diagram; its left end is called the "left end" and its right end the "right end". In the track diagram (output/snapshots.txt in [Output files]), it is drawn with the characters of row <row> from column <col1> to column <col2> (<col1> <= <col2>), with the left end on the left.
- JOINT <section1> <section2>: the right end of <section1> is connected to the left end of <section2>.
- POINT <name> <side> <common section> <normal section> <reverse section> <row> <col>: a point. <side> is R or L. When <side> is R, the right end of <common section> is connected to the left end of <normal section> if the point is normal, and to the left end of <reverse section> if it is reverse. When <side> is L, the left end of <common section> is connected to the right end of <normal section> if the point is normal, and to the right end of <reverse section> if it is reverse. In the diagram it is drawn as the single character at row <row>, column <col>.
- SIGNAL <name> <section> <direction> <row> <col>: a signal. <direction> is UP or DOWN, and the signal applies only to trains moving in that direction ([Processing] Part 1). An UP signal stands at the right end of <section>, and a DOWN signal stands at the left end of <section>. <section> is called the "approach section" of this signal. In the diagram it is drawn as the single character at row <row>, column <col>.
- BUFFER <name> <section> <end> <row> <col>: a buffer stop. The line ends at end <end> of <section> (L means the left end, R the right end). In the diagram it is drawn as the single character at row <row>, column <col>.
- BOUNDARY <name> <section> <end>: a boundary. End <end> (L or R) of <section> is connected to the outside of the simulated area; trains enter from here and leave through here. It is not drawn in the diagram.
- LABEL <row> <col> <text>: a decorative string drawn in the diagram. <text> is a sequence of one or more printable ASCII characters containing no spaces, drawn one character at a time to the right, starting at row <row>, column <col> of the diagram.
Each of the left end and the right end of every section appears in exactly one of the following: a JOINT line, a POINT line (as the common section, the normal section or the reverse section), a BUFFER line, or a BOUNDARY line.

== input/routes.txt (route table) ==
Each line is one route, in the following form.
ROUTE <name> <start> <finish> <points> <sections> <overlap> <flank> <conflicts> <approach time>
- <start> is the name of a signal. The direction of this signal is the direction of the route.
- <finish> is the name of a signal, a buffer stop or a boundary.
- <points> and <flank> are lists of items "<point name>:<position>" (<position> is N, meaning normal, or R, meaning reverse) joined by commas (,), or - if there are none. <points> gives the point positions required for a train to pass through the route's sections and overlap sections. <flank> gives the point positions that keep vehicles on other tracks from entering the route. For both, the points are set to the specified positions and locked when the route is set.
- <sections> is the names of the route's sections joined by commas in the order in which a train passes them (one or more). <overlap> is the names of the overlap sections joined in the same way, or - if there are none. <conflicts> is the names of the conflicting routes joined by commas, or - if there are none.
- <approach time> is a time in seconds ([Processing] Part 9).
The sections of a route start with the section beyond the end at which the start signal stands, and follow, in order, the sections connected according to the positions in <points>. At the exit end ([Processing] Part 1) of the last section there is the <finish> signal (one with the same direction as the route), buffer stop or boundary. The overlap sections are the sections followed in the same way beyond the last section.

== input/trains.txt (trains and operating plan) ==
It consists of the following five kinds of lines.
- TRAIN <name> <length> <acceleration> <deceleration> <max speed> <entry> <time> <speed>: a train. <length> is in dm, <acceleration> and <deceleration> are in dm/s^2, and <max speed> and <speed> are in dm/s. The train is scheduled to enter from boundary <entry> at time <time> with speed <speed> ([Processing] Part 12).
- REQUEST <train name> <route name> <time>: at time <time>, request route <route name> to be set for this train ([Processing] Part 8).
- STOP <train name> <section> <dwell time> <scheduled arrival> <scheduled departure>: the train stops with its head at the exit end (the stopping position) of section <section>, stays stopped for at least <dwell time> seconds, and does not finish the stop before time <scheduled departure>. <scheduled arrival> and <scheduled departure> are timetable times.
- TURN <train name> <section> <dwell time> <scheduled arrival> <scheduled departure>: the same as STOP, except that the train reverses (turns its direction of travel around) when it finishes the stop.
- EXIT <train name> <exit> <scheduled time>: the train leaves through boundary <exit>. <scheduled time> is its timetable time.
For each train, its TRAIN line comes before the other lines of that train. The STOP and TURN lines (together called "stops") are listed in the order in which the train makes them (zero or more per train). Each train has exactly one EXIT line.

== input/events.txt (dispatcher instructions and failures) ==
Each line has the form "<time> <command> <arguments...>" and is one of the following seven kinds. The lines are sorted in increasing order of time (lines with the same time are executed in file order). The file may have no lines at all (be empty).
- <time> REQUEST <route>: the dispatcher requests that the route be set.
- <time> CANCEL <route>: the dispatcher cancels the route.
- <time> THROW <point> <position>: the dispatcher individually throws the point to <position> (N or R).
- <time> TCFAIL <section>: the track circuit of the section fails.
- <time> TCFIX <section>: the track circuit of the section is restored.
- <time> POINTFAIL <point>: the point fails.
- <time> POINTFIX <point>: the point is restored.

== input/params.txt (settings) ==
The following four lines, in this order.
- END <T>: the final time of the simulation. Times 0 through T are processed.
- THROW <W>: the time (in seconds) a point takes to throw.
- OVERLAP <V>: the time (in seconds) until overlap sections are unlocked by time ([Processing] Part 13).
- SNAPSHOTS <t1> <t2> ...: the times at which track diagrams are written (one or more, in increasing order, distinct from one another).

[Processing]
Part 1. Notation and common rules
- Time is an integer t in seconds. The simulation proceeds in the order t = 0, 1, 2, ..., T, and at each time performs the 12 steps of Part 5.
- For an integer a >= 0 and a positive integer b, a div b is the integer obtained by discarding the fractional part of a / b.
- There are two directions, UP and DOWN. An UP train moves through a section from its left end to its right end, and a DOWN train from its right end to its left end. For a direction d, the "exit end" of a section is the right end if d is UP and the left end if d is DOWN, and the "entrance end" is the opposite end. Following the connections of Part 2 does not change the direction (a train's direction changes only when it reverses (Part 12)).
- The "section order", "point order" and "signal order" are the order of the respective lines in layout.txt, the "route order" is the order of the lines in routes.txt, and the "train order" is the order of the TRAIN lines in trains.txt.
- Each train is given a lowercase letter as its "symbol": a, b, c, ... in train order.
- To "log" means to append one line to output/events.txt. Each line has the form "<time> <content>", where <time> is the value of t at the moment of logging. Below, only the content to be logged is shown, in double quotes (for example, "HOLD B2 A" is actually written as "59 HOLD B2 A").

Part 2. Connections and "beyond"
When a train moving in direction d reaches the exit end of section s, what lies "beyond" that end is determined as follows.
(1) If the end is a boundary, beyond is "boundary".
(2) If there is a buffer stop at the end, beyond is "buffer stop".
(3) If the end is connected to another section by a JOINT line, beyond is that section.
(4) If s is the common section of point p and the end is the one connected to p: if p is moving (Part 3), beyond is "impassable". Otherwise, beyond is the section of p's current position (the normal section if it is normal, the reverse section if it is reverse).
(5) If s is the normal section or the reverse section of point p and the end is the one connected to p: if p is moving, or the section of p's current position is not s, beyond is "impassable". Otherwise, beyond is the common section of p.

Part 3. Points
- Each point has a position (N or R), whether it is moving (if it is moving, also the target position and the completion time; the completion time may be "none"), and whether it has failed. At time 0, every point is normal (N), and no point is moving or failed.
- The "section" of a point means its common section.
- For a point that is neither moving nor failed, its position is said to be "detected".
- To "start throwing" a point means to make it moving with target q and completion time t + W, and to log "MOVE <point> <q> until <t + W>".
- In step 1, for each point that is moving and whose completion time is t, in point order: set its position to the target, make it not moving, log "POINT <point> <new position>", and increase the point's throw count by 1.
- POINTFAIL: make the point failed, and if it is moving, set its completion time to "none" (it stays moving and stops midway). Log "POINTFAIL <point>".
- POINTFIX: make the point not failed, and log "POINTFIX <point>". If it is moving, then set its completion time to t + W and log "MOVE <point> <target> until <t + W>" (the throw restarts from the beginning).

Part 4. Routes and locking
- The state of a route is one of "none", LOCKED (locked), USED (in use) and APPROACH (approach-locked). At time 0, the state of every route is "none". A route whose state is not "none" is said to be "set".
- Two routes whose directions differ are said to be in "opposite directions" to each other.
- Each section has a "route lock" by at most one route and "overlap locks" by zero or more routes. Each point has zero or more "locks", and each lock is a pair of a route and a position.
- To "lock" route r means to set r's state to LOCKED, put a route lock by r on each section of r and an overlap lock by r on each overlap section of r, and put the lock (r, specified position) on each point listed in r's <points> and <flank>. At this time, also remove the "passed" mark (Part 13) from all sections of r, make r's overlap "locked", and set r's overlap counter (Part 13) to 0.
- To "unlock" section s of route r means to remove the route lock by r from s, and remove r's locks from the points whose section is s.
- To "unlock" the overlap of route r means to remove the overlap lock by r from each overlap section of r, remove r's locks from the points whose section is one of r's overlap sections, and make r's overlap not "locked".
- To "release" route r means to remove the route lock by r from the sections that still have it, unlock r's overlap if it is locked, remove r's locks from all points, and set r's state to "none".

Part 5. Advancing time
At each time t, perform the following 12 steps in this order.
- Step 1: completion of point throws (Part 3).
- Step 2: release of APPROACH routes (Part 9).
- Step 3: for the REQUEST lines of trains.txt whose <time> is t, add requests in file order (Part 8).
- Step 4: execute the lines of events.txt whose <time> is t, in file order (Part 7).
- Step 5: judging requests and setting routes (Part 8).
- Step 6: deciding signal aspects (Part 10).
- Step 7: running each train, in train order (Part 12).
- Step 8: recomputing physical occupancy (Part 6).
- Step 9: sectional release and overlap unlocking (Part 13).
- Step 10: safety checks (Part 14).
- Step 11: statistics (Part 15).
- Step 12: if t is one of the SNAPSHOTS times, write the track diagram (output/snapshots.txt in [Output files]).

Part 6. Track circuits and track occupancy
- A section is "physically occupied" when one or more trains occupy it at the train positions (Part 11) computed in step 8. Until step 8 is performed, the result of the most recent step 8 is used; before step 8 of time 0, no section is physically occupied.
- The track circuit of each section has a failed state (it is not failed at time 0). TCFAIL makes it failed and TCFIX makes it not failed; log "TCFAIL <section>" and "TCFIX <section>" respectively. A failure is reflected immediately in the track-occupied judgement.
- A section is "track-occupied" when it is physically occupied or its track circuit has failed. The interlocking (Parts 7 to 10 and Part 13) judges whether a train is in a section only by whether the section is track-occupied.

Part 7. Executing events (step 4)
- REQUEST r: add a request for route r with requester OPERATOR (Part 8).
- CANCEL r: perform it as described in Part 9.
- THROW p q: check the following in order and take the first one that applies as the result: locked if p has at least one lock, failed if p has failed, moving if p is moving, occupied if p's section is track-occupied, and none if p's position is q. If none of these applies, the result is start. Log "THROW <p> <q> <result>", and if the result is start, then start throwing p (Part 3).
- TCFAIL and TCFIX are performed as described in Part 6, and POINTFAIL and POINTFIX as described in Part 3.

Part 8. Requesting and setting routes
- Requests are given request numbers 1, 2, 3, ... in the order in which they are added. In the output, the request with request number k is written q<k> (example: q7). Each request has a route, the time it was requested, and the last logged reason (initially "none").
- When adding a request, log "REQUEST q<k> <route> <requester>". The requester is the train name for a request from a REQUEST line of trains.txt, and OPERATOR for a request from events.txt. Increase that route's request count by 1, and put the request into the "queue".
- In step 5, examine the requests in the queue one at a time, in increasing order of request number. Let r be the route of the request, and check (1) to (5) below in order; r cannot be set, for the first reason that applies. If none of them applies, r can be set.
(1) busy: r is set.
(2) conflict <route>: r's <conflicts> contains a route whose state is LOCKED or APPROACH. <route> is the first such route in the order listed in <conflicts>.
(3) Examine the sections of r in the listed order. For section s: if s is track-occupied, the reason is occupied <s>; if s has a route lock, or has an overlap lock by a route in the opposite direction to r, the reason is locked <s>.
(4) Examine the overlap sections of r in the listed order. For section s: if s is track-occupied, the reason is occupied <s>; if s has a route lock by a route in the opposite direction to r, or an overlap lock by a route in the opposite direction to r, the reason is locked <s> (locks by routes in the same direction as r are no reason here).
(5) Examine the points listed in r's <points>, followed by those listed in <flank>, in the listed order. For point p and specified position q: if p has a lock whose position is not q, the reason is point <p> locked. Otherwise, if p is not moving and its position is q, or p is moving and its target is q, p has no problem (it does not matter whether p has failed). Otherwise, the reason is the first of the following that applies: point <p> failed if p has failed, point <p> moving if p is moving, and point <p> occupied if p's section is track-occupied. If none of these applies, p has no problem either.
- When r can be set, remove the request from the queue, lock r (Part 4), and log "GRANT q<k> <r> wait <t - requested time>". Increase r's grant count by 1, add the waiting time t - requested time to r's total waiting time, and update r's maximum waiting time. Then, for each point p with specified position q, taking r's <points> and then <flank>, each in the listed order: if p is not moving and p's position is not q, start throwing p (Part 3).
- When r cannot be set, if the reason differs from the request's last logged reason (reasons are compared including the names filled in for <route>, <s> and <p>), log "WAIT q<k> <r> <reason>" and set the last logged reason to that reason. The request remains in the queue and is examined again at the next time.
- Within the same time, the locks and throws of a route set earlier affect the judgement of later requests.

Part 9. Cancellation and approach locking
- CANCEL r is performed as follows.
(1) If the queue contains requests whose route is r, remove all of them from the queue in increasing order of request number, and log "DROP q<k> <r>" for each of them. In this case, nothing else is done.
(2) Otherwise, if r's state is LOCKED, increase r's cancel count by 1. If the aspect of r's start signal (the one decided in the most recent step 6) is not RED and that signal's approach section is track-occupied, set r's state to APPROACH, set r's release time to t + <approach time>, and log "CANCEL <r> approach until <release time>". Otherwise, release r (Part 4) and log "CANCEL <r> released".
(3) Otherwise, if r is not set, log "CANCEL <r> none", and if r's state is USED or APPROACH, log "CANCEL <r> refused".
- In step 2, release the routes whose state is APPROACH and whose release time is t, in route order (Part 4), and log "TIMEOUT <route>" for each of them.
- An APPROACH route keeps all its locks until it is released. When a train passes its start signal, its state becomes USED (Part 12 (7)).

Part 10. Signal aspects (step 6)
- A signal g is said to be "cleared" when there is a route r whose start is g and whose state is LOCKED, the positions of all points listed in r's <points> and <flank> are detected and equal to the specified positions, and none of r's sections and overlap sections is track-occupied. (Two or more routes with the same start never become LOCKED at the same time.)
- The aspect of a signal that is not cleared is RED. For a cleared signal g with its route r: if r's <finish> is a signal, the aspect of g is GREEN if that signal is cleared and YELLOW if it is not cleared. If <finish> is a buffer stop, the aspect is YELLOW, and if it is a boundary, GREEN.
- At the beginning of time 0, the aspect of every signal is RED. After deciding the aspects of all signals in step 6, log "SIGNAL <signal> <new aspect>" for each signal whose aspect changed from before, in signal order. The aspect decided in step 6 does not change until the next step 6.

Part 11. Train position and occupancy
- A train that has entered has a "path". The path is the sequence of the sections the train has entered, in the order entered, and each section of the path has a "start coordinate". Coordinates are integers in dm measured in the train's direction of travel. If the start coordinate of section k of the path is x_k and its length is len_k, the coordinate of the entrance end of section k is x_k and the coordinate of its exit end is x_k + len_k. The start coordinate of the first section of the path is 0, and when a section is appended to the end of the path, its start coordinate is set to the coordinate of the exit end of the section that was last before it.
- If the coordinate of the train's head is h and the train's length is L, the coordinate of its tail is h - L. The part with coordinates less than 0 (while entering) and the part beyond the boundary (while leaving) are in no section.
- The "head section" is the last section of the path that satisfies x_k < h. If there is no such section (when h = 0), the first section of the path is the head section. When the head is exactly where two sections meet, the section behind is the head section, and the head is at its exit end.
- Section k of the path is "occupied" by the train when x_k < h and x_k + len_k > h - L. In that case, the part of the train body inside section k extends from coordinate max(h - L, x_k) to min(h, x_k + len_k).
- Sections of the path that come before the occupied sections (ones the tail has already passed) may be forgotten.

Part 12. Running trains (step 7)
Each train has a state (not entered, running, exited), a direction, a path, the head coordinate h, a speed v, the next stop (the first stop not yet finished; "none" if there is none), whether it is dwelling and the time it arrived, the stop awaiting departure (a stop that has been finished but whose departure has not been logged yet; initially "none"), whether it is under emergency braking, and whether it has crossed a boundary together with the coordinate of that boundary. At time 0, every train is not entered. In step 7, for each train in train order, perform (1) to (6) below.
(1) Entry: an exited train does nothing. A train that has not entered enters when t is at least the <time> of its TRAIN line, the section where the <entry> boundary is located is not track-occupied, and no other train has already entered from the same boundary in step 7 of this time. A train that enters becomes running; its direction is UP if the boundary is at the left end of the section and DOWN if it is at the right end; its path consists of that section only (start coordinate 0); h = 0 and v = the <speed> of its TRAIN line. Log "ENTER <train> <boundary>", and then continue with (2) and the rest. A train that has not entered and did not enter does nothing at this time.
(2) Dwelling: a dwelling train finishes its stop if t >= arrival time + <dwell time> and t >= <scheduled departure>. In that case, make it not dwelling, make that stop the stop awaiting departure, and set the next stop to the train's stop that follows this one ("none" if there is none). If that stop is a TURN, the train then reverses. To reverse: let E be the coordinate of the exit end of the head section; replace the path with the sections occupied by the train in reverse order, giving each section the new start coordinate E - (old start coordinate + length of that section); set the new h to E - (old h - L); reverse the direction; and log "REVERSE <train> <section of the stop>". A dwelling train that did not finish its stop does nothing more at this time.
(3) Limit distance: if the train has crossed a boundary, the limit distance D is infinite. Otherwise, let s be the head section and d = x_s + len_s - h (the distance from the head to the exit end of s), and repeat the following.
- If there is a signal for the train's direction at the exit end of s and its aspect is RED, D = d.
- Otherwise, if there is a next stop and its section is s, D = d.
- Otherwise, examine what is beyond the exit end of s (Part 2). If beyond is a boundary, D is infinite; if it is a buffer stop or "impassable", D = d. If beyond is a section s', add the length of s' to d, set s to s', and repeat.
(4) Speed selection: for an integer u >= 0, define the braking distance B(u) as B(u) = n * u - b * n * (n + 1) / 2 with n = u div b (b is the train's <deceleration>; n * (n + 1) is even, so the division is exact). This is the distance a train at speed u travels before it stops when it decelerates by b every second and moves for 1 second at the speed after each deceleration. Let lo = max(v - b, 0). For a train under emergency braking, the new speed v' is lo. Otherwise, examine u from min(v + a, <max speed>) down to lo, decreasing it by 1 each time (a is the train's <acceleration>), and let v' be the first u for which D is infinite or u + B(u) <= D holds. If no u satisfies this, v' = lo.
(5) Movement: with the remaining distance R = v' and the "current section" s = the head section, repeat the following while R > 0.
- If the train has crossed a boundary, add R to h and end the movement.
- Let e = x_s + len_s. If h + R <= e, add R to h and end the movement.
- Otherwise, subtract e - h from R, set h = e, and the head passes the exit end of s. At this point, perform (a) to (c) below in this order.
 (a) If there is a signal g for the train's direction at the exit end of s, perform the passing of g ((7)).
 (b) If there is a stop awaiting departure and its section is s, log "DEPART <train> <s>", take t as the actual departure time of that stop, and set the stop awaiting departure to "none".
 (c) If beyond the exit end of s (Part 2) is a boundary, the train has crossed the boundary, and the coordinate of that boundary is e. If beyond is a section s', append s' to the end of the path with start coordinate e, and make s' the current section. (Beyond is never a buffer stop or "impassable".)
(6) After movement: set v = v', add v' to the train's distance travelled, and set the train's recorded maximum speed to max(recorded maximum speed, v') (its initial value is 0). If v' = 0, make the train not under emergency braking. Then perform the following three in this order.
- Arrival: if there is a next stop, v' = 0, the head section is the section of that stop, and h equals the coordinate of that section's exit end, the train arrives at that stop. Make it dwelling, set its arrival time to t, and log "ARRIVE <train> <section>".
- Standing: if v' = 0 and the train did not arrive at this time, increase the train's standing time by 1. Furthermore, if the speed v before performing (4) (at the time of entry, the <speed> of the TRAIN line) was greater than 0, log "HOLD <train> <head section>".
- Exit: if the train has crossed a boundary and h - L >= the coordinate of that boundary, the train becomes exited; log "EXIT <train> <boundary>".
(7) Passing signal g: let r be the route whose start is g and whose state is LOCKED or APPROACH (there is at most one such route). Log "PASS <train> <g> <aspect of g> <r>" (if there is no r, write - instead of <r>). Increase g's pass count and the train's pass count by 1 each. If r exists, set r's state to USED and increase r's use count by 1. If g's aspect is RED, the train has passed a stop signal (signal passed at danger), so log "SPAD <train> <g>", record a SIGNAL_PASSING violation (Part 14), and put the train under emergency braking.

Part 13. Sectional release and overlap unlocking (step 9)
For each route r whose state is USED, in route order, perform (1) to (4) below.
(1) For each section of r that still has the route lock by r, put the "passed" mark on it if it is track-occupied.
(2) Look at the sections of r in the listed order, starting from the first section that still has the route lock by r, and repeat the following: while that section s has the passed mark and is not track-occupied, unlock s (Part 4), log "RELEASE <r> <s>", and move on to the next section. Stop when a section without the passed mark or a track-occupied section is encountered.
(3) If all sections of r have been unlocked, release r (Part 4) and log "DONE <r>".
(4) Otherwise, if r has overlap sections and r's overlap is locked: increase the overlap counter by 1 if the last section of r is track-occupied, and set the counter to 0 if it is not track-occupied. When the counter reaches V, unlock r's overlap (Part 4) and log "OVERLAP <r>".

Part 14. Safety checks
For each of the following four properties, count the number of checks and the number of violations. Each violation is written, in the order recorded, as a VIOLATION line of output/safety.txt ([Output files]).
(1) OCCUPANCY: in step 10, check each section once, in section order. If two or more trains occupy the section, it is a violation, and the details are "<section> <names of the occupying trains joined by commas in train order>".
(2) POINT_MOTION: in step 10, check each moving point once, in point order. If its section is physically occupied, it is a violation, and the details are "<point> occupied". Otherwise, if the point has a lock by a route whose state is USED or APPROACH, it is a violation, and the details are "<point> locked".
(3) CONFLICT: in step 10, check once each pair of two different routes whose state is LOCKED or APPROACH. Let A be the earlier one in route order and B the later one; examine A in route order, and for the same A, examine B in route order. If B is in A's <conflicts>, or A is in B's <conflicts>, it is a violation, and the details are "<A> <B>".
(4) SIGNAL_PASSING: check once each time a train passes a signal (Part 12 (7)). If the aspect of that signal is RED, it is a violation, and the details are "<train> <signal>".

Part 15. Statistics
In step 11, count the following values for the state at that moment.
- Each route: the number of seconds (values of t) at which it is set.
- Each point: the number of seconds at which it has one or more locks, and the number of seconds at which it is moving.
- Each section: the number of seconds at which it is track-occupied, and the number of seconds at which it has one or more route locks or overlap locks. Also, the set of trains that physically occupied the section in step 8 (what is output is the number of distinct trains).
- Each signal: the number of seconds at which its aspect is RED, YELLOW and GREEN, respectively.
In addition, there are the values counted in Parts 3, 8, 9 and 12 (throw count, request count, grant count, waiting time, cancel count, pass count, use count, distance travelled, recorded maximum speed, standing time). Every statistic starts at 0.

[Output files]
Write the following five files in the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, every line ends with a line feed (LF), and there are no spaces at the end of a line. Except for the diagram lines of output/snapshots.txt, there are no empty lines, no spaces at the beginning of a line, and the items in a line are separated by a single space (output/train_log.csv is separated by commas). Integers are written in decimal, with - only for negative numbers.

== output/snapshots.txt ==
For each SNAPSHOTS time, in increasing order of time, write the lines (1) to (4) below in step 12 of that time.
(1) One line "SNAPSHOT <time>".
(2) The lines of the track diagram. The diagram is a grid of characters with H rows and K columns. H is the maximum of the <row> values of the sections, the <row> values of the points, signals and buffer stops, and the <row> values of the LABEL lines, plus 1. K is the maximum of the <col2> values of the sections, the <col> values of the points, signals and buffer stops, and the column of the last character of each LABEL text, plus 1. Fill the whole grid with spaces, and then write characters in the following order (a character written later replaces the earlier one).
 (a) LABEL: write each LABEL's text one character at a time to the right, starting at its position.
 (b) Sections: in section order, in each column from <col1> to <col2> of each section, write # if its track circuit has failed, otherwise = if it has a route lock, otherwise ~ if it has an overlap lock, and - if none of these applies.
 (c) Trains: for each running train, in train order, draw the train body in each section the train occupies. Let len be the length of the section and n = <col2> - <col1> + 1, let the part of the body inside the section (Part 11) extend from coordinate lo to hi, and let x be the start coordinate of that section. Convert the body part into distances [p, q] from the left end of the section: if the train's direction is UP, p = lo - x and q = hi - x; if it is DOWN, p = len - (hi - x) and q = len - (lo - x). For each j = 0, 1, ..., n - 1, if p * n < (j + 1) * len and q * n > j * len, change the character in column <col1> + j to * if it is already a lowercase letter or *, and to the train's symbol otherwise.
 (d) Buffer stops: write | at the position of each buffer stop.
 (e) Points: at the position of each point, write its position (N or R) if its position is detected, and ? otherwise.
 (f) Signals: at the position of each signal, write X if its aspect is RED, Y if YELLOW, and G if GREEN.

(3) For each running train, in train order, write one line.

<section list> is the names of the sections the train occupies, joined by commas in path order (from the tail side to the head side).
(4) Write one line. For each set route, in route order, form "<route>/<state>" (example: R2/USED), and write them after "ROUTES", separated by single spaces. If no route is set, the line is "ROUTES -".

[Source files]
Organize the program into the following six source files (name the files according to each language's conventions).
- main: holds the overall flow and the core of the processing: reading params.txt and events.txt, performing the 12 steps at each time, executing events (route requests and cancellations, individual point throws, failures and restorations), judging requests (deciding the reason) and setting routes, approach locking, running trains (entry, stops and reversal, path and coordinates, limit distance, movement, passing signals, departure, arrival, standing, exit), physical occupancy and track occupancy, sectional release and overlap unlocking, statistics, building the track diagram, and assembling and writing the lines of all output files (events.txt, snapshots.txt, train_log.csv, safety.txt, summary.txt).
- layout: reading layout.txt; the representation of sections, points (including position, whether moving and whether failed), signals, buffer stops, boundaries and labels; and determining what lies "beyond" an end of a section (Part 2).
- interlocking: reading routes.txt; the representation of routes (state, passed marks, overlap counter, statistics), the route locks and overlap locks of sections, and the table of point locks; and the lock, unlock and release operations (Part 4).
- signals: deciding whether a signal is cleared, and deciding the aspects (Part 10).
- trains: reading trains.txt; the representation of train performance, operating plans (requests, stops, exit) and state; and the braking distance B(u) and speed selection (Part 12 (4)).
- safety: checking the four safety properties (Part 14), recording violations, and assembling the lines of output/safety.txt.

[Constraints]
- layout.txt: there are 2 to 40 sections, at most 20 points, 1 to 30 signals, at most 10 buffer stops, 1 to 10 boundaries, and at most 20 LABEL lines. The <length> of a section is from 1 to 20000. <row> is from 0 to 20, <col>, <col1> and <col2> are from 0 to 150, and the column of the last character of a LABEL text is also at most 150. All positions where characters are written in the diagram (each column of a section, the points, the signals, the buffer stops, and each character of a LABEL) are distinct from one another.
- There is at most one signal for each pair of the same section and the same direction. The common section, normal section and reverse section of each point are distinct from one another. Following "beyond" from any section in either direction (regardless of the point positions) never returns to the same section.
- routes.txt: there are 1 to 40 routes, and each route has at most 10 sections and at most 5 overlap sections. <approach time> is from 1 to 600. Routes satisfy the conditions described in [Input files], and a route never appears in its own <conflicts>. Routes with the same start have the same first section.
- trains.txt: there are 1 to 26 trains. 1 <= <acceleration> <= 20, 1 <= <deceleration> <= 20, 1 <= <max speed> <= 400, <max speed> <= <length> <= 5000, and 0 <= <speed> <= <max speed> (the distance a train moves in 1 second is at most the train's length). Each train has at most 5 stops, and there are at most 500 REQUEST lines in total. Every time appearing in the lines is from 0 to T. No train is named OPERATOR.
- events.txt: there are at most 200 lines, and the times are from 0 to T.
- params.txt: 1 <= T <= 10000, 1 <= W <= 100, 1 <= V <= 1000. There are 1 to 20 SNAPSHOTS times, all from 0 to T.
- The input data are constructed so that none of the following happens. (a) During movement (Part 12 (5)), beyond is a buffer stop or "impassable". (b) While there is a next stop, the head passes the exit end of that stop's section (overruns the stopping position). (c) A train crosses a boundary other than the <exit> of its EXIT line.
- Every name of a section, point, signal, buffer stop, boundary, route or train that appears in the input is the name of something of that kind defined in layout.txt, routes.txt or trains.txt.
- Every integer that appears in the computation has an absolute value less than 2^31.
