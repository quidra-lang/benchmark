[Overview]
Write a program that simulates a roguelike game.
The player explores a dungeon consisting of several levels, in order from the top level downward. The map of each level is made by dividing the map into regions with binary space partitioning (BSP) using random numbers, placing a room in each region, and connecting the rooms with L-shaped corridors. On each level, an up staircase at the arrival point, a down staircase (on the bottom level, instead, the amulet that is the winning condition), monsters and items are placed based on the type tables.
The player acts according to a script of commands. The actions are: moving in 8 directions (moving into a cell occupied by a monster is an attack), picking up an item, quaffing a potion, reading a scroll, wielding a weapon, wearing armor, eating food, descending stairs, and waiting. Monsters act in order of their ID numbers; when the player is in view, a monster chases the player based on distances computed by breadth-first search (BFS), and otherwise it wanders around randomly. The field of view is determined by a radius and by line of sight along Bresenham lines, and every cell once seen is remembered per level. In combat, random dice decide hits and damage. Levelling up through experience points, satiation (hunger), poison and natural regeneration of hit points are also handled.
The program reads the 4 files in the input/ folder and writes the message log, the maps at the specified turns and at the end, and the final statistics to 3 files in the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output. All computation is done with integers; floating-point numbers are not used. All random numbers are generated with MINSTD, and the order in which they are drawn is defined in this statement. Use the standard library only.

[Input files]
All 4 input files consist of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a newline (LF). Each line consists of one or more tokens separated by single spaces; there are no spaces at the beginning or end of a line, never two or more consecutive spaces, and no empty lines. All integers are non-negative and written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
A "dice" is a token of the form "<a>d<b>" (example: 2d6), where a and b are integers >= 1.

== input/config.txt ==
The game settings. The following 16 lines appear in this order.
- Line 1 "seed <D> <G>": D is the seed of the generation random number generator, and G is the seed of the game random number generator (section 0).
- Line 2 "map <W> <H>": the width W and the height H (in cells) of the map of every level.
- Line 3 "bsp <M> <K>": the minimum size M of a BSP region and the maximum split depth K (section 2).
- Line 4 "room <Rmin> <Rmax>": the minimum and the maximum of the room size (width and height, including the walls).
- Line 5 "levels <L>": the number of levels. The levels are given depths 1 to L in order from the top.
- Line 6 "monsters <a> <b>": the number of monsters placed on the level with depth d is a + b * (d - 1).
- Line 7 "items <a> <b>": the number of items (excluding the amulet) placed on the level with depth d is a + b * (d - 1).
- Line 8 "sleep <S> <A>": the probability S (in percent) that a monster is created asleep, and the probability A (in percent) that a sleeping monster wakes up.
- Line 9 "fov <R>": the radius of the field of view.
- Line 10 "player <HP> <N>": the player's initial maximum hit points HP, and the interval N (in turns) of natural regeneration of hit points.
- Line 11 "hitdie <dice>": the dice for the value added to the maximum hit points on levelling up.
- Line 12 "food <F0> <Fmax> <Fh> <Fw>": the initial satiation F0, its upper limit Fmax, the value Fh at which the player becomes hungry, and the value Fw at which the player becomes weak (section 3).
- Line 13 "pack <P>": the maximum number of items in the pack.
- Line 14 "start <name1> <name2> ...": the names of the item types the player carries at the start (<name> tokens of items.txt). There are 0 or more of them (with 0 names the line is just "start").
- Line 15 "xp <T2> <T3> ...": the experience points needed to reach level 2, 3, ... (1 or more values).
- Line 16 "checkpoints <C1> <C2> ...": the turn numbers at which the map is written (1 or more values).

== input/monsters.txt ==
The table of monster types. Each line represents one type and consists of the following 12 tokens.
"<name> <symbol> <min depth> <max depth> <weight> <hp> <to-hit> <defense> <damage> <speed> <xp> <traits>"
- <name> is a string of 1 to 12 lowercase letters. <symbol> is one letter that represents the monster on the map.
- The type appears only on levels whose depth is from <min depth> to <max depth> inclusive. <weight> is the weight used when choosing a type.
- <hp> and <damage> are dice: <hp> gives the hit points of a monster when it is created, and <damage> gives the damage when its attack hits. <to-hit> and <defense> are used for the hit check of section 5.
- <speed> is an integer from 1 to 4, the energy gained per turn (section 6). 2 is normal speed. <xp> is the experience points the player gains for killing the monster.
- <traits> is "-" (no traits), or a string of one or more of the following letters (no letter appears twice). E: moves erratically, S: never moves from its place, R: regenerates hit points, P: poisons when its attack hits.

== input/items.txt ==
The table of item types. Each line represents one type and has the form "<class> <name> <min depth> <max depth> <weight> <arguments...>".
- <name> is a string of 1 to 20 characters consisting of lowercase letters and "_", and is different for each type. <min depth>, <max depth> and <weight> mean the same as for monsters. A type whose <min depth> and <max depth> are 0 never appears in the dungeon; it is used only to be given on the start line.
- <class> and <arguments> are one of the following.
 - "weapon ... <damage> <to-hit>": a weapon. <damage> is a dice.
 - "armor ... <armor value>": armor.
 - "potion ... heal <dice>", "potion ... poison <n>", "potion ... level": potions. They are, respectively, a potion that restores hit points, a potion of poison, and a potion of experience.
 - "scroll ... mapping", "scroll ... teleport", "scroll ... enchant_weapon", "scroll ... enchant_armor": scrolls. They are, respectively, a scroll of mapping, a scroll of teleportation, a scroll of enchant weapon, and a scroll of enchant armor.
 - "food ... <satiation>": food.
 - "gold ... <dice>": gold. The dice determines the amount.

== input/commands.txt ==
The player's command script. Each line is one command, in one of the following forms.
- "move <direction>", "move <direction> <count>": move. <direction> is one of N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW.
- "wait", "wait <count>": wait one turn, doing nothing.
- "pickup": pick up the item at the player's feet.
- "descend": go down the down staircase.
- "quaff <number>", "read <number>", "wield <number>", "wear <number>", "eat <number>": respectively quaff, read, wield, wear or eat the item with number <number> in the pack.
<count> is an integer >= 1; a line with <count> means the same as the same line without <count> written <count> times in a row. Below, each single line after this expansion is treated as one command. <number> is an integer >= 1.

[Processing]
== 0. Notation and common rules ==
- All computation is done with integers. For an integer a >= 0 and a positive integer b, a div b is the integer a / b with its fractional part truncated, and a mod b is the remainder (at least 0 and less than b).
- dec_1(a, b) (a and b are integers >= 0): "-" if b = 0. Otherwise, the decimal notation of a / b rounded to 1 decimal place. Specifically, let z = (20 * a + b) div (2 * b), and concatenate the decimal notation of z div 10, ".", and the single digit of z mod 10, in this order. For example, dec_1(2, 3) = 0.7, dec_1(1, 4) = 0.3, dec_1(100, 1) = 100.0. Do not use floating-point numbers; compute with integers exactly as in this formula.
- Coordinates: the map of each level is a grid of cells of width W and height H, and a cell is denoted (x, y) (0 <= x < W, 0 <= y < H). x increases from left to right, and y increases from top to bottom.
- Directions: the 8 directions and their displacements (dx, dy) are N (0, -1), NE (1, -1), E (1, 0), SE (1, 1), S (0, 1), SW (-1, 1), W (-1, 0), NW (-1, -1). This sequence is called "the direction order", and the directions are given direction numbers 0 to 7 from the first. A direction in which both dx and dy are nonzero is called "diagonal".
- Two cells (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are said to be "adjacent" when max(|x1 - x2|, |y1 - y2|) = 1.
- MINSTD: a random number generator with a state x. "Drawing a random number once" updates x to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647 and takes the updated x as the drawn value. The initial state is the seed. The product can exceed 2^31 (it is less than 2^47), so compute it with 64-bit integers or with a type that represents integers below 2^53 exactly. There are two generators: the "generation" generator is initialized with D of config.txt, and the "game" generator with G. Level generation in section 2 uses only the generation generator, and everything else (section 3 onward) uses only the game generator.
- rnd(n) (n is a positive integer): draw a random number once and take the remainder of its value divided by n.
- Rolling a dice "<a>d<b>": compute 1 + rnd(b) a times (drawing a random number a times), and the value is their sum.
- select(w_1, ..., w_k): the operation that chooses one index from positive integer weights w_1, ..., w_k. Draw a random number once and let r be its value; let q = r mod (w_1 + ... + w_k), and choose the smallest i satisfying q < w_1 + ... + w_i.

== 1. Terrain ==
- The terrain of each cell is one of rock (symbol " ", a space), wall (symbol "-" or "|"), floor ("."), corridor ("#"), door ("+"), up staircase ("<"), and down staircase (">"). These symbols are used when the map is displayed.
- Cells of floor, corridor, door, up staircase and down staircase are called "passable" cells. Rock and wall cells cannot be passed through and also block line of sight.
- Movement rule: one step from cell (x, y) in direction (dx, dy) is possible only when the destination cell (x + dx, y + dy) is passable and, if the direction is diagonal, neither the original cell nor the destination cell is a door. In this case we say that "one can move from (x, y) in direction (dx, dy)". This rule depends on the terrain only and does not consider the positions of the player or the monsters.
- By the construction of section 2, a passable cell never lies on the border of the map (x = 0, x = W - 1, y = 0, y = H - 1). Therefore one step from a passable cell always stays inside the map.

== 2. Level generation ==
At the start of the program, before processing any command, all levels are generated in the order of depth 1, 2, ..., L. Each level is generated by performing the following (1) to (6) in this order, and all random numbers are drawn from the generation generator. Initially every cell is rock.

(1) Splitting into regions (BSP): a region is a rectangle (x, y, w, h) (the cells in columns x to x + w - 1 inclusive and in rows y to y + h - 1 inclusive). The region of the whole map (0, 0, W, H) has split depth 0 and is split by the following procedure split.
 - Let cv be "w >= 2 * M" and ch be "h >= 2 * M". If the split depth of the region equals K, or both cv and ch are false, this region is a "leaf" and is not split.
 - Otherwise decide the orientation of the split. When both cv and ch are true: if 4 * w >= 5 * h, split vertically; otherwise, if 4 * h >= 5 * w, split horizontally; if neither holds, draw a random number once, let r be its value, and split vertically if r mod 2 = 0, horizontally otherwise. If only cv is true, split vertically; if only ch is true, split horizontally; in these cases no random number is drawn.
 - In a vertical split, let c = M + rnd(w - 2 * M + 1); (x, y, c, h) is the first child and (x + c, y, w - c, h) is the second child. In a horizontal split, let c = M + rnd(h - 2 * M + 1); (x, y, w, c) is the first child and (x, y + c, w, h - c) is the second child. The split depth of a child is the split depth of its parent + 1.
 - Then perform split on the first child to completion (including all splitting of its descendants), and after that perform split on the second child.
 The leaves are numbered 0, 1, 2, ... in the order in which this procedure determines them to be leaves. A region that is not a leaf is called a "node".

(2) Rooms: in order of leaf number, one room is made in each leaf (x, y, w, h). The room number is the same as the leaf number.
 - Compute rw = Rmin + rnd(min(Rmax, w - 1) - Rmin + 1), rh = Rmin + rnd(min(Rmax, h - 1) - Rmin + 1), rx = x + rnd(w - rw), ry = y + rnd(h - rh), in this order.
 - The room occupies the cells in columns rx to rx + rw - 1 inclusive and in rows ry to ry + rh - 1 inclusive. Of these, the cells in rows ry and ry + rh - 1 get the terrain "-" wall; of the remaining cells, those in columns rx and rx + rw - 1 get the terrain "|" wall; all other cells (the "interior" of the room) get the terrain floor. Rooms never overlap or touch each other.
 - The "center" of a room is (rx + rw div 2, ry + rh div 2), and it always lies in the interior.
 - "Drawing an interior cell of room i" means computing x' = rx + 1 + rnd(rw - 2) and y' = ry + 1 + rnd(rh - 2) in this order for room i and obtaining the cell (x', y'). "Drawing a room cell" means computing i = rnd(n), where n is the number of rooms, and then drawing an interior cell of room i (3 random numbers are drawn).

(3) Corridors: all nodes are handled one at a time in post-order (the subtree of the first child, then the subtree of the second child, then the node itself). For each node, let A be the set of rooms of the leaves contained in the subtree of the first child, and B the set of rooms of the leaves contained in the subtree of the second child. Among the pairs (a, b) where a is a room in A and b is a room in B, choose the one with the smallest distance between the centers |ax - bx| + |ay - by| ((ax, ay) and (bx, by) are the centers of a and b, respectively; if several pairs attain the minimum, choose the one in which the number of a is smallest, and among those the one in which the number of b is smallest).
 Then draw a random number once and let r be its value; if r mod 2 = 0 it is "horizontal first", otherwise "vertical first", and the following sequence of cells is the path.
 - Horizontal first: the cells (x, ay) as x goes from ax to bx, approaching by 1 each time (ax and bx included), followed by the cells (bx, y) as y goes from ay to by, approaching by 1 each time.
 - Vertical first: the cells (ax, y) as y goes from ay to by, approaching by 1 each time, followed by the cells (x, by) as x goes from ax to bx, approaching by 1 each time.
 Look at the cells of the path in order, and change rock into corridor and wall into door. Other terrain is not changed.

(4) Stairs: draw a room cell and make its terrain an up staircase. This cell is called the level's "arrival cell", and the drawn room i is called the "arrival room". Then compute j = rnd(n - 1), and if j >= i, add 1 to j (so a room other than the arrival room is chosen). Draw an interior cell of room j; this is called the "target cell". If the depth is less than L, make the terrain of the target cell a down staircase. On the bottom level (depth L), the terrain of the target cell stays floor, and the amulet is placed there (the amulet is an item with class amulet and name amulet).

(5) Monsters: let d be the depth; a + b * (d - 1) monsters (a and b are the values of the monsters line) are created one at a time. The candidates are the types of monsters.txt satisfying min depth <= d <= max depth, in file order. For each monster, do the following in this order.
 - Perform select on the sequence of the candidates' weights to decide the type.
 - Repeat drawing a room cell until "the drawn room is not the arrival room, the cell's terrain is floor, and no already created monster is on that cell" holds, and place the monster on the last drawn cell.
 - Roll the type's <hp> dice; its value becomes the monster's hit points and maximum hit points.
 - Draw a random number once and let r be its value; the monster is asleep if r mod 100 < S, and awake otherwise.
 The monsters are given ID numbers 1, 2, 3, ... in the order in which they are created within that level. Their energy is initially 0, and their "was visible" flag is initially false (section 4).

(6) Items: a + b * (d - 1) items (a and b are the values of the items line) are placed one at a time. The candidates are the types of items.txt satisfying min depth <= d <= max depth, in file order. For each item, do the following in this order.
 - Perform select on the sequence of the candidates' weights to decide the type.
 - Repeat drawing a room cell until "the cell's terrain is floor and no already placed item (including the amulet) is on that cell" holds, and place the item on the last drawn cell.
 - If the class is gold, roll the type's dice; its value is the amount.
 Two or more items are never placed on one cell. Items are only on floor cells, and an item may be on the same cell as a monster.

In addition to the terrain, the rooms, the arrival cell, the target cell, the list of monsters (in ID order) and the items, each level holds a "memory" for every cell (section 4), the number of monsters killed, and the number of items picked up. The memory is initially " " (a space) for every cell, and the two numbers are initially 0.

== 3. The player and items ==
- An item has a type and a bonus (initially 0); gold also has an amount.
- The symbol of an item is ")" if its class is weapon, "[" for armor, "!" for potion, "?" for scroll, "%" for food, "$" for gold, and the double quote character (") for amulet.
- The "display name" of an item: let N be <name> with every "_" replaced by a space. The display name of a weapon or armor is "+<bonus> N" (examples: +0 short sword, +2 chain mail), the display name of gold is "<amount> gold", and the display name of anything else is N (examples: healing potion, amulet).
- The "reference name" of an item: for gold, the display name itself; otherwise "the " followed by the display name (examples: 12 gold, the +0 mace).
- The player has a position, a current depth, hit points, maximum hit points, a level, experience points, gold, satiation, remaining poison turns, a pack, a wielded weapon, and worn armor. Initially, the position is the arrival cell of depth 1, the depth is 1, the hit points and the maximum hit points are HP of the player line, the level is 1, the experience points are 0, the gold is 0, the satiation is F0, and the remaining poison turns are 0. The pack holds items (bonus 0) of the types named on the start line, in that order; the player wields the first weapon among them and wears the first armor among them (if there is none, no weapon is wielded or no armor is worn, respectively).
- The pack is a sequence of items, referred to by the numbers 1, 2, 3, ... from the first. When an item is removed from the pack, the numbers of the items after it each move up by one. A newly obtained item is added at the end. Weapons and armor are never removed from the pack.
- Defense: the player's defense is 10 + the armor's <armor value> + the armor's bonus when wearing armor, and 10 otherwise.
- Hunger state: with satiation f, the state is normal if f > Fh, hungry if Fw < f <= Fh, weak if 0 < f <= Fw, and starving if f = 0.

== 4. Field of view and memory ==
- Line: the sequence of cells line(x0, y0, x1, y1) of the line from cell (x0, y0) to cell (x1, y1) is computed by the following steps. Let dx = |x1 - x0|, dy = |y1 - y0|, sx = 1 if x1 > x0 and sx = -1 otherwise, sy = 1 if y1 > y0 and sy = -1 otherwise, and err = dx - dy. Starting with (x, y) = (x0, y0), repeat the following until (x, y) equals (x1, y1): let e2 = 2 * err; if e2 > -dy, subtract dy from err and add sx to x; then (using the e2 computed at the beginning) if e2 < dx, add dx to err and add sy to y; append the resulting cell (x, y) to the end of the sequence. The sequence does not contain (x0, y0), and its last cell is (x1, y1).
- Field of view: let (px, py) be the player's position. The field of view V is the set of cells (x, y) of the player's level such that (x - px)^2 + (y - py)^2 <= R^2 and all cells of line(px, py, x, y) except the last one are passable. The player's cell is always in V. Line of sight is judged by the terrain only; monsters and items do not block it.
- Updating the field of view: perform the following steps in this order.
 1. Recompute V from the player's current position.
 2. For every cell of V, set the memory of that cell on the player's level to the item's symbol if there is an item on the cell, and to the terrain's symbol otherwise.
 3. Examine the monsters on the player's level in ID order; if a monster's cell is in V and its "was visible" flag is false, record the message "You see the <name>.". After that, for every monster, set the "was visible" flag to true if its cell is in V, and to false otherwise.
 The field of view is updated at the start of the game (before processing the first command) and in (b) of section 6. V does not change until the next update of the field of view (it is not recomputed even if monsters or the player move in the meantime).

== 5. Combat ==
- Attack resolution: an attack with to-hit modifier h, defense D, a damage dice, and damage bonus c is resolved as follows. First compute roll = 1 + rnd(20). If roll = 1, it is a "miss". If roll = 20, it is a "critical", and the damage is "the value of rolling the dice + the value of rolling the dice once more + c". Otherwise, if roll + h >= D it is a "hit" and the damage is "the value of rolling the dice + c"; otherwise it is a miss. If the damage is less than 1, it becomes 1. On a miss the dice is not rolled.
- The player's attack: when the player attacks a monster, the monster first becomes awake (it wakes up even if the attack misses). If the player wields a weapon, h = level + the weapon's <to-hit> + the weapon's bonus, the dice is the weapon's <damage>, and c is the weapon's bonus. If not, h = level, the dice is 1d2, and c = 0. If the hunger state is weak or starving, a further 2 is subtracted from h. D is the <defense> of the monster's type.
 - Miss: "You miss the <name>."
 - Hit: record "You hit the <name> for <damage>.", or on a critical "You hit the <name> hard for <damage>.", and subtract the damage from the monster's hit points. If its hit points become 0 or less, record "You kill the <name>.", remove the monster from the level, add 1 to that level's number of monsters killed, and the player gains the type's <xp> experience points ("Gaining experience points" below).
- A monster's attack: h is the <to-hit> of the monster's type, D is the player's defense, the dice is the type's <damage>, and c = 0.
 - Miss: "The <name> misses you."
 - Hit: record "The <name> hits you for <damage>.", or on a critical "The <name> hits you hard for <damage>.", and subtract the damage from the player's hit points. If the hit points become 0 or less, the player dies (the cause of death is "killed_by <name>"). If the player did not die and the type's <traits> contains P, add 5 to the remaining poison turns and record "You are poisoned.".
- Gaining experience points: add the gained value to the experience points, and then repeat the following. With m the number of values on the xp line, while the level is at most m and the experience points are at least the value needed to reach level + 1 (the level-th value on the xp line), add 1 to the level, roll the dice of the hitdie line and add its value to both the maximum hit points and the hit points, and record "Welcome to level <new level>.".
- The player dies: record "You die." and end the game (the result is died).

== 6. Game progress ==
The turn number t is initially 1. Whenever a message is recorded, the t at that moment is recorded with it. After updating the field of view (section 4) at the start of the game, the commands are processed one at a time from the first.
- If the game has already ended, the command is only counted as "unused", and nothing is done.
- Otherwise the command is executed by the rules of section 7. Some commands "fail". A failed command only records a message and does not use a turn (t does not change, and (a) to (f) below are not performed).
- If the command succeeds, perform the following (a) to (f) in this order.
 (a) If the command picked up the amulet and the game has ended, the processing ends here.
 (b) Update the field of view.
 (c) Monster actions: compute the distances of section 8 for the player's level. Then handle the monsters of that level one at a time in ID order: add the type's <speed> to the monster's energy, and while the energy is 2 or more, subtract 2 from the energy and perform one action of section 9. If the player dies, the game ends at that moment, and the remaining monsters are not handled.
 (d) Perform the end-of-turn processing (section 10). If the player dies, the game ends at that moment.
 (e) If t equals one of the values on the checkpoints line, write a map block (a checkpoint block of output/maps.txt).
 (f) Add 1 to t.
Monsters on other levels do not act, and their energy does not increase. After all commands have been processed (if the game has not ended, the result is alive), write the output files.

== 7. Executing commands ==
Let (px, py) be the player's position.
- move <direction>: let (dx, dy) be the displacement of the direction and let the destination be the cell (px + dx, py + dy). Do the first of the following that applies.
 1. If the destination is not passable, record "You bump into a wall." and fail.
 2. If one cannot move from (px, py) in direction (dx, dy) (a diagonal move involving a door), record "You cannot move diagonally through a door." and fail.
 3. If there is a monster on the destination, attack that monster (section 5) and succeed.
 4. Otherwise, move the player to the destination and add 1 to the number of steps walked. If there is an item on the new cell, record "You see <reference name> here."; if there is no item and the terrain is a down staircase, record "There is a staircase down here.". Succeed.
- wait: do nothing and succeed.
- pickup: if there is no item on the player's cell, record "There is nothing here." and fail. If there is an item, do the following according to its class.
 - Gold: add the amount to the gold and record "You pick up <display name>.".
 - Amulet: record "You pick up the amulet." and "You win!" in this order, and end the game (the result is won).
 - Anything else: if the pack already holds P items, record "Your pack is full." and fail. Otherwise add the item to the end of the pack and record "You pick up the <display name> (<number in the pack>).".
 If it did not fail, remove the item from the level, add 1 each to that level's number of items picked up and to the pickup count, and succeed.
- Commands that specify a number (quaff, read, wield, wear, eat): if <number> is greater than the number of items in the pack, record "You do not have that item." and fail. If the class of the item with that number is not potion for quaff, scroll for read, weapon for wield, armor for wear, or food for eat, record "You cannot <command name> that." (example: You cannot quaff that.) and fail. Otherwise do the respective following and succeed (wield and wear have further cases in which they fail).
- quaff: remove the potion from the pack, add 1 to the quaff count, and record "You quaff the <display name>.". Then do the following according to the effect.
 - heal: roll the dice and add its value to the hit points. If the hit points exceed the maximum hit points, add 1 to the maximum hit points and make the hit points equal to the maximum hit points. Set the remaining poison turns to 0 and record "You feel better.".
 - poison: add n to the remaining poison turns and record "You feel very sick.".
 - level: record "You feel more experienced.". Then, if the level is at most the number of values on the xp line, gain exactly the experience points needed to make the experience points equal to the value needed to reach level + 1 (section 5; the level goes up by 1). Otherwise nothing happens.
- read: remove the scroll from the pack, add 1 to the read count, and record "You read the <display name>.". Then do the following according to the effect.
 - mapping: for every cell of the player's level whose terrain is not rock and whose memory is " ", set the memory to the symbol of that cell's terrain. Record "A map forms in your mind.".
 - teleport: on the player's level, repeat drawing a room cell until "the drawn cell differs from the player's position and there is no monster on that cell" holds, and move the player to the last drawn cell. Record "You are teleported.".
 - enchant_weapon: if the player wields a weapon, record "Your <display name of the weapon> glows blue." and then add 1 to the weapon's bonus. If not, record "Your hands twitch.".
 - enchant_armor: if the player wears armor, record "Your <display name of the armor> glows silver." and then add 1 to the armor's bonus. If not, record "Your skin tingles.".
- wield: if the player already wields that weapon, record "You are already wielding that." and fail. Otherwise wield that weapon (the previous weapon remains in the pack), add 1 to the wield count, and record "You are now wielding the <display name>.".
- wear: if the player already wears that armor, record "You are already wearing that." and fail. Otherwise wear that armor, add 1 to the wear count, and record "You are now wearing the <display name>.".
- eat: remove the food from the pack and add 1 to the eat count. Set the satiation to the smaller of (satiation + the food's <satiation>) and Fmax, and record "You eat the <display name>.".
- descend: if the terrain of the player's cell is not a down staircase, record "There are no stairs here." and fail. Otherwise add 1 to the depth, move the player to the arrival cell of the new level, and record "You descend to depth <new depth>.". Succeed.

== 8. Distance ==
At the beginning of (c) of section 6, for each cell c of the player's level, let dist(c) be the minimum number of steps needed to reach c from the player's cell, moving one step at a time according to the movement rule (section 1) (compute it by breadth-first search; the positions of monsters are not considered). The dist of a cell that cannot be reached is "none". Since the movement rule stays the same when the direction is reversed, dist(c) is also the minimum number of steps from c to the player's cell. dist is not recomputed during (c).

== 9. Monster actions ==
Let (mx, my) be the position of the acting monster. "The monster can see the player" means that (mx, my) is in the field of view V (the one computed in (b) of this turn). It is judged by the position at the time of the action.
Do the first of the following that applies.
 1. If the monster is asleep: only when it can see the player, draw a random number once and let r be its value; if r mod 100 < A, make the monster awake and record "The <name> wakes up.". When it cannot see the player, do nothing (no random number is drawn either).
 2. If <traits> contains S: if the monster can attack (defined below), attack the player. Otherwise do nothing.
 3. If <traits> contains E and, after drawing a random number once, its value r satisfies r mod 2 = 0: wander. (If r mod 2 = 1, go on to 4. If there is no E, go on to 4 without drawing a random number.)
 4. If the monster can see the player, chase; otherwise wander.
- Can attack: the player's cell is adjacent to the monster's cell, and one can move from (mx, my) in direction (player's x - mx, player's y - my).
- Wander: draw a random number once and let r be its value; let (dx, dy) be the direction with direction number r mod 8. If one cannot move from (mx, my) in direction (dx, dy), do nothing. If one can, then if the destination is the player's cell, attack the player; otherwise, if there is no other monster on the destination, move the monster to that cell, and if there is another monster, do nothing.
- Chase: if the monster can attack, attack the player. Otherwise, examine the 8 directions (dx, dy) in the direction order, and take as candidates the destination cells c that satisfy all of the following: one can move from (mx, my) in direction (dx, dy); dist(c) is not "none"; dist((mx, my)) is "none" or dist(c) < dist((mx, my)); there is no other monster on c; c is not the player's cell. Move the monster to the candidate with the smallest dist(c) (if several have the smallest value, the one earliest in the direction order). If there is no candidate, do nothing.
Monsters never pick up items, and monsters never attack each other.

== 10. End-of-turn processing ==
Perform the following 1 to 4 in this order.
1. Examine the monsters on the player's level in ID order, and add 1 to the hit points of each monster whose <traits> contains R and whose hit points are less than its maximum hit points.
2. If the remaining poison turns are positive, subtract 1 from the player's hit points and add 1 to the total poison damage. If the hit points become 0 or less, the player dies (the cause of death is "poison") and this processing ends here. Otherwise subtract 1 from the remaining poison turns, and if they become 0, record "You feel less sick.".
3. If the satiation is 0, subtract 1 from the player's hit points and add 1 to the total starvation damage. If the hit points become 0 or less, the player dies (the cause of death is "starvation") and this processing ends here. If the satiation is positive, subtract 1 from the satiation, and if this changes the hunger state, record one of "You are getting hungry." (hungry), "You feel weak from hunger." (weak), "You are starving." (starving) according to the new state.
4. If t mod N = 0, the remaining poison turns are 0, the satiation is positive, and the hit points are less than the maximum hit points, add 1 to the hit points.

[Output files]
Common format:
- Everything consists of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No line has trailing spaces, and no blank lines are written unless specified.
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Negative numbers (the hit points after death) have a leading "-".
- In the formats below, a part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and all other characters are written as they are.
- All 3 files are always created. A file with no lines to write is empty (0 bytes).

== output/messages.txt ==
Write the recorded messages in the order recorded, one per line, in the form "<turn number> <text>". <turn number> is the t at the time of recording in decimal, padded with leading zeros to 4 digits if it has fewer than 4 digits (examples: 0007, 0123). <text> is the message string defined in [Processing]; <name> is replaced by the name of the monster's type, and <display name> and <reference name> by the strings of section 3.

== output/maps.txt ==
Write the checkpoint blocks written in (e) of section 6, in the order written, and at the end write the final block.
- Map frame: a map is represented by H + 2 lines. The first and the last line consist of "+", W characters "-", and "+", in this order. Each of the H lines in between, in the order y = 0, 1, ..., H - 1, consists of "|", the W display characters of the cells x = 0, 1, ..., W - 1, and "|", in this order.
- Display characters: when the player's level is displayed as "the current view", a cell in V (the one computed by the last update of the field of view) is shown as "@" if it is the player's cell, otherwise as the <symbol> of the monster's type if a monster is on it, otherwise as the item's symbol if an item is on it, and otherwise as the terrain's symbol. A cell not in V is shown by its memory. When a level is displayed as "memory only", every cell is shown by its memory.
- Status line: "STATUS hp <hp>/<max hp> level <level> xp <xp> defense <defense> gold <gold> food <satiation> <hunger state> poison <remaining poison turns>". <hunger state> is one of normal, hungry, weak, starving.
- Checkpoint block: "CHECKPOINT turn <t> depth <current depth>", the map of the player's level displayed as the current view, and the status line, in this order.
- Final block: first write "FINAL turn <number of successful commands> depth <current depth>". Then, for depth k = 1, 2, ..., current depth in this order, write "LEVEL <k> explored <explored cells>/<non-rock cells>" and the map of that level. The map is displayed as the current view for the current level, and as memory only for the levels above it. Finally write the status line. <non-rock cells> is the number of cells of that level whose terrain is not rock, and <explored cells> is the number of those cells whose memory is not " ".

== output/stats.txt ==
Write the following lines in this order.
- "result <result>": one of won, died, alive.
- "cause <cause>": amulet if the result is won, the cause of death if it is died (one of killed_by <name>, poison, starvation; example: killed_by orc), and commands_exhausted if it is alive.
- "turns <number of successful commands>"
- "depth <current depth>"
- "level <level>"
- "xp <xp>"
- "hp <hp>/<max hp>"
- "defense <defense>"
- "gold <gold>"
- "food <satiation> <hunger state>"
- "poison <remaining poison turns>"
- "weapon <display name of the wielded weapon>" ("weapon -" if no weapon is wielded)
- "armor <display name of the worn armor>" ("armor -" if no armor is worn)
- "commands <total commands> executed <succeeded> failed <failed> unused <unused>": <total commands> is the number of commands after expansion, and it equals the sum of the numbers of succeeded, failed and unused commands.
- "moves <steps walked>"
- "player_attacks <count> hits <hits> criticals <criticals> damage <total damage> accuracy <dec_1(100 * hits, count)>": values about the player's attacks. <hits> is the sum of the numbers of hits and criticals, <criticals> is the number of criticals, and <total damage> is the sum of the damage of hits and criticals (including the part exceeding the monster's remaining hit points).
- "monster_attacks <count> hits <hits> criticals <criticals> damage <total damage> accuracy <dec_1(100 * hits, count)>": the same values about the monsters' attacks.
- "poison_damage <total poison damage> starvation_damage <total starvation damage>"
- "picked <pickup count> quaffed <quaff count> read <read count> eaten <eat count> wielded <wield count> worn <wear count>"
- "kills <total number of monsters killed>"
- In the order of monsters.txt, for each type killed 1 or more times: "KILL <name> <number killed>"
- For depth d = 1, 2, ..., L in this order (including levels not yet visited): "LEVEL <d> rooms <number of rooms> explored <explored cells>/<non-rock cells> <dec_1(100 * explored cells, non-rock cells)> monsters <monsters killed>/<monsters created> items <items picked up>/<items placed>". <items placed> is the number placed in (6) of section 2, plus 1 for the amulet on the bottom level.
- For each item, in order of pack number: "INVENTORY <number> <display name>". If it is the wielded weapon, append " (wielded)" at the end; if it is the worn armor, append " (worn)" at the end.

[Source files]
Split the source files into files with the following 6 roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow and the largest share of the processing. Reading the 4 input files (settings, type tables, expansion of commands), placing the amulet, monsters and items on each level (the handling of the target cell in (4) of section 2, and (5) and (6)), initializing the player, the game progress of section 6 (including counting succeeded, failed and unused commands), executing all commands of section 7, updating the field of view and the memory, the monster actions of section 9 (sleep, the S and E traits, chasing, wandering), the end-of-turn processing of section 10 (regeneration, poison, hunger, natural regeneration), experience points and levelling up, recording messages, and building and writing the display characters of maps and the lines of the 3 output files.
- minstd: the MINSTD generator, rnd, rolling dice, and select.
- dungeon_gen: the container of a level (terrain, memory, rooms, lists of monsters and items), and (1) splitting into regions, (2) rooms, (3) corridors and (4) choosing the arrival cell and the target cell of section 2, and drawing a room cell.
- entities: monster types and item types (interpreting the input lines), monsters and items (hit points, sleep, energy, bonus, amount), and the symbols, display names and reference names of items.
- combat: interpreting dice tokens, and the attack resolution of section 5 (miss, hit, critical and damage).
- fov: the direction table, the passability check, the movement rule, lines, the field of view, and the distances of section 8 (breadth-first search).

[Constraints]
- config.txt correctly follows the format of [Input files]. 1 <= D <= 2147483646, 1 <= G <= 2147483646, 20 <= W <= 80, 8 <= H <= 30, 1 <= K <= 6, 3 <= Rmin <= Rmax, Rmin + 1 <= M, 2 * M <= W, M <= H, 1 <= L <= 9. a, b, S, A, R, N, F0, Fmax, Fh, Fw, P and HP are all at most 10000, and 0 <= S <= 100, 0 <= A <= 100, R >= 1, N >= 1, HP >= 1, P >= 1, 0 < Fw < Fh < F0 <= Fmax. The values on the xp line and the values on the checkpoints line are each >= 1 and strictly increasing.
- monsters.txt has 1 to 20 lines, and the names of the types are all different. 1 <= min depth <= max depth, 1 <= weight <= 1000, 0 <= to-hit <= 20, 1 <= defense <= 30, 1 <= speed <= 4, 1 <= xp <= 1000, and a and b of every dice are at most 20.
- items.txt has 1 to 30 lines. min depth <= max depth, 1 <= weight <= 1000; <armor value>, <satiation> and n of poison are at most 1000, a and b of every dice are at most 20, and the <to-hit> of a weapon is from 0 to 20. The names on the start line exist in items.txt and are not types whose class is gold, and their number is at most P.
- For every level from depth 1 to L, there are at least one monster candidate and at least one item candidate.
- commands.txt has 1 to 2000 lines, <count> is at most 99, and <number> is at most 99. The number of commands after expansion is at most 9999.
- In the given input, every level has 2 or more rooms, and the repetitions in (5) and (6) of section 2 and in the teleport scroll always terminate.
- The absolute value of every integer appearing during the computation is less than 2^31 (except for the MINSTD products).
