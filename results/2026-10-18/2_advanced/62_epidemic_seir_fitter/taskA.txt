[Overview]
Write a program that fits compartmental models to the daily reported data of an infectious disease that spread in a city, and analyzes the characteristics of the epidemic. A compartmental model divides the population into compartments such as "people not yet infected", "people in the latent period" and "infectious people", and describes the flow of people between the compartments by differential equations.
The central model is the SEIR model with deaths (hereafter the SEIRD model). For comparison, the program also uses the SIR model and an age-structured SEIRD model, which divides the population into age groups and represents how often the age groups contact one another by a contact matrix. Increases and decreases in contact caused by interventions such as stay-at-home restrictions are represented by a multiplier that changes from day to day. The differential equations are solved by the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method with a step size of 1/4 day.
To fit a model, the parameters that minimize the sum of squared differences between the reported cases predicted by the model and the actual reported cases are found in three stages: a grid search, a golden-section search and the Nelder-Mead method. The program then computes residual diagnostics, an analysis of deaths, standard errors of the parameters, a backtest (validation of a retrospective forecast), estimates of the effects of the interventions, a sensitivity analysis, the basic reproduction number and the final size (the fraction of people infected by the end of the epidemic), the generation interval and the effective reproduction number, the next-generation matrix of the age-structured model and its largest eigenvalue, scenario projections of the future epidemic, a plot of observed and fitted values, and more.
The program reads the 4 files in the input/ folder and writes 5 files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output. The only operations used in the computation are the four arithmetic operations, the square root and the exponential function; no random numbers are used.

[Input files]
There are 4 input files: input/cases.csv, input/population.txt, input/interventions.txt and input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There are no spaces at the start or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. In the files other than cases.csv, the values on a line are separated by single spaces. The description below uses the following terms.
- integer: a non-negative decimal integer without extra leading zeros.
- real: a non-negative decimal number consisting of a sequence of one or more digits followed by "." and a sequence of one or more digits (this part may be absent), in this order (e.g. 0.25, 13.5, 300.0, 0.20, 60).
- name: a string of 1 to 12 characters consisting of lowercase English letters only.

* input/cases.csv (reported data)
The first line is day,new_cases,new_deaths. Of the following T lines, line d (d = 1, 2, ..., T) has the form "d,c_d,q_d": the day number d, and the number of new cases c_d and the number of new deaths q_d reported on that day (all integers), separated by commas. The day numbers start at 1 and increase by 1. T is called the number of observed days.

* input/population.txt (population and contact matrix)
G group lines are followed by G contact lines.
- A group line has the form "group NAME N_g f_g": the name of the age group, the population N_g (a positive integer), and the fraction f_g (real) of people in that age group who die when infected. The age groups are numbered g = 1, 2, ..., G in line order, and this order is called the "age-group order".
- The g-th contact line has the form "contact K_g1 K_g2 ... K_gG". K_gh (real) is the average number of contacts per day that one person in age group g has with people in age group h.

* input/interventions.txt (interventions)
Each line has the form "day_k mu_k": the day day_k (integer) on which the intervention starts, and the contact multiplier mu_k (positive real) applied from that day on. The interventions are numbered k = 1, 2, ..., K in line order. day_1 = 1, and day_k increases with k. (The multiplier expresses by what factor person-to-person contact has changed compared with the state without interventions.)

* input/params.txt (settings)
The following lines appear in this order. There are one or more scenario lines and one or more contact_change lines, and lines of the same kind are consecutive.
- sigma sigma: the rate sigma (real, in 1/day) at which people in the latent period become infectious.
- gamma gamma0: the recovery rate gamma0 (real, in 1/day) used at the start of the fitting.
- initial E0 I0: the number of people in the latent period E0 and the number of infectious people I0 at the start of day 1 (reals).
- beta_grid beta_min beta_max n_beta: the range and the number of points of the transmission rate beta in the grid search (real, real, integer).
- rho_grid rho_min rho_max n_rho: the range and the number of points of the reporting fraction rho in the grid search (real, real, integer). The reporting fraction is the fraction of newly infected people who are reported.
- golden n_g R: the number of iterations n_g of the golden-section search and the number of rounds R of the coordinate-wise search (integers).
- simplex delta_beta delta_gamma: the size of the initial simplex of the Nelder-Mead method (reals).
- nelder_mead alpha chi psi omega K_nm: the reflection, expansion, contraction and shrink coefficients alpha, chi, psi, omega (reals) and the number of iterations K_nm (integer) of the Nelder-Mead method.
- holdout H_b: the number of days H_b forecast in the backtest (integer).
- effect_width w_e: the width w_e of the range searched when estimating the effects of the interventions (real).
- outlier z_lim: the threshold z_lim for detecting outliers (real).
- death_lag L_d: the maximum number of days L_d examined in the death lag analysis (integer).
- milestones X_1 X_2 ... X_p: the target values for which the day on which the cumulative number of reported cases reaches them is determined (integers, p >= 1). This order is called the "target-value order".
- generation L: the number of days L over which the generation interval distribution is computed (integer).
- horizon H: the number of days H of the projection (integer).
- threshold theta: the threshold theta for the reported cases in the projection (real).
- scenario NAME m: the name of a projection scenario and the multiplier m (real) applied over the projected period. The order of these lines is called the "scenario order".
- contact_change NAME GROUP phi: the name of a contact change in the age-structured model, the name GROUP of the target age group, and the factor phi (real) by which contacts are multiplied. The order of these lines is called the "contact-change order".

[Processing]
Process as described in steps 0 to 19 below.

* 0. Computation rules
- Evaluate formulas exactly as written. * and / are evaluated before + and -, and operations of the same precedence are evaluated from left to right. Parenthesized parts are evaluated first. For example, beta * m * S * I / N is computed by first obtaining ((beta * m) * S) * I and then dividing it by N. -x is x with its sign changed.
- Unless stated otherwise, a "sum" is accumulated one term at a time from the left, in the order listed. That is, the second term is added to the first term, the third term is added to that result, and so on. A sum with no terms is 0. A "sum over days" adds in increasing order of day number, and a "sum over age groups" adds in age-group order.
- Input integers, sums and products of integers, and counts of things are treated as integers. All other computations (including computations between an integer and a real, and divisions of an integer by an integer) are done in reals (double-precision floating point). floor(x) is the largest integer less than or equal to x.
- sqrt is the square root, exp is the exponential function, |x| is the absolute value of x, and min is the minimum. The square of x is computed as x * x.
- Choosing the largest element: "the largest element of x_1, x_2, ..., x_n" is the index j whose value is the largest; if there are several such indices, it is the smallest one. For day numbers, this is the earliest day.

* 1. Multiplier sequences and population
- Day d is the one-day interval from time d - 1 to time d, where time 0 is the start of day 1.
- The n-day multiplier sequence built from a sequence of intervention lines (day_1, mu_1), (day_2, mu_2), ... is the sequence in which, for d = 1, 2, ..., n, the multiplier of day d is the mu_k of the last line satisfying day_k <= d. Let m_1, m_2, ..., m_T be the T-day multiplier sequence built from the input sequence of intervention lines.
- Let the total population N be N_1 + N_2 + ... + N_G (integer), and let the overall fatality fraction f be (N_1 * f_1 + N_2 * f_2 + ... + N_G * f_G) / N.

* 2. Models
The state of a model is the list of the numbers of people in its compartments. Given a state and a multiplier m, a model gives the rate of change (per day) of the number of people in each compartment. C (C_g in the age-structured model, A in the generation interval model) is a "cumulative inflow", which is reset to 0 at the start of every day as described in step 3.
(a) SEIRD model: the parameters are the transmission rate beta and the recovery rate gamma, and the state is (S, E, I, R, D, C). S, E, I, R and D are the numbers of people not yet infected, people in the latent period, infectious people, recovered people and dead people, and C counts the people who entered I on that day. With F = beta * m * S * I / N, the rates of change are as follows.
  S: -F   E: F - sigma * E   I: sigma * E - gamma * I   R: (1 - f) * gamma * I   D: f * gamma * I   C: sigma * E
  The initial state is (N - E0 - I0, E0, I0, 0, 0, 0). It is written Y0.
(b) SIR model: the parameters are beta and gamma, and the state is (S, I, R, C). With F = beta * m * S * I / N, S: -F   I: F - gamma * I   R: gamma * I   C: F. The initial state is (N - E0 - I0, E0 + I0, 0, 0).
(c) Age-structured SEIRD model: the parameters are beta, gamma and a G-by-G matrix W, and the state is (S_g, E_g, I_g, R_g, D_g, C_g) for each age group (g = 1, 2, ..., G). For age group g, with
  lambda_g = beta * m * (W_g1 * I_1 / N_1 + W_g2 * I_2 / N_2 + ... + W_gG * I_G / N_G), F_g = lambda_g * S_g
  (the parenthesized part is a sum over age groups), S_g: -F_g   E_g: F_g - sigma * E_g   I_g: sigma * E_g - gamma * I_g   R_g: (1 - f_g) * gamma * I_g   D_g: f_g * gamma * I_g   C_g: sigma * E_g.
  With e_g = E0 * N_g / N and i_g = I0 * N_g / N, the initial state is (N_g - e_g - i_g, e_g, i_g, 0, 0, 0) for each age group.
(d) Generation interval model: the parameter is gamma, and the state is (E, I, A). E: -sigma * E   I: sigma * E - gamma * I   A: gamma * I; no multiplier is used. The initial state is (1, 0, 0).

* 3. Numerical integration and simulation
- Let the step size be h = 0.25 (days). One step for a state y and a multiplier m is the following classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method. k1 to k4 are rates of change given by the model; the addition of states and the multiplication of a state by a real are done compartment by compartment.
  k1 = rate of change at state y
  k2 = rate of change at state y + (h / 2) * k1
  k3 = rate of change at state y + (h / 2) * k2
  k4 = rate of change at state y + h * k3
  state after one step = y + (h / 6) * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4)
  For one compartment, if the value of y is a and the values of k1 to k4 are p, q, r, s, then the value of the state at which k2 is computed is a + (h / 2) * p, the value of the state at which k4 is computed is a + h * r, and the value after one step is a + (h / 6) * (((p + 2 * q) + 2 * r) + s). h / 2 and h / 6 are the reals obtained by dividing h by 2 and by 6, respectively. The multiplier m is the same for all four rates.
- "Simulate from state y with the multiplier sequence m'_1, m'_2, ..., m'_n" means: for j = 1, 2, ..., n in order, set all cumulative inflows of the state to 0 and then perform one step 4 times with the multiplier m'_j. The state after the j-th set of 4 steps is called the "state at the end of day j".
- The n-day incidence of the SEIRD model with beta, gamma is the sequence of C at the end of day j (j = 1, 2, ..., n) when simulating from Y0 with an n-day multiplier sequence. When no multiplier sequence is specified, m_1, m_2, ..., m_T (n = T) is used.

* 4. Errors
- The sum of squared errors of a series x_1, x_2, ..., x_n with coefficient s against observations y_1, y_2, ..., y_n is the sum over j of the square of (s * x_j - y_j) (added in the order j = 1, 2, ..., n). When no observations are specified, the reported cases c of the corresponding days are used.
- The RMSE of a sum of squared errors Q and a count n is sqrt(Q / n). When n is not specified, n = T.
- SSE(beta, gamma, rho) is the sum of squared errors of the T-day incidence of the SEIRD model with beta, gamma, with coefficient rho, against observations c_1, c_2, ..., c_T. rho * incidence is the number of reported cases predicted by the model. (Since the incidence does not depend on rho, the incidence may be reused among computations with the same beta and gamma.)

* 5. Grid search
- For i = 0, 1, ..., n_beta - 1 let beta_i = beta_min + (beta_max - beta_min) * i / (n_beta - 1), and for j = 0, 1, ..., n_rho - 1 let rho_j = rho_min + (rho_max - rho_min) * j / (n_rho - 1). Also let the grid spacings be Delta_beta = (beta_max - beta_min) / (n_beta - 1) and Delta_rho = (rho_max - rho_min) / (n_rho - 1).
- Compute SSE(beta_i, gamma0, rho_j) for every pair (i, j). For the pair with the smallest value (if several pairs have the same value, the pair with the smallest i, and among those the pair with the smallest j), let its beta_i and rho_j be beta_grid and rho_grid, and let its value be SSE_grid.

* 6. Golden-section search
- Use the constant phi = 0.6180339887498949. The golden-section search with a function F, an interval [a, b] and a number of iterations n is as follows.
  (1) Let c = b - phi * (b - a), d = a + phi * (b - a), F_c = F(c), F_d = F(d).
  (2) Repeat the following n times. If F_c < F_d, replace b with d, d with c and F_d with F_c, and then compute the new c = b - phi * (b - a) and F_c = F(c). Otherwise, replace a with c, c with d and F_c with F_d, and then compute the new d = a + phi * (b - a) and F_d = F(d). (F is not recomputed for the points that were moved.)
  (3) Finally, if F_c < F_d the result of the search is c, otherwise it is d, and the value of F at that point is the result value.
  The values (a, b, c, d, F_c, F_d) after (1) and after each repetition of (2) are, in order, record 0, record 1, ..., record n.
- Coordinate-wise search: with beta* = beta_grid and rho* = rho_grid, do the following for rounds r = 1, 2, ..., R in order.
  (1) Run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on F(x) = SSE(x, gamma0, rho*) and the interval [beta* - Delta_beta, beta* + Delta_beta], and let its result be the new beta*.
  (2) Run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on F(x) = SSE(beta*, gamma0, x) and the interval [rho* - Delta_rho, rho* + Delta_rho], and let its result be the new rho*.
  Let the final beta* and rho* be beta_golden and rho_hat, and let the result value of the last (2) be SSE_golden. rho_hat is the fitted reporting fraction.

* 7. Nelder-Mead method
- The objective function is F(beta, gamma) = SSE(beta, gamma, rho_hat). The simplex consists of 3 vertices; each vertex has a point (beta, gamma), the value of F at that point, and a serial number. The initial vertices are number 0 at (beta_golden, gamma0), number 1 at (beta_golden + delta_beta, gamma0) and number 2 at (beta_golden, gamma0 + delta_gamma). Vertices created afterwards are numbered 3, 4, ... in the order in which they are created.
- The "vertex order" is increasing order of the value of F (if equal, increasing order of number).
- The point u + t * (v - u) built from points u and v and a real t is computed separately for beta and for gamma.
- Perform K_nm iterations. In each iteration, sort the vertices in vertex order and call them x1, x2, x3 from the first, with F values F1, F2, F3. Let the centroid o be (value of x1 + value of x2) / 2 for each of beta and gamma, compute the reflected point xr = o + alpha * (o - x3) and Fr = F(xr), and do one of the following.
  (i) If Fr < F1: compute the expanded point xe = o + chi * (xr - o) and Fe = F(xe); if Fe < Fr accept xe (operation name expand), otherwise accept xr (operation name reflect).
  (ii) If F1 <= Fr < F2: accept xr (operation name reflect).
  (iii) If F2 <= Fr < F3: compute the outside contraction point xc = o + psi * (xr - o) and Fc = F(xc); if Fc <= Fr accept xc (operation name contract_out), otherwise shrink (operation name shrink).
  (iv) If F3 <= Fr: compute the inside contraction point xc = o + psi * (x3 - o) and Fc = F(xc); if Fc < F3 accept xc (operation name contract_in), otherwise shrink (operation name shrink).
  "Accept" means removing the vertex x3 and adding the accepted point as a vertex with a new number. "Shrink" means keeping x1 and, for x2 and then x3 in this order, replacing each point x with x1 + omega * (x - x1), computing F, and giving it a new number.
  For each iteration, record the operation name and the first vertex in vertex order after that iteration.
- After the K_nm iterations, let the beta and gamma of the first vertex in vertex order be beta_hat and gamma_hat, and let its F value be SSE_nm. The fitted parameters are beta_hat, gamma_hat, rho_hat.

* 8. Fitted model and residuals
- Let the basic reproduction number be R0 = beta_hat / gamma_hat.
- Simulate the SEIRD model with beta_hat, gamma_hat from Y0 with m_1, ..., m_T, and let the state at the end of day d be (S_d, E_d, I_d, R_d, D_d, J_d). J_d is the incidence of day d, P_d = rho_hat * J_d is the fitted reported cases of day d, e_d = c_d - P_d is the residual of day d, and V_d = D_d - D_(d-1) (where D_0 = 0) is the model deaths of day d. Also let S_0 = N - E0 - I0.
- The coefficient of determination is r2 = 1 - SSE_nm / SST, where c_bar = (c_1 + ... + c_T) / T and SST is the sum over days of the square of (c_d - c_bar).
- Residual summary: the residual sum (the sum over days of e_d), the mean absolute error (the sum over days of |e_d| divided by T), the residual with the largest absolute value (the e_d of the day of the largest element of |e_1|, ..., |e_T|, and that day), the number of positive residuals (the number of days with e_d > 0), the number of runs (1 plus the number of d among d = 2, ..., T for which the truth values of "e_d > 0" and "e_(d-1) > 0" differ), and the Durbin-Watson ratio (the sum over d = 2, ..., T of the square of (e_d - e_(d-1)), divided by U, where U is the sum over days of the square of e_d).
- Autocorrelation: for k = 1, 2, ..., 7, the sum over d = 1, ..., T - k of e_d * e_(d+k), divided by U.
- Outliers: let z_d = e_d / sqrt(P_d); the days with |z_d| > z_lim are the outlier days.
- Weekday factors: for k = 1, 2, ..., 7, the set of days d for which the remainder of (d - 1) divided by 7 is k - 1 is weekday k. Compute the observed count O_k of weekday k (the sum of c_d over those days, integer), the fitted count B_k (the sum over days of P_d over those days), and the factor O_k / B_k. Also compute the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series of P_d multiplied by the factor of that day's weekday, with coefficient 1 (this is the RMSE corrected for the bias in reporting by weekday).
- Infection burden: the total infections Z (the sum over days of J_d), the ascertainment rate ((c_1 + ... + c_T) / Z), and the deaths per infection ((q_1 + ... + q_T) / Z).
- Weekly totals: for w = 1, 2, ..., floor((T + 6) / 7), week w is from day 7 * (w - 1) + 1 to day min(7 * w, T); compute over that period the sum of c_d (integer), the sum of P_d, the sum of q_d (integer) and the sum of V_d. For the weeks with w >= 2, also compute the growth ratio (the sum of c_d of that week / the sum of c_d of the previous week).

* 9. Death analysis
- Death summary: the total number of reported deaths (the sum of q_d), the total number of model deaths D_T, the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series V with coefficient 1 against observations q, the death scale kappa = (the sum over days of V_d * q_d) / (the sum over days of the square of V_d), the implied fatality fraction f * kappa, and the crude case fatality rate (q_1 + ... + q_T) / (c_1 + ... + c_T).
- Death lag: for k = 0, 1, ..., L_d, assuming that deaths are reported k days after the reported cases, compute the following values.
  With a_k = the sum over d = 1, ..., T - k of c_d * q_(d+k) (integer), b_k = the sum over d = 1, ..., T - k of c_d * c_d (integer), and y_k = the sum over d = k + 1, ..., T of q_d * q_d (integer), the correlation is r_k = a_k / sqrt(b_k * y_k), where b_k * y_k is computed as a real.
  The case fatality rate at lag k = (q_(k+1) + ... + q_T) / (c_1 + ... + c_(T-k)).
  The k of the largest element of r_0, r_1, ..., r_(L_d) is the best-fitting lag.

* 10. Profiles and standard errors
For the parameters beta, gamma, rho in this order, with the fitted value v (beta_hat, gamma_hat, rho_hat), do the following.
- For k = -5, -4, ..., 5 let x_k = v * (1 + k / 100), and let P(k) be the SSE with only that parameter replaced by x_k (for example, for beta, P(k) = SSE(x_k, gamma_hat, rho_hat)).
- With h = v / 100, curvature = (P(1) - 2 * P(0) + P(-1)) / (h * h), and s^2 = SSE_nm / (T - 3), the standard error = sqrt(2 * s^2 / curvature). The standard error of beta is written se_beta.

* 11. Backtest
Forecast the last H_b days and compare them with the actual values. Let n_t = T - H_b.
- SSE_t(beta, gamma, rho) is the sum of squared errors of the n_t-day incidence of the SEIRD model with beta, gamma (multiplier sequence m_1, ..., m_(n_t)), with coefficient rho, against observations c_1, ..., c_(n_t).
- Run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on F(x) = SSE_t(x, gamma_hat, rho_hat) and the interval [beta_hat - Delta_beta, beta_hat + Delta_beta], and let its result be beta_b. Next, run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on F(x) = SSE_t(beta_b, gamma_hat, x) and the interval [rho_hat - Delta_rho, rho_hat + Delta_rho], and let its result be rho_b and its result value SSE_b.
- Let J'_d be the T-day incidence of the SEIRD model with beta_b, gamma_hat, and let the forecasts for d = n_t + 1, ..., T be g_d = rho_b * J'_d.
- The training RMSE = the RMSE of SSE_b and count n_t; the test RMSE = the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series g_(n_t+1), ..., g_T with coefficient 1, and count H_b; the full-fit test RMSE = the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series P_(n_t+1), ..., P_T with coefficient 1, and count H_b. The error of each day is c_d - g_d.

* 12. Estimating the effects of the interventions
- For k = 2, 3, ..., K in order, do the following. Let F(x) be the sum of squared errors, with coefficient rho_hat, of the incidence of the SEIRD model with beta_hat, gamma_hat computed with the T-day multiplier sequence built from the sequence of intervention lines in which the multiplier of the k-th line is replaced by x (and the multipliers of lines 2 to k - 1 are replaced by the values already estimated). Run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on F and the interval [mu_k - w_e, mu_k + w_e]; its result is the estimated multiplier of intervention k, and its result value is the sum of squared errors at that point.
- The result value of the last estimation is the sum of squared errors after estimation (SSE_nm when K = 1).

* 13. Sensitivity analysis
For the parameters beta, gamma, sigma, rho in this order, and for each of them with the factors 0.9, 1.1 in this order, do the following. Let (beta', gamma', sigma', rho') be (beta_hat, gamma_hat, sigma, rho_hat) with only that parameter multiplied by the factor (the value of the parameter * the factor), and simulate the SEIRD model with beta', gamma' and with sigma replaced by sigma' from Y0 with m_1, ..., m_T. With J''_d its incidence and S''_d the S at the end of day d, compute beta' / gamma', the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series J'' with coefficient rho', the peak day (the day of the largest element of J''_1, ..., J''_T), the peak reported cases (rho' * the J''_d of the peak day), and the infected fraction ((N - S''_T) / N).

* 14. Model comparison
- For each of the following series x: the series of C at the end of each day obtained by simulating the SIR model (beta_hat, gamma_hat) from the initial state of the SIR model with m_1, ..., m_T; the series J of the SEIRD model; and the series of the sum over age groups of C_g at the end of each day of the age-structured model (the fitted age-structured model of step 18), compute the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series x with coefficient rho_hat, the peak day (the day of the largest element of x), the optimal reporting fraction rho_opt = (the sum over days of c_d * x_d) / (the sum over days of the square of x_d), and the RMSE of the sum of squared errors of the series x with coefficient rho_opt.
- Fitting the SIR model: let F(beta) be the sum of squared errors of the series C of the SIR model with beta (recovery rate gamma_hat) with coefficient rho_hat, run a golden-section search with n_g iterations on the interval [beta_min, beta_max], and let its result be beta_sir. Compute beta_sir, the RMSE of the result value, and the day of the largest element of the series C of the SIR model with beta_sir.

* 15. Basic reproduction number and final size
- The herd immunity threshold is 1 - 1 / R0.
- The final size z(R) for a real R is computed as follows. If R <= 1, z(R) = 0. Otherwise, with lo = 1 - 1 / R and hi = 1, repeat the following 20 times: compute mid = (lo + hi) / 2 and g = 1 - mid - exp(-R * mid); if g > 0 replace lo with mid, otherwise replace hi with mid. The final (lo + hi) / 2 is z(R). (z(R) is the positive solution of the equation z = 1 - exp(-R * z), and is the fraction of people eventually infected in an epidemic without interventions.)
- Final size by simulation: simulate the SEIRD model with beta_hat, gamma_hat from Y0 with a 730-day multiplier sequence whose multipliers are all 1, and compute (N - S) / N for the S of the final state.

* 16. Generation interval and effective reproduction number
- Generation interval: simulate the generation interval model with gamma_hat from the initial state (1, 0, 0) for L days (all multipliers are 1), and let a_s be the A at the end of day s (s = 1, 2, ..., L). coverage = the sum of a_s (in order of s), weight w_s = a_s / coverage, mean = the sum over s of (s - 0.5) * w_s, and theoretical value = 1 / sigma + 1 / gamma_hat. (w_s is the fraction of the opportunities for an infected person to infect other people that fall on day s after infection, normalized so that the total up to day L is 1.)
- Model effective reproduction number: for d = 1, 2, ..., T let Rt_d = R0 * m_d * S_(d-1) / N.
- Days on which Rt crosses 1: the days d among d = 2, 3, ..., T for which the truth values of "Rt_d < 1" and "Rt_(d-1) < 1" differ. If Rt_d < 1 the direction is downward (down), otherwise it is upward (up).
- Effective reproduction number from the data: for d = 1, 2, ..., T, let the infection pressure Lambda_d be the sum over s = 1, 2, ..., min(L, d - 1) of w_s * c_(d-s) (it is 0 for d = 1). For the days with d >= L + 7, let R_tilde_d = (c_(d-6) + ... + c_d) / (Lambda_(d-6) + ... + Lambda_d) (the numerator is a sum of integers). It is not computed for the days with d < L + 7.

* 17. Peaks, periods, milestone days, counterfactuals and intervention timing
- Model peak: the day of the largest element of J_1, ..., J_T, and its J_d and P_d. The day of the largest element of I_1, ..., I_T, and its I_d.
- Observed peak: the day of the largest element of c_1, ..., c_T, and its c_d. Also, let the 7-day average of day d be a_bar_d = (c_(max(1, d-6)) + ... + c_d) / min(d, 7) (the numerator is a sum of integers); the day of the largest element of a_bar_1, ..., a_bar_T, and its a_bar_d.
- Intervention periods: for k = 1, 2, ..., K, the period of intervention k is from day day_k to its end day (day_(k+1) - 1 if k < K, T if k = K), and let n_k be the number of days in the period. Compute the Rt of the first day of the period and the Rt of its end day, the sum of c_d (integer), the sum of P_d, the period RMSE (the sqrt of the value obtained by dividing the sum over the days of the period of the square of e_d by n_k), and the bias (the sum over the days of the period of e_d, divided by n_k).
- Milestone days: for each target value X, find the smallest d with c_1 + ... + c_d >= X (observed), and the smallest d for which the value obtained by adding P_1 through P_d in order is X or more (fitted). If there is no such d, it is "not reached".
- Let P_total be the total of the fitted reported cases (the sum over days of P_d). In the counterfactual and intervention timing computations below, for each sequence of intervention lines, compute the incidence J*_d of the SEIRD model with beta_hat, gamma_hat with the T-day multiplier sequence built from that sequence; the reported cases are rho_hat * J*_d, and their total is the sum over days of rho_hat * J*_d.
- Without interventions: with the sequence in which the multiplier of every day is mu_1, compute the total, the peak day (the day of the largest element of the reported cases), the reported cases of that day, and the number averted (total - P_total).
- Without intervention k (k = 2, ..., K): with the sequence obtained by removing the k-th line from the input sequence of intervention lines, compute the total and the number averted (total - P_total).
- Changing the timing of an intervention (for each k = 2, ..., K, with the shifts -7, 7 in this order): with the sequence obtained by replacing the day of the k-th line of the input sequence of intervention lines with day_k + shift, compute the total and the change (total - P_total).

* 18. Age-structured model
- Let the average number of contacts be k_bar = (the sum over age groups of N_g * r_g) / N (r_g is K_g1 + K_g2 + ... + K_gG), and let W_gh = K_gh / k_bar. Below, the age-structured SEIRD model with this W and beta_hat, gamma_hat is called the "fitted age-structured model".
- Largest eigenvalue: the largest eigenvalue and the eigenvector of a G-by-G matrix M are computed by the following power method. Let v_g = 1 / G for all g, and repeat the following 200 times: u_g = M_g1 * v_1 + M_g2 * v_2 + ... + M_gG * v_G (g = 1, ..., G), nu = u_1 + u_2 + ... + u_G, v_g = u_g / nu. The final nu is the largest eigenvalue and the final v is the eigenvector.
- Compute the largest eigenvalue R0_age and the eigenvector of the next-generation matrix M_gh = R0 * W_gh * N_g / N_h. Also compute the ratio R0_age / R0.
- Final size by age group: let z_g = 1 for all g, and 100 times, replace z_g for all g simultaneously (using the z from before the update) with 1 - exp(-(R0 * W_g1 * z_1 + R0 * W_g2 * z_2 + ... + R0 * W_gG * z_G)). The overall final size is (the sum over age groups of N_g * z_g) / N.
- Final size by age group by simulation: simulate the fitted age-structured model from its initial state for 730 days with all multipliers 1, and for the final state let zeta_g = (N_g - S_g) / N_g. The overall value is (the sum over age groups of N_g * zeta_g) / N.
- Simulate the fitted age-structured model from its initial state with m_1, ..., m_T, and write the values of the state at the end of day d as S_g,d and so on (d = 0 is the initial state).
- Age-structured effective reproduction number: for d = 1, 8, 15, ... (the days <= T whose remainder when divided by 7 is 1), compute the largest eigenvalue of the matrix M_gh = m_d * R0 * W_gh * S_g,(d-1) / N_h, and list it together with Rt_d.
- Weekly incidence by age group: for each week w (the same periods as in step 8), the sum over the days of the period of C_g,d for each age group.
- Summary by age group: the infected fraction (N_g - S_g,T) / N_g, the infections Z_g (the sum over days of C_g,d), the share Z_g / (Z_1 + ... + Z_G), the deaths D_g,T, and the peak day (the day of the largest element of C_g,1, ..., C_g,T) and its C_g,d.
- Contact changes: let t be the target age group, and let W'_gh = W_gh * tau_g * tau_h, where tau_x is phi if x = t and 1 otherwise (W_gh * tau_g is computed first and then multiplied by tau_h). Compute the largest eigenvalue of the matrix R0 * W'_gh * N_g / N_h, and, from the result of simulating the age-structured model with W replaced by W' from its initial state with m_1, ..., m_T, the infections (for each day the sum over age groups of C_g, then the sum of these values over days), the deaths (the sum over age groups of D_g at the end of day T), and the infected fraction ((the sum over age groups of (N_g - S_g)) / N, where S_g is the value at the end of day T).

* 19. Projection scenarios
In scenario order, do the following for each scenario (multiplier m).
- From the state at the end of day T of the fitted SEIRD model, simulate H days with all multipliers m; the C at the end of day j is the incidence of day T + j, and rho_hat times it is the reported cases (j = 1, ..., H).
- Compute Rt = R0 * m * S_T / N, the total reported cases (the sum in order of j), the peak day (the day of the largest element of the reported cases), its reported cases, the infected fraction ((N - S) / N, where S is the value of the final state), the final size z(R0 * m), the number averted (the total reported cases of the first scenario - the total reported cases of this scenario), the day falling below the threshold (the first day with reported cases < theta; if there is none, "does not fall below"), and the number of days at or above the threshold (the number of days with reported cases >= theta). The peak day and the day falling below the threshold are expressed by the day number T + j, not by j.
- Band: for each of beta_hat - 2 * se_beta and beta_hat + 2 * se_beta as beta, simulate the SEIRD model with beta, gamma_hat from Y0 with the (T + H)-day multiplier sequence consisting of m_1, ..., m_T followed by H days of the multiplier m, and compute the total of rho_hat times the incidence of days T + 1 to T + H.
- Age-structured projection: from the state at the end of day T of the fitted age-structured model, simulate H days with all multipliers m, and for the final state compute the infected fraction (N_g - S_g) / N_g of each age group and the deaths (the sum over age groups of D_g).

* Common rules
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). Do not put spaces at the end of a line, and do not write empty lines. Except for the leading spaces of curve_plot.txt (described below), do not put spaces at the start of a line either.
- In the formats below, each part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and every other character is written as is. Values are separated by single spaces (by commas, only in the .csv files).
- <value:k> means that a real value is written rounded to k digits after the decimal point, in fixed-point notation with exactly k digits after the decimal point. Exponent notation is not used, and 0 is written when the integer part is 0. A negative number is prefixed with -, but a value that becomes 0 after rounding is written without - (e.g. with k = 1, -0.04 is written 0.0).
- <value> without :k is an integer written in decimal without extra leading zeros (a negative number is prefixed with -), or a name or word. Days are written as day numbers (integers).

* output/curve_plot.txt (plot of observed and fitted values)

- Column x (x = 0, 1, ..., 69) represents the day day_x = 1 + floor(x * (T - 1) / 69) (computed with integers).
- The row y (y = 0, 1, ..., 19; 0 is the bottom) is min(19, floor(20 * c / Y)) for the observed value (c is the c_d of the column's day; computed with integers), and min(19, floor(20 * P / Y)) for the fitted value (P is the P_d of the column's day; 20 * P / Y is computed in reals).
- The character at each row of each column is # if the rows of both the observed value and the fitted value are that row, o if only the row of the observed value is that row, * if only the row of the fitted value is that row, and . if neither is.
The file consists of the following 23 lines. Let w be the number of decimal digits of Y.
  Line 1: plot observed o fitted * both # days 1 <T> max <Y>
  Lines 2 to 21: for y = 19, 18, ..., 0 in this order, a line consisting of floor(Y * y / 20) right-aligned to w characters (padded with leading spaces where it is shorter), one space, |, and the 70 characters of that row for the columns x = 0, 1, ..., 69, in this order.
  Line 22: a line consisting of w spaces, one space, +, and then, for the columns x = 0, 1, ..., 69, + if x is divisible by 10 and - otherwise.
  Line 23: a line consisting of w + 2 spaces followed by a 70-character field. The field is initially all spaces, and for each of x = 0, 10, 20, ..., 60, the decimal representation of day_x is written into the field starting at character x of the field (counting from 0). Finally, the trailing spaces of the line are removed.

[Source files]
Split the source into the following 6 files. The names are language-neutral role names; follow each language's conventions for file names and extensions.
- main: handles the overall flow and contains most of the processing: preparing the multiplier sequence and the total population (step 1), sums of squared errors and RMSE (step 4), driving the grid search and the coordinate-wise search (step 5, second half of step 6), calling the Nelder-Mead method (step 7), residual diagnostics, weekdays and weekly totals of the fitted model (step 8), the death analysis (step 9), profiles and standard errors (step 10), the backtest (step 11), estimating the effects of the interventions (step 12), the sensitivity analysis (step 13), the model comparison (step 14), the bisection and the simulation for the final size (step 15), the generation interval and the effective reproduction number (step 16), peaks, periods, milestone days, counterfactuals and intervention timing (step 17), the analysis of the age-structured model (step 18: the average number of contacts, the next-generation matrix, the power method, the final size by age group, contact changes, etc.), the projection scenarios (step 19), and assembling the lines of all output files.
- data_io: reads the 4 input files and extracts their values, and writes the output files.
- models: holds the rates of change (step 2) of the SEIRD model, the SIR model, the age-structured SEIRD model and the generation interval model.
- integrator: holds one step of the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method and the simulation that computes the state of each day while resetting the cumulative inflows to 0 every day (step 3).
- fitting: holds the golden-section search (including its records) and the Nelder-Mead method (including the vertex order, the four operations and shrinking, and its records) (first half of step 6, step 7).
- report: holds the formatting of reals (rounding, and the handling of - for values that become 0) and the assembly of the plot in curve_plot.txt.

- 1 <= K <= 10. day_1 = 1, day_(k+1) - day_k >= 8 (k = 1, ..., K - 1), day_K + 7 <= T, mu_k > 0.
- 0 < sigma <= 1, 0 < gamma0 <= 1, E0 >= 0, I0 >= 0, E0 + I0 > 0, E0 + I0 < N. 0 < beta_min < beta_max, 0 < rho_min < rho_max <= 1, 2 <= n_beta <= 15, 2 <= n_rho <= 15, beta_grid - Delta_beta > 0, rho_grid - Delta_rho > 0.
- 1 <= n_g <= 40, 1 <= R <= 5, delta_beta > 0, delta_gamma > 0, alpha > 0, chi > 1, 0 < psi < 1, 0 < omega < 1, 1 <= K_nm <= 100.
- 1 <= H_b <= T - 7, 0 < w_e < min(mu_2, ..., mu_K) (when K >= 2), z_lim > 0, 0 <= L_d <= T - 1, 1 <= p <= 5, 1 <= X_1 < X_2 < ... < X_p <= 10000000, 1 <= L <= 60, 1 <= H <= 365, theta > 0.
- There are 1 to 5 scenarios and 1 to 5 contact changes; the scenario names are distinct from one another, and the contact change names are distinct from one another. The group of a contact change is one of the age group names, and phi > 0. The multiplier of a scenario satisfies m > 0.
- The input files correctly follow the format above. There is no need to check for input errors.
- For the given input data, beta > 0, gamma > 0 and rho > 0 at every point at which a search evaluates the objective function. Also, no argument of sqrt and no denominator of a division is zero or negative (P_d > 0, curvature > 0, the sums of Lambda > 0, etc.), and the nu of the power method for the largest eigenvalue is always positive.
- The absolute values of all integers that appear during the computation are less than 2^31 (b_k * y_k of the death lag is computed as a real).
- The given data have T = 150, G = 4, K = 5. Over all computations, the SEIRD model is simulated for about 34000 days in total (about 230 T-day simulations), and in addition the SIR model is simulated for about 2400 days and the age-structured model for about 1500 days.
- For the given input data, in every comparison that affects the output (comparisons of objective function values in the grid search, the golden-section search and the Nelder-Mead method, selection of the largest element, the signs of residuals, outliers, milestone days, threshold decisions, comparisons of Rt with 1, comparisons of R with 1 in the final size, and the determination of plot rows), the difference between the two compared values is at least 10^-7 times the larger of their absolute values (comparisons between integers excepted). The absolute value of g in each step of the final-size bisection is at least 10^-8. Also, for every real value that is output, the difference between the value and a rounding boundary (the value exactly halfway between two adjacent numbers at the number of digits written) is at least max(1, v) * 10^-10, where v is the absolute value of the value. Therefore the results do not change because of differences in the order of additions, fused multiply-add, or differences in the last digit of exp. Nevertheless, perform the computations with the formulas and in the order defined in this statement.
