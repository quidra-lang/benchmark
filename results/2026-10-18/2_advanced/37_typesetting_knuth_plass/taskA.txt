[Overview]
Write a typesetting program that lays out a document in a monospaced font. It reads the three files in the input/ folder (a document with dot directives, a hyphenation dictionary, and cost and layout settings). It divides the document into blocks such as the title, headings, paragraphs, bullet items and tables, splits paragraphs into lines (line breaking), gathers lines into pages (page breaking), adds running heads (header and footer), a table of contents, a list of tables, an index, references and footnotes, and assembles everything into pages.
Paragraph line breaking is done in two ways. One is the Knuth-Plass method (optimal line breaking), which uses dynamic programming to find the breaking that minimizes, over the whole paragraph, the total of a cost defined for each line; the other is the greedy method, which packs as many words as possible into each line. For each of the two methods, the whole document is laid out, including page breaking and the resolution of page references. The results are written to four files in the output/ folder (pages laid out with the Knuth-Plass method, pages laid out with the greedy method, per-paragraph statistics, and a warning log); nothing is printed to standard output.
All computation uses only integers and strings; no floating-point numbers are used.

[Input files]
The input files are input/params.txt, input/hyphenation.txt and input/document.txt. Each consists only of printable ASCII characters (character codes 32 to 126), and every line (including the last) ends with a newline (LF). They contain no tabs and no trailing spaces.

* input/params.txt (cost and layout settings)
It consists of 12 lines, each of the form "name value" (the name, one space, and an integer >= 0). The order of the lines is not fixed. The names are the following 12, and each appears exactly once. How the values are used is defined in [Processing].
- line_penalty: a value added to the cost of every line.
- hyphen_penalty: a value added to the cost of a line that ends in the middle of a word.
- double_hyphen_demerit: when two consecutive lines end in the middle of a word, a value added to the cost of the later line.
- final_hyphen_demerit: when the line just before the last line of a text ends in the middle of a word, a value added to the cost of the last line.
- fitness_demerit: when the tightness classes of adjacent lines differ by 2 or more, a value added to the cost of the later line.
- overfull_penalty: the badness of a line that overflows the line width.
- left_min, right_min: the minimum numbers of characters before and after a split point required to use a dictionary split point.
- orphan_lines, widow_lines: when a block is split at a page boundary, the minimum number of lines left on the earlier page and the minimum number of lines carried to the next page.
- underfull_slack: a warning is given when the slack of a justified line is this value or more.
- underfull_page: a warning is given when the number of empty lines left at the bottom of a page is this number or more.

* input/hyphenation.txt (hyphenation dictionary)
It contains one word per line. Each line consists only of lowercase letters (a to z) and "-", where "-" marks a position at which the word may be split. A "-" never appears at the start or end of a line, and two "-" never appear consecutively. No two lines become the same word when their "-" characters are removed. For example, "al-go-rithm" means that the word algorithm may be split between "al" and "gorithm" and between "algo" and "rithm". A line without "-" means that the word is never split anywhere.
The split points of a dictionary word are expressed as numbers of characters counted from the start. For example, the split points of algorithm are 2 and 4.

* input/document.txt (document)
The document to typeset. Its lines are numbered 1, 2, ... from the top (line numbers). Lines fall into the following three kinds (except that lines inside verbatim blocks and tables, described later, are treated separately).
- Directive line: a line that starts with ".".
- Empty line: a line of length 0.
- Text line: any other line.
For a directive line, the directive name is the part from the second character up to just before the first space (up to the end of the line if there is no space), and the argument is the part from the character after the first space to the end of the line. If there is no space, the argument is the empty string. For example, ".width 64" has directive name width and argument "64".
There are the following 23 directives. How a directive that does not satisfy the "valid argument" condition is handled is defined in step 1 of [Processing]. An "integer" means a string of 1 to 9 characters consisting only of digits (0 to 9), together with its value.

(Setting directives: they apply to blocks started after them)
- .width: the line width. Valid argument: an integer from 20 to 80. Initially 60.
- .indent: the number of characters by which the first line of a paragraph is indented. Valid argument: an integer from 0 to 8. Initially 0.
- .align: the alignment. Valid argument: one of left (left-aligned), right (right-aligned), center (centered), justify (justified). Initially left.
(Directives usable only in the preamble)
- .page: the number of body lines per page (H below). Valid argument: an integer from 10 to 100. Without this directive, H is 40.
- .header, .footer: the header and the footer. Valid argument: a string containing exactly two "|"; the three parts separated by "|" (which may be empty) are, in order, the left, center and right fields. Without the directive, all three fields are empty.
(Other directives)
- .newpage: makes the next block always start on a new page. Valid argument: the empty string.
- .hyphenation: adds words to the dictionary. Valid argument: one or more words separated by single spaces, where each word must have the same form as a dictionary line (only lowercase letters and "-", with no "-" at the start or end and no two consecutive "-").
- .bib: registers a bibliography entry. Valid argument: of the form "key author|year|title". That is, the part before the first space (the key) is a string of one or more characters consisting only of lowercase letters and digits, the part after it splits at "|" into exactly three parts, the first (the author) is non-empty, the second (the year) is exactly 4 digits, and the third (the title) is non-empty. When there are several authors, their surnames are written joined with " and " (one space before and after).
(Block-creating directives)
- .title: the title. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .author: the author name. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .h1, .h2: chapter and section headings. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .item: a bullet item. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .enum: a numbered item. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .quote: a quotation. Valid argument: a non-empty string.
- .term: a term and its description. Valid argument: a string that contains "|" and in which the part before the first "|" (the term) and the part after it (the description) are both non-empty.
- .table: a table. The part of the argument before the first space (the whole argument if there is no space) is the column spec, and the part after it is the caption. Valid argument: one whose column spec is a string of one or more characters consisting only of l, r and c. The caption may be empty.
- .verbatim: a verbatim block. Valid argument: the empty string.
- .toc: a table of contents. Valid argument: the empty string.
- .lot: a list of tables. Valid argument: the empty string.
- .index: an index. Valid argument: the empty string.
- .bibliography: the list of references. Valid argument: the empty string.

The argument of .h1 and .h2 and the caption of .table may end with " {#label}" (a space, "{#", the label, "}"). When the string ends with "}" and contains " {#", the remainder after removing everything from the last " {#" to the end is the heading string (or the caption), and the part between that " {#" and the final "}" is the label. A label is a string of one or more characters consisting of lowercase letters, digits and "-".
Examples: ".h2 The greedy method {#greedy}" is a section heading with string "The greedy method" and label greedy. ".table lrl Penalties {#weights}" is a table with column spec lrl, caption "Penalties" and label weights.

The inner lines of a .verbatim or .table block are the lines from the line after the directive up to just before the line that is exactly ".end". Inner lines of any kind (including empty lines and lines starting with ".") are taken as content as they are, and the ".end" line belongs to no block. If the file ends without a ".end" appearing, the inner lines extend to the last line. In a table, each inner line is one row of the table, and the first one is the column header row. The parts of each row separated by "|" are the cells, and the leading and trailing spaces of each cell are removed.

Text lines, the arguments of .item, .enum and .quote, and the description of .term may contain the following inline commands. An inline command starts with "{" and ends at the next "}". An inline command never spans two lines.
- {ref:label}: replaced by the number of the heading with that label (e.g. 2.1) or the number of the table with that label (e.g. 3).
- {page:label}: replaced by the number of the page on which the heading or table with that label is placed.
- {fn:text}: a footnote. A footnote mark is inserted at this position, and the text is set as a footnote at the bottom of that page.
- {idx:phrase}: an index mark. Nothing is displayed, and the page number of this position is listed in the index.
- {cite:key,key,...}: a citation. Replaced by the list of labels of the cited entries.
"~" (tie) is displayed as a space, but it does not separate words, and no line break is made there.

[Processing]
* Common notation
- "Letter" means only A to Z and a to z, and "digit" means only 0 to 9. To "lowercase" means changing A-Z to a-z, and to "uppercase" means changing a-z to A-Z.
- "Ascending order" of strings is lexicographic order by character code (when one string is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first). Tuples (a, b, ...) are compared lexicographically, element by element from the first.
- Text in "..." is a literal string, and the parts in <...> are replaced by values. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- To "left-justify a string s in width w" means appending spaces after s to make its length w (if s has w or more characters, it is left unchanged).
- The detailed procedure of each stage is defined in this section. How intermediate results are stored and the order of computation are free, but the output must agree with what this section defines.

1. Reading the document
Read document.txt in order from the first line, and build the sequence of blocks, the preamble settings, and the document warnings (pairs of a line number and a message). Each block records the line width, indentation and alignment at the time it is created (the current values of the setting directives), and the line number of the line that started the block. The line width at the time the first block is created is the "page width" (P below) (if there are no blocks at all, P is 60). The part before the first block is created is called the "preamble".
While reading, keep track of the "open body block" (one of a paragraph, bullet item, numbered item, quotation or term, or none) and the "pending page break" (initially none). Handle each line as follows.
- While inner lines of a verbatim block or table are being read, collect them as inner lines, as described in [Input files].
- Empty line: close the open body block (set it to none).
- Text line: if there is an open body block, add the line to that block as a body line. Otherwise, create a new paragraph block, make the line its first body line, and make that block the open body block.
- Directive line: first close the open body block. Then perform the following checks in order; for the first one that applies, record its warning and ignore the directive line.
(1) The directive name is none of the 23 kinds: "unknown directive .<directive name>"
(2) The argument is not a valid argument: "bad argument for .<directive name>"
(3) The directive name is page, header or footer, and a block has already been created: ".<directive name> ignored after the preamble"
(4) The directive name is page, header or footer, and a directive with the same name has already been accepted: "duplicate .<directive name>"
If none applies, the directive is accepted. The setting directives and .page, .header and .footer set their values. .newpage makes a page break pending. .hyphenation remembers, in order, the words of its argument as words to add to the dictionary, and .bib remembers, in order, (line number, key, author, year, title) as a bibliography entry. A block-creating directive creates a block of its kind. For .item, .enum and .quote the argument, and for .term the description, becomes the first body line of the block (its line number is the line number of the directive line), and that block becomes the open body block. The term of .term is recorded as is for display. .verbatim and .table collect inner lines from the next line on.
When a block is created while a page break is pending, give that block a "page break" mark and clear the pending page break.
If the file ends while inner lines of a verbatim block or table are being read, record "unterminated verbatim" or "unterminated table" with the line number of the line that started that block.
The lines added to body blocks (text lines, the arguments of .item, .enum and .quote, and the description of .term) are called "body lines" below. Each body line has a line number.

2. Registering numbers, labels and bibliography entries
Examine the blocks in order from the first and do the following.
- Chapter and section numbers: for each h1, increase the chapter number by 1 and reset the section number to 0; for each h2, increase the section number by 1 (both start at 0). The number of an h1 is "<chapter number>", and the number of an h2 is "<chapter number>.<section number>".
- Chapter start: an h1 that is not the first block gets a page break mark.
- Numbered item numbers: the number of a numbered block is the number of the previous block plus 1 if the previous block is also numbered, otherwise 1.
- Table numbers: tables with a non-empty caption are numbered 1, 2, ... in order from the first. A table with an empty caption has no number.
- Labels: for each block with a label (headings and tables), if the same label is already registered, record "duplicate label <label>" with the line number of the block and treat this block as having no label. Otherwise, register the pair of the label and the block.
- Indentation: a paragraph whose previous block is an h1 or h2 gets indentation 0.
Next, examine the bibliography entries in the order they were remembered; if the same key is already registered, record "duplicate bibliography key <key>" with the line number of the entry and discard the entry. Otherwise, register it.

3. Checking body text, tables and verbatim blocks
Examine the blocks in order from the first.
- Table: examine the inner lines in order (their line numbers are the line numbers in the file); if the number of cells differs from the number C of characters of the column spec, record "table row has <number of cells> cells, expected <C>" with the line number of that row, and make it C cells by padding the missing cells with empty strings or discarding the excess cells from the end. Next, if the length of the longest of all the lines of the table as laid out in step 12 (including the caption line and the rule line) exceeds the line width W of the block by D (D>0), record "table too wide by <D>" with the line number of the block.
- Verbatim block: for each inner line whose length exceeds W by D (D>0), record "verbatim line too long by <D>" with the line number of that line.
- Body block (paragraph, bullet item, numbered item, quotation, term): parse each body line in order, from its start, as follows, turning it into a sequence of strings and inline commands. If there is a "{" in the line, add the part before the first "{" as a string. If there is no "}" after that "{", record "unclosed brace", add the part from that "{" to the end of the line as a string, and end the parsing of this line. If there is a "}", the part of the text between them (the content) before its first ":" is the command name, and the part after that ":" is the argument. If the command name is one of ref, page, fn, idx, cite, there is a ":", and the argument is non-empty, add that command. Otherwise, record "bad command {<content>}" and discard the part from that "{" to the "}". In either case, continue parsing from the character after the "}". When there is no more "{", add the rest as a string.
When a command is added: for ref or page, if the label in the argument is not registered, record "undefined label <label>". For cite, for each key obtained by splitting the argument at ",", in order, give the entry a "cited" mark if the key is registered, otherwise record "undefined citation <key>". The line number of the warnings here is the line number of that body line.
The body of the block is the parse results of its body lines in this order, with a string consisting of one space inserted between one line and the next.
- Footnotes and index: list the arguments of fn commands (footnote texts) in the order they appear in the document and number them from 1. List the arguments of idx commands (index phrases) in order of appearance as well.
Finally, for each registered bibliography entry without the "cited" mark, in the order remembered, record "unused bibliography entry <key>" with the line number of that entry.

4. Ordering the document warnings
Sort the warnings recorded in steps 1 to 3 in ascending order of line number. Warnings with the same line number keep the order in which they were recorded (the order of the stages 1, 2, 3, and within the same stage the order of recording). This sequence is called the "document warnings".

5. Bibliography labels
Give labels to the cited entries by the following procedure.
- Base label: split the author at " and " into a list of surnames. If there is one surname, take its first 3 characters (the whole surname if it is shorter than 3 characters). If there are 2 to 4 surnames, concatenate the first character of each surname in this order. If there are 5 or more, concatenate the first characters of the first three surnames and append "+". Finally, append the last two digits of the year. Examples: "Knuth", 1984 gives Knu84; "Knuth and Plass", 1981 gives KP81.
- Sort the cited entries in ascending order of (base label, author, title, key). This order is called the "bibliography order".
- If two or more entries share the same base label, append a, b, c, ... after their base labels in bibliography order. If only one entry has that base label, nothing is appended. The result is the label of the entry.

6. Expanding the body, and words
Each time layout is performed (layout is repeated, as described in step 14), concatenate the body of each body block with each command replaced by the following string, producing one string (the expanded body).
- ref: if the label is registered, the number of that heading or table. If it is not registered, "??".
- page: if the label is registered, "the page value of that label" defined in step 14. If it is not registered, "??".
- cite: the labels of the registered keys among the keys of the argument, with duplicates removed and sorted in ascending order, followed at the end by "?" if at least one key is not registered, all joined with ", " (a comma and a space) and enclosed in "[" and "]". Examples: "[KP81, Knu84a]" "[Lia83, ?]" "[?]".
- fn: "[<footnote number>]".
- idx: the empty string.
Split the expanded body at spaces; the parts of length 1 or more are, in order, the "words". The words are numbered from 0 (word numbers), and the characters within a word are given positions from 0.
Remember the following two kinds of "marks".
- Footnote mark: the (word number, position) of the "[" character of "[<number>]".
- Index mark: the (word number, position) of the character immediately before the idx command (the character one before the position of that command in the expanded body).
When a word is displayed, each "~" in it is replaced by a space. The length of a word is its number of characters when displayed (the same as its original number of characters).
The line whose pieces (step 8) contain the character of a mark is called "the line containing the mark", and the page on which that line is placed is called "the page of the mark". Each character is contained in the pieces of exactly one line.

7. Split points
The dictionary is built by registering the lines of hyphenation.txt in order, then the words remembered from .hyphenation in order. When the same word is registered again, its split points are replaced by the later ones.
The split points of a word w (the positions inside the word where a line may end) are of the following two kinds. A position k means "after the first k characters of the word", and 1 <= k <= len(w)-1.
- Dictionary split points: the "core" is what remains of w after removing the run of non-letter characters at its start (the prefix) and the run of non-letter characters at its end (the suffix). If the core is non-empty and consists only of letters, and the core lowercased is a word in the dictionary, then for each split point j of that word that satisfies j >= left_min and len(core)-j >= right_min, the position len(prefix)+j is a dictionary split point. When a line ends here, "-" is appended to the end of the line.
- Explicit-hyphen split points: for each "-" character of w whose preceding character and following character are both letters, the position immediately after that "-". When a line ends here, nothing is appended (the "-" remains at the end of the earlier line).
Example: if the dictionary contains al-go-rithm and left_min=2, right_min=3, the split points of the word "algorithm[1]," are 2 and 4 (it can be divided into "algo-" and "rithm[1],"). The split point of the word "well-known" is 5. The word "Mr.~Knuth" has no split points.

8. Lines and costs
The texts of body blocks, footnotes and bibliography entries are set from a sequence of words, the capacity C1 of the first line, and the capacity C2 of the second and later lines (the capacities are defined in step 12). In every text, "~" in a word is displayed as a space, and the length of a word is its original number of characters.
* Break positions
The following are the "break positions"; they are ordered by word, and within the same word by position, and numbered from 1. Number 0 represents the start of the text.
- Each split point of each word (dictionary split points and explicit-hyphen split points).
- The point right after each word (the point right after the last word is the end of the text).
Let m be the total number of break positions. Number m is the end of the text. A break position inside a word (a split point) is called a "mid-word break".
* Lines
For each break position, define an "end position" and a "start position" as a pair (word number, position).
- Number 0 (the start of the text): the start position is (0, 0).
- The split point at position k of word i: both the end position and the start position are (i, k).
- The point right after word i: the end position is (i, len(word i)), and the start position is (i+1, 0).
For 0 <= a < b <= m, "line (a, b)" is the span from the start position (i1, k1) of a to the end position (i2, k2) of b. The "pieces" of the line are: if i1=i2, the single piece of word i1 from the character at position k1 to the character just before position k2; if i1<i2, the part of word i1 from the character at position k1 to its end, the whole of each word from word i1+1 to word i2-1, and the part of word i2 from its start to the character just before position k2, in this order (positions count from 0). If b is a dictionary split point, "-" is appended to the end of the last piece. The number of pieces minus 1 is the number of "word gaps". The length L of the line is the sum of the lengths of the pieces (including an appended "-") plus the number of word gaps.
The capacity of the line is C1 if a=0, otherwise C2. The slack s is the capacity minus L. A line with s >= 0, or a line with b=a+1, is a "feasible line" (a line with b=a+1 consists of a single piece of a single word, so it can be set even if that piece is longer than the capacity). A line with s<0 is called an "overfull line".
* Costs
The class c of line (a, b) is 1 if b=m (the last line of the text); otherwise it is 0 if s <= 1, 1 if 2 <= s <= 3, 2 if 4 <= s <= 6, and 3 if s >= 7 (an overfull line that is not the last line of the text has class 0).
Let c' be the class of the previous line (c'=1 when a=0). The cost of the line is the sum of the following values.
- line_penalty.
- Badness: overfull_penalty if s<0; 0 if s >= 0 and b=m; otherwise s*s.
- hyphen_penalty if b is a mid-word break. In addition, double_hyphen_demerit if a is also a mid-word break.
- final_hyphen_demerit if b=m and a is a mid-word break.
- fitness_demerit if |c-c'| >= 2.
The cost of a breaking (a sequence 0 < b1 < b2 < ... < bn = m in which each line (b(i-1), bi) is a feasible line, with b0 = 0) is the sum of the costs of its lines, each computed using the class of the previous line as c'.

9. The Knuth-Plass method
For a break position b (1 <= b <= m) and a class c, let T(b, c) be the minimum cost of "a breaking from the start up to b whose last line ends at b and has class c", and record, for that minimum, the previous break position a and the class c' of the line ending at a. As the initial state, only T(0, 1)=0 is defined.
Process b in order from 1 to m. For each b, for every pair of an a (0 <= a < b) for which line (a, b) is a feasible line and a c' for which T(a, c') is defined, compute the class c of line (a, b) and the candidate value T(a, c') + (the cost of line (a, b) with c' as the previous class). T(b, c) is the smallest value among the candidates whose class is c. If several candidates have equal values, choose the one with the smaller a, and then the one with the smaller c'. If there is no candidate whose class is c, T(b, c) is undefined.
Since the class of the last line is always 1, T(m, 1) is the minimum cost of the paragraph. The sequence of break positions obtained by following the recorded (a, c') from (m, 1) back to the start is the Knuth-Plass breaking.

10. The greedy method
Starting with a=0, repeat the following while a<m. If line (a, a+1) is an overfull line, let b=a+1. Otherwise, let b be the largest b for which line (a, b) has slack 0 or more. Add b to the breaking, and set a=b.
The cost of the breaking obtained this way is computed by the rules of step 8 (it is never smaller than the cost of the Knuth-Plass method).

11. Displaying lines
Each line of a breaking is turned into a display string according to its capacity and the alignment. Let t (of length L) be the string of the line's pieces joined with single spaces, and let s be the slack.
- If s <= 0, it is t.
- If the alignment is justify, the line is not the last line of the text, and the number of word gaps g is 1 or more, the line is justified. Let q be the quotient and r the remainder of s divided by g; the pieces are joined with q+2 spaces in the k-th word gap from the left (k counts from 0) if k<r, and with q+1 spaces otherwise.
- If the alignment is right, it is s spaces followed by t.
- If the alignment is center, it is floor(s/2) spaces followed by t.
- Otherwise (left, and the last line of justify or a line with g=0), it is t.
The display string preceded by the line prefix defined in step 12 is the line placed on the page.

12. Laying out blocks
Each block is turned into a sequence of lines to be placed on pages. W is the line width of the block and I is its indentation. For line breaking, whether in body blocks, bibliography entries or footnotes, step 9 is used when laying out with the Knuth-Plass method and step 10 when laying out with the greedy method.
* Body blocks
Break the sequence of words of the expanded body with the following capacities and line prefixes, and display the lines with the alignment of the block.
- Paragraph: C1=W-I, C2=W. The prefix of the first line is I spaces, and the prefix of the second and later lines is the empty string.
- Bullet item: C1=C2=W-2. The prefix of the first line is "- ", and that of the second and later lines is 2 spaces.
- Numbered item: C1=C2=W-4. The prefix of the first line is the 4 characters made by right-justifying the number in width 2 (one space before it if it has 1 digit) and appending ". " (e.g. " 1. " "12. "), and that of the second and later lines is 4 spaces.
- Quotation: C1=C2=W-8. The prefix of every line is 4 spaces.
- Term: C1=C2=W-12. If the length of the term is 10 or less, the prefix of the first line is the term left-justified in width 12, and that of the second and later lines is 12 spaces. If the length of the term is 11 or more, the prefix of every line is 12 spaces, and in addition one line consisting of the term alone is placed before those lines.
The "broken lines" of a body block, a bibliography entry or a footnote are the lines obtained by line breaking (step 9 or 10) (the line consisting of the term alone is not included).
* Title and author
For the title, t is the argument uppercased; for the author, t is the argument itself. The block is one line consisting of floor((W-len(t))/2) spaces followed by t.
* Headings
The first line is "<number> <string uppercased>" for h1 and "<number> <string>" for h2, and the second line is a run of "=" (h1) or "-" (h2) of the same length as the first line.
* Tables
The width of each column is the maximum length of the cells of that column over all rows. Each cell is padded to the column width according to the corresponding character of the column spec: for l, spaces are added after it; for r, spaces are added before it. For c, with d the number of missing characters, floor(d/2) spaces are added before it and the rest after it. A table row is the cells of a row joined with " | ", with trailing spaces removed. After the column header row, a rule row is placed, consisting of, for each column, as many "-" as the column width, joined with "-+-". If the caption is non-empty, a line "Table <table number>. <caption>" is placed first.
Example: a table with column spec lr, rows "Item | Cost" "hyphen | 50", caption "Costs" and number 1 consists of the 4 lines "Table 1. Costs" "Item   | Cost" "-------+-----" "hyphen |   50".
* Verbatim blocks
The inner lines are placed as they are (lines longer than W also stay as they are).
* Table of contents
It consists of the heading lines "CONTENTS" "========" and the entry lines. There is one entry for each h1 and h2 of the document, in document order. With the left string x being "<number> <string>" for an h1 and "  <number> <string>" (with two leading spaces) for an h2, and with n the display number (step 14) of the page of that heading, the entry is "<x> <W-len(x)-len(n)-2 copies of "."> <n>" (its length is exactly W).
* List of tables
It consists of the heading lines "TABLES" "======" and the entry lines, one for each numbered table in document order. An entry has the same form as in the table of contents, with x being "Table <table number>. <caption>" and n the display number of the page of that table.
* Index
It consists of the heading lines "INDEX" "=====" and the following entry lines. Split the phrase of each index mark into the part before the first "|" (the entry part) and the part after it (the cross-reference), and split the entry part into the part before the first "!" (the parent entry) and the part after it (the child entry) (if there is no "|" or "!", there is no cross-reference or child entry, respectively). A mark belongs to its child entry if it has one, otherwise to its parent entry. A mark with a cross-reference adds that cross-reference, and a mark without a cross-reference adds the body number (step 14) of the page of the mark, to the entry it belongs to.
Sort the parent entries in ascending order of (the entry lowercased, the entry as is), and after the line of each parent entry place its child entries in the same order. If the first character of a parent entry is a letter, its "group" is that character uppercased; otherwise its group is "Symbols". When the group differs from that of the previous parent entry (or it is the first parent entry), a line consisting only of the group name is placed before the line of the parent entry.
An entry line starts with the entry name for a parent entry, or with two spaces and the entry name for a child entry, followed, in this order, by ", " and the page list if there are pages, and by ", see " and the cross-references, with duplicates removed, in ascending order and joined with ", ", if there are cross-references. The page list is formed by sorting the page numbers in ascending order with duplicates removed, collapsing each run of 3 or more consecutive numbers (e.g. 3, 4, 5, 6) into "<first>-<last>" (e.g. 3-6), leaving the other numbers as they are, and joining the items with ", ". Examples: the numbers 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 give "3-6, 8", and the numbers 9, 10 give "9, 10".
* References
It consists of the heading lines "REFERENCES" "==========" and a sequence of lines for each cited entry (in bibliography order). Let w be the maximum length of the labels of the cited entries plus 3. The sequence of the parts of length 1 or more (the sequence of words) obtained by splitting each entry's text "<author>. <year>. <title>" at spaces is broken with C1=C2=W-w and alignment left. The prefix of the first line is "[<label>]" left-justified in width w, and that of the second and later lines is w spaces.
* Footnotes
With the prefix p="[<number>] ", the sequence of the parts of length 1 or more (the sequence of words) obtained by splitting the footnote text at spaces is broken with C1=C2=P-len(p) and alignment left. The prefix of the first line is p, and that of the second and later lines is len(p) spaces. Footnotes contain no inline commands.

13. Page breaking
* The sequence of layout blocks
Replace each block by the following "layout blocks" and arrange them in document order. A layout block has a sequence of lines, a type (heading type, solid type or flow type), and the presence or absence of a "list" mark.
- Title, author, h1, h2: one heading-type layout block.
- Table: one solid-type layout block.
- Paragraph, quotation, verbatim block: one flow-type layout block.
- Bullet item, numbered item, term: one flow-type layout block with the list mark.
- Table of contents, list of tables, index: two blocks, a heading-type layout block consisting of the 2 heading lines and a flow-type layout block consisting of the entry lines.
- References: a heading-type layout block consisting of the 2 heading lines, and flow-type layout blocks with the list mark, each consisting of the lines of one entry (as many as there are entries).
If a block has a page break mark, the first layout block made from it gets the page break mark.
* Page breaks and height
A "gap" is placed between adjacent layout blocks. The number of lines of a gap is 0 if both blocks have the list mark, otherwise 1. The positions at which a page boundary may be placed (page breaks) are as follows.
- A gap: if the later layout block has a page break mark, the gap is a "forced break" and is always an allowed break position. Otherwise, it is an allowed break position only when the earlier layout block is not of heading type.
- Within a flow-type layout block (of n lines), between line k and line k+1 (1 <= k < n): an allowed break position only when k >= orphan_lines and n-k >= widow_lines.
- The end of the document.
A gap at which a page break is made is placed on neither the previous page nor the next page. The content of a page is the lines from just after the previous break (or the start of the document) up to the next break, together with the empty lines of the gaps between them. If the content lines of a page include lines containing footnote marks, those footnotes are "the footnotes of the page", and the height of the page is the number of content lines (including the empty lines of gaps) plus the sum of 1 (the separator line) and the number of lines of the footnotes of the page. If there are no footnotes, the height is the number of content lines.
* Choosing pages
Determine the pages one by one from the start of the document, as follows. Examine the allowed break positions after the start of the current page in order, and, within the range up to the first forced break (the end of the document if there is none), break at the last position at which the height is H or less. If the break is a forced break or the end of the document, the page is a page that "ends by force".
* The page body
The page body is exactly H lines. From the top, the content lines of the page are placed (gaps as empty lines). If the page has footnotes, the separator line "----------" (10 "-") and the footnote lines of the page (in increasing order of footnote number) are placed in this order so that the last footnote line is the last line of the body. The remaining lines are empty lines.

14. Page numbers, blank pages and reference iteration
* Blank pages and front matter
Arrange the pages determined in step 13 in order and give them "serial numbers" 1, 2, ... from the first. While doing so, if a page whose first line is the first line of an h1 would get an even serial number, insert one blank page just before it (blank pages also get serial numbers).
If the document has an h1, F is the serial number of the page containing the first line of the first h1, minus 1. Otherwise, F=0. The pages whose serial number is F or less are called the "front matter".
- Display number of a page: for a front-matter page, its serial number written in lowercase Roman numerals; for any other page, "serial number - F" written in decimal. In Roman numerals, 1 to 9 are i, ii, iii, iv, v, vi, vii, viii, ix respectively, and a number n of 10 or more is x repeated as many times as the quotient of n divided by 10, followed by the Roman numeral of the remainder if the remainder is not 0 (e.g. 10 is x, 14 is xiv, 20 is xx).
- Body number: the "serial number - F" (an integer) of the page. It is used for the page numbers listed in the index.
- The "page value" of a label: the display number of the page containing the first line of the heading, or the first line of the table.
- Likewise, the "page" of a heading or table is the page containing its first line.
* Reference iteration
The values of page references (page commands) are determined by the layout result, and the layout result changes with those values, so layout is repeated as follows. First, set the value of every registered label to "??". Lay out the whole document using the current values (steps 6 to 14, up to blank pages and front matter), and compute the page value of each label. If, for every label, the computed value equals the current value, stop; otherwise, take the computed values as the current values and lay out again. The result of the last layout is used for the output. Since the number of entry lines of the table of contents, the list of tables and the index does not depend on page numbers, their page numbers are computed from the result of the last layout and do not affect page breaking.
This iteration is performed separately for layout with the Knuth-Plass method and for layout with the greedy method.

15. Running heads (header and footer)
For each page (except blank pages), compute the values of the following four fields.
- {page}: the display number of the page.
- {pages}: the serial number of the last page minus F.
- {chapter}: when the headings whose first lines are placed on pages up to this page (including this page) are followed in document order, "<number> <string>" of the last h1. The empty string if there is none.
- {section}: following them in the same way, "<number> <string>" of the last h2 that comes after the last h1. The empty string if there is none.
In the strings of the three fields of the header and of the footer, replace {page}, {pages}, {chapter} and {section} with their values. On pages with an even serial number, swap the left field and the right field. On a line of P spaces, place the left field from the start, the center field starting at character floor((P-len(center field))/2) (counted from 0), and the right field aligned with the end; the result with trailing spaces removed is the running-head line.
On a page whose first line is the first line of an h1 (the first page of a chapter), the header line is an empty line. On a blank page, both the header line and the footer line are empty lines, and the body consists entirely of empty lines.

16. Layout warnings
For each of the Knuth-Plass method and the greedy method, produce the following warnings from the result of the last layout. The message of each warning is preceded by "kp: " or "greedy: ".
(1) Line warnings: in document order, examine the broken lines of body blocks and bibliography entries (bibliography entries at the position of .bibliography, in bibliography order), followed by the broken lines of footnotes (in order of footnote number), each starting from its first line. An overfull line gives "<name> line <k>: overfull by <-s>"; a line of a body block whose alignment is justify that is not the last line of the text, is not overfull, and has a slack of underfull_slack or more gives "<name> line <k>: underfull, slack <s>". k is the number of the broken line counted from 1. The name is "paragraph <body block number>" for a body block (body blocks are numbered from 1 in document order), "reference <label>" for a bibliography entry, and "footnote <number>" for a footnote.
(2) Page warnings: for each page that is neither a blank page nor a page that ends by force, in order of serial number, if the value E obtained by subtracting the height of the page from H is underfull_page or more, "page <serial number>: underfull, <E> empty lines".
(3) Summary: "<number of pages (including blank pages)> pages, <number of broken lines of body blocks that end at a mid-word break> hyphenated lines".

[Output files]
Write the following four files in the output/ folder. Every file consists only of ASCII characters, and every line (including the last) ends with a newline (LF). By the rules of [Processing] (table rows and running-head lines have their trailing spaces removed) and by [Constraints], no line ever ends with a space.

* output/pages.txt (pages laid out with the Knuth-Plass method)
Write each page, in order of serial number, as the following H+5 lines.
- "--- page <serial number> ---"
- The header line
- An empty line
- The page body (H lines)
- An empty line
- The footer line

* output/greedy_pages.txt (pages laid out with the greedy method)
Write the result laid out with the greedy method in the same format as pages.txt.

* output/stats.csv (per-paragraph statistics)
The first line is "paragraph,lines_kp,cost_kp,lines_greedy,cost_greedy". Then write one line for each body block (paragraph, bullet item, numbered item, quotation, term) in document order: "<body block number>,<number of broken lines with the Knuth-Plass method>,<cost of that breaking>,<number of broken lines with the greedy method>,<cost of that breaking>". The values are those of the last layout of each method. The last line is "total," followed by the sums of the four columns separated by ",".

* output/log.txt (warning log)
Write the document warnings in order in the form "line <line number>: <message>", then the layout warnings of the Knuth-Plass method (in the order (1), (2), (3) of step 16), then the layout warnings of the greedy method (in the same order).

[Source files]
Split the source into the following 6 files (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: handles the overall flow and the core of the processing, and holds the most logic. It reads the input files, registers numbers, labels and bibliography entries, parses the inline commands of body lines and checks tables and verbatim blocks (document warnings), assigns bibliography labels, expands bodies, splits them into words and locates the marks, builds the sequence of break positions and measures lines (pieces, length, slack), lays out each block (body blocks, title, headings, tables, table of contents, list of tables, index, references, footnotes), builds the sequence of layout blocks, assigns lines to pages and numbers blank pages and front matter, computes the running-head field values, performs the reference iteration, collects the layout warnings and statistics, and writes the four files.
- document_parser: classifies document lines, checks directive arguments, handles the preamble, assembles blocks (recording settings, collecting body lines and inner lines, pending page breaks), and handles the warnings that arise while reading.
- hyphenation: registers the dictionary (file lines and .hyphenation words), extracts the core of a word and its dictionary split points, and finds explicit-hyphen split points.
- line_breaker: handles line classes and costs, the dynamic programming of the Knuth-Plass method (including tie handling), the greedy method, and computing the cost of a breaking.
- page_builder: builds the gaps and allowed break positions from the sequence of layout blocks, and chooses the pages, taking footnote heights into account.
- renderer: handles line display (alignment and justification), formatting of table rows, assembling running-head lines (field replacement, swapping left and right, placement), and assembling the output text of pages.

[Constraints]
- document.txt has at most 2000 lines, and hyphenation.txt has at most 1000 lines. The values in params.txt are at most 100000, and left_min, right_min, orphan_lines and widow_lines are at least 1.
- A footnote text contains at least one character that is not a space. An index phrase contains at most one "|" and at most one "!", and each part separated by them is non-empty and neither starts nor ends with a space. The argument of a citation consists of non-empty keys separated only by ",".
- The character immediately before an index mark (idx command) is a non-space character in the expanded body (a mark never appears at the start of a body). Index marks appear only in blocks after the first h1.
- The first heading is an h1. The heading string with the label removed is non-empty, and heading strings, table captions and .term terms do not end with a space. Heading strings and .term terms do not contain "{", "}" or "~" (no inline command or "~" processing is done for titles, authors, headings, terms, table cells and captions, or verbatim block lines).
- In body lines and .bib arguments, the characters immediately before and after a "~" are both non-space characters ("~" never appears at the start or end of a word). Each surname obtained by splitting a .bib author at " and " is non-empty and does not start with a space.
- The labels attached to headings and table captions are all strings of one or more characters consisting of lowercase letters, digits and "-". The caption (with the label removed) of a table with a label is non-empty.
- Every body block has at least one word in its expanded body. Every table and verbatim block has at least one inner line. .toc is used only when there is at least one heading, .lot only when there is at least one numbered table, .index only when there is at least one index mark, and .bibliography only when there is at least one cited entry. At most 26 cited entries share the same base label. Numbered item numbers are at most 99.
- The lengths of the title, the author and the first line of each heading are at most W. In the entry lines of the table of contents and the list of tables, the number of "." is at least 2.
- In the running-head lines, on every page, each field fits in a line of length P, and there is at least one space between any two non-empty fields (fields never overlap).
- When choosing pages, for every page there is at least one allowed break position at which the height is H or less. The reference iteration finishes within 10 layouts for both methods.
- Integer values, including intermediate ones, have absolute values below 2^31.
- Errors other than the conditions stated here (unknown directives, wrong arguments, .page outside the preamble, duplicate labels or keys, undefined references, bad inline commands, wrong cell counts, tables or verbatim lines exceeding the width, unterminated verbatim blocks, overfull words, and so on) may appear in the input, and all of them are handled according to the rules in [Processing].
