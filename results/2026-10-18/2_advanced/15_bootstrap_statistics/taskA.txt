[Overview]
Write a program that performs statistical inference based on resampling (the bootstrap, the jackknife and permutation tests) on measurements obtained in an experiment. The measurements are positive numbers with at most 2 decimal places, and there are two kinds of samples: independent samples divided into several treatment groups (hereafter, groups), and a paired sample in which the same subjects were measured before and after a treatment.
For each group, the program computes descriptive statistics such as the mean, median, trimmed mean, standard deviation and quartiles, and draws a character-based box plot and a stem-and-leaf display. With the ordinary bootstrap and the balanced bootstrap using MINSTD random numbers, it computes the standard error, bias, percentile interval and basic interval of each statistic, and for the specified pairs of groups it makes the same estimates for the difference of means, the ratio of medians, the Hodges-Lehmann estimator and Cohen's d. In addition, it computes the jackknife bias and standard error, BCa intervals, and diagnostics of the influence of observations by jackknife-after-bootstrap.
As tests, it performs permutation tests for each pair of groups (4 kinds of statistics), a bootstrap simulation of power, exact permutation tests using a small number of leading observations of each group, a studentized bootstrap test, multiple comparisons by Holm's method, a sign-flip test for the paired sample, one-way analysis of variance and the Kruskal-Wallis test together with their permutation tests, and pairwise comparisons of groups using the maximum statistic. Decisions in the permutation tests are made, as far as possible, by exact comparisons between integers.
The results are written to 5 files: descriptive statistics, bootstrap summary, jackknife, tests and histograms.
The program reads the files in the input/ folder and writes files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output.

[Input files]
Every input file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a newline (LF). There are no empty lines and no spaces at the start or end of a line.
A measurement is written as an integer part of one or more digits, optionally followed by "." and a fractional part of 1 or 2 digits (there is no sign or exponent; for example: 12, 12.5, 12.05). Hereafter, the integer equal to 100 times a measurement is called the "value in hundredths" of that measurement. For example, 12 is 1200, 12.5 is 1250 and 12.05 is 1205. Inside the program, every measurement is handled as its value in hundredths (an integer).

(1) input/samples.csv: independent samples
The first line is the header group,value. Each following line consists of a group name and a measurement separated by "," (there are no spaces). A group name starts with a lowercase English letter and consists of 1 to 16 characters, each a lowercase English letter, a digit or "_".
Lines of different groups may appear intermixed. The groups are given group numbers 1, 2, ..., G in the order of the lines where their group names first appear. The observations of each group are given observation numbers 1, 2, ... in the order in which the lines of that group appear. Let n_g be the number of observations of group g, and h_g,1, h_g,2, ..., h_g,n_g their values in hundredths in order of observation number. This sequence is called the sample of group g. Let N be the total number of observations over all groups.

(2) input/paired.csv: paired sample
The first line is the header subject,before,after. Each following line consists of the subject number, the measurement before the treatment and the measurement after the treatment, separated by ",". The subject numbers are 1, 2, ..., M in order, starting from the second line. Let b_i and a_i be the values in hundredths of subject i before and after the treatment, and let the difference be d_i = a_i - b_i (an integer). b, a and d denote the sequences of b_i, a_i and d_i, respectively, in order of subject number.

(3) input/params.txt: settings
Each line is a name followed by one or more values, separated by single spaces. The lines appear in the following order. The symbol after the name is the symbol that denotes that value in the description below. Values other than group names are integers written in decimal.
- seed s: the random seed.
- bootstrap B: the number of bootstrap replicates.
- permutations R: the number of permutations in the permutation tests.
- confidence L: the confidence level (percent). It is one of 90, 95 and 99.
- pilot q: the number of leading observations of each group used in the exact permutation test.
- power E Q: the number E of power simulations, and the number Q of permutations performed within one simulation.
- bins K: the number of histogram bins.
- compare X Y: a pair of groups to compare. X and Y are the group names of different groups. There are one or more of these lines, and they continue to the end of the file. In line order they are pair 1, pair 2, ..., pair P. The statistics of a pair are computed with group X first and group Y second (the difference of means is X - Y and the ratio of medians is X / Y).
For example, line 1 is a line like "seed 12345", line 6 is a line like "power 20 19" and line 8 is a line like "compare dose_low control".

[Processing]
Process as described in Steps 1 to 20 below.

# Notation and arithmetic conventions
- All real numbers are computed in double-precision floating point (IEEE 754 64-bit format). The real constants written below (such as 0.2316419) are treated as the double-precision values nearest to the written decimal numbers.
- Integer computations are exact (by [Constraints], the absolute values, including intermediate values, are less than 2^53). An expression written "a / b" for integers a and b and used as a real value denotes the value obtained by converting the integers a and b each to a real and then dividing once. In an operation between an integer and a real, the integer is converted to a real before computing.
- Expressions are evaluated parentheses first, multiplication and division before addition and subtraction, and operations of equal precedence from left to right (for example, A * B * C computes A * B and then multiplies by C). x^2 means x * x, x^3 means x * x * x and x^4 means x * x * x * x; they are computed before the surrounding multiplications and divisions. sqrt is the square root, exp is the exponential function and ln is the natural logarithm.
- A sum written with Sum is computed by starting from 0 and adding the terms one at a time in the specified order (in increasing order of index if no order is specified).
- floor(x) is the largest integer <= x (every value to which floor is applied in this task is 0 or more). For positive integers u and v, ceil(u / v) means floor((u + v - 1) / v). |x| is the absolute value, and min and max are the minimum and the maximum.
- All numbers count from 1.
- When a sequence of reals or integers is sorted in increasing order, its "r-th smallest value" is the r-th value counted from the smallest (the order among equal values does not affect the result).
- Number list: the numbers in increasing order, separated by single spaces. If there are no numbers, write none.

# Step 1. Random numbers
Random numbers are generated with MINSTD. The generator has an integer state x whose initial value is s. "Draw a random number" means updating x to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647 and taking the updated x as the value. "Draw a random number that is at least 0 and less than m" means drawing one random number and taking as the value the remainder x mod m of its value x divided by m.
To "shuffle" a list c_1, c_2, ..., c_l of length l means, for i = l, l - 1, ..., 2 in this order, drawing a random number u that is at least 0 and less than i and swapping c_i and c_(u+1) (when u + 1 = i nothing changes).
There is only one generator in the whole program, and its state is never reinitialized. Random numbers are used in the following order, and only in the processes listed here.
  (1) the ordinary bootstrap of Step 5 (in the order group 1, group 2, ..., group G)
  (2) the balanced bootstrap of Step 6 (in the order group 1, group 2, ..., group G)
  (3) the bootstrap of the paired sample in Step 7
  (4) for pair 1, pair 2, ..., pair P in this order, the permutations of Step 12 and the power simulation of Step 13 for that pair (in this order)
  (5) the sign-flip test of Step 18
  (6) the permutations of the analysis of variance in Step 20

# Step 2. Sample statistics
A sequence y_1, y_2, ..., y_m (m >= 2) of values in hundredths is called a sample. With z_1 <= z_2 <= ... <= z_m the sequence y sorted in increasing order, the sample statistics are defined as follows.
- Sum: S(y) = y_1 + y_2 + ... + y_m (an integer).
- Mean: mean(y) = S(y) / (100 * m).
- Twice the median: Md(y) = z_c + z_e (an integer), where c = floor((m + 1) / 2) and e = floor((m + 2) / 2) (if m is odd, c = e). The median is med(y) = Md(y) / 200.
- Trimmed mean: with the trim count t = floor(m / 10), trim(y) = (z_(t+1) + z_(t+2) + ... + z_(m-t)) / (100 * (m - 2 * t)).
- Standard deviation: with the deviations e_j = y_j / 100 - mean(y) (j = 1, ..., m), sd(y) = sqrt((Sum e_j^2) / (m - 1)). Also, m2(y) = (Sum e_j^2) / m, m3(y) = (Sum e_j^3) / m and m4(y) = (Sum e_j^4) / m. Every Sum here is taken over j = 1, ..., m in this order (in the order of y, not of the sorted sequence z).
- Median absolute deviation: with D twice the median (the same definition as Md above) of the sequence of the m integers |2 * y_j - Md(y)| (j = 1, ..., m), mad(y) = D / 400.
- Quantile: for an integer p from 0 to 100, with u = (m - 1) * p, j = floor(u / 100) and f = u - 100 * j, the numerator of the quantile is Qn(y, p) = 100 * z_(j+1) + f * (z_(j+2) - z_(j+1)) (an integer; when f = 0 it is 100 * z_(j+1)). The p-th percentile is Qn(y, p) / 10000. Note that Qn(y, 50) = 50 * Md(y).
- Winsorized sequence: with t the trim count, wins(y) is the sequence obtained by replacing each y_j with min(max(y_j, z_(t+1)), z_(m-t)) (keeping the order of y).

# Step 3. Two-sample statistics and doubled ranks
For a sample x = (x_1, ..., x_k) and a sample y = (y_1, ..., y_m), the following statistics are defined. Let s_x = sd(x) and s_y = sd(y).
- Difference of means: mdiff(x, y) = (m * S(x) - k * S(y)) / (100 * k * m).
- Ratio of medians: mratio(x, y) = Md(x) / Md(y).
- Hodges-Lehmann estimator: with D twice the median of the sequence of the k * m integers x_i - y_j (i = 1, ..., k, j = 1, ..., m), hl(x, y) = D / 200.
- Pooled standard deviation: sp(x, y) = sqrt(((k - 1) * s_x * s_x + (m - 1) * s_y * s_y) / (k + m - 2)).
- Cohen's d: cd(x, y) = mdiff(x, y) / sp(x, y).
- Correlation coefficient (when k = m): with e_j = x_j / 100 - mean(x) and f_j = y_j / 100 - mean(y), corr(x, y) = (Sum e_j * f_j) / sqrt((Sum e_j^2) * (Sum f_j^2)).
Also, for a sequence of integers v_1, ..., v_k, the doubled rank of v_i is rho_i = 2 * (the number of elements smaller than v_i) + (the number of elements equal to v_i (including v_i itself)) + 1. This is twice the rank obtained when equal values are given their average rank, and it is always an integer.

# Step 4. Descriptive statistics
For a series y (of length m) and two values in hundredths lo < hi that give the display range, the following are computed.
- Outliers: let Qn1 = Qn(y, 25) and Qn3 = Qn(y, 75); an observation y_j with 200 * y_j < 5 * Qn1 - 3 * Qn3 or 200 * y_j > 5 * Qn3 - 3 * Qn1 is an outlier (these are the values smaller than the first quartile minus 1.5 times the interquartile range, or larger than the third quartile plus 1.5 times the interquartile range; the comparisons are done exactly in integers). The minimum w1 and the maximum w2 of the observations that are not outliers are the whisker ends.
- Box plot: let Delta = 100 * (hi - lo), and for an integer V in units of 1/10000 (for example V = 100 * y_j for an observation y_j, and V = Qn(y, p) for a quantile), let its position be pos(V) = floor((120 * (V - 100 * lo) + Delta) / (2 * Delta)) (an integer computation; it is the position obtained by mapping V / 10000 onto 0 to 60 over the display range and rounding half up). All positions of a band of 61 characters at positions 0 to 60 are first set to ".", and then characters are placed in the following order (at the same position, the character placed later remains).
  (a) "-" at every position from pos(100 * w1) to pos(100 * w2)
  (b) "=" at every position from pos(Qn1) to pos(Qn3)
  (c) "|" at pos(Qn(y, 50))
  (d) "o" at pos(100 * y_j) for each outlier y_j
- Stem-and-leaf display: with ymin the minimum of y and ymax the maximum, for each stem sigma = floor(ymin / 100), floor(ymin / 100) + 1, ..., floor(ymax / 100), compute the string of the leaves floor((y_j mod 100) / 10) (the first decimal digit of the measurement) of the observations y_j with floor(y_j / 100) = sigma, arranged in increasing order of y_j (if there is no such observation, it is the empty string).
The series and display ranges are as follows. The stem-and-leaf display is made only for groups.
- The sample of group g: lo and hi are the minimum and the maximum of all observations of all groups.
- b and a of the paired sample: lo and hi are the minimum and the maximum of the 2 * M values of b and a together.
- d of the paired sample: lo and hi are the minimum and the maximum of d.

# Step 5. Ordinary bootstrap
For each group g, the sample of the r-th replicate is made for r = 1, 2, ..., B in this order. In the r-th replicate, for k = 1, 2, ..., n_g in this order, a random number u that is at least 0 and less than n_g is drawn, and the observation with observation number u + 1 becomes the k-th value of the sample. The mean, med, trim and sd of this sample are the values theta*_r of the r-th replicate of the statistics mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd, respectively. The estimates theta_hat of these statistics are the mean, med, trim and sd of the sample of group g (the original sample).
For each pair (group X and group Y), from the sample x*_r of the r-th replicate of group X and the sample y*_r of the r-th replicate of group Y, the values of the r-th replicate of the statistics mean_diff, median_ratio, hodges_lehmann and cohen_d are mdiff(x*_r, y*_r), mratio(x*_r, y*_r), hl(x*_r, y*_r) and cd(x*_r, y*_r), respectively. The estimates are the same statistics computed on the original samples of group X and group Y.

# Step 6. Balanced bootstrap
For each group g, with n = n_g, a list c of length B * n is made with c_((r-1)*n+k) = k (r = 1, ..., B, k = 1, ..., n) (the list 1, 2, ..., n repeated B times), and it is shuffled as in Step 1. The sample of the r-th replicate is the sequence of the observations with observation numbers c_((r-1)*n+1), c_((r-1)*n+2), ..., c_(r*n), in this order. The values of the r-th replicate of the statistics balanced_mean, balanced_median, balanced_trimmed_mean and balanced_sd are the mean, med, trim and sd of this sample. The estimates are the same as the estimates of mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd of Step 5, respectively.

# Step 7. Bootstrap of the paired sample
For r = 1, 2, ..., B in this order: for k = 1, 2, ..., M in this order, a random number u that is at least 0 and less than M is drawn and subject u + 1 is chosen as the k-th; let d*, b* and a* be the sequences of d_i, b_i and a_i, respectively, of the chosen subjects in the order in which they were chosen. The value of the r-th replicate of the difference of means is mean(d*), and the value of the r-th replicate of the correlation coefficient is corr(b*, a*). The estimates are mean(d) and corr(b, a).

# Step 8. Bootstrap summary
The "mean and standard error" of a sequence of reals v_1, v_2, ..., v_k (k >= 2) are avg = (Sum v_i) / k and se = sqrt((Sum (v_i - avg)^2) / (k - 1)) (the sums are in index order).
For an estimate theta_hat and B replicate values theta*_1, ..., theta*_B, the following values are computed.
- avg and se: the mean and standard error of theta*_1, ..., theta*_B (in replicate order). The bias is bias = avg - theta_hat.
- Percentile interval: let r_lo = floor((B + 1) * (100 - L) / 200) (an integer computation) and r_hi = B + 1 - r_lo; with theta*_1, ..., theta*_B sorted in increasing order, the r_lo-th smallest value is the lower limit theta_lo and the r_hi-th smallest value is the upper limit theta_hi.
- Basic interval: the lower limit is 2 * theta_hat - theta_hi and the upper limit is 2 * theta_hat - theta_lo.
This summary is computed for every statistic of Steps 5 to 7 and for eta_squared of Step 19.

# Step 9. Histograms
For each of the statistics mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd of groups 1 to G (in group order, and within each group in this order of statistics), followed by the statistics mean_diff, median_ratio, hodges_lehmann and cohen_d of pairs 1 to P (in pair order, and within each pair in this order of statistics), a histogram of the B replicate values is made (no histograms are made for the statistics of the balanced bootstrap or of the paired sample).
- Let lo be the minimum and hi the maximum of the replicate values (by [Constraints], lo < hi), and let the bin width be width = (hi - lo) / K.
- The bin number of a value v is beta(v) = min(floor(K * (v - lo) / (hi - lo)), K - 1) + 1 (an integer from 1 to K; K * (v - lo) is computed first and then divided by hi - lo).
- The count c_j of bin j (j = 1, ..., K) is the number of replicates with beta(theta*_r) = j. Let top be the maximum count; the bar length of bin j is ceil(40 * c_j / top) (0 if c_j = 0). The lower edge of bin j is lo + (hi - lo) * (j - 1) / K.
- Marks: mark L is put on the bin of the lower limit theta_lo of the percentile interval of Step 8, mark E on the bin beta(theta_hat) of the estimate theta_hat if theta_hat is at least lo and at most hi, and mark H on the bin of the upper limit theta_hi. When a bin has more than one mark, they are listed in the order L, E, H.
- Convergence: for j = 1, 2, 3, 4, let nu_j = floor(j * B / 4), and compute the standard error (the mean and standard error of Step 8) of the first nu_j replicate values theta*_1, ..., theta*_nu_j.

# Step 10. Jackknife and BCa intervals
- Group jackknife: for each group g (n = n_g) and each of the statistics mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd, for i = 1, ..., n in this order, let theta_(i) be the value of the statistic on the sample of length n - 1 obtained by removing the observation with observation number i from the original sample (the remaining observations in order of observation number). Let theta_(.) = (Sum theta_(i)) / n, bias = (n - 1) * (theta_(.) - theta_hat), and standard error = sqrt((n - 1) * (Sum (theta_(i) - theta_(.))^2) / n) (theta_hat is the estimate of Step 5).
- Pair jackknife: for each pair (group X and group Y, samples x and y) and each of the statistics mean_diff, median_ratio, hodges_lehmann and cohen_d, let theta_(X,i) (i = 1, ..., n_X) be the value of the statistic on x_(i) (the sample x with the observation with observation number i removed) and y, and let theta_(Y,j) (j = 1, ..., n_Y) be the value of the statistic on x and y_(j) (the sample y with the observation with observation number j removed). For each part pi of X and Y, let theta_(pi.) be the mean of the values of that part (the Sum divided by the number n_pi of values of the part), D_pi = (n_pi - 1) * (theta_(pi.) - theta_hat) and V_pi = (n_pi - 1) * (Sum (theta_(pi,i) - theta_(pi.))^2) / n_pi; then bias = D_X + D_Y and standard error = sqrt(V_X + V_Y) (D_X + D_Y and V_X + V_Y are computed by adding to 0 the X part and then the Y part).
- Standard normal functions: for a real x, if x >= 0, let eta = 1 / (1 + 0.2316419 * x), psi = eta * (0.319381530 + eta * (-0.356563782 + eta * (1.781477937 + eta * (-1.821255978 + eta * 1.330274429)))) and Phi(x) = 1 - 0.3989422804014327 * exp(-(x * x) / 2) * psi; if x < 0, Phi(x) = 1 - Phi(-x). For 0 < p < 1, if p > 0.5, PhiInv(p) = -PhiInv(1 - p); if p <= 0.5, eta = sqrt(-2 * ln(p)) and PhiInv(p) = -(eta - (2.515517 + eta * (0.802853 + eta * 0.010328)) / (1 + eta * (1.432788 + eta * (0.189269 + eta * 0.001308)))). Also let zL = PhiInv((100 - L) / 200).
- BCa interval: for each group g and each of the statistics mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd, let cnt be the number of replicate values of Step 5 with theta*_r < theta_hat, and z0 = PhiInv(cnt / B). From the group jackknife values, let U_i = theta_(.) - theta_(i) (i = 1, ..., n), V2 = Sum U_i^2 and V3 = Sum U_i^3, and let the acceleration be acc = V3 / (6 * V2 * sqrt(V2)). For a real z, let alpha(z) = Phi(z0 + (z0 + z) / (1 - acc * (z0 + z))), and let k1 = floor((B + 1) * alpha(zL)) and k2 = B + 1 - floor((B + 1) * (1 - alpha(-zL))). Each of k1 and k2 is replaced by 1 if it is less than 1, and by B if it is greater than B. With the replicate values of Step 5 sorted in increasing order, the k1-th smallest value and the k2-th smallest value are the lower and upper limits of the BCa interval.

# Step 11. Jackknife-after-bootstrap
For each group g (n = n_g), the replicates of the ordinary bootstrap of Step 5 are used. For observation number i (i = 1, ..., n), let C_i be the set of replicates r in which observation number i was never chosen when the sample of the r-th replicate was made, and m_i the number of these replicates.
- For each of the statistics mean, median, trimmed_mean and sd, let SE_(i) be the standard error (the mean and standard error of Step 8) of the sequence of the replicate values theta*_r of C_i in replicate order; let SE_(.) = (Sum SE_(i)) / n and the standard error of SE = sqrt((n - 1) * (Sum (SE_(i) - SE_(.))^2) / n).
- Influence values: from the group jackknife values (Step 10) of the statistic mean, the influence value of observation number i is l_i = (n - 1) * (theta_(.) - theta_(i)).
- Most influential observations: when the observation numbers are sorted in decreasing order of the integer |n * h_g,i - S(the sample of group g)| (ties in increasing order of observation number), the first 5 are the observations with influence ranks 1 to 5. For each of these observations i, the values theta*_r - theta_hat (for the statistic mean), that is, the replicate values of C_i minus the estimate, are sorted in increasing order, and the ceil(5 * m_i / 100)-th, ceil(50 * m_i / 100)-th and ceil(95 * m_i / 100)-th smallest values are q05, q50 and q95, respectively.

# Step 12. Two-group permutation test
For each pair (group X and group Y), let k and m be the numbers of observations of group X and group Y, respectively. The list of length k + m consisting of the observations of group X (in order of observation number) followed by the observations of group Y (in order of observation number) is the combined list, and each observation is given the doubled rank (Step 3) of its value in hundredths within the combined list. When the observations of the combined list are divided into group X' consisting of the first k and group Y' consisting of the remaining m (each taken as a sample in list order), the following 4 integer statistics are computed.
  T1 = m * S(X') - k * S(Y')
  T2 = Md(X') - Md(Y')
  T3 = (sum of the doubled ranks of the observations of X') - k * (k + m + 1)
  T4 = m * A(X') - k * A(Y'), where A(z) = Sum |2 * z_j - Md(z)| (an integer) for a sample z.
The values for the list in its original order are the observed values T1, ..., T4. In each of the R permutations, the combined list restored to its original order is shuffled as in Step 1 (each observation moves together with its doubled rank), and T1*, ..., T4* are computed on that list. For each statistic T_nu, let extreme be the number of permutations with |T_nu*| >= |T_nu| and greater the number of permutations with T_nu* >= T_nu; the two-sided p-value is (extreme + 1) / (R + 1) and the one-sided p-value is (greater + 1) / (R + 1).
T1, T2, T3 and T4 are called the statistics mean, median, ranksum and absdev, respectively. The reported values are as follows.
- mean: difference = mdiff(sample of group X, sample of group Y).
- median: difference = T2 / 200.
- ranksum: w = (sum of the doubled ranks of the observations of group X) / 2 and u = ((sum of the doubled ranks of the observations of group X) - k * (k + 1)) / 2 (for the original list).
- absdev: difference = T4 / (200 * k * m) (for the original list; this is the difference of the mean absolute deviations from the median).

# Step 13. Power simulation
For each pair (group X and group Y, sizes k and m), after the permutations of Step 12 for that pair, the following simulation is performed E times.
  (a) For j = 1, ..., k in this order, a random number u that is at least 0 and less than k is drawn, and the sample x' whose j-th value is the observation with observation number u + 1 of group X is made. Then, for j = 1, ..., m in this order, a random number u that is at least 0 and less than m is drawn, and the sample y' whose j-th value is the observation with observation number u + 1 of group Y is made.
  (b) The list of x' followed by y' is the combined list, and T = |m * S(x') - k * S(y')|. In each of the Q permutations, the combined list restored to its original order is shuffled, and T* = |m * S1 - k * S2| is computed from the sum S1 of the first k values and the sum S2 of the remaining m values. Let ct be the number of permutations with T* >= T.
  (c) If 100 * (ct + 1) <= (100 - L) * (Q + 1), this simulation is a rejection.
With rej the number of rejections, the power pw = rej / E and its standard error sqrt(pw * (1 - pw) / E) are computed.

# Step 14. Exact permutation test on the leading observations
For each pair (group X and group Y), a list of length 2 * q is made of the observations with observation numbers 1 to q of group X followed by the observations with observation numbers 1 to q of group Y, and each observation is given its doubled rank among these 2 * q values. All ways of choosing q positions from positions 1 to 2 * q (C = (2q)! / (q! * q!) ways in total) are enumerated in lexicographic order of the tuples of the chosen positions in increasing order (the first is the choice of positions 1, 2, ..., q). For each choice, with the observations at the chosen positions as group X' and the observations at the remaining positions as group Y', T1* = q * S(X') - q * S(Y') and T3* = (sum of the doubled ranks of the observations of X') - q * (2 * q + 1) are computed. The values for the first choice are the observed values T1 and T3.
For each statistic, let extreme be the number of choices with |T_nu*| >= |T_nu| and greater the number of choices with T_nu* >= T_nu (the first choice is also counted); the two-sided p-value is extreme / C and the one-sided p-value is greater / C. T1 is called the statistic mean and T3 the statistic ranksum. The reported values are the difference of mean = mdiff(x_q, y_q) (x_q and y_q are the samples of the first q observations of group X and group Y), and w = (sum of the doubled ranks of the first q of X) / 2 and u = ((sum of the doubled ranks of the first q of X) - q * (q + 1)) / 2 of ranksum.
- Critical value: let v_1 > v_2 > ... be the distinct values of |T1*| in decreasing order, and let F_j be the number of choices with |T1*| >= v_j. For the largest j satisfying 100 * F_j <= (100 - L) * C, report the critical value v_j / (100 * q * q) and the attained significance level F_j / C. If 100 * F_1 > (100 - L) * C, there is no critical value.

# Step 15. Studentized bootstrap and effect sizes
For each pair (group X and group Y, sizes k and m, samples x and y), let theta_hat = mdiff(x, y), s_X = sd(x), s_Y = sd(y), v_X = s_X * s_X / k, v_Y = s_Y * s_Y / m, se0 = sqrt(v_X + v_Y) and t = theta_hat / se0.
- For the r-th replicate of Step 5, with theta*_r the replicate value of mean_diff and s*_X and s*_Y the values of the r-th replicate of sd of group X and group Y, let t*_r = (theta*_r - theta_hat) / sqrt(s*_X * s*_X / k + s*_Y * s*_Y / m). Let extreme be the number of replicates with |t*_r| >= |t|; the p-value is (extreme + 1) / (B + 1). With t*_1, ..., t*_B sorted in increasing order, let tau_lo be the r_lo-th smallest value and tau_hi the r_hi-th smallest value (r_lo and r_hi are those of Step 8); the lower limit of the interval is theta_hat - tau_hi * se0 and the upper limit is theta_hat - tau_lo * se0.
- Welch degrees of freedom: (v_X + v_Y) * (v_X + v_Y) / (v_X * v_X / (k - 1) + v_Y * v_Y / (m - 1)).
- Effect sizes: the pooled standard deviation sp(x, y), Cohen's d = cd(x, y), Hedges' g = d * (1 - 3 / (4 * (k + m - 2) - 1)), and the common language effect size cles = (2 * gt + eq) / (2 * k * m), where gt is the number of pairs (i, j) with x_i > y_j and eq is the number of pairs (i, j) with x_i = y_j.

# Step 16. Holm's method
For each of the 4 statistics mean, median, ranksum and absdev of Step 12, let e_1, ..., e_P be the two-sided extreme values of the pairs. The pairs are sorted in increasing order of e (ties in increasing order of pair number), and for their ranks i = 1, ..., P, let adj_i = max(adj_(i-1), min(R + 1, (P - i + 1) * (e + 1))) (e is the extreme of the pair at rank i, and adj_0 = 0; integer computation). The adjusted p-value is adj_i / (R + 1); the decision is reject if 100 * adj_i <= (100 - L) * (R + 1), and retain otherwise.

# Step 17. Summary and correlation of the paired sample
- Count the subjects with d_i > 0, with d_i < 0 and with d_i = 0.
- Compute mean(b), mean(a), mean(d), sd(d), t = mean(d) / (sd(d) / sqrt(M)) and dz = mean(d) / sd(d).
- For each of the difference of means and the correlation coefficient of Step 7, compute the summary of Step 8.
- Interval by Fisher's z transformation: let r = corr(b, a), z = 0.5 * ln((1 + r) / (1 - r)) and hw = -zL / sqrt(M - 3); the lower limit is tanh(z - hw) and the upper limit is tanh(z + hw), where tanh(w) = (exp(2 * w) - 1) / (exp(2 * w) + 1).

# Step 18. Sign-flip test
Let rho_i be the doubled rank of |d_i| of each subject within the sequence of the values |d_i| of the subjects with d_i != 0, and let the signed doubled rank be phi_i = rho_i if d_i > 0, phi_i = -rho_i if d_i < 0, and phi_i = 0 if d_i = 0. The observed values are T = Sum d_i and Sg = Sum phi_i (integers).
In each of the R sign flips, for i = 1, ..., M in this order, one random number is drawn, and eps_i = -1 if its value is odd and eps_i = 1 if it is even (a random number is drawn even for a subject with d_i = 0); then T* = Sum eps_i * d_i and Sg* = Sum eps_i * phi_i are computed. For each of T and Sg, as in Step 12, compute extreme (the number with |T*| >= |T|) and greater (the number with T* >= T), the two-sided p-value (extreme + 1) / (R + 1) and the one-sided p-value (greater + 1) / (R + 1). Also let w_plus = (sum of rho_i of the subjects with d_i > 0) / 2.

# Step 19. Analysis of variance and the Kruskal-Wallis test
For the samples y_1, ..., y_G of the G groups (sizes n_1, ..., n_G, total N), compute the following values.
- The grand mean xbar = (sum of the observations of all groups) / (100 * N) and the group means xbar_g = mean(y_g).
- The between-group sum of squares SSB = Sum_g n_g * (xbar_g - xbar)^2 (sum in group order).
- The within-group sum of squares SSW = Sum_g W_g (sum in group order), where W_g = Sum_j (y_g,j / 100 - xbar_g)^2 (sum in order of the observations of group g).
- F = SSB / (G - 1) / (SSW / (N - G)), eta_squared = SSB / (SSB + SSW), MSW = SSW / (N - G), omega_squared = (SSB - (G - 1) * MSW) / (SSB + SSW + MSW).
- Bootstrap of eta_squared: for r = 1, ..., B, with the samples of the r-th replicate of Step 5 of groups 1 to G as the samples of the G groups, SSB and SSW are computed with the same formulas, and SSB / (SSB + SSW) is the value of the r-th replicate. The estimate is eta_squared.
- Welch's analysis of variance: let s_g = sd(y_g), w_g = n_g / (s_g * s_g), Wt = Sum_g w_g, xtilde = (Sum_g w_g * xbar_g) / Wt, Aw = (Sum_g w_g * (xbar_g - xtilde)^2) / (G - 1) and lambda = Sum_g ((1 - w_g / Wt)^2 / (n_g - 1)) (every sum in group order); then F_W = Aw / (1 + 2 * (G - 2) * lambda / (G * G - 1)) and the degrees of freedom df2 = (G * G - 1) / (3 * lambda).
- Kruskal-Wallis test: each observation is given its doubled rank among the N observations of all groups, and Rk_g (an integer) is the sum of the doubled ranks of the observations of group g. Let Tk = Sum_g Rk_g * Rk_g / (4 * n_g) (each term is the integer Rk_g * Rk_g divided by the integer 4 * n_g, and the sum is in group order), let Z (an integer) be the total of t * t * t - t over every value that occurs t times (t >= 2), and let the correction be Cr = 1 - Z / (N * N * N - N) and H = (12 * Tk / (N * (N + 1)) - 3 * (N + 1)) / Cr. Also, the number of distinct values that occur 2 or more times is the number of tie groups.

# Step 20. Permutation tests of the analysis of variance and pairwise comparisons
The list of length N of the observations of groups 1 to G, in order of group number and within each group in order of observation number, is the combined list, and each observation is given its doubled rank of Step 19. In each of the R permutations, the combined list restored to its original order is shuffled (each observation moves together with its doubled rank), and it is divided from the start into n_1, n_2, ..., n_G observations, which are the samples of groups 1' to G'.
- F* and H*: F* and H* are computed on the samples of groups 1' to G' with the same formulas as in Step 19 (Cr keeps its value from Step 19). Let f_extreme be the number of permutations with F* >= F - 0.000000001 * F and h_extreme the number of permutations with H* >= H - 0.000000001 * H; the p-values are (f_extreme + 1) / (R + 1) and (h_extreme + 1) / (R + 1), respectively.
- Pairwise comparisons: the pairs of groups (i, j) (i < j) are taken in the order (1, 2), (1, 3), ..., (1, G), (2, 3), ..., (G - 1, G); with S_g the sum of the sample of group g, let delta_ij = |n_j * S_i - n_i * S_j| / (n_i * n_j) (a rational number). With delta_ij the value for the original groups and delta*_ij the value for the groups after a permutation, let extreme be the number of permutations with delta*_ij >= delta_ij, and maxt the number of permutations in which the maximum of delta* over all pairs is at least delta_ij. The p-value is (extreme + 1) / (R + 1) and the adjusted p-value is (maxt + 1) / (R + 1). Comparisons of delta are done exactly, for example by comparing integers after clearing the denominators. The reported difference is mdiff(sample of group i, sample of group j).

[Output files]
The program writes the following 5 files to the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No empty lines are output. In the formats below, each part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and everything else is output as is. The words in a line are separated by single spaces, with no spaces at the start or end of a line (bootstrap.csv is separated by ",").
- Integers (counts, numbers, and values defined as integers such as n_g) are written in decimal without extra zeros. Every integer that is output is 0 or more.
- Reals are written rounded to 4 decimal places (for example: 9.3793, 0.0500, -0.3100). However, if the resulting string is -0.0000, write 0.0000. When a value in hundredths h is written as a measurement, h / 100 is written as a real (for example: 4.0000). By [Constraints], the exact value of a real that is output is never exactly halfway between two rounding candidates.
- <p-value> is written as follows: the fraction u / v representing the p-value (u and v integers with u >= 0 and v > 0) is reduced to lowest terms u' / v' and written in the form "<u'>/<v'>", followed by one space and u' / v' written as a real (for example: 21/200 0.1050, 1/1 1.0000).

[Source files]
There are the following 6 source files. The names are language-neutral role names; extensions and capitalization follow the conventions of each language.
- main: handles the overall flow and contains most of the processing: calling each module, the descriptive statistics block (outliers, whisker ends, box plot band, stem-and-leaf display; Step 4), running the ordinary, balanced and paired-sample bootstraps and the replicate values of the pair statistics (Steps 5 to 7), summaries and intervals (Step 8), histograms (Step 9), the group and pair jackknife and BCa intervals (Step 10), jackknife-after-bootstrap (Step 11), the two-group permutation test, the power simulation, the exact permutation test and critical value, the studentized bootstrap and effect sizes, and Holm's method (Steps 12 to 16), the summary of the paired sample, the correlation and the sign-flip test (Steps 17 and 18), the values of the analysis of variance, the bootstrap of eta_squared, Welch's analysis of variance, and the permutation tests and pairwise comparisons (Steps 19 and 20), and assembling the contents of the 5 files.
- dataset: reading the 3 input files (converting measurements to values in hundredths, assigning group numbers and observation numbers, the setting values).
- minstd: the MINSTD random number generator (drawing a random number, a random number at least 0 and less than m, shuffling a list; Step 1).
- resampling: sample statistics and two-sample statistics (mean, Md, med, trim, sd, m2 to m4, mad, Qn, wins, mdiff, mratio, hl, sp, cd and corr of Steps 2 and 3), making the index sequences of the bootstraps (Steps 5 and 6), samples with one observation removed, the mean and standard error (Step 8), and Phi and PhiInv (Step 10).
- tests: building blocks of the permutation tests (lexicographic enumeration of position choices, reduced fractions, integer contrasts of the form of T1, doubled ranks, counts of equal values, groups obtained by dividing a list, and computing SSB, SSW, F and H of Step 19).
- report: formatting of reals and p-values, number lists, histogram bin numbers and bars, and writing lists of lines to files.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= s <= 2147483646, 199 <= B <= 2000, 99 <= R <= 5000, 5 <= q <= 8, 10 <= E <= 200, 19 <= Q <= 199, 5 <= K <= 40, 1 <= P <= 6.
- 2 <= G <= 4, 10 <= n_g <= 80, 10 <= M <= 60. Measurements are from 1 to 999.99 (values in hundredths are from 100 to 99999).
- The minimum and the maximum of the observations of all groups differ, the minimum and the maximum of the values of b and a together differ, and the minimum and the maximum of d differ.
- For the sample of every group and the sample of every bootstrap replicate (and also every jackknife sample with one observation removed), sd is positive and Md is positive. The mean of each group and of d is not 0, and m2 is positive.
- In every replicate of Step 7, Sum e_j^2 and Sum f_j^2 are positive and |corr(b, a)| < 1.
- In every histogram of Step 9, lo < hi. For the BCa intervals of Step 10, 1 <= cnt <= B - 1, V2 > 0, 1 - acc * (z0 + zL) > 0 and 1 - acc * (z0 - zL) > 0. For every observation in Step 11, m_i >= 2.
- In Step 19, SSW > 0, F > 0, H > 0 and lambda > 0.
- The absolute values of intermediate values of integer computations are less than 2^53 (including, for example, the values after clearing the denominators in the comparisons of delta in Step 20).
- In the given data, every comparison of reals used for a decision (the floor and range comparisons that determine the histogram bin numbers and beta(theta_hat), the floor of (B + 1) * alpha in the BCa intervals, the comparisons theta*_r < theta_hat, the comparisons |t*_r| >= |t|, the comparisons of F* and H*, etc.) is far enough from its boundary that its result is not changed by small errors of double-precision computation (differences in the order of summation, or relative errors of about 10^-9). However, when mathematically equal values are obtained by the same computation, such as values each obtained by a single division of integers, they may be equal, and in that case they are judged as equal. F* and F, and H* and H, are at least 10^-8 apart in relative terms unless they are mathematically equal.
- The exact value of every real that is output is at least 10^-9 away from a rounding halfway point.
