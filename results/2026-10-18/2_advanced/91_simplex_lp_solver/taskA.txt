[Overview]
Write a solver program for linear programming problems (LP) and integer programming problems. All computation is done exactly in rational numbers (fractions); floating-point numbers are not used.
The program reads input/models.lp, which contains one or more models (optimization problems) written in a text format, and input/params.txt, which sets the level of detail of the log and the node limit of branch and bound. For each model it checks the syntax and the content, then converts the model to standard form by expressing the variable bounds through variable substitutions and constraints, and solves it with the two-phase simplex method under Bland's rule. It also detects the case where no solution satisfies the constraints (infeasible) and the case where the objective function value can be improved without limit (unbounded), and computes the evidence for each (Farkas multipliers, an unbounded direction). For a model for which an optimal solution is obtained, it computes, in addition to the variable values and the constraint values, the dual prices (shadow prices), the reduced costs, and the sensitivity analysis of the objective coefficients and of the constraint right-hand sides (the ranges over which the current optimal basis does not change). Models containing integer variables are solved by depth-first branch and bound.
The results are written to five files in the output/ folder (the simplex log, the solutions, the sensitivity analysis, the branch-and-bound tree, and a summary table). Nothing is printed to standard output. No random numbers are used.

[Input files]
There are two input files, input/models.lp and input/params.txt. Both files consist of ASCII characters only, contain no tab characters, and every line (including the last line) ends with a line feed (LF).

* input/models.lp (models)
(1) Line kinds: lines of 0 characters (empty lines) and lines whose first character is "#" (comment lines) are ignored wherever they appear. All other lines are called "effective lines". An effective line is a sequence of one or more items separated by single spaces, with no space at the start or at the end of the line.
(2) Line numbers: the first line of the file is line 1, and the numbering counts empty lines and comment lines as well.
(3) Blocks: the effective lines are divided into one or more blocks. A block starts with a line "model <model name>" and ends with the first "end" line that appears after it. There are no effective lines outside blocks. One block represents one model, and the order of the blocks is called "the model order". A model name is a name as defined in (5), and it differs from block to block. The effective lines between the "model" line and the "end" line are called "body lines". The "model" line and the "end" line are always written correctly, but body lines may contain errors (they are checked in sections 2 and 3 of [Processing]).
(4) When written correctly, the body lines appear in the following order.
  (a) The objective direction line: "maximize" (maximization) or "minimize" (minimization)
  (b) The objective function line: "<name>: <expression>". The first item is the name immediately followed by ":" (example: "profit: 3 x + 2 y"). The name of the objective function is not used anywhere else.
  (c) The "subject to" line
  (d) One or more constraint lines: "<constraint name>: <expression> <comparison> <signed number>". The constraint name is a name, and the comparison is one of "<=", ">=", "=" (example: "wood: 6 table + 2 chair <= 180"). The value to the right of the comparison is called the right-hand side.
  (e) The bounds part (may be omitted): a "bounds" line followed by zero or more bound lines
  (f) The integer part (may be omitted): an "integer" line followed by zero or more name lines
  (g) The 0-1 variable part (may be omitted): a "binary" line followed by zero or more name lines
(5) Item rules:
  - Name: a string whose first character is a lowercase letter (a to z) and whose second and later characters (there may be none) are each a lowercase letter, a digit (0 to 9) or "_", excluding the five strings bounds, integer, binary, free and inf.
  - Number: a string of one of the following three forms; it contains no sign.
    Integer form: one or more digits. Its value is their decimal value (examples: "12", "0").
    Decimal form: one or more digits, ".", and one or more digits, concatenated in this order. Its value is the decimal value of the digit string with "." removed, divided by 10^d (d is the number of digits after ".") (examples: "2.5" is 5/2, "0.125" is 1/8).
    Fraction form: one or more digits, "/", and one or more digits, concatenated in this order, where the value of the digit string after "/" is not 0. Its value is the value of the digit string before "/" divided by the value of the digit string after it (example: "3/4").
  - Signed number: a number, or "-" immediately followed by a number. The value of the latter is the value of the number with its sign reversed (examples: "-4", "-2.5").
  - Expression: one or more terms joined by "+" or "-" items; a "-" item may also precede the first term. A term is "<number> <name>" (a coefficient and a variable) or "<name>" (a variable with coefficient 1). A "-" immediately before a term reverses the sign of that term's coefficient. For example, "- 0.75 x4 + 20 x5 - x6" is an expression consisting of three terms with the variables x4, x5, x6, whose coefficients are -3/4, 20, -1. The same variable may appear two or more times in one expression.
  - Bound line: one of the following five forms. <lower value> is a signed number or "-inf", and <upper value> is a signed number or "inf".
    "<name> free", "<name> >= <lower value>", "<name> <= <upper value>", "<name> = <signed number>", "<lower value> <= <name> <= <upper value>"
  - Name line: a line consisting only of one or more names.

* input/params.txt (settings)
Line 1 is "log_level <L>", line 2 is "node_limit <N>", and from line 3 on there are zero or more lines of the following two kinds. Items are separated by single spaces, and L and N are decimal integers consisting of digits only.
  "log <model name> <L>"
  "limit <model name> <N>"
- The "log level" of each model is the L of that model's log line if there is one, and otherwise the L of line 1 (used in output/iterations.txt).
- The "node limit" of each model is the N of that model's limit line if there is one, and otherwise the N of line 2 (used in section 9 of [Processing]).
The model names on log lines and limit lines are model names that exist in models.lp, and for one model there is at most one log line and at most one limit line. There may be a log line that refers to an erroneous model, or a limit line that refers to a model other than an integer model (section 3 of [Processing]); in that case its value is not used.

[Processing]
For each model, in the model order, perform the processing of sections 2 to 10.

* 1. Fractions and notation
(1) All values are rational numbers, and all computations and comparisons are exact (for example, 1/3 + 1/6 is exactly 1/2; there is no rounding). Because the computation is exact, the result does not change with things such as the order in which sums are computed.
(2) A value described as a "fraction" in the output is written as an irreducible fraction: if the denominator is 1, only the numerator (examples: "12", "-3", "0"), and otherwise "<numerator>/<denominator>" (examples: "1678/115", "-5/4"). The denominator is positive, and a negative number has "-" on the numerator.
(3) Lower bounds, upper bounds, range ends and the like may take, besides finite values, -infinity and +infinity. In the output, -infinity is written "-inf" and +infinity is written "inf".
(4) Approximate value: for a fraction v, let t be the largest integer <= |v| * 10^6 + 1/2. The approximate value of v is the string formed by concatenating, in this order, the quotient of t divided by 10^6, ".", and the remainder of t divided by 10^6 padded with leading zeros to 6 digits, with a leading "-" added only when v < 0 and t > 0 (this is the decimal notation to 6 decimal places, rounded half up at the 7th decimal place. Examples: 20/3 is "6.666667", -5/4 is "-1.250000", 1665 is "1665.000000"). t is also computed exactly, as a fraction.
(5) floor(v) is the largest integer <= v, and ceil(v) is the smallest integer >= v.

* 2. Syntax check
Examine the body lines from top to bottom according to the following reading states. The initial reading state is A.
  - A: if the line is "maximize" or "minimize", take it as the objective direction and move to B.
  - B: if the line has two or more items, the first item is a name immediately followed by ":", and the sequence of the second and later items is an expression, take that expression as the objective function and move to C.
  - C: if the line is "subject to", move to D.
  - D: if the line is a constraint line, register it as a constraint and move to E.
  - E: if the line is "bounds", move to F; if it is "integer", move to G; if it is "binary", move to H. Otherwise, if the line is a constraint line, register it as a constraint (staying in E).
  - F: if the line is "integer", move to G; if it is "binary", move to H. Otherwise, if the line is a bound line, register it.
  - G: if the line is "binary", move to H. Otherwise, if the line is a name line, register it as an integer declaration.
  - H: if the line is a name line, register it as a 0-1 variable declaration.
If a line does not satisfy the condition of the current reading state, it is a syntax error at that line (the message is "syntax error"), and the check of that model ends. If, when all body lines have been examined (including the case where there are no body lines), the reading state is one of A, B, C, D, it is a syntax error at the "end" line of that block.
Whether a sequence of items "is an expression" is decided by the following procedure. Focus on the first item of the sequence; if it is "-", move the focus to the next item.
  (a) If the focused item is a number and the item after it is a name, take these two as one term and move the focus to the item after them. Otherwise, if the focused item is a name, take it as one term and move the focus to the next item. If neither holds (including the case where there is no focused item), the sequence is not an expression.
  (b) If no items of the sequence remain, the sequence is an expression. If the focused item is "+" or "-", move the focus to the next item and return to (a). Otherwise, the sequence is not an expression.
A line "is a constraint line" if it has four or more items, the first item is a name immediately followed by ":", the second-to-last item is one of "<=", ">=", "=", the last item is a signed number, and the sequence of the items from the second to the third-to-last is an expression. "Bound line" and "name line" are as in [Input files] (5). For example, "x >= inf", "x <= -inf" and "x = inf" are not bound lines. Also, since bounds, integer and binary are not names, an "integer" line in G, and an "integer" or "binary" line in H, are syntax errors.

* 3. Content check and model data
(1) Variables: the names that appear in the expressions of the objective function and the constraints are the variables. The variable order is the order of first appearance, looking at the objective function's expression and then the constraints' expressions in registration order, and within each expression from left to right. Let n be the number of variables, and number them 1, ..., n in the variable order. Number the constraints 1, ..., m in registration order (m is the number of constraints).
(2) Coefficients: if one expression has several terms of the same variable, the sum of their coefficients is the coefficient of that variable in that expression (the sum may be 0). A variable that does not appear in an expression has coefficient 0 in it. Let c_j be the coefficient of variable j in the objective function, a_ij its coefficient in constraint i, b_i the value of the right-hand side of constraint i, and op_i its comparison.
(3) Set the lower bound of each variable to 0 and its upper bound to +infinity, then perform the following steps (a) to (d) in order. When an error is found, the check of that model ends at that point.
  (a) Look at the constraints in order; if a constraint's name is the same as the name of a constraint before it, it is the error "duplicate constraint <constraint name>" at the line of that constraint.
  (b) Look at the bound lines in order. If the name on the line is not a variable, it is the error "unknown variable <name>" at that line. If it is a variable: for the "free" form, set the lower bound to -infinity and the upper bound to +infinity; for the ">=" form set the lower bound, for the "<=" form the upper bound, for the "=" form both the lower and the upper bound, and for the "<lower value> <= <name> <= <upper value>" form the lower and the upper bound, each to the written value ("-inf" is -infinity, "inf" is +infinity). After that, if the lower and upper bounds are both finite and lower bound > upper bound, it is the error "inconsistent bounds <name>" at that line.
  (c) Look at the integer declaration lines in order, and at the names on each line from left to right. If a name is not a variable, it is the error "unknown variable <name>" at that line. If it is a variable, make it an integer variable, change its lower bound to ceil(lower bound) if the lower bound is finite, and change its upper bound to floor(upper bound) if the upper bound is finite. After that, if the lower and upper bounds are both finite and lower bound > upper bound, it is the error "inconsistent bounds <name>" at that line.
  (d) Look at the 0-1 variable declaration lines in order, and at the names on each line from left to right. If a name is not a variable, it is the error "unknown variable <name>" at that line. If it is a variable, make it an integer variable and set its lower bound to 0 and its upper bound to 1.
  The same variable may be declared any number of times (each time it is processed as described above).
(4) A model with a syntax error or an error of (3) is called an "erroneous model"; it is not solved, and only its error is reported as described in [Output files]. The line number of an error is the line number of [Input files] (2). A model without errors is called a "valid model"; a valid model with one or more integer variables is called an "integer model", and one with none is called a "continuous model". The lower bound l_j and the upper bound u_j of variable j when (3) is finished are called "the model bounds".

* 4. Conversion to standard form
The combination of the objective direction, c_j, a_ij, op_i, b_i, and the lower bound l_j and upper bound u_j of each variable (which may be -infinity or +infinity) is called an "LP". For a continuous model the LP with the model bounds is solved, and for an integer model the LP with the bounds of each node of section 9 is solved. In an LP, integrality conditions are not considered. An LP is converted by the following procedure into a form (standard form) consisting of variables whose values are 0 or more (hereafter called columns) and constraints on linear expressions of the columns (hereafter called rows). Once the slack columns and artificial columns of (4) are added, all rows are equalities.
(1) Variable substitution: for each variable j, do one of the following.
  - If l_j is finite, "shift": x_j = l_j + y, and create one column for the variable y >= 0. The column name is the variable name.
  - If l_j = -infinity and u_j is finite, "reflect": x_j = u_j - y, and create one column. The column name is the variable name followed by "'".
  - If l_j = -infinity and u_j = +infinity, "split": x_j = y_plus - y_minus, and create two columns, y_plus then y_minus. Their column names are the variable name followed by "+" and by "-", respectively.
  The direction e of each column is +1 for a shift column and a y_plus column, and -1 for a reflect column and a y_minus column. The offset s_j of variable j is l_j for shift, u_j for reflect, and 0 for split.
(2) Rows: create one row per constraint, in constraint order. The row of constraint i has the constraint name as its row name and op_i as its comparison. The coefficient of this row in column k of variable j is e_k * a_ij, and its right-hand side is b_i - sum_j a_ij * s_j (the sum is over all variables). Then, for each shift variable whose u_j is finite, create one row (an upper-bound row), in variable order. An upper-bound row has the row name "<variable name>.ub", coefficient 1 in the column of that variable and 0 in the other columns, comparison "<=", and right-hand side u_j - l_j. The rows are numbered in the order they are created (the row of constraint i is the i-th row).
(3) Sign adjustment: for a row whose right-hand side is negative, reverse the signs of all its coefficients and of its right-hand side, and swap "<=" and ">=" in its comparison ("=" stays as it is). The sign sigma of a row so adjusted is -1, and sigma of every other row is +1. Hereafter, the comparison, the coefficients and the right-hand side of a row are those after this adjustment.
(4) Slack columns and artificial columns: in row order, create one slack column for each row whose comparison is "<=" or ">=". The coefficient of a slack column is +1 in its row if the comparison is "<=" and -1 if it is ">=", and 0 in the other rows. Its column name is "s:<row name>". Then, in row order, create one artificial column for each row whose comparison is ">=" or "=". The coefficient of an artificial column is +1 in its row and 0 in the other rows, and its column name is "a:<row name>".
(5) Column order: arrange the columns of (1) (in variable order; for a split variable, y_plus then y_minus), then the slack columns of (4), then the artificial columns, and give them the column numbers 0, 1, 2, ... from the start. Hereafter, "column-number order" means this order. Let R be the number of rows and K the number of columns.
(6) Costs: the cost of column k of variable j is e_k * c_j, and the cost of slack columns and artificial columns is 0. The phase-2 cost of each column is the cost itself in a minimization model, and the cost with its sign reversed in a maximization model. The phase-1 cost of each column is 1 for an artificial column and 0 for any other column. Also, let the constant C0 = sum_j c_j * s_j, and let the direction sign g be +1 for minimization and -1 for maximization.
(7) Initial columns: the "initial column" of a row is its slack column for a row whose comparison is "<=", and its artificial column for any other row. The coefficient of the initial column is +1 in that row and 0 in the other rows.

* 5. Simplex tableau
(1) A tableau consists of the value T[i][k] for each row i and each column k, the value beta_i of each row, and the basic column B(i) of each row. Initially, T[i][k] is the coefficient of section 4, beta_i is the right-hand side of section 4, and B(i) is the initial column of row i. A column that is one of the B(i) is called a "basic column", and any other column is called a "nonbasic column".
(2) Reduced costs and the phase value: for the cost d_k of each column k (the phase-1 or the phase-2 cost), the reduced cost of column k is r_k = d_k - sum_i d_B(i) * T[i][k], and the phase value is z = sum_i d_B(i) * beta_i (each sum is over all rows, and d_B(i) is the cost of column B(i)). From z under the phase-2 costs, the objective value is defined as C0 + g * z (this is the value of the objective function of the original model).
(3) Pivot at row p, column q: first, divide T[p][k] for all columns, and beta_p, by T[p][q]. Next, for each row i other than p, with f being T[i][q] at that moment, subtract f * T[p][k] from T[i][k] for all columns, and subtract f * beta_p from beta_i. Finally, set B(p) to q.
(4) Tableau solution: the value of each column is beta_i for the basic column B(i), and 0 for a nonbasic column. The value x_j of variable j is l_j + (the value of its column) for shift, u_j - (the value of its column) for reflect, and (the value of the y_plus column) - (the value of the y_minus column) for split.

* 6. Two-phase simplex method
(1) Phase procedure: given the cost of each column (phase 1 or phase 2) and a set of candidate columns, repeat the following steps (a) to (c). Reduced costs are those for the given costs.
  (a) Entering column: among the candidate columns with r_k < 0, let q be the column with the smallest column number. If there is no such column, the phase procedure ends with "optimal".
  (b) Leaving row: for each row i with T[i][q] > 0, compute the ratio theta_i = beta_i / T[i][q], and let p be the row with the smallest theta_i. If several rows have the smallest value, let p be the one among them whose B(i) has the smallest column number. If there is no row with T[i][q] > 0, the phase procedure ends with "unbounded", and q is the unbounded column.
  (c) Pivot at row p, column q, and add 1 to the iteration count of that phase (iteration counts start from 0 for each phase).
(2) Phase 1: if there is at least one artificial column, perform the phase procedure with the phase-1 costs and with all columns (including artificial columns) as candidates (it always ends with "optimal"). Let w be the phase value at its end. If w > 0, the LP is "infeasible", and the processing of this LP ends. If there are no artificial columns, phase 1 and (3) are not performed.
(3) Drive-out: if w = 0, examine, in row order, each row i whose B(i) is an artificial column. If there are columns that are not artificial columns and have T[i][k] != 0, pivot at row i and column k, where k is the one among them with the smallest column number (this is called a drive-out; T[i][k] may be negative; it is not counted as an iteration). If there is no such column, row i is a "redundant row", and nothing is done.
(4) Phase 2: perform the phase procedure with the phase-2 costs and with all columns other than artificial columns as candidates. If it ends with "optimal", the LP is "optimal"; if it ends with "unbounded", the LP is "unbounded". (In a redundant row, T[i][k] of the non-artificial columns stays 0 throughout, so a redundant row is never chosen as the leaving row.)
(5) When the LP is optimal, the objective value at the end is called the optimal value of the LP, and the tableau solution (section 5 (4)) at the end is called the solution of the LP.
(6) Farkas multipliers: when the LP is infeasible, compute the reduced costs r_k for the phase-1 costs in the tableau at the end of phase 1. For each row i, with c being the initial column of row i, mu_i = sigma_i * (the phase-1 cost of column c - r_c) is the Farkas multiplier of row i.
(7) Unbounded direction: when the LP is unbounded, for the unbounded column q, let the direction delta of each column be 1 for column q, -T[i][q] for the column B(i) of each row i, and 0 for the other columns. The direction Delta_j of variable j is delta(its column) for shift, -delta(its column) for reflect, and delta(the y_plus column) - delta(the y_minus column) for split. The rate of increase is sum_j c_j * Delta_j. (Moving from the tableau solution at the end in the direction Delta, the constraints and bounds stay satisfied while the value of the objective function keeps changing at the rate of increase.)

* 7. Constraint values and coefficient statistics
(1) Constraint values: for variable values x, the value of constraint i is A_i = sum_j a_ij * x_j, and its margin S_i is b_i - A_i if op_i is "<=", and A_i - b_i if op_i is ">=" or "=" (a_ij, b_i, op_i are those of the original model, before the adjustment of section 4 (3)).
(2) Coefficient statistics: for a valid model, compute the number of nonzero a_ij (the nonzero count), and, for each of the following four sets, the minimum and the maximum of the absolute values of its values.
  - matrix: the nonzero a_ij (over all i and j)
  - objective: the nonzero c_j
  - rhs: the nonzero b_i
  - bounds: those of the model bounds l_j and u_j that are finite and nonzero
  If a set is empty, its minimum and maximum are both written "-".

* 8. Sensitivity analysis
When the LP is optimal, compute the following values in the tableau at the end. r_k is the reduced cost for the phase-2 costs, and it is computed for artificial columns as well (with cost 0). For constraint i, let k_i be the initial column of row i.
(1) Dual prices: for each constraint i, y_i = -g * sigma_i * r_(k_i). (This represents how much the optimal value increases when the right-hand side b_i is increased slightly. It is not computed for upper-bound rows.)
(2) Reduced costs: for each variable j, c_j - sum_i y_i * a_ij (the sum is over constraints 1, ..., m).
(3) Basis status: for each variable j, as follows.
  - Split variable: "zero" if the y_plus column and the y_minus column are both nonbasic columns, and "basic" otherwise.
  - Shift variable: "lower" if its column is a nonbasic column. "upper" if its column is a basic column, the variable has an upper-bound row, and the slack column of that row is a nonbasic column. "basic" if neither holds.
  - Reflect variable: "upper" if its column is a nonbasic column, and "basic" if it is a basic column.
(4) Range of an objective coefficient: for variable j, let the value h_k of each column k be g * e_k for a column of variable j and 0 for any other column. For each nonbasic column k that is not an artificial column, compute eta_k = h_k - sum_i h_B(i) * T[i][k] (h_B(i) is the h of column B(i)). Let L be the maximum of -r_k / eta_k over the columns k with eta_k > 0 (L = -infinity if there is no such column), and let U be the minimum of -r_k / eta_k over the columns k with eta_k < 0 (U = +infinity if there is no such column); the lower end of the range is c_j + L and the upper end is c_j + U (if L = -infinity the lower end is -infinity, and if U = +infinity the upper end is +infinity). This is the range of c_j over which the current basis stays optimal when only c_j is changed.
(5) Range of a right-hand side: for constraint i, let rho_t = sigma_i * T[t][k_i] for each row t. If there is a row t with rho_t != 0 whose B(t) is an artificial column, the lower and upper ends of the range are both b_i. Otherwise, let L be the maximum of -beta_t / rho_t over the rows t with rho_t > 0 (L = -infinity if there is no such row), and let U be the minimum of -beta_t / rho_t over the rows t with rho_t < 0 (U = +infinity if there is no such row); the lower end of the range is b_i + L and the upper end is b_i + U. This is the range of b_i over which the current basis stays feasible when only b_i is changed.
(6) Degeneracy counts: the primal degeneracy count is the number of rows i whose B(i) is not an artificial column and whose beta_i = 0. The dual degeneracy count is the number of nonbasic columns k that are not artificial columns and have r_k = 0.
(7) Dual objective value: sum_i y_i * b_i + sum_j (the reduced cost of variable j) * x_j (x is the solution of the LP, and the first sum is over constraints 1, ..., m). For an optimal LP, this value equals the optimal value.

* 9. Branch and bound
An integer model is solved by the following depth-first search.
(1) A node has a lower bound and an upper bound for each variable, a depth, a parent number, a branch description, and a parent value. The root node has the model bounds, depth 0, no parent, the branch description "root", and no parent value.
(2) Use a stack (the node pushed last is popped first) that holds unprocessed nodes. Initially the stack holds only the root node. Let N be the node limit, and count the number of processed nodes (initially 0).
(3) While the stack is not empty, repeat the following. If the number of processed nodes equals N, cut off the search. Otherwise, pop one node from the stack, add 1 to the number of processed nodes, and use that value as the number of this node. Solve the LP with the node's bounds by sections 4 to 6, and perform the first of the following (a) to (e) that applies.
  (a) The LP is infeasible: do nothing (the outcome of the node is infeasible).
  (b) The LP is unbounded: end the search (the outcome of the node is unbounded; this happens only at the root node).
  (c) There is an incumbent, and the optimal value of the LP is not better than the objective value of the incumbent (for maximization, <= the objective value of the incumbent; for minimization, >= the objective value of the incumbent): do nothing (the outcome of the node is pruned).
  (d) In the solution of the LP, every integer variable has an integer value: make the solution of this LP the new incumbent, and the optimal value of the LP the objective value of the incumbent (the outcome of the node is incumbent).
  (e) Otherwise: among the integer variables whose values in the solution of the LP are not integers, let j be the first one in variable order and v its value. Create a child in which, of the node's bounds, only the upper bound of variable j is changed to floor(v), and a child in which only the lower bound of variable j is changed to ceil(v). The depth of the children is the depth of this node + 1, their parent number is the number of this node, their parent value is the optimal value of this LP, and their branch descriptions are "<variable name><=<floor(v)>" and "<variable name>>=<ceil(v)>", respectively (with no spaces; example: "a<=3"). Push the ceil(v) child and then the floor(v) child onto the stack (so the floor(v) child is processed first). (The outcome of the node is branch.)
(4) Status of an integer model: UNBOUNDED if the search was ended in (b), NODE_LIMIT if it was cut off, and otherwise OPTIMAL if there is an incumbent and INFEASIBLE if there is none.
(5) Bound value: for NODE_LIMIT, the best value (the maximum for maximization, the minimum for minimization) among the objective value of the incumbent (when there is an incumbent) and the parent values of the nodes left on the stack. Otherwise, the objective value of the incumbent, or "-" if there is no incumbent.
(Since the lower and upper bounds of integer variables were made integers in section 3, floor(v) is >= the lower bound and ceil(v) is <= the upper bound.)

* 10. Model results
- Continuous model: the LP with the model bounds is solved exactly once (its node number is taken to be 1). The model status is OPTIMAL if the LP is optimal, INFEASIBLE if it is infeasible, and UNBOUNDED if it is unbounded. When it is OPTIMAL, the solution of the LP is the solution of the model, and the optimal value of the LP is the objective value of the model.
- Integer model: search as described in section 9. If there is an incumbent (including the NODE_LIMIT case), the incumbent is the solution of the model, and the objective value of the incumbent is the objective value of the model.
- The "root LP" is that LP for a continuous model, and the LP of the root node for an integer model.

[Output files]
Write the following five files to output/. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, every line ends with a line feed (LF), and no line has trailing spaces. No empty lines are written. Text inside double quotes is written exactly as shown, and each part in < > is replaced by a value. The separator is a single comma in summary.csv and a single space in the other files. Counts, sequence numbers, line numbers, depths, log levels and node limits are decimal integers, and all other numeric values are written as fractions (section 1 (2)).

* output/iterations.txt (simplex log)
For each model, in the model order, output the following lines, and finally output the total line.
(1) Erroneous model: only the single line "MODEL <model name> error".
(2) Valid model: "MODEL <model name> level <log level>", followed, for each LP solved (once for a continuous model; for an integer model, in the order in which the nodes are processed), by the following lines.   [1] For each variable, in variable order: for shift, "MAP <variable name> shift <l_j>" (if u_j is finite, followed by " upper <u_j>"); for reflect, "MAP <variable name> reflect <u_j>"; for split, "MAP <variable name> split"

  If there are artificial columns, then output the following lines.

* output/summary.csv (summary table)
Line 1 is "model,status,objective,approx,relaxation,nodes,pivots". From line 2 on, for each model in the model order, "<model name>,<status>,<objective value>,<approximate value>,<optimal value of the root LP>,<number of LPs>,<number of iterations>".
- <status> is the model status, and ERROR for an erroneous model.
- <objective value> is the objective value of the model and <approximate value> is its approximate value; if there is no solution (including erroneous models), both are "-".
- <optimal value of the root LP> is "-" if the root LP is not optimal (including erroneous models). It is written for continuous models as well.
- <number of LPs> is the number of LPs solved for that model, and <number of iterations> is the value of the PIVOTS line of solutions.txt; both are 0 for an erroneous model.

[Source files]
Split the source into the following six files. The names are language-independent role names; follow each language's conventions for file names and extensions.
- main: handles the overall flow and the core of the processing, and holds the most logic. It performs the content check of section 3 (variable order, summing of coefficients, duplicate constraint names, changing bounds and rounding for integer variables, error messages) and the creation of the model data; the conversion to standard form of section 4 (variable substitution, directions and offsets, constraint rows and upper-bound rows, sign adjustment, slack columns and artificial columns, costs and C0, initial columns); the driving of the two-phase simplex method of section 6 (2) to (4) (calling phase 1 and phase 2, detecting infeasibility, drive-outs and redundant rows); the creation of the lines of iterations.txt and their selection by log level; the solution of section 5 (4); the Farkas multipliers of section 6 (6) and the unbounded direction of section 6 (7); the constraint values and coefficient statistics of section 7; the sensitivity analysis of section 8; putting together the model results of section 10; and the assembly and writing of every line of the five output files.
- rational: handles the fraction (irreducible fraction) type, addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, comparison, floor and ceil, reading the value of a number item, and the formatting of fractions and of approximate values (in a language whose standard library has a fraction type, you may use that type and put only the reading and the formatting here).
- lp_parser: handles reading the two input files: splitting models.lp into lines with line numbers, handling empty lines and comment lines, splitting into blocks, the syntax check of section 2 (transitions of the reading state, deciding expressions, constraint lines, bound lines and name lines), returning the registered objective direction, objective function, constraints, bound lines and declaration lines (each including its line number), and reading the values of params.txt (the log level and the node limit, and the per-model settings).
- tableau: handles the tableau type (T, beta, B) and, from section 5, the pivot, the reduced costs, the phase value, and the creation of the lines of the tableau display.
- simplex: handles the phase procedure of section 6 (1) (selecting the entering column and the leaving row by Bland's rule, calling the pivot, iteration counts, creating the PIVOT lines).
- branch_bound: handles the branch and bound of section 9 (the node type and the stack, node numbers, calling the LP solving process, the decisions (a) to (e) and updating the incumbent, creating children, the cutoff and the remaining nodes, the bound value, creating the NODE, OPEN and RESULT lines).

[Constraints]
- input/models.lp has at most 1000 lines, and each line has at most 200 characters. There are at least 1 and at most 30 models, and names (including model names) have at most 16 characters.
- Among numbers, the integer form has at most 6 characters, the decimal form has at most 6 characters before "." and at most 4 characters after ".", and the fraction form has at most 4 characters both before and after "/".
- A valid model has at most 15 variables and at most 15 constraints.
- In input/params.txt, L is one of 0, 1, 2, and N is at least 1 and at most 1000.
- Every fraction that appears in the procedure of [Processing] (including intermediate values of pivots and sums, ratios, reduced costs, range ends, and so on), written as an irreducible fraction, has a numerator whose absolute value is less than 2^20 and a denominator less than 2^20. Therefore, even when numerators and denominators are multiplied together in addition, subtraction, multiplication, division or comparison of two fractions, the absolute values of the integers that appear are less than 2^53, and they can be computed exactly with 64-bit integers (or with double-precision floating-point numbers, which represent integers less than 2^53 exactly). The integers that appear in computing approximate values are also less than 2^53.
- In the given data there are 20 models (of which 8 are erroneous models and 4 are integer models), 58 LPs are solved in total, and the standard form of a single LP has at most 11 rows and 25 columns.
