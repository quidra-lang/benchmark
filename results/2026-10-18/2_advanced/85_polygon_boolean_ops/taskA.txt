[Overview]
Write a program that performs Boolean operations (union, intersection, difference, and exclusive or) between polygons with integer-coordinate vertices (holes are allowed), computing exactly with rational numbers. The operations use the Greiner-Hormann algorithm: the crossings between the edges of the two polygons are computed as exact fractions and inserted into the vertex sequences of both polygons, each crossing is classified as "entering" or "exiting" the other polygon, and then the vertex sequences are traversed to build the result rings (closed polylines). The result rings are split into outer boundaries and holes by containment depth and printed in a canonical form with normalized orientation and starting point.
When reading the polygons, format errors and geometric errors such as self-intersections are checked exactly with integers. If two polygons are not in general position (they share a vertex, edges overlap, or a vertex lies on an edge of the other polygon), the operation is rejected and the reason is recorded. For clipping by a convex window (CLIP), the clipping is also done with the Sutherland-Hodgman algorithm to confirm that the results agree. In addition, the program computes properties of a polygon such as its area and centroid, defines new polygons by translation, rotation, and reflection, defines new polygons by rounding the vertices of a result to integers, locates points, and draws results with characters.
The program reads polygons.txt (polygons) and operations.txt (operations) from the input/ folder and writes four files to the output/ folder: results.txt (results), areas.csv (table of areas), render.txt (drawings), and rejections.txt (rejected items). It prints nothing to standard output. No random numbers are used.

[Input files]
There are two input files, input/polygons.txt and input/operations.txt. Both consist of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). Lines have no leading or trailing spaces and contain no tab characters. The words within a line are separated by single spaces. In both files, empty lines (lines of 0 characters) and comment lines (lines whose first character is "#") are skipped.
Below, an "integer word" is a string of 1 to 9 digits (0-9), optionally preceded by a single "-" (e.g. 17, -4, 007), and its value is the value read as a decimal number. A "name" is a word of 1 to 16 characters consisting of lowercase letters (a-z), digits (0-9), and "_".

== input/polygons.txt (polygons)
Line numbers are counted 1, 2, ... from the start of the file, including empty lines and comment lines. The lines other than empty lines and comment lines are divided into blocks. A block starts with a line "POLYGON <name>" (the start line) and ends with a line "END" (the end line). The lines between the start line and the end line, other than empty lines and comment lines, are called content lines. The start line and the end line are always written correctly (the <name> of the start line follows the rules for a name). There are no lines other than empty lines and comment lines outside blocks, and the first word of a content line is never POLYGON or END.
When written correctly, the content lines are as follows. First comes a ring header line "OUTER <n>" followed by n vertex lines, and after that come zero or more groups of a ring header line "HOLE <n>" and n vertex lines. n is the number of vertices of the ring, and a vertex line "<x> <y>" gives the coordinates of a vertex. The OUTER ring is called the outer boundary and a HOLE ring is called a hole; in their order within the block, the outer boundary is called ring 1 and the holes ring 2, ring 3, .... The vertices of a ring are v_1, v_2, ..., v_n in line order.
The content lines may contain errors. Read the content lines from the beginning by the following procedure; the first erroneous line found is the format error line of that block.
(1) Set the number of rings read to 0, and repeat (2) to (4) while content lines remain.
(2) If the next content line (read as a header line) does not satisfy the condition that it consists of exactly 2 words, the first word is OUTER or HOLE, and the second word is a string of 1 to 9 digits whose value n is 1 or more, this line is the erroneous line. This line is also the erroneous line if the first word is OUTER and the number of rings read is 1 or more, or if the first word is HOLE and the number of rings read is 0.
(3) Read the following content lines one at a time as vertex lines, n lines in total. If no content line remains when one is to be read, the end line is the erroneous line; if a line read does not consist of exactly 2 integer words, that line is the erroneous line.
(4) Add 1 to the number of rings read.
(5) If the number of rings read is 0 when the content lines run out, the end line is the erroneous line.
For example, a vertex line "7l0 170", fewer vertex lines than the n of the header, and contents that start with a HOLE header are all format errors. Even a ring without format errors may have fewer than 3 vertices or coordinate values not between 0 and 1000 inclusive (these are checked in section 3).

== input/operations.txt (operations)
Each line other than empty lines and comment lines is one operation. Operations are numbered 1, 2, ... in file order (the operation number). The first word of each line is called the kind, and the second and later words are called the arguments. There are the following 12 kinds of operations (< > marks a part replaced by its value).
- UNION <A> <B>: compute the union of polygons A and B (the set of points in the interior of at least one of A and B).
- INTERSECTION <A> <B>: compute the intersection of A and B (the set of points in the interior of both).
- DIFFERENCE <A> <B>: compute the difference of A minus B (the set of points in the interior of A and not in the interior of B).
- XOR <A> <B>: compute the exclusive or of A and B (the set of points in the interior of exactly one of them).
- CLIP <A> <W>: clip A by the convex polygon W (compute the intersection of A and W) and confirm it with the Sutherland-Hodgman algorithm.
- AREA <A>: take polygon A itself as the result and compute its properties.
- MOVE <A> <dx> <dy> <N>: define the polygon obtained by translating A as the polygon named N.
- ROTATE <A> <cx> <cy> <q> <N>: define the polygon obtained by rotating A counterclockwise by q * 90 degrees around the point (cx, cy) as N.
- MIRROR <A> <axis> <c> <N>: define the polygon obtained by reflecting A in the line x = c if axis is x, or in the line y = c if axis is y, as N.
- SNAP <T> <N>: define the polygon obtained by rounding the vertices of the result of target T to integers as N.
- LOCATE <T> <x> <y>: determine whether the point (x, y) is in the interior of, on the boundary of, or outside the result of target T.
- RENDER <T> <x0> <y0> <cw> <ch> <cols> <rows>: draw the result of target T with characters.
<A>, <B>, <W> are polygon names, <N> is the name of the polygon to be newly defined, and <T> is a target (section 5). Some operation lines contain errors (section 5).

[Processing]
== Notation
- For points p = (p_x, p_y), o, a, b, define cross(o, a, b) = (a_x - o_x) * (b_y - o_y) - (a_y - o_y) * (b_x - o_x). When cross(o, a, b) > 0, b lies to the left of the line directed from o to a. Addition and subtraction of points and multiplication of a point by a number are done component-wise.
- Coordinates and values computed from coordinates (coordinates of crossings, values of cross, and so on) are computed exactly as rational numbers unless stated otherwise. Coordinates of crossings are fractions in general. A way to compute exactly with 64-bit integers only is given in [Constraints].
- Point order: point p precedes point q if p_y < q_y, or p_y = q_y and p_x < q_x. The point that precedes all others in a set of points is called its lowest point.
- Fraction notation: a rational number is written as a reduced fraction "n/d" (d a positive integer), or just "n" when d is 1 (e.g. 64690/727, -3, 0). A point is written "(<x coordinate>,<y coordinate>)" (e.g. (10,10), (365/8,165/4)). A segment is written as its two endpoints "<point>-<point>" (e.g. (0,0)-(10,10)).
- Real numbers are computed in double-precision floating point. To convert a rational number n / d (n, d integers) to a real number, convert n and d each to the real number with the same value and then divide. sqrt is the square root. "Add in order" means starting from 0.0 and adding the values one at a time in the specified order. Real numbers are printed rounded to the specified number of digits (3 or 6 digits after the decimal point) (e.g. 4334.000, -190.000, 0.066013).
- Point p lies on segment ab if cross(a, b, p) = 0 and both min(a_x, b_x) <= p_x <= max(a_x, b_x) and min(a_y, b_y) <= p_y <= max(a_y, b_y) hold (this includes lying on an endpoint).
- Segments ab and cd touch if cross(a, b, c) * cross(a, b, d) < 0 and cross(c, d, a) * cross(c, d, b) < 0, or at least one of c and d lies on segment ab, or at least one of a and b lies on segment cd (this is the same as the two segments having a common point).
- Segments ab and cd overlap if cross(a, b, c) = 0 and cross(a, b, d) = 0, and furthermore, using the x coordinate if a_x != b_x and the y coordinate if a_x = b_x, and letting alpha, beta, gamma, delta be that coordinate of a, b, c, d respectively, max(min(alpha, beta), min(gamma, delta)) < min(max(alpha, beta), max(gamma, delta)) holds (this is the same as lying on the same line and sharing a part of positive length).
- Ring: the closed polyline that connects a sequence of n vertices v_1, v_2, ..., v_n in this order and returns from v_n to v_1 is called a ring. The edge from v_i to v_{i+1} is e_i, and its edge number is i. Here v_{n+1} means v_1 and v_0 means v_n.
- Even-odd test: for a point p and a ring R, count the edges e_i of R (let u = v_i, w = v_{i+1}) for which exactly one of "u_y > p_y" and "w_y > p_y" holds and, letting lo be the one of u and w with the smaller y coordinate and hi the one with the larger, cross(lo, hi, p) > 0. If this count is odd, p is said to be inside R (this is the parity of the number of times the ray from p in the direction of increasing x crosses R). This test is used only when p lies on no edge of R.
- For a set S of rings, p is said to be in the interior of S if the number of rings of S that p is inside is odd. The interior of a polygon is the interior of the set of all its rings.
- Rotating a ring: cyclically shift the vertex sequence of the ring, keeping its cyclic order, so that the lowest vertex becomes v_1.
- Orientation: a ring whose lowest vertex is v_1 is counterclockwise if cross(v_n, v_1, v_2) > 0, and clockwise otherwise. Reversing a ring means reordering its vertex sequence as v_1, v_n, v_{n-1}, ..., v_2 (v_1 does not change).
- Ring area: convert the coordinates of the ring's vertices to real numbers, add x_i * y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} * y_i in order for i = 1, 2, ..., n, and divide the sum by 2 (positive for a counterclockwise ring, negative for a clockwise ring).

== 1. Overall flow
(1) Read polygons.txt and examine the blocks in file order (sections 2 and 3). Bring each valid polygon into canonical form (section 4).
(2) Check the operations in operations.txt one at a time in order of operation number (section 5), and execute each accepted operation (sections 6 to 14). A rejected operation does nothing; only its reason is recorded.
(3) Write the four output files ([Output files]).

== 2. Handling blocks
Examine each block by (1) to (3) below, in this order.
(1) If the name of the start line equals the name of the start line of an earlier block, the block is a duplicate name (reason "duplicate_name line <line number of the start line>"). This block is ignored, and only the earlier block is in effect.
(2) Otherwise, if there is a format error line, the block is a format error (reason "format line <line number of the format error line>").
(3) Otherwise, check the rings as in section 3; if there is an error, the block is a block with that reason, and if there is no error, it is a valid polygon block.
The names of the blocks, except those ignored in (1), become used names regardless of whether the block is valid. Names defined by MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP are also added to the used names. The names of valid polygon blocks and the defined names are called valid polygon names.

== 3. Checking a polygon
Check the ring sequence R_1 (outer boundary), R_2, ..., R_m (holes) by (1) to (4) below, in this order, and the first error found is the reason. <k>, <i>, <j> are ring numbers, <p> is a point, and <e>, <f> are edges (segments), all written in the notation of the Notation section.
(1) For rings k = 1, 2, ..., m in this order, check (a) to (e) below in order. Let the vertices of ring k be v_1, ..., v_n.
  (a) If n < 3: "too_few_vertices ring <k>".
  (b) If there is a vertex whose x coordinate or y coordinate is not between 0 and 1000 inclusive, for the first such vertex p: "out_of_range ring <k> <p>".
  (c) If there is a vertex v_j that equals an earlier vertex, for the smallest such j, with p = v_j: "duplicate_vertex ring <k> <p>".
  (d) If there is an i (1 <= i <= n) with cross(v_{i-1}, v_i, v_{i+1}) = 0, for the smallest such i, with p = v_i: "collinear ring <k> <p>".
  (e) Examine the pairs of non-adjacent edges e_i, e_j (i < j, j is not i + 1, and (i, j) is not (1, n)) in increasing order of i, and for equal i in increasing order of j; for the first pair in which e_i and e_j touch: "self_intersection ring <k> <e_i> <e_j>".
(2) Examine the pairs of rings (i, j) (i < j) in increasing order of i, and for equal i in increasing order of j. For each pair, examine the pairs of an edge e of ring i and an edge f of ring j in increasing order of the edge number of e, and for equal numbers in increasing order of the edge number of f; for the first pair in which e and f touch: "rings_touch rings <i> <j> <e> <f>".
(3) For holes k = 2, 3, ..., m in this order, if v_1 of ring k is not inside the outer boundary: "hole_outside ring <k> <v_1 of ring k>".
(4) Examine the pairs of holes (i, j) (2 <= i < j) in the same order as in (2); if v_1 of ring j is inside ring i: "hole_in_hole rings <i> <j> <v_1 of ring j>"; otherwise, if v_1 of ring i is inside ring j: "hole_in_hole rings <i> <j> <v_1 of ring i>".
If there is no error, the polygon is a valid polygon. In that case each ring does not intersect itself, no two rings share a point, every hole is inside the outer boundary, and no hole is inside another hole. (In the even-odd tests of (3) and (4), the point never lies on an edge.)

== 4. Canonical form
The rings of a valid polygon normalized as follows are called the canonical form of that polygon. Below, the rings, vertices, and edges of a polygon mean those of the canonical form unless stated otherwise.
(1) Rotate each ring.
(2) If the outer boundary is clockwise, reverse it; if a hole is counterclockwise, reverse it. Now the outer boundary is counterclockwise and the holes are clockwise.
(3) Sort the holes by the point order of their v_1.
The outer boundary followed by the holes in the order sorted in (3) is called the ring sequence of the polygon.
Separately, the sequence of rings in the order written in the block (both the ring order and the vertex order as written) is called the input rings of the polygon. The input rings of a defined polygon are specified in section 12.

== 5. Checking operations
For each operation, check (1) to (8) below in order; the operation is rejected with the first one that applies as its reason. If none applies, the operation is accepted. UNION, INTERSECTION, DIFFERENCE, XOR, CLIP are called binary operations, and the W of CLIP is sometimes also called B. The binary operations and AREA, MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP are called region operations.
(1) If the kind is none of the 12 kinds: "unknown_operation <kind>".
(2) If the number of words in the line (including the kind) is not 3 for binary operations and SNAP, 2 for AREA, 4 for LOCATE, 5 for MOVE and MIRROR, 6 for ROTATE, or 8 for RENDER, or if the following conditions are not satisfied: "bad_arguments".
  - LOCATE: x, y are integer words with values between 0 and 1000 inclusive.
  - RENDER: x0, y0, cw, ch, cols, rows are integer words satisfying 0 <= x0, 0 <= y0, 1 <= cw <= 100, 1 <= ch <= 100, 1 <= cols <= 100, 1 <= rows <= 60, x0 + cw * cols <= 1000, y0 + ch * rows <= 1000.
  - MOVE: dx, dy are integer words with values between -1000 and 1000 inclusive. N is a name.
  - ROTATE: cx, cy, q are integer words; cx and cy are between 0 and 1000 inclusive, and q is between 1 and 3 inclusive. N is a name.
  - MIRROR: axis is "x" or "y", and c is an integer word with a value between 0 and 1000 inclusive. N is a name.
  - SNAP: N is a name.
(3) Examine the polygon-name arguments (A and B of a binary operation in this order; A of AREA, MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR) in order: if an argument is not a used name: "unknown_polygon <argument>"; if it is a used name but not a valid polygon name: "invalid_polygon <argument>".
  A target argument (T of LOCATE, RENDER, SNAP) is checked as follows. If T starts with "@": if the part following "@" is a string of 1 to 9 digits and its value k is the operation number of a region operation accepted before this operation, the target is operation k; otherwise "bad_target <T>". If T does not start with "@", T is checked as a polygon name in the same way as above (unknown_polygon, invalid_polygon), and if it is a valid polygon name, the target is polygon T.
(4) For MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP, if N is a used name: "duplicate_name <N>".
(5) For SNAP, if the number of polygons in the result of the target (see below) is not 1: "not_single <T>".
(6) For CLIP, if W has 2 or more rings, or cross(v_{i-1}, v_i, v_{i+1}) <= 0 for some vertex v_i of the outer boundary of W: "not_convex <W>".
(7) For a binary operation, if A and B are not in general position: the reason (section 6).
(8) For MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, if a transformed vertex (section 12) has an x coordinate or y coordinate that is not between 0 and 1000 inclusive: "out_of_range <N>". For SNAP, if checking the rounded ring sequence (section 12) as in section 3 finds an error: "invalid_result <reason from section 3>" (e.g. invalid_result duplicate_vertex ring 1 (563,765)).
The result and the operands of a target are defined as follows. When the target is polygon T, the result is a list of one polygon whose polygon 1 has the outer boundary of T as its outer boundary and the holes of T (in ring-sequence order) as its holes 1, 2, ..., and there are no operands. When the target is operation k, the result is the result of operation k (section 9), and if operation k is a binary operation, its A and B are the operands (if it is not a binary operation, there are no operands).

== 6. General position
The vertices and edges of polygons A and B are examined in ring-sequence order, and within each ring in order of number.
(1) Examine the vertices of A in order; for the first vertex p that equals some vertex of B: "shared_vertex <p>".
(2) Examine the edges e of A in order, and for each e examine the edges f of B in order; for the first pair in which e and f overlap: "overlapping_edges <e> <f>".
(3) Examine the vertices p of A in order, and for each p examine the edges e of B in order; for the first pair in which p lies on e: "vertex_on_edge <name of A> <p> <name of B> <e>". If none is found for the vertices of A, examine the vertices p of B and the edges e of A in the same way; for the first pair: "vertex_on_edge <name of B> <p> <name of A> <e>".
If none of (1) to (3) applies, A and B are in general position. In that case, each pair of an edge of A and an edge of B either has no common point or crosses at a single point that is an endpoint of neither edge.

== 7. Greiner-Hormann algorithm (1): crossings and classification
For polygons P and Q in general position (P is the first, Q the second), find and classify the crossings as follows.
(1) Crossings: for an edge e of P (start point p1, end point p2) and an edge f of Q (start point q1, end point q2), let r = p2 - p1, s = q2 - q1, den = r_x * s_y - r_y * s_x. When den != 0, let g = q1 - p1, t = (g_x * s_y - g_y * s_x) / den, u = (g_x * r_y - g_y * r_x) / den; if 0 < t < 1 and 0 < u < 1, e and f cross at the crossing X = p1 + t * r. t is called the position of X on e, and u the position of X on f. When den = 0, or when t, u are not in this range, there is no crossing. This computation is done for every pair of an edge of P and an edge of Q.
(2) Node lists: for each ring of P, the list v_1, the crossings on e_1 (in increasing order of position on e_1), v_2, the crossings on e_2 (likewise in increasing order of position), ..., v_n, the crossings on e_n, in this order, is called the node list of that ring, and each of its elements is called a node. A node list is circularly linked: the node after the last node is the first node, and the node before the first node is the last node. For each ring of Q, a node list is built in the same way (in order of position on the edges of Q). Each crossing has one node on the P side and one node on the Q side. Two crossings on the same edge never have equal positions.
(3) Entering and exiting: for each ring of P, let s be whether v_1 of that ring is in the interior of Q; go through the node list from the beginning, and each time a crossing node is met, mark that node "entering" if s is false and "exiting" if s is true, and then negate s. Each ring of Q is classified in the same way, starting from whether its v_1 is in the interior of P. (An entering node is a node at which, moving along the ring in the direction v_1, v_2, ..., one enters the interior of the other polygon.)
(4) Crossing order: the crossings listed in the ring-sequence order of P, and within each ring in the order in which they appear in its node list, are said to be in crossing order.

== 8. Greiner-Hormann algorithm (2): traversal
For each operation, decide for each of P and Q whether to "invert" it. For union (UNION), both P and Q are inverted; for intersection (INTERSECTION, CLIP), neither is inverted; for difference (P minus Q), only P is inverted. The travel direction of a crossing node is forward (toward the next node) if exactly one of "the node is an entering node" and "the polygon of the node is inverted" holds, and backward (toward the previous node) otherwise.
(1) Choose the first crossing c in crossing order that has not been traversed yet, prepare an empty point list L, and make the P-side node of c the current node.
(2) Mark the crossing of the current node as traversed. Append the point of the current node to the end of L.
(3) Move along the node list one node at a time in the travel direction of the current node. Each time a node that is not a crossing (a vertex) is reached, append its point to the end of L; stop when a crossing node is reached.
(4) If the crossing of the node where we stopped is c, complete L as one result ring. Otherwise, make the node on the other side of the crossing of the node where we stopped (the Q-side node if it is a P-side node, the P-side node if it is a Q-side node) the current node, and go back to (2).
(5) Repeat (1) to (4) until no untraversed crossing remains.
(6) A ring of P that has no crossing node is added to the result rings if its v_1 is in the interior of Q and P is not inverted, or if its v_1 is not in the interior of Q and P is inverted. For the rings of Q, decide in the same way from whether v_1 is in the interior of P and whether Q is inverted. The vertex sequence of an added ring is the vertex sequence of that ring as it is.
The rings completed in (1) to (5) and the rings added in (6) are the result rings of the operation. No result ring intersects itself, and no two rings share a point. The interior of the set of result rings coincides (except for the boundary) with the point set the operation represents, and the result normalized in section 9 is uniquely determined regardless of the traversal order.

== 9. Assembling the result
From the set of result rings, build a list of polygons as follows.
(1) Rotate each ring.
(2) The depth of each ring is the number of other result rings that its v_1 is inside.
(3) Rings of even depth are outer boundaries, and rings of odd depth are holes. If an outer boundary is clockwise, reverse it; if a hole is counterclockwise, reverse it.
(4) Sort the outer boundaries by the point order of their v_1, and make them, in that order, the outer boundaries of polygons 1, 2, .... The holes of polygon k are the holes whose depth is 1 greater than the depth of the outer boundary of polygon k and whose v_1 is inside the outer boundary of polygon k; sort them by the point order of their v_1 and make them holes 1, 2, ....
The order outer boundary of polygon 1, its hole 1, hole 2, ..., outer boundary of polygon 2, its hole 1, hole 2, ... is called the output order of the result rings.
The result of each operation is as follows (P, Q are the P, Q of sections 7 and 8).
- UNION: the assembly of the result rings traversed as a union with P = A, Q = B.
- INTERSECTION, CLIP: the assembly of the result rings traversed as an intersection with P = A, Q = B.
- DIFFERENCE: the assembly of the result rings traversed as a difference with P = A, Q = B.
- XOR: the polygon list of the difference with P = A, Q = B combined with the polygon list of the difference with P = B, Q = A, with the polygons re-sorted by the point order of the v_1 of their outer boundaries (the holes of each polygon and their order stay as they are).
- AREA: a list of one polygon whose polygon 1 has the outer boundary of polygon A as its outer boundary and the holes of A (in ring-sequence order) as its holes 1, 2, ....
- MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP: a list of one polygon built in the same way as for AREA from the defined polygon N.

== 10. Result values
For the result of a region operation, compute the following values.
- The number of polygons, the number of rings, the number of holes, and the number of vertices (the total number of vertices of all rings).
- The area of each ring (Notation section) and the total area: the sum of the areas of the rings added in order, in the output order of the result rings. If there are no rings, it is 0.0.
- Bounding box: the point low formed by the minimum x coordinate and the minimum y coordinate over all vertices of all rings, and the point high formed by the maximum x coordinate and the maximum y coordinate (exact values).
- Centroid: let sx = 0.0, sy = 0.0; in the output order of the result rings, and for each ring in order i = 1, 2, ..., n, let (x1, y1), (x2, y2) be the coordinates of v_i and v_{i+1} converted to real numbers, c = x1 * y2 - x2 * y1, and add (x1 + x2) * c to sx and (y1 + y2) * c to sy in order. The centroid is (sx / (6 * total area), sy / (6 * total area)) (6 * total area is also computed in real numbers).
- Perimeter: for each ring, in order i = 1, 2, ..., n, let (x1, y1), (x2, y2) be the coordinates of v_i and v_{i+1} converted to real numbers, dx = x2 - x1, dy = y2 - y1; the values sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy) added in order form the perimeter of that ring. The perimeters of the rings added in order, in the output order of the result rings, form the perimeter.
- Vertex origins: for the operands A and B (in the case of a binary operation; for AREA only polygon A, and for MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP only the defined polygon N, is taken as A, and there is no B), classify every vertex of every ring of the result as a if it equals a vertex of A, otherwise as b if it equals a vertex of B, and otherwise as crossing, and count each.
For binary operations, also compute the following values.
- Relation: with P = A, Q = B, if there are 1 or more crossings, the relation is overlap. If there are no crossings, let n_a be the number of rings of A whose v_1 is in the interior of B, and n_b the number of rings of B whose v_1 is in the interior of A; if n_a equals the number of rings of A and n_b = 0, the relation is within (A is contained in B); otherwise, if n_b equals the number of rings of B and n_a = 0, it is contains (A contains B); otherwise, if n_a = 0 and n_b = 0, it is disjoint (there is no common part); if none of these, it is overlap.
- iou: the total area of the result of the intersection of A and B divided by the total area of the result of the union of A and B (both are real numbers computed in the same way as the results of INTERSECTION and UNION).
For AREA, MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP, also compute the following values for the target polygon (A for AREA, the defined polygon N for the others).
- Orientation of the input rings: for each of the input rings, with its vertices in written order, the orientation is ccw if the sum (an integer) of x_i * y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} * y_i for i = 1, 2, ..., n is positive, and cw otherwise.
- Number of reflex vertices: the number of vertices v_i, over all vertices of all rings in the ring sequence, with cross(v_{i-1}, v_i, v_{i+1}) < 0.
- Convex hull: let S be the vertices of the outer boundary sorted in increasing order of x coordinate, and for equal x coordinates in increasing order of y coordinate. Add the points of S in order to an initially empty list H; however, before adding a point p, while H has 2 or more points and cross(h1, h2, p) <= 0 for the second-to-last point h1 and the last point h2 of H, remove the last point of H. Let U be the list obtained by doing the same with S in reverse order. H without its last point, followed by U without its last point, is the vertex sequence of the convex hull (counterclockwise, not including intermediate points on a straight line). Compute the number of convex hull vertices and the convex hull area (the ring-area formula applied to the convex hull vertex sequence as a ring, computed exactly in integers).
- solidity: the total area of the result (a real number) divided by the convex hull area (a real number).

== 11. Cross-check of CLIP (Sutherland-Hodgman algorithm)
Let the vertices of the outer boundary of W be w_1, w_2, ..., w_m (counterclockwise, with w_1 the lowest point). For each ring R in the ring sequence of A, let the point list L be the vertex sequence of R, and for j = 1, 2, ..., m in order, with c1 = w_j, c2 = w_{j+1} (w_{m+1} means w_1), replace L with a new list M by the following procedure. Here, a point p is inside if cross(c1, c2, p) > 0.
  Let M be an empty list. Let the points of L be l_1, ..., l_k; for i = 1, 2, ..., k in order, let prev = l_{i-1} (l_0 means l_k), cur = l_i, and
  - if exactly one of prev and cur is inside, let a = cross(c1, c2, prev), b = cross(c1, c2, cur), and append the point prev + (a / (a - b)) * (cur - prev) (the intersection of line prev cur and line c1 c2) to the end of M.
  - if cur is inside, append cur to the end of M (if a crossing point was appended above, after it).
  If L is empty, M is also empty.
From L after the m replacements, remove each point that equals its preceding point (for the first point, the last point). Which points to remove is decided on the list before removal. If 3 or more points remain, that point list is the clipped ring of R.
Let K be the number of clipped rings and V the total number of vertices of the clipped rings. If the number of result rings of CLIP equals K, and for every result ring of CLIP there is a clipped ring that is cyclically equal to its vertex sequence (the same points in the same order, apart from where it starts), the cross-check result is match; otherwise it is mismatch. (For example, it is mismatch when a concave part of A is split by the window, or when a hole crosses an edge of the window.)

== 12. Defining polygons (MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP)
For MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, the ring sequence obtained by transforming each vertex (x, y) of the input rings of A as follows (with the ring order and vertex order of the input rings of A unchanged) is the input rings of N.
- MOVE: make it (x + dx, y + dy).
- ROTATE: repeat q times the operation that replaces (x, y) with (cx - (y - cy), cy + (x - cx)).
- MIRROR: make it (2 * c - x, y) if axis is x, and (x, 2 * c - y) if axis is y.
For SNAP, the ring sequence obtained by replacing the x coordinate and the y coordinate of each vertex of the outer boundary, hole 1, hole 2, ... of the single polygon of the target's result each with floor(v + 1/2) (the largest integer not greater than v + 1/2; that is, v rounded to an integer, with exact halves going to the larger one) (with the ring order and vertex order unchanged) is the input rings of N.
The N of an accepted MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR, SNAP is a valid polygon (the transformations of MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR do not change the validity of a polygon, except for the coordinate range). The canonical form of N is built as in section 4. In later operations, N can be used as a valid polygon name.

== 13. LOCATE
For the point p = (x, y) and the polygon list of the target's result, decide as follows.
(1) For polygons k = 1, 2, ... in order: if p lies on some edge of the outer boundary of polygon k, "boundary outer <k>"; otherwise, if p lies on some edge of hole j of polygon k (for j = 1, 2, ... in order), "boundary hole <k> <j>".
(2) If p lies on no edge of any ring: if there is a k such that p is inside the outer boundary of polygon k and inside none of the holes of polygon k, "inside polygon <k>"; if there is none, "outside".
When the target has operands A, B, for each of A and B: boundary if p lies on some edge of some ring of that polygon, otherwise inside if p is in the interior of that polygon, and outside if neither.

== 14. RENDER
Draw a grid of cells with cols columns and rows rows. The center of the cell in column i (i = 0, 1, ..., cols - 1, counted from the left) and row j (j = 0, 1, ..., rows - 1, counted from the top) is the point (x0 + (2 * i + 1) * cw / 2, y0 + (2 * (rows - 1 - j) + 1) * ch / 2) (row 0 is the row with the largest y coordinates). The character of each cell is as follows.
- "#" if the center is in the interior of the target's result (the interior of the set of all rings of the result).
- Otherwise, if the target has operands A, B: "+" if the center is in the interior of both A and B, "a" if it is in the interior of A only, and "b" if it is in the interior of B only.
- "." otherwise.

[Output files]
Write all four files output/results.txt, output/areas.csv, output/render.txt, output/rejections.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, each line ends with a newline (LF), and there are no trailing spaces. Below, < > marks a part replaced by its value. Words within a line are separated by single spaces. Points, segments, and fractions use the notation of the Notation section, and real numbers are written with 3 digits after the decimal point unless stated otherwise. Integers such as counts and numbers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. However, the operation line and its words, as well as <kind>, <T>, <N>, <W>, <argument>, are written exactly as in operations.txt.

[Source files]
Organize the program into the following 6 source files (follow each language's conventions for file names and extensions).
- main: the overall flow and the core of the processing. Reading polygons.txt (blocks, format errors, duplicate names), checking polygons (all reasons of section 3), managing canonical forms and input rings, reading operations.txt and checking operations (section 5), resolving targets, the general position check (section 6), executing each operation (including combining XOR), defining polygons (section 12), the Sutherland-Hodgman cross-check (section 11), relation and iou, result values (area, bounding box, centroid, perimeter, vertex origins; section 10), polygon properties (orientation of the input rings, reflex vertices, convex hull, solidity), LOCATE, the summary, and assembling and writing the lines of the four output files.
- rational: representation and arithmetic of rational points (building the coordinates of crossings, reduction), cross, comparison by point order, and fraction and point notation.
- intersections: segment predicates and crossings (whether a point lies on a segment, whether two segments touch or overlap, the positions t, u of section 7 (1), and the intersection of two lines in section 11).
- greiner_hormann: the Greiner-Hormann algorithm (sections 7 and 8) (node lists with inserted crossings, entering/exiting classification, inversion and traversal, handling of rings without crossings, the list in crossing order, and n_a, n_b for the relation).
- rings: ring operations (even-odd test, interior of a set of rings, whether a point lies on an edge of a ring, rotating a ring, orientation and reversal, assembling the result in section 9 (depth, outer boundaries and holes, hole assignment, sorting), ring area and perimeter).
- render: the RENDER grid (cell centers and characters, character counts).

[Constraints]
- polygons.txt has at most 3000 lines, and each line has at most 100 characters. There are at most 100 blocks. A block without format errors has at most 10 rings, and each ring has at most 200 vertices.
- operations.txt has at most 200 operations, and each line has at most 100 characters.
- A cell center of RENDER never lies on an edge of a ring of the target's result or on an edge of the operands A, B.
- In section 11, a point p (prev or cur) with cross(c1, c2, p) = 0 never occurs.
- In the given data, the exact value (the value of the formulas of this statement computed without error) of every output real number written with 3 digits after the decimal point is at least 10^-6 away from a rounding boundary, and that of every one written with 6 digits is at least 10^-8 away. Also, the absolute value of the area of every result ring is at least 0.02. Therefore, differences in the order of additions, differences in the last digit of square roots, fused multiply-add, and the like do not change the output. Nevertheless, compute with the formulas and orders specified in this statement.
- The rational arithmetic can be done exactly with 64-bit integers as follows. Represent a point by an integer triple (X, Y, D) (D > 0, and the point is (X / D, Y / D); if it is reduced so that the greatest common divisor of X, Y, D is 1, the same point always has the same triple). Input vertices have D = 1. A crossing of section 7 (1) can be represented, after adjusting the signs of den and the numerators of t and u so that den is positive, by D = den, X = p1_x * den + (numerator of t) * r_x, Y = p1_y * den + (numerator of t) * r_y. Since coordinates are between 0 and 1000 inclusive, D <= 2 * 10^6, |X| <= 1000 * D, |Y| <= 1000 * D, and the values appearing in a comparison of the coordinates of two points (for example, comparing Y1 * D2 with Y2 * D1) are less than 4 * 10^15. Every edge of a result ring and every edge of the intermediate lists of section 11 lies on a line containing an edge of an original polygon (or an edge of W). Therefore, by remembering for each edge the integer endpoints of its original edge, the signs of cross used for the even-odd test and for the test of whether a point lies on an edge, the orientation of a ring (the sign of cross(v_n, v_1, v_2) equals the sign of the cross product of the original edge vectors giving the directions of edges v_n v_1 and v_1 v_2), and the intersections of section 11 (intersections of two lines, whose D is the cross product of the two original edge vectors) can all be computed with integers whose absolute values are less than 2^53. floor(v + 1/2) is floor((2 * X + D) / (2 * D)).
