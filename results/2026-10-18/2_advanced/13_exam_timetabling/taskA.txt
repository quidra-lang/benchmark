[Overview]
Write a program that automatically builds a university final-exam timetable. It reads the files of courses, enrolments, rooms, the calendar (the slots of the exam period and various directives) and the students to query from the input/ folder. It first validates the input and builds a conflict graph that joins courses taken by the same student. Next, it assigns each course's exam to a slot by combining the DSatur method (assigning slots to courses in decreasing order of saturation) with backtracking limited in count. In each slot, rooms are assigned by putting courses, in decreasing order of enrolment count, into the first room they fit in (first-fit decreasing). Courses that could not be assigned are later tried for insertion. Then the penalty caused by each student's back-to-back exams, exams on the same day and so on is reduced by slot exchanges and Kempe chain swaps. The final timetable, the room and seat assignment, the history of the penalty, the exam schedules of the queried students, the status of each course and a processing report are written to six files in the output/ folder. Nothing is printed to standard output.

[Input files]
The common format is as follows.
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and each line ends with a newline (LF). There are no blank lines.
- In a file with the .csv extension, line 1 is the header line (its content is fixed as shown below), and lines 2 onwards are data lines, one record per line. Fields are separated by a single comma (,), and there are no quotes and no spaces before or after fields.
- Line numbers in a file count line 1 as 1 (in a .csv file the header line is line 1 and the first data line is line 2).
- Course IDs, student IDs and room IDs are strings of 1 to 8 characters consisting of uppercase letters and digits.
- Numeric fields are decimal integers >= 0 without extra leading zeros.
- Course names are strings of 1 to 40 characters consisting of letters, digits and spaces, and do not start or end with a space. The course name is always the last field of its line.
- In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes. The symbols < and > themselves appear in neither the input nor the output.

(1) input/courses.csv (courses)
Header line: course,duration,name
The fields are the course ID, the exam duration (minutes) and the course name.

(2) input/enrolments.csv (enrolments)
Header line: student,course
The fields are the student ID and the course ID. A line means that the student takes the exam of the course.

(3) input/rooms.csv (rooms)
Header line: room,capacity
The fields are the room ID and the capacity (number of seats).

(4) input/calendar.txt (calendar)
The tokens on each line are separated by a single space. Lines 1 to 6 have the following form (these six lines are not validated).
days <D>
periods <M1> <M2> ... <MP>
backtrack_limit <L>
weights <W1> <W2> <W3> <W4>
front_load <F> <T>
invigilator_ratio <R>
<D> is the number of days of the exam period. The periods line lists the length (minutes) of each period of a day in order from period 1, and their count P is the number of periods per day. <L> is the upper limit on the dead-end count per attempt (section 6), <W1> to <W4> are the penalty weights (section 8), <F> and <T> are the number of front-load courses and the deadline slot number (sections 3 and 8), and <R> is the number of examinees one invigilator looks after (section 11).
From line 7 onwards come the following four kinds of lines, zero or more of each, in this order: the closed lines, the unavailable lines, the fixed lines, the after lines.
closed <day> <period>
unavailable <student ID> <day> <period>
fixed <course ID> <day> <period>
after <course ID1> <course ID2>
<day> and <period> are integers between 0 and 99 inclusive and may be out of range. A closed line means that the slot is closed, an unavailable line means that the student cannot take an exam in the slot, a fixed line means that the course's exam is fixed to the slot, and an after line means that the exam of course ID1 is held in a later slot than the exam of course ID2 (an order constraint).

(5) input/queries.txt (queries)
One student ID per line.

[Processing]
1. Common rules
- ID order: IDs are compared in lexicographic order by character code (ASCII), comparing from the first character (digits are smaller than letters, and when one ID equals the beginning of the other, the shorter one is smaller). Every "in increasing order of ID" uses this order.
- Slots: period p (1 <= p <= P) of day d (1 <= d <= D) is called a slot, and its slot number is (d - 1) * P + p. Slot numbers run from 1 to D * P. Below, a slot is identified by its slot number: "slot s" means the slot whose slot number is s, and "in increasing order of slot" means in increasing order of slot number. The name of slot s is "D<d>P<p>" (for example, when P = 3, the name of slot 8 is D3P2). The length of slot s is the length Mp (minutes) of period p, and the day of slot s is d.
- Room order: the order of the rooms sorted in increasing order of capacity, and for equal capacity in increasing order of room ID. Whenever rooms are listed, this order is used.
- Ceiling and rounding: for an integer a >= 0 and a positive integer b, "ceil(a / b)" is the smallest integer >= a / b, "floor(a / b)" is the largest integer <= a / b, and "100 * a / b rounded" is floor((200 * a + b) / (2 * b)). Do every computation exactly in integers.

2. Validating the input (courses.csv, enrolments.csv, rooms.csv)
Examine the data lines one at a time: the files in the order courses.csv, enrolments.csv, rooms.csv, and within each file in increasing order of line number. For each line, check the conditions listed below in the order listed; if a condition holds, the line is rejected as an error at the first condition that holds, and "<file name> line <line number>: <message>" (<file name> is the name without the folder, such as courses.csv) is recorded as an error. A line that matches none of the conditions is accepted. Only accepted lines are used in the rest of the processing.

* courses.csv
1. A line with the same course ID has already been accepted: duplicate course <course ID>
From here on, the courses of the accepted lines are simply called "courses".

* enrolments.csv
1. The course ID is not a course (not accepted in courses.csv): unknown course <course ID>
2. A line with the same pair of student ID and course ID has already been accepted: duplicate enrolment <student ID> <course ID>

* rooms.csv
1. A line with the same room ID has already been accepted: duplicate room <room ID>
2. The capacity is less than 1: bad capacity <capacity>

3. Course classification and the conflict graph
- Enrolled students: the enrolled students of course c are the students of the accepted enrolments.csv lines whose course is c, and their number is the enrolment count of c. From here on, a "student" means a student ID that has one or more accepted enrolments.csv lines. A student's courses are the courses in which the student is an enrolled student.
- Empty course: a course whose enrolment count is 0.
- Oversized course: a course whose enrolment count is greater than the maximum capacity of the accepted rooms.
- Schedulable course: a course that is neither an empty course nor an oversized course. Only schedulable courses are put into the timetable.
- Conflict graph: the graph whose vertices are the courses with an enrolment count of 1 or more (including oversized courses), in which two courses that have one or more common enrolled students are joined by an edge. Two courses joined by an edge are called neighbours of each other, and the number of their common enrolled students is called the shared count of the two courses. The degree of a course is the number of its neighbours (oversized courses are counted too). The degree of an empty course is 0.
- Front-load courses: the first F courses (all of them if there are fewer than F schedulable courses) when the schedulable courses are sorted in decreasing order of enrolment count, and for equal counts in increasing order of course ID. This sequence is called the front-load order.

4. Slot suitability and packing
- Suitable: slot s is said to be suitable for course c when the length of slot s is greater than or equal to the exam duration of c and no enrolled student of c is unable to take slot s (a pair of student and slot from an unavailable line accepted in section 5).
- Packing: the packing of a set G of courses is done as follows. Each room has "remaining seats" (initially its capacity) and an "exam duration" (initially undetermined). Take the courses of G one at a time in decreasing order of enrolment count, and for equal counts in increasing order of course ID (this order is called the packing order), examine the rooms in room order, and put the course into the first room whose remaining seats are greater than or equal to the course's enrolment count n and whose exam duration is undetermined or equal to the course's exam duration. In the room it was put into, the course's seats are the n seats starting from seat number "capacity - remaining seats + 1" (from the first seat to the last seat); then subtract n from the room's remaining seats and set the room's exam duration to the course's exam duration. If there is even one course that cannot be put into any room, the packing fails (if G is empty, it succeeds). In other words, several courses can be put into one room, but only courses with equal exam durations.

5. Validating the calendar (calendar.txt)
Examine lines 7 onwards of calendar.txt one at a time in increasing order of line number. For each line, check the conditions listed below for its kind in the order listed; if a condition holds, the line is rejected at the first condition that holds, and "calendar.txt line <line number>: <message>" is recorded as an error. A line that matches none of the conditions is accepted.
Below, "out of range" means that <day> is not between 1 and D inclusive, or <period> is not between 1 and P inclusive. When the line is not out of range, let s be the slot of that day and period. <day> and <period> in the messages are the values as written in the file.

* closed
1. Out of range: slot <day> <period> outside calendar
2. Slot s is already closed (there is an already accepted closed line for it): duplicate closed <name of s>
The slots of the accepted closed lines are called closed slots, and the other slots are called open slots.

* unavailable
1. The student ID is not a student: unknown student <student ID>
2. Out of range: slot <day> <period> outside calendar
3. A line with the same pair of student ID and slot s has already been accepted: duplicate unavailable <student ID> <name of s>

* fixed
1. The course ID is not a course: unknown course <course ID>
2. The course is not a schedulable course: course <course ID> not schedulable
3. Out of range: slot <day> <period> outside calendar
4. Slot s is a closed slot: slot <name of s> closed
5. Slot s is not suitable for this course: slot <name of s> unsuitable for <course ID>
6. A line with the same course ID has already been accepted: duplicate fixed <course ID>
7. Among the courses already fixed to slot s (the courses of already accepted fixed lines whose slot is s) there is a neighbour of this course: conflict <course ID> <the smallest course ID among such neighbours>
8. The packing of the set consisting of the courses already fixed to slot s together with this course fails: rooms full <name of s>
The courses of the accepted fixed lines are called fixed courses, and their slot is called the fixed slot of the course.

* after (let A be course ID1 and B be course ID2)
1. A or B is not a course: unknown course <the first one found when checking A, then B>
2. A or B is not a schedulable course: course <the first one found when checking A, then B> not schedulable
3. A and B are the same: bad order <A> <B>
4. A line with the same pair of A and B (in the same order) has already been accepted: duplicate order <A> <B>
5. A cycle is formed: cycle <A> <B>
6. A and B are both fixed courses, and the fixed slot of A is less than or equal to the fixed slot of B: fixed order <A> <B>
Condition 5 means that, when each already accepted after line (X, Y) is regarded as an arrow from X to Y, A can be reached from B by following zero or more arrows. An accepted after line is called the order constraint "A after B". It means that the slot of A must be greater than the slot of B.

6. Building the timetable
A timetable assigns one slot to each of some schedulable courses. A course that has a slot in the timetable is called assigned, and the slot of an assigned course is simply called "the course's slot". A timetable is said to satisfy the order constraints when, for every order constraint "A after B" in which both courses are assigned, the slot of A is greater than the slot of B.
- Candidate slots: for a course c that is not assigned, a slot s that satisfies all of the following four conditions is called a candidate slot of c.
  1. s is an open slot and is suitable for c.
  2. Among the neighbours of c there is no assigned course whose slot is s.
  3. For every order constraint involving c whose other course is assigned: for "c after x", s is greater than the slot of x, and for "x after c", s is less than the slot of x.
  4. The packing of the set consisting of all assigned courses whose slot is s together with c succeeds.
- Saturation: the saturation of course c is the number of distinct values among the slots of the neighbours of c that are assigned.
- Attempt: for a subset U of the schedulable courses, an attempt means starting from the timetable in which only the fixed courses are assigned, each to its fixed slot, setting the dead-end count to 0, and performing the following "search" once.
  (a) If all courses of U are assigned, return success.
  (b) Among the courses of U that are not assigned, choose the one with the largest saturation, if equal the one with the largest degree, and if that is also equal the one with the smallest course ID, and call it c. Record the saturation of c at this moment, find all candidate slots of c and sort them in increasing order.
  (c) Try the candidate slots in order from the first. Assign c to the slot and perform the search (recursively). If it returns success, return success, keeping the assignment of c. If it returns failure, undo the assignment of c and go on to the next candidate slot.
  (d) When all candidate slots have been tried (including the case where there is no candidate slot), a dead end has been reached at c: increase the dead-end count by 1. If the dead-end count exceeds L, abort the attempt immediately (every search in progress ends there). If it does not exceed L, return failure.
  The attempt succeeds if the first search returns success, and fails if it returns failure or is aborted. In a failed attempt, the course at which the last dead end was reached is the dead-end course of the attempt. In a successful attempt, the courses chosen in (b) by the searches at each level that returned success, listed in the order they were chosen, form the assignment order (for each course of the assignment order, its slot and its recorded saturation are used in the output).
- Repeating attempts: let the excluded set X be the empty set, and perform an attempt with U = "the schedulable courses minus the fixed courses and the courses in X". If the attempt fails, add its dead-end course to X and redo the attempt. Repeat this until an attempt succeeds (if U is empty, the attempt succeeds immediately). The timetable of the successful attempt is used in the rest of the processing, and the courses of the final X are called excluded courses.

7. Insertion
Process the excluded courses one at a time in increasing order of course ID. Find the candidate slots (section 6) of course c in the timetable at that moment, and:
- If there are candidate slots, assign c to the candidate slot for which the penalty (section 8) of the timetable with c assigned to that slot is the smallest, and for equal penalties the smaller slot. Then record c, that slot and the penalty increase (penalty after the assignment - penalty before the assignment) as an insertion.
- If there is no candidate slot, do not assign c, and for every slot s from slot 1 to slot D * P, check the following reasons from the top and record the first one that applies.
  1. s is a closed slot: closed
  2. The length of s is shorter than the exam duration of c: too_short
  3. Among the enrolled students of c there is a student who cannot take slot s: unavailable <the smallest student ID among such students>
  4. Among the neighbours of c there is one whose slot is s: conflict <course ID> shared <shared count> (for the one among such neighbours with the largest shared count with c, and for equal counts the one with the smallest course ID)
  5. If the slot of c were s, among the order constraints involving c whose other course is assigned there is one that does not satisfy condition 3 of the candidate slots in section 6: order <the smallest course ID among such other courses>
  6. Otherwise (the packing fails): rooms
After the insertion, the excluded courses that are not assigned and the oversized courses are called unscheduled courses. From here on, no course is ever added to the timetable.

8. Penalty
The penalty of a timetable is defined as follows.
- The relation of two slots x < y is classified as follows. If x and y are on the same day and y - x = 1, it is back-to-back (back_to_back); if x and y are on the same day and y - x >= 2, it is same-day (same_day); if x and y are on different days and y - x = 1 (x is the last period of a day and y is the first period of the next day), it is overnight (overnight). Otherwise it is unclassified. Closed slots are also counted according to their slot numbers.
- Penalty pairs: when two assigned courses a and b are neighbours of each other and the relation of their slots is not unclassified, (a, b) is called a penalty pair, and its shared count k is called its strength. The "day" of a penalty pair is the day of the smaller of the two slots.
- Let N1, N2 and N3 be the sums of the strengths of the back-to-back, same-day and overnight penalty pairs respectively. This is the same as taking, for each student, the number of pairs (two at a time) of the student's assigned courses that fall into each class, and summing over all students.
- Late: the front-load courses that are assigned and whose slot is greater than T are the late courses, and their number is N4.
- Penalty = W1 * N1 + W2 * N2 + W3 * N3 + W4 * N4.
- Student penalty: for each student, classify each pair (two at a time) of the student's assigned courses by the relation of their slots, let n1, n2 and n3 be the numbers of back-to-back, same-day and overnight pairs, and take W1 * n1 + W2 * n2 + W3 * n3 (lateness is not included).
- Course contribution: for an assigned course c, the sum over the penalty pairs containing c of "the weight of its class (one of W1, W2, W3) * strength", plus W4 if c is a late course. The contribution of a course that is not assigned is 0.
The penalty of the timetable at the end of the insertion is called the initial penalty.

9. Slot exchange
Repeat the following "round".
- In one round, examine pairs (h, t) one at a time: the open slots h in increasing order, and for each h the open slots t with t > h in increasing order. Judge each pair in the following order.
  1. Slot h or slot t has a fixed course: fixed
  2. The timetable in which the slot of every course whose slot is h is changed to t, and the slot of every course whose slot is t is changed to h, is the trial. Among the courses whose slot changed in the trial there is one for which the new slot is not suitable (section 4), or the trial does not satisfy the order constraints (section 6): invalid
  3. The penalty of the trial is greater than or equal to the penalty of the current timetable: worse
  4. Otherwise: applied. The trial becomes the current timetable, and the exchange (h, t, penalty before the exchange, penalty after the exchange) is recorded. The following pairs are examined with this new timetable.
  Since the course set of each slot is merely swapped as it is, the packing is not checked.
- If applied occurred once or more in the round, perform the next round; if it never occurred, the slot exchange ends (the last round, with no applied, is also counted in the number of rounds).

10. Kempe chain swaps
- Kempe chain: for an assigned course c whose slot is h and a slot t different from h, the set of all courses (including c) that can be reached from c by following neighbours while passing only through assigned courses whose slot is h or t is called the Kempe chain K(c, h, t). Changing the slot of the courses of K whose slot is h to t, and the slot of those whose slot is t to h, is called swapping K.
- Repeat the following "pass". In one pass, list the courses that are assigned and are not fixed courses at the start of the pass in increasing order of course ID; for each such course c, let h be the slot of c at that moment, and examine the open slots t (t != h) in increasing order. Judge each t (called a candidate) in the following order.
  1. K(c, h, t) contains a fixed course: fixed
  2. The timetable with K(c, h, t) swapped is the trial. Among the courses of K there is one for which the new slot is not suitable, or the trial does not satisfy the order constraints: invalid
  3. In the trial, the packing of the set of courses whose slot is h, or of the set of courses whose slot is t, fails: rooms
  4. The penalty of the trial is greater than or equal to the penalty of the current timetable: worse
  5. Otherwise: applied. The trial becomes the current timetable, and the swap (c, h, t, the slots before and after the swap of each course of K, penalty before the swap, penalty after the swap) is recorded. No further t is examined for this c; go on to the next course.
- If applied occurred once or more in the pass, perform the next pass; if it never occurred, finish (the last pass, with no applied, is also counted in the number of passes).
The timetable at this point is called the final timetable, and its penalty the final penalty. The outputs below refer to the final timetable unless stated otherwise.

11. Room and seat assignment
For each open slot of the final timetable, pack (section 4) the set of courses of that slot and determine each course's room and seats (from the first seat to the last seat). A student's seat number is "the first seat of the course + the number of the course's enrolled students who come before that student when they are sorted in increasing order of student ID".
The invigilator count of a slot is the sum, over the rooms into which one or more courses were put in that slot, of "ceil(total enrolment count of the courses put into that room / R)".

[Output files]
Write the following six files into the output/ folder. In every file, the items within a line are separated by a single space (by a single comma in csv files; the lines of fixed-width tables use the widths given below), and no line has trailing spaces. Only the lines that are specified below as written after "two spaces" start with spaces. Every line ends with a newline (LF), and no blank lines are output. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros (negative numbers do not occur). <slot name> is the name of a slot (D<d>P<p>).

(1) output/timetable.txt
Line 1 is "TIMETABLE days <D> periods <P> open <number of open slots> courses <number of courses> scheduled <number of assigned courses in the final timetable> unscheduled <number of unscheduled courses> empty <number of empty courses>".
From line 2 comes a grid by day. Each line writes a label left-aligned in width 4 (padded with spaces on the right), followed by P columns and a TOTAL column, each right-aligned in width 10 (padded with spaces on the left).
- Header line: the label is DAY, and the columns are P1, P2, ..., P<P>, TOTAL.
- Line for day d = 1, 2, ..., D: the label is D<d>; the column for period p is closed if that slot is a closed slot, and otherwise "<number of courses in the slot>/<total enrolment count of the courses in the slot>". The TOTAL column is "<total number of courses on that day>/<total enrolment count on that day>".
Then output the following for slot 1 to slot D * P in order.
- Closed slot: SLOT <slot number> <slot name> minutes <slot length> closed
- Open slot: SLOT <slot number> <slot name> minutes <slot length> exams <number of courses> students <total enrolment count> rooms <number of rooms used> seats <total capacity of the rooms used> invigilators <invigilator count>
  A room used is a room into which one or more courses were put in that slot. Then, for the courses of that slot in packing order, output one line each, consisting of two spaces followed by "<course ID> <exam duration>min students <enrolment count> room <room ID> seats <first seat>-<last seat><fixed> <course name>". <fixed> is " fixed" (with one leading space) for a fixed course, and the empty string otherwise.
Then, for each room in room order, output "ROOM <room ID> capacity <capacity> slots <number of slots in which the room was used> exams <number of courses put into the room> seated <total enrolment count of the courses put into the room> utilization <utilization>". <utilization> is "100 * <seated value> / (capacity * number of open slots) rounded".
Finally, output "INVIGILATORS total <sum of the invigilator counts of all slots> ratio <R>".

(2) output/rooms.csv
Line 1 is the header line "slot,room,course,students,first_seat,last_seat". Then output one line "<slot name>,<room ID>,<course ID>,<enrolment count>,<first seat>,<last seat>" each, for the open slots in increasing order, within each slot in room order, and within the same room in increasing order of first seat.

(3) output/penalties.txt
Below, <breakdown> is "back_to_back <N1> same_day <N2> overnight <N3> front_load <N4> penalty <penalty>" (the values of section 8). Output the following in this order.
- WEIGHTS back_to_back <W1> same_day <W2> overnight <W3> front_load <W4>
- FRONT_LOAD courses <number of front-load courses> last_slot <name of T>
- INITIAL <breakdown of the timetable at the end of the insertion>
- For each round of the slot exchange, first output "ROUND <round number> pairs <number of pairs examined> fixed <count> invalid <count> worse <count> applied <count>" (each <count> is the number of pairs in that round with that judgement), then the exchanges recorded in that round, in the order recorded, in the form "EXCHANGE <serial number> <name of h> <name of t> penalty <penalty before the exchange> -> <penalty after the exchange>". Round numbers and serial numbers each count from 1 (serial numbers continue across rounds).
- EXCHANGED <breakdown of the timetable at the end of the slot exchange>
- For each pass of the Kempe chain swaps, first output "PASS <pass number> candidates <number of candidates examined> fixed <count> invalid <count> rooms <count> worse <count> applied <count>", then the swaps recorded in that pass, in the order recorded, in the form "SWAP <serial number> <course ID of course c> <name of h> <name of t> chain <number of courses in K> penalty <penalty before the swap> -> <penalty after the swap>", and immediately after each such line, for the courses of K in increasing order of course ID, one line each, consisting of two spaces followed by "MOVE <course ID> <name of the slot before the swap> -> <name of the slot after the swap>". Pass numbers and serial numbers each count from 1 (serial numbers continue across passes).
- FINAL <breakdown of the final timetable>
- SAVED <initial penalty - final penalty>
- For each day d = 1, 2, ..., D: "DAY D<d> back_to_back <count> same_day <count> overnight <count>". Each <count> is the sum of the strengths of the penalty pairs whose day is d and that are of that class.
- For each late course, in front-load order: "LATE <course ID> <slot name> students <enrolment count>".
- Among the first 10 assigned courses (all of them if there are fewer than 10) when the assigned courses are sorted in decreasing order of contribution, and for equal contributions in increasing order of course ID, for those whose contribution is greater than 0, in that order: "COURSE <course ID> <slot name> penalty <contribution>".
- Compute the student penalty of every student, and for each value that occurs, in increasing order of value: "HISTOGRAM <value> <number of students with that value>".
- For each student, sort the slots of the student's assigned courses in increasing order, and call the difference of each two adjacent slots (larger - smaller) a gap. Over the gaps of all students, for each value that occurs, in increasing order of value: "GAP <gap> <number of occurrences of that value>".
- For each student who has one or more assigned courses, find the maximum number of the student's assigned courses taken on one day, and for each value that occurs, in increasing order of value: "DAYLOAD <maximum> <number of students with that value>".
- Among the first 5 students (all of them if there are fewer than 5) when the students are sorted in decreasing order of student penalty, and for equal penalties in increasing order of student ID, for the students whose student penalty is greater than 0, in that order: "WORST <student ID> penalty <student penalty> back_to_back <n1> same_day <n2> overnight <n3>".

(4) output/student_schedules.txt
For each student ID in queries.txt, in the order written (each time, if the same student ID is written more than once), output the following.
- A student ID that is not a student: STUDENT <student ID> not found
- A student: line 1 is "STUDENT <student ID> exams <number of the student's courses> scheduled <number of the student's assigned courses> penalty <student penalty>". Then output the following lines, each consisting of two spaces followed by:
  1. For each of the student's assigned courses, in increasing order of slot: <slot name> <course ID> room <room ID> seat <seat number> <course name>
  2. For each of the student's courses that is not assigned, in increasing order of course ID: UNSCHEDULED <course ID> <course name>
  3. For each pair (two at a time) of the student's assigned courses whose slot relation is not unclassified, in increasing order of the smaller slot, and for equal smaller slots in increasing order of the larger slot: <class> <name of the smaller slot> <its course ID> <name of the larger slot> <its course ID>. <class> is BACK_TO_BACK for back-to-back, SAME_DAY for same-day and OVERNIGHT for overnight.
  4. For each slot of the student's accepted unavailable lines, in increasing order of slot: UNAVAILABLE <slot name>

(5) output/course_status.csv
Line 1 is the header line "course,status,slot,students,minutes,degree,penalty". Then output every course, in increasing order of course ID, in the form "<course ID>,<status>,<slot>,<enrolment count>,<exam duration>,<degree>,<contribution>". <status> is fixed for a fixed course, scheduled for any other assigned course, too_large for an oversized course, no_slot for an excluded course that could not be assigned, and empty for an empty course. <slot> is the slot name if the course is assigned, and - otherwise.

(6) output/report.txt
Output the following in this order.
- INPUT courses <number of courses> enrolments <number of accepted enrolments.csv lines> rooms <number of accepted rooms> students <number of students> errors <number of errors>
- For each recorded error, in the order recorded: "ERROR <recorded error>".
- GRAPH courses <number of vertices V of the conflict graph> edges <number of edges E> shared <sum of the shared counts of all edges> density <density>. <density> is floor(2000 * E / (V * (V - 1))) if V is 2 or more, and 0 otherwise.
- For the first 5 vertices (all of them if there are fewer than 5) when the vertices of the conflict graph are sorted in decreasing order of degree, and for equal degrees in increasing order of course ID, in that order: "DEGREE <course ID> neighbours <degree> shared <sum of the shared counts of the course with all its neighbours> students <enrolment count>".
- COMPONENTS <number of connected components of the conflict graph>. Then sort the connected components in decreasing order of number of courses, and for equal numbers in increasing order of the smallest course ID in the component, number them from 1, and output one line each: "COMPONENT <number> size <number of courses> first <smallest course ID in the component>".
- CLIQUE size <k> <course ID1> <course ID2> ... <course IDk>. Starting with all schedulable courses as the candidates, repeat "choose the candidate with the largest degree (if equal, the one with the smallest course ID), append it to the sequence, and narrow the candidates down to those that are neighbours of that course" until the candidates are empty; output the resulting sequence (in the order chosen) and its length k.
- LOAD max_exams <maximum number of courses of a student> students <number of students attaining that maximum>
- SEATS demand <total enrolment count of the schedulable courses> capacity <total capacity of the accepted rooms> min_slots <ceil(demand value / capacity value)>
- Two lines, HEURISTIC largest_degree slots <k1> and HEURISTIC largest_enrolment slots <k2>. Sort the schedulable courses in decreasing order of degree for k1 and in decreasing order of enrolment count for k2 (in both cases, for equal values in increasing order of course ID), and in that order give each course as its number "the smallest positive integer not given to any of its neighbours already numbered"; the value is the maximum of the numbers (slot lengths, closures, rooms and so on are not considered).
- The MATRIX table. The table courses are the first 8 vertices (all of them if there are fewer than 8) when the vertices of the conflict graph are sorted in decreasing order of enrolment count, and for equal counts in increasing order of course ID. The header line is MATRIX left-aligned in width 9, followed by the course IDs of the table courses, each right-aligned in width 9. Then, for each table course, output a line consisting of its course ID left-aligned in width 9, followed by the cells for each table course, each right-aligned in width 9. A cell is - for the same course, and otherwise the shared count (0 if they are not neighbours).
- For each empty course, in increasing order of course ID: "EMPTY <course ID>".
- For each oversized course, in increasing order of course ID: "TOO_LARGE <course ID> students <enrolment count> largest_room <maximum capacity>".
- For each closed slot, in increasing order of slot: "CLOSED <slot name>".
- For each fixed course, in increasing order of course ID: "FIXED <course ID> <name of the fixed slot>".
- For each order constraint, in the order accepted: "AFTER <A> <B>".
- For each attempt, in the order performed: "ATTEMPT <number> courses <number of courses in U> backtracks <dead-end count> <result>". Numbers count from 1. <result> is success for a successful attempt, and "failed <dead-end course>" for a failed one. The dead-end count is the value at the end of the attempt (L + 1 for an aborted attempt).
- For each course of the assignment order of the successful attempt, in that order, numbered from 1: "ORDER <number> <course ID> <slot name> saturation <recorded saturation> degree <degree>". <slot name> is the slot assigned to that course in that attempt.
- For each recorded insertion, in the order recorded: "INSERTED <course ID> <slot name> penalty <penalty increase>".
- For each unscheduled course, in increasing order of course ID: "UNSCHEDULED <course ID> reason <reason> students <enrolment count>". <reason> is too_large for an oversized course, and no_slot for an excluded course that could not be assigned. For a no_slot course, then output the per-slot reasons recorded in section 7, from slot 1 in order, one line each, consisting of two spaces followed by "<slot name> <recorded reason>".
- SUMMARY scheduled <number of assigned courses in the final timetable> unscheduled <number of unscheduled courses> slots_used <number of slots with one or more courses in the final timetable> penalty <final penalty>

[Source files]
Organize the program into the following six source files (name the files according to the conventions of each language).
- main: holds the overall flow and the core of the processing. It reads calendar.txt and queries.txt, validates the calendar, classifies the courses, decides slot suitability and candidate slots, repeats the attempts, performs the insertion, computes penalties, performs the slot exchange and the Kempe chain swaps, does the final room and seat assignment, computes the various statistics, and builds the lines of all output files.
- enrolment: reads the .csv files, validates courses.csv and enrolments.csv, and builds the roster of enrolled students of each course.
- conflict_graph: builds the conflict graph and handles connected components, the greedy clique, the Kempe chain search, and the reachability test that follows the arrows of the order constraints.
- coloring: performs the search of an attempt (the DSatur method with backtracking limited in count) and the greedy numbering used for comparison.
- rooms: reads and validates rooms.csv, sorts the rooms into room order, and performs packing.
- report: handles writing to files, formatting the lines of fixed-width tables, and computing percentages with rounding.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= D <= 7, 1 <= P <= 5, 30 <= Mp <= 300. 1 <= L <= 1000, 0 <= W1, W2, W3, W4 <= 100, 0 <= F <= 20, 1 <= T <= D * P, 1 <= R <= 100.
- The number of lines of each file: at most 100 data lines in courses.csv, at most 3000 data lines in enrolments.csv, at most 20 data lines in rooms.csv, at most 100 lines in calendar.txt, and 1 to 20 lines in queries.txt.
- Exam durations are between 30 and 300, and the exam duration of every accepted course is less than or equal to the maximum of M1, ..., MP. Capacities are at most 1000.
- Considering only the accepted lines, there is at least one room, at least one student, at least one schedulable course and at least one open slot, and each student has at most 12 courses.
- Every string in a grid column of timetable.txt is at most 9 characters long (so there is always a space between columns).
- The absolute value of every integer that appears in the computation is less than 2^31. The recursion depth of the search is at most the number of schedulable courses.
- All penalties are computed in integers, so the result does not depend on the order of computation. Every choice and every sort has a rule for ties, so the result is uniquely determined.
