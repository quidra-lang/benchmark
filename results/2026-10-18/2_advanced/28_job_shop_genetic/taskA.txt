[Overview]
Write a program that solves the job-shop scheduling problem with dispatching rules, a genetic algorithm and tabu search. Each job is a sequence of operations processed on several machines in a fixed order, and the goal is to make the time at which all operations have finished (the makespan) as small as possible. From the input/ folder, the program reads an instance file that describes the operations (machine and processing time) of each job, and a file of parameters for the genetic algorithm and other steps. First it checks the instance, removes invalid jobs, and computes three kinds of lower bounds on the makespan. Next it builds active schedules with the Giffler-Thompson method under four dispatching rules (SPT, LPT, MWKR, FIFO) and uses them as baselines for comparison. Then it minimizes the makespan with a genetic algorithm that uses MINSTD random numbers (chromosomes made of repeated job numbers, tournament selection, order-preserving crossover, swap and insertion mutation, elitism). The best solution obtained is made active by left shifts and then improved by a tabu search that swaps adjacent operations on the critical path. Finally it computes, for the final schedule, the critical path, the slack of the operations, the sensitivity of each machine, a Gantt chart and more. The results are written to the output/ folder as 4 files: the baseline schedules, the progress of the genetic algorithm, the final schedule, and the critical path. Nothing is written to standard output.

[Input files]
The common format is as follows.
- Both files consist of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a newline (LF). There are no empty lines, and there are no spaces at the beginning or end of a line. Items on a line are separated by a single space.
- Integers are non-negative decimal numbers without extra leading zeros.
- In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes. The symbols < > themselves appear in neither the input nor the output.

(1) input/instance.txt (the instance)
Line 1 is "<J> <M>". J is the number of job lines and M is the number of machines; the machines are numbered from 1 to M.
Lines 2 to J + 1 are the job lines. Line i + 1 (1 <= i <= J) is the line of job i, and i is called the job number. A job line consists of one or more tokens, and each token has the form "<machine number>:<processing time>" (two integers joined by a single colon). Each token represents one operation, and the job must perform its operations from left to right (in the order 1st operation, 2nd operation, ...). Before the check (Section 2), machine numbers and processing times may be out of range.

(2) input/ga_params.txt (the parameters)
The following 12 lines appear in this order.
seed <s>
population <P>
generations <G>
tournament <k>
crossover <a1>/<b1>
swap_mutation <a2>/<b2>
insert_mutation <a3>/<b3>
elitism <E>
checkpoints <g1> <g2> ... <gn>
tabu_iterations <T>
tabu_tenure <L>
gantt_width <W>
<s> is the random seed (Section 6), <P> is the population size, <G> is the number of generations, <k> is the tournament size, <a1>/<b1>, <a2>/<b2> and <a3>/<b3> are the fractions giving the probabilities of performing crossover, swap mutation and insertion mutation respectively, <E> is the number of elites (all of the above in Section 7), <g1> to <gn> are the numbers of the generations whose progress is output (in increasing order), <T> is the maximum number of tabu search iterations, <L> is the tabu tenure (both in Section 9), and <W> is the maximum number of columns of the Gantt chart (Section 11).

[Processing]
Section 1. Common rules and terms
- Valid job: a job whose job line was accepted by the check in Section 2. Below, "job" alone always means a valid job. "Job order" means increasing order of job number. Let n_j be the number of operations of job j, and let N be the total number of operations of all jobs.
- Operation: the k-th operation of job j is written (j, k), and its notation is "J<j>-<k>" (e.g. J3-2). The machine of operation o is mu(o), and its processing time is p(o). The notation of machine m is "M<m>". For operation (j, k), (j, k - 1) is called its job predecessor and (j, k + 1) its job successor (when they exist).
- head and tail: head(o) of operation o is the sum of the processing times of all operations of the same job that come before o, and tail(o) is the sum of the processing times of all operations of the same job that come after o (0 if there are none).
- Work and load: the work of job j is the sum of the processing times of all operations of job j. The load of machine m is the sum of the processing times of all operations whose machine is m.
- Job letter: the letter of job j is the j-th uppercase English letter (A for 1, B for 2, ..., L for 12).
- Percentage notation: for a non-negative integer a and a positive integer b, let t be floor((2000 * a + b) / (2 * b)), and write "<the quotient of t divided by 10>.<the remainder of t divided by 10>%". This is 100 * a / b rounded half up to one decimal place (e.g. a = 1, b = 3 gives 33.3%, and a = 0 gives 0.0%). Below, this is called "the percentage of a relative to b".
- Ceiling: for positive integers a and b, "ceil(a / b)" is the smallest integer that is >= a / b.
- Perform all computations exactly in integers.

Section 2. Checking the instance
Examine the job lines in increasing order of job number. In each line, examine the tokens from left to right, and for the k-th token "<m>:<d>" check the following conditions from top to bottom.
1. m is not between 1 and M inclusive: unknown machine <m>
2. d is not between 1 and 99 inclusive: bad duration <d>
3. a token to its left on the same line has machine number m: repeated machine <m>
When a token that meets a condition is found, do not examine the remaining tokens of that line; record "job <i> operation <k>: <message>" (<i> is the job number) as an error, and reject that line. A line in which no token meets any condition is accepted. Hence a valid job never uses the same machine twice or more.

Section 3. Lower bounds
- Job bound: the maximum work over the jobs. Among the jobs that attain the maximum, the one with the smallest number is the job of the job bound.
- Simple bound of machine m: "the minimum head of the operations whose machine is m" + "the load of machine m" + "the minimum tail of the operations whose machine is m".
- Jackson bound of machine m: for each operation o whose machine is m, consider a "task" with release time head(o), processing time p(o) and delivery time tail(o). The bound is found by the following procedure, which processes these tasks on a single machine with preemption allowed. Set the time t to 0, and set the remaining time of each task to its processing time. While there is a task whose remaining time is not 0, repeat (a) to (c) below.
  (a) If there is no task whose release time is <= t and whose remaining time is not 0, set t to the minimum release time of the tasks whose release time is greater than t.
  (b) Among the tasks whose release time is <= t and whose remaining time is not 0, select the one with the largest delivery time (ties: the smallest job number). If there is a task whose release time is greater than t, let t' be the minimum of those release times, and let delta be the smaller of the selected task's remaining time and t' - t. If there is no such task, delta is the selected task's remaining time. Add delta to t, and subtract delta from the selected task's remaining time.
  (c) If the selected task's remaining time has become 0, record the current t plus that task's delivery time.
  The maximum of the recorded values is the Jackson bound of machine m.
- The machine bound is the maximum of the simple bounds of all machines, and the Jackson bound (overall) is the maximum of the Jackson bounds of all machines. For each of them, among the machines that attain the maximum, the one with the smallest number is the machine of that bound.
- The lower bound LB is the maximum of the job bound, the machine bound and the Jackson bound.

Section 4. Definitions about schedules
- Schedule: an assignment of a start time S(o) (a non-negative integer) to every operation o. The completion time of o is C(o) = S(o) + p(o), and the makespan of the schedule is the maximum of C(o). In the schedules handled in this task, a job successor starts at or after the completion time of its job predecessor, and the processing times of operations on the same machine never overlap.
- Machine order: for each machine m, a sequence that lists all operations whose machine is m. The machine order of a schedule lists the operations of each machine in increasing order of start time. In the sequence of machine m, the operation immediately before operation o is called the machine predecessor of o, and the operation immediately after it is called the machine successor of o.
- Schedule of a machine order: given a machine order, the schedule in which the start time of each operation o is defined by "S(o) = the larger of the completion time of the job predecessor of o and the completion time of the machine predecessor of o (one that does not exist is treated as 0)". If the start times are determined one after another, each time for an operation whose job predecessor and machine predecessor already have their start times determined, the start times of all operations are determined uniquely (for the machine orders handled in this task, the start times of all operations are always determined). Below, "the makespan of a machine order" and similar phrases refer to the schedule of that machine order. Every schedule built in Section 5 and every schedule obtained by decoding in Section 7 coincides with the schedule of its own machine order.
- Critical path: for a schedule, let o be the operation with the smallest job number among the operations whose completion time equals the makespan, and repeat the following. If S(o) = 0, stop. Otherwise, if o has a machine predecessor and its completion time equals S(o), that machine predecessor becomes the new o; otherwise the job predecessor of o (whose completion time then equals S(o)) becomes the new o. The sequence of all operations visited (including the first o), arranged in the reverse of the visiting order (increasing order of start time), is the critical path. The "link" of each operation on the critical path is start for the first operation of the sequence; for every other operation it is machine if the previous operation in the sequence is its machine predecessor, and job if that operation is its job predecessor.
- Blocks: the critical path split into the parts in which consecutive operations have the same machine, arranged in sequence order. Each block consists of one or more operations.
- Bottleneck machine: the machine with the largest number of operations on the critical path (ties: the smallest machine number).
- Number of critical paths: there is an arrow from operation x to operation y when the completion time of x equals the start time of y and x is the job predecessor or the machine predecessor of y. The number of critical paths is the number of sequences of operations that start at an operation whose start time is 0, follow arrows one after another, and end at an operation whose completion time equals the makespan (an operation whose start time is 0 and whose completion time equals the makespan counts as one path by itself). It is obtained by computing, in increasing order of start time, a value for each operation y (1 if S(y) = 0, otherwise the sum of the values of the source operations of the arrows into y), and summing the values of the operations whose completion time equals the makespan.
- N1 neighbourhood: the move list of a schedule lists, for the blocks of the schedule in block order and within each block in sequence order, all pairs (u, v) of adjacent operations (u first) (a block of one operation produces no move). Applying a move (u, v) to a machine order means swapping the positions of u and v, which are adjacent in the sequence of their machine. The evaluation value of a move is the makespan of the machine order after the move is applied.
- Left shift: in a schedule, for the i-th operation o (i >= 2) in the sequence of machine m, let r be the completion time of the job predecessor of o (0 if there is none). As the candidate idle intervals of m, consider the interval [0, S(1st operation)) and, for each t = 1, 2, ..., i - 2, the interval [C(t-th operation), S((t + 1)-th operation)), in this order (if i = 2, the only candidate is the first interval). If there is an interval [x, y) for which "(the larger of x and r) + p(o) <= y" holds, o is said to be left-shiftable, and for the first such interval, the larger of x and r is the new start time of o. The insertion position of o is the beginning of the sequence if that interval is [0, ...), and immediately after the t-th operation if it is [C(t-th operation), ...). The first operation of a sequence is not left-shiftable.
- Slack: let Cmax be the makespan of the schedule. Define the latest start time LS(o) of each operation o, going from the operations with larger start times to those with smaller ones, as "the smaller of the LS of the job successor of o and the LS of the machine successor of o (considering only those that exist; Cmax if neither exists) - p(o)". The slack of o is LS(o) - S(o), and the free slack of o is "the smaller of the start time of the job successor of o and the start time of the machine successor of o (considering only those that exist; Cmax if neither exists) - C(o)". An operation whose slack is 0 is called a critical operation.
- Distance: for two schedules A and B consisting of the same operations and a machine m, the distance between A and B on machine m is the number of pairs of operations whose machine is m whose relative order in the machine order of A is the reverse of their relative order in the machine order of B. The sum over all machines is the distance between A and B.
- Machine and operation statistics: let Cmax be the makespan of the schedule. The idle time of machine m is Cmax - (the load of machine m), and its utilization is the percentage of the load of machine m relative to Cmax. For adjacent operations a, b (a first) in the sequence of machine m, when S(b) - C(a) is positive, that value is called a gap. The idle intervals of machine m are those of the interval [0, S(1st operation)), the interval [C(a), S(b)) of each gap, and the interval [C(last operation), Cmax) that have positive length, arranged in time order. The completion time of job j is the completion time of its last operation, and its waiting time is (completion time) - (work). The total flow time is the sum of the completion times of all jobs.

Section 5. Schedules by dispatching rules (the Giffler-Thompson method)
Keep, for each job, its "next operation" (initially the 1st operation) and its "ready time" (initially 0), and, for each machine, its "free time" (initially 0), and repeat (a) to (d) below until the start times of all operations are determined (one repetition is called a step).
(a) For the next operation o of each job that still has operations left, compute the earliest start time es(o) = (the larger of the job's ready time and the free time of machine mu(o)) and the earliest completion time ec(o) = es(o) + p(o).
(b) Let C* be the minimum of ec, and let m* be the smallest machine number among the machines of the operations with ec(o) = C*.
(c) The operations from (a) whose machine is m* and for which es(o) < C* form the conflict set (it is never empty).
(d) From the conflict set, select the operation with the smallest priority value under the rule (ties: the smallest job number), and set its start time to es(o). Set the ready time of the selected operation's job and the free time of machine m* to the completion time of that operation, and advance that job's next operation by one.
The priority value of operation o (the next operation of job j) under each rule is as follows.
- SPT: p(o)
- LPT: -p(o) (the operation with the largest processing time is selected)
- MWKR: -(the sum of the processing times of the operations of job j from o onward (including o))
- FIFO: the ready time of job j at that point
- GENES (used only in the active decoding of Section 8): the position of the gene corresponding to o in the GA best chromosome (Section 7)
The number of steps in which the conflict set had two or more operations is the decision count, and the number of steps in which two or more operations in the conflict set had the minimum priority value is the tie count.
The schedules of the four rules SPT, LPT, MWKR and FIFO are called the baseline schedules of the respective rules. For each baseline schedule, compute the makespan, the total flow time, the total idle time of all machines, the decision count, the tie count, the critical path, the number of critical paths, the bottleneck machine and the utilization of each machine, and also run the tabu search of Section 9 from its machine order.

Section 6. Random numbers (MINSTD)
MINSTD is used for random numbers. The generator has an integer state x whose initial value is s. "Draw one random number" means updating x to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647 and taking the updated x as the value. "Draw a random number at least 0 and less than m" means drawing one random number and taking the remainder of its value divided by m as the value. "Decide with probability a/b whether to perform it" means drawing one random number and deciding to perform it if the remainder of its value divided by b is less than a.
To "shuffle" a sequence c_1, c_2, ..., c_len of length len means, for i = len, len - 1, ..., 2 in this order, drawing a random number u at least 0 and less than i and swapping c_i and c_(u+1) (nothing changes when u + 1 = i).
There is only one generator in the whole program, and its state is never reinitialized. Random numbers are used only for creating the initial population and for producing new generations in Section 7, in the order described there.

Section 7. Genetic algorithm
- Chromosome: a sequence of job numbers of length N in which each job j appears exactly n_j times. Each element of the sequence is called a gene, and positions are numbered from 1 to N. When the gene at position i is j and it is the c-th occurrence of j among positions 1 to i, the gene is said to correspond to operation (j, c).
- Decoding: prepare, for each job, its "next operation" (initially the 1st operation) and its "ready time" (initially 0), and, for each machine, its "free time" (initially 0). Going through the chromosome from position 1 onward, for gene j, set the start time of the next operation o of job j to "the larger of the ready time of job j and the free time of machine mu(o)", set the ready time of job j and the free time of mu(o) to the completion time of o, and advance the next operation of job j by one. The schedule obtained in this way is the decoded schedule of the chromosome, and its makespan is the evaluation value of the chromosome.
- Chromosome of a schedule: the sequence obtained by arranging all operations of the schedule in increasing order of start time (ties: increasing job number) and replacing each operation by its job number.
- Population: a sequence of P chromosomes (individuals), given the individual numbers 1 to P from the front. The generations run from generation 0 to generation G. The best individual of a generation is the individual with the smallest evaluation value (ties: the smallest individual number).
- Initial population (generation 0): individuals 1 to 4 are the chromosomes of the baseline schedules of SPT, LPT, MWKR and FIFO, respectively. Individuals 5 to P are created one at a time, starting from individual 5, each by preparing a fresh basic sequence (for each job j in job order, the number j repeated n_j times) and shuffling it.
- In each generation g (g = 0, 1, ..., G), first compute the evaluation values of all individuals. If g < G, then build the population of generation g + 1 as follows (no new generation is built after generation G has been evaluated).
  1. Elites: arrange the individuals of generation g in increasing order of evaluation value (ties: increasing individual number); the first E of them become individuals 1 to E of generation g + 1, in that order.
  2. Create the remaining P - E individuals by repeating (a) to (d) below (P - E) / 2 times.
    (a) Select parent 1 by tournament selection, and then parent 2.
    (b) Decide with probability a1/b1 whether to perform crossover. If it is performed, create child 1 and child 2 by crossover. If not, child 1 is a copy of parent 1 and child 2 is a copy of parent 2.
    (c) For child 1 and then child 2, for each of them: decide with probability a2/b2 whether to perform swap mutation, and if so perform it in place; then decide with probability a3/b3 whether to perform insertion mutation, and if so perform it in place.
    (d) Append child 1 and then child 2, in this order, to the end of the population of generation g + 1.
- Tournament selection: draw a random number at least 0 and less than P, k times; among the individuals whose numbers are these values plus 1 (the same individual may be chosen any number of times), select the one with the smallest evaluation value (ties: the smallest individual number).
- Crossover (order-preserving crossover): for each job in job order, draw one random number at least 0 and less than 2, and if it is 0, put that job into the set S. Child 1 has parent 1's gene, unchanged, at each position where parent 1's gene is a job in S, and the remaining positions are filled, in increasing order of position, with the genes of parent 2 that are not jobs in S, kept in their order in parent 2. Child 2 is created in the same way with the roles of parent 1 and parent 2 exchanged.
- Swap mutation: draw two random numbers at least 0 and less than N in succession as a and b, and swap the genes at positions a + 1 and b + 1 (nothing changes if a = b).
- Insertion mutation: draw two random numbers at least 0 and less than N in succession as a and b, remove the gene at position a + 1, and then insert it into the sequence of the remaining N - 1 genes so that it ends up at position b + 1.
- Records: when g = 0, and when the evaluation value of the best individual of generation g is smaller than the evaluation value of the best individual of generation g - 1, g is a record generation, and (g, that evaluation value, the number of the best individual) is recorded.
- Progress: for each of the generations <g1> to <gn>, compute the minimum evaluation value, the mean evaluation value (the sum of the evaluation values / P as a reduced fraction), the maximum evaluation value, and the number of distinct chromosomes (distinct as sequences).
- GA results: the chromosome of the best individual of generation G is the GA best chromosome, its decoded schedule is the GA best schedule, and its evaluation value is C_GA. For each of crossover, swap mutation and insertion mutation, count the number of times it was decided to perform it over the whole GA (a mutation with a = b that left the chromosome unchanged also counts as one).
- Top individuals: arrange the individuals of generation G in increasing order of evaluation value (ties: increasing individual number), remove every individual that has an individual with the same chromosome before it, and take the first 5 (all of them if there are fewer than 5) as the top individuals. For each top individual, compute the distance between its decoded schedule and the GA best schedule.

Section 8. Activating the GA best solution
- Active decoding: the schedule obtained by the procedure of Section 5 with the priority value GENES. Its makespan and decision count are used in the output (they are not used in any other processing).
- Activation: starting from the machine order of the GA best schedule, repeat the following. If the schedule of the current machine order has no left-shiftable operation, stop. Otherwise, let o be the left-shiftable operation with the smallest start time (ties: the smallest job number), remove o from its sequence in the machine order, and reinsert it at the insertion position of o. This is called a shift; record o, the original start time of o, and the new start time of o (the value found by the left shift of Section 4). The repetition always ends after a finite number of times. The machine order when it ends is the activated order, and its makespan is C_A.

Section 9. Tabu search
The tabu search from a starting machine order X0 is performed as follows. Set both the current machine order X and the best machine order B to X0, the best value c_B to the makespan of X0, and the best iteration number f to 0, and make the tabu list (a mapping from pairs of operations to expiries) empty. For the iterations it = 1, 2, ..., T, perform steps 1 to 6 below.
1. Find the move list of the N1 neighbourhood of the schedule of X. If there are no moves at all, end the search with the stop reason no_moves.
2. Compute the evaluation value of each move. A move (u, v) is called tabu when the tabu list contains the pair (u, v) and its expiry is >= it.
3. The moves that are not tabu, and the tabu moves whose evaluation value is smaller than c_B, are the selectable moves. If there are no selectable moves, end the search with the stop reason all_tabu.
4. Let (u, v) be the selectable move with the smallest evaluation value (ties: the one that comes first in the move list), and apply it to X. Set the expiry of the pair (v, u) in the tabu list to it + L (replacing any existing entry for that pair).
5. If its evaluation value is smaller than c_B, set B to the new X, c_B to that evaluation value, and f to the current it.
6. As the record of the iteration, record it, the number of moves, the number of tabu moves, the selected move, whether the selected move was tabu, the evaluation value of the selected move, and c_B at this point.
When all T iterations have been completed, the stop reason is limit. The number of iterations performed is the number of recorded iterations. The result of the search is B, c_B, f, the number of iterations performed, and the stop reason.
The search from the machine order of each baseline schedule and the search from the activated order are each performed independently (with a fresh tabu list and so on). The B of the search from the activated order is the final machine order, its schedule is the final schedule, and its makespan is C_F.

Section 10. Analysis of the final schedule
For the final schedule, find the critical path, the blocks, the number of critical paths, the move list of the N1 neighbourhood and the evaluation value of each move, the slack and free slack of each operation, the number of left-shiftable operations, the machine and operation statistics, and the bottleneck machine. Also, for each machine m, increase the processing time of each operation whose machine is m to "p + ceil(p / 10)", and take the makespan of the schedule obtained with the final machine order unchanged as the sensitivity of machine m (the processing times of the operations on other machines are not changed). Furthermore, find the per-machine distances between the final schedule and each of the four baseline schedules and the GA best schedule.

Section 11. Gantt chart
Build the Gantt chart of the final schedule as follows.
- Scale: among 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, ... (the values 1, 2 and 5 each multiplied by powers of 10, in increasing order), the smallest value for which "ceil(C_F / value)" is <= W is the scale Q, and the corresponding "ceil(C_F / Q)" is the number of columns n. Column c (c = 0, 1, ..., n - 1) represents the time interval [c * Q, (c + 1) * Q).
- Characters of a row: a row consists of several intervals [start time, completion time), each with a mark (one character). For each interval, let its overlap be "(the smaller of the completion time and (c + 1) * Q) - (the larger of the start time and c * Q)"; the character of column c is the mark of the interval with the largest overlap among the intervals whose overlap is positive (ties: the one with the smaller start time). If no interval has a positive overlap, it is ".".
- Machine rows: for each machine m = 1, 2, ..., M, the row made of the interval [S(o), C(o)) of each operation whose machine is m, with the job letter of the operation's job as its mark. However, the mark of an operation that is on the critical path of the final schedule is the lowercase form of the job letter.
- Busy row: the character of column c is the number of machines that have an operation o with S(o) <= c * Q < C(o), written as one decimal digit (X for 10).
- Job rows: for each job j in job order, the row made of the interval [S(o), C(o)) of each operation of job j, with the single digit "the remainder of mu(o) divided by 10" as its mark.
- Time row: start with "time", and for each c = 0, 10, 20, ... (less than n), write the decimal representation of c * Q so that it starts at character position 6 + c, counting the first character of the line as position 0 (fill the characters in between with spaces).
- Rule row: 6 spaces followed by, for c = 0, 1, ..., n - 1, "+" if c is a multiple of 10 and "-" otherwise.
- A machine row, the busy row and a job row consist of the label (M<m> for a machine row, busy for the busy row, J<j> for a job row) left-aligned in a field of width 4 (padded with spaces on the right), followed by " |" (one space and a vertical bar), the n column characters, and "|".

[Output files]
Write the following 4 files to the output/ folder. In every file, items on a line are separated by a single space (a single comma in the csv file), and no spaces are put at the beginning or end of a line (except that the time row and the rule row of the Gantt chart follow Section 11). Every line ends with a newline (LF), and no empty lines are output. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Below, "notation" means the notation of an operation or a machine (J<j>-<k>, M<m>).

(1) output/baselines.txt
Output the following, in this order.
- INSTANCE jobs <number of valid jobs> machines <M> operations <N> work <sum of the processing times of all operations> errors <number of errors>
- For each recorded error, in recording order, "ERROR <recorded error>".
- For each job j in job order, "JOB J<j> operations <n_j> work <work> route <notation of the machine of the 1st operation> <notation of the machine of the 2nd operation> ... <notation of the machine of the n_j-th operation>".
- For each machine m = 1, 2, ..., M, "MACHINE M<m> operations <number of operations whose machine is m> load <load> head <minimum head of the operations whose machine is m> tail <minimum tail of the operations whose machine is m> bound <simple bound> jackson <Jackson bound>".
- BOUND job <job bound> J<job of the job bound>
- BOUND machine <machine bound> M<machine of the machine bound>
- BOUND jackson <Jackson bound> M<machine of the Jackson bound>
- BOUND lower <LB>
- For each rule in the order SPT, LPT, MWKR, FIFO, the following 3 lines.
  RULE <rule> makespan <makespan C> gap <percentage of C - LB relative to LB> flow <total flow time> idle <total idle time> decisions <decision count> ties <tie count>
  PATH <rule> operations <number of operations on the critical path> blocks <number of blocks> critical_paths <number of critical paths> bottleneck M<bottleneck machine>
  TABU <rule> makespan <C> -> <c_B of the search result> found <f> iterations <number of iterations performed> stop <stop reason>
- BEST <rule> makespan <makespan>. This is the rule whose baseline schedule has the smallest makespan (ties: the one that comes first in the order SPT, LPT, MWKR, FIFO).
- For each machine m = 1, 2, ..., M, "UTILIZATION M<m> SPT <utilization> LPT <utilization> MWKR <utilization> FIFO <utilization>". Each utilization is the utilization of machine m in the baseline schedule of that rule.

(2) output/ga_progress.csv
Line 1 is the header line "generation,best,mean,worst,distinct". Then, for each generation g in the order <g1>, ..., <gn>, output one line "<g>,<minimum evaluation value>,<mean>,<maximum evaluation value>,<number of distinct chromosomes>". <mean> is the reduced fraction written in the form "<numerator>/<denominator>" (write "/1" even when the denominator is 1).

[Source files]
Organize the program into the following 6 source files (name the files according to each language's conventions).
- main: holds the overall flow and the core of the processing. It reads ga_params.txt and handles the combination of the lower bounds and the Jackson bound, machine orders and the computation of their schedules, the critical path, the blocks, the number of critical paths, the N1 neighbourhood and the tabu search, slack and free slack, left shifts and activation, distances, sensitivities, the machine and operation statistics, the generational flow of the genetic algorithm (initial population, elites, the order of creating children, records, progress, top individuals), and the building and writing of the lines of all output files.
- instance: reads and checks instance.txt (recording the errors), and computes for each machine the number of operations, the load, the minimum head and tail, and the simple bound.
- minstd: holds the MINSTD generator (drawing a random number, a random number at least 0 and less than m, deciding with probability a/b, shuffling a sequence).
- dispatch_rules: performs the Giffler-Thompson method (the rules SPT, LPT, MWKR, FIFO and the priority value GENES, counting decisions and ties).
- genetic: performs chromosome decoding, tournament selection, order-preserving crossover, swap mutation and insertion mutation.
- gantt: determines the scale and number of columns of the Gantt chart, produces the time row and the rule row, determines the character of each column and builds the rows, and creates the busy row.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= J <= 13 and 1 <= M <= 10. A job line has at least 1 and at most 12 tokens, and the two integers of each token are between 0 and 999 inclusive.
- There are at least 1 and at most 10 valid jobs, and each machine m = 1, ..., M has at least one operation of a valid job. N <= 100.
- 1 <= s <= 2147483646, 6 <= P <= 30, 1 <= G <= 100 and 2 <= k <= 5. 1 <= E <= P - 2, and P - E is even.
- 1 <= b1, b2, b3 <= 1000, 0 <= a1 <= b1, 0 <= a2 <= b2 and 0 <= a3 <= b3.
- 1 <= n, and 0 <= g1 < g2 < ... < gn <= G.
- 1 <= T <= 100, 1 <= L <= 20 and 10 <= W <= 100.
- In the MINSTD computation, 48271 * x is a value less than 2^47, so compute it with integers that represent that range exactly (such as 64-bit integers). The absolute values of all other integers appearing in the computation (including the number of critical paths and the sum of the evaluation values) are less than 2^31.
- All computations are done in integers, and a tie-breaking rule is specified for every selection and sort, so the result is uniquely determined.
