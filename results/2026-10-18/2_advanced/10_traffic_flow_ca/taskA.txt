[Overview]
Write a program that simulates the traffic flow on a small road network with a cellular automaton based on the Nagel-Schreckenberg model (NaSch model).
The road network consists of one-way segments and of nodes that connect segments. A segment is divided into some number of lanes, and each lane is a row of cells (a cell is the space for one vehicle). There are three kinds of nodes: sources, where vehicles enter the road network; sinks, where vehicles leave the road network; and junctions. A junction either has a signal phase plan or has no signal. Vehicles are generated at sources according to random numbers, choose the next segment at each junction according to integer weights, and leave the road network at sinks. Each vehicle has a class (maximum speed, whether it overtakes, whether it stops at bus stops), and at each step it changes lanes, accelerates, slows down to fit the free space ahead, slows down randomly according to a random number, and moves. Lane blockages caused by incidents and buses stopping at bus stops are also handled. All random numbers are generated with MINSTD, and the order in which they are drawn is defined in this statement.
After the simulation, the program writes a space-time diagram, the flow and occupancy at each detector, the travel-time distribution, statistics per class, statistics of segments and junctions, the evolution per aggregation interval, the trajectories of specified vehicles, and a log of events. In addition, it runs NaSch model experiments on a one-lane ring road and writes the relation between density and flow (the fundamental diagram).
The program reads 3 files from the input/ folder and writes 9 files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output. All computation is done with integers; no floating-point numbers are used. Use only the standard library.

[Input files]
All 3 input files consist of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a newline (LF). Each line is a sequence of one or more tokens separated by single spaces; there are no spaces at the beginning or end of a line, never two or more consecutive spaces, and no blank lines. The first token of each line gives the kind of the line. All integers are >= 0 and written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Segment IDs, node names, class names and detector names (below collectively called "names") are strings of 1 to 8 characters consisting of uppercase letters and digits.
The order in which the lines of one kind appear within a file is called the "input order" of that kind (example: the input order of the SEGMENT lines).

== input/network.txt ==
The road network. SEGMENT lines (1 or more), JUNCTION lines (1 or more), INCIDENT lines (0 or more) and STOP lines (0 or more) appear in this order.
- "SEGMENT <ID> <from node> <to node> <length> <lanes> <limit>": a one-way segment from node <from node> to node <to node>. The segment has <lanes> lanes, each a row of <length> cells. <limit> is the upper limit of the speed (the number of cells advanced in one step) on this segment.
- "JUNCTION <node> <offset> <phase 1> <phase 2> ...": a junction. Each phase is a token of the form "<duration>:<green segments>". <green segments> is either the IDs of the segments (segments whose end node is this junction) that are green during that phase, separated by ",", or "-" (all red). A JUNCTION line with no phases at all represents a junction without a signal, and its <offset> is 0.
- "INCIDENT <segment> <lane> <cell> <start> <end>": an incident. It means that cell <cell> of lane <lane> of segment <segment> is scheduled to be blocked by an obstacle from step <start> through step <end> (the period during which it is actually blocked is determined by (1) of section 3).
- "STOP <segment> <cell> <dwell>": a bus stop. There is a bus stop at cell <cell> of lane 1 of segment <segment>, and a vehicle that stops there then stays stopped for <dwell> steps.

== input/demand.txt ==
The traffic demand. CLASS lines (1 or more), SOURCE lines (1 or more), SINK lines (1 or more) and TURN lines (1 or more) appear in this order.
- "CLASS <name> <max speed> <weight> <overtake> <stop>": a vehicle class. <weight> is the weight used when choosing the class of a generated vehicle. <overtake> and <stop> are Y or N; vehicles of a class whose <overtake> is Y make lane changes for overtaking (rule C of section 4), and vehicles of a class whose <stop> is Y stop at bus stops.
- "SOURCE <node> <rate 1>:<end 1> <rate 2>:<end 2> ...": a source. There are one or more tokens of the form "<rate>:<end>", and the <end> values are strictly increasing in this order. The generation rate rate(t) (in permille) at step t is the <rate> of the first token that satisfies t <= <end>. If there is no such token, rate(t) = 0.
- "SINK <node>": a sink.
- "TURN <from segment> <to segment> <weight>": a turn at a junction. It means that a vehicle that has reached the junction at the end node of segment <from segment> chooses to proceed next to segment <to segment> with weight <weight>.

== input/params.txt ==
The simulation settings. Lines 1 to 5 are as follows.
- Line 1 "seed <S>": the MINSTD seed used for the road network simulation.
- Line 2 "steps <N>": the number of steps of the road network simulation.
- Line 3 "dawdle <p> <p0>": the probability of random slowdown (in permille). <p> is used for vehicles that were moving in the previous step, and <p0> for vehicles that were stopped in the previous step. The ring road experiments use the same values.
- Line 4 "interval <I>": the length of an aggregation interval (number of steps).
- Line 5 "bin <B>": the bin width of the travel-time histograms.
From line 6 on, detector lines (1 or more), diagram lines (1 or more), a track line (exactly 1) and ring lines (1 or more) appear in this order.
- "detector <name> <segment> <cell> <width>": a detector. Its measurement range is cells <cell> through <cell> + <width> - 1 of all lanes of segment <segment>, and its measurement line is the boundary between cell <cell> - 1 and cell <cell>.
- "diagram <segment> <lane> <start> <end>": means that a space-time diagram is written for lane <lane> of segment <segment>, from step <start> through step <end>.
- "track <number 1> <number 2> ...": the numbers of the vehicles whose trajectories are written (1 or more).
- "ring <L> <vmax> <H> <Q> <R> <count 1> <count 2> ...": a ring road experiment (section 7). L is the length of the ring road, vmax the maximum speed, H the number of warm-up steps, Q the number of measurement steps, R the MINSTD seed, followed by one or more vehicle counts.

[Processing]
== 0. Notation and common rules ==
- All computation is done with integers. For an integer a >= 0 and a positive integer b, a div b is the integer obtained by discarding the fractional part of a / b. For an integer a and a positive integer b, a mod b is the integer that is >= 0 and < b obtained by subtracting a multiple of b from a (it is >= 0 even when a is negative; example: -3 mod 10 = 7).
- dec_d(a, b) (a and b are integers >= 0): if b = 0, it is "-". Otherwise it is the decimal representation of a / b rounded to d decimal places (half up). Specifically, let z = (2 * a * 10^d + b) div (2 * b); the string consists of the decimal representation of z div 10^d, ".", and z mod 10^d written with d digits (missing digits are filled with leading zeros), in this order. For example, dec_2(2, 3) = 0.67, dec_3(7, 4) = 1.750, dec_4(0, 9) = 0.0000. Do not use floating-point numbers; compute exactly this formula with integers.
- frac(a, b) (a and b are integers >= 0): if b = 0, it is "-". Otherwise, with a' and b' being a and b each divided by the greatest common divisor of a and b, it is "<a'>/<b'>" (if a = 0, it is "0/1"). Examples: frac(6, 4) = 3/2, frac(5, 5) = 1/1.
- Reduced fraction: a positive rational number e written in the form "<numerator>/<denominator>", where the numerator and denominator are coprime positive integers (if e = 1, it is "1/1").
- Time notation: the decimal representation of a step number t, padded with leading zeros to 4 digits if it has fewer than 4 digits (example: 7 is 0007, 512 is 0512).
- MINSTD: a random number generator with a state x. "Drawing a random number once" updates x to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647, and the updated x is the drawn value. The initial state is the seed. The product exceeds 2^31, so compute it with 64-bit integers. The road network simulation uses a single generator initialized with the seed of the seed line, only in the order described in the procedure of section 3. The ring road experiments use a separate generator for each experiment (section 7).
- select(w_1, ..., w_k ; r): an operation that chooses one index from positive integer weights w_1, ..., w_k and a random value r. Let q = r mod (w_1 + ... + w_k), and choose the smallest i that satisfies q < w_1 + ... + w_i.

== 1. Road network ==
- The length, number of lanes and speed limit of a segment s are written len(s), lanes(s) and lim(s). The cells of each lane are numbered 0, 1, ..., len(s) - 1 in order from the start node side of s. Vehicles move in the direction of increasing cell number. The lanes are numbered 1 to lanes(s). Lane 1 is the driving lane, and the larger the number, the further toward the passing side the lane is.
- The state of each cell is one of "empty", "vehicle" (one vehicle is there) and "obstacle" (incident). Initially all cells are empty.
- A node is a source (the node of a SOURCE line), a sink (the node of a SINK line) or a junction (the node of a JUNCTION line). There is exactly one segment whose start node is a source; it is called the "entry segment" of that source. A segment whose end node is a junction J is called a "segment entering J".
- For a segment s whose end node is a junction, there are one or more TURN lines with s as <from segment>. These TURN lines in input order are called the "turn" list of s, and each turn has a <to segment> (a segment whose start node is that junction) and a <weight>. A segment whose end node is a sink has no turns.
- Signals: let the phases of the JUNCTION line of junction J be (d_1, G_1), ..., (d_m, G_m) in order (d_k is the duration and G_k the set of green segments), let C = d_1 + ... + d_m be the cycle and o the offset. Whether a segment s entering J is green at step t is written green(s, t). If m >= 1, let tau = (t - 1 + o) mod C; for the phase k that satisfies d_1 + ... + d_(k-1) <= tau < d_1 + ... + d_k, green(s, t) is true if and only if s is in G_k. At a junction without a signal (m = 0), green(s, t) is always true.
- The following three functions are used. Each is evaluated with the cell states at the moment it is called.
  - free(s, l, x, c): in lane l of segment s, the number of consecutive empty cells counted in the order cell x + 1, x + 2, ..., with c as the upper limit. That is, the largest integer k >= 0 such that k <= c, x + k <= len(s) - 1, and cells x + 1 through x + k are all empty. When x = -1, counting starts from cell 0.
  - first(s, l): in lane l of segment s, the smallest number among the cells that are not empty (that hold a vehicle or an obstacle). If all cells are empty, it is len(s).
  - safe(s, l, x): true if cells max(0, x - lim(s)) through x of lane l of segment s are all empty, false otherwise.

== 2. Vehicles ==
- The maximum speed of a class is called the vmax of that class. The maximum speed V of a vehicle on segment s is min(vmax of its class, lim(s)).
- Vehicles are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in the order in which they are generated. Each vehicle holds: class, source, arrival step (the step in which it was generated), insertion step, exit step, sink it exited at, position (segment, lane, cell), speed, turn, route, steps in network, distance, stopped steps, stop count, lane-change count, remaining dwell, and information on the bus stops at which it has completed its stop. The speed is 0 when the vehicle is generated, and the route is initially empty. Initially there are no bus stops at which it has completed its stop. Each count, the steps in network, the distance and the remaining dwell are initially 0.
- Entering a segment: when a vehicle enters a segment s (when it is inserted into an entry segment, and when it passes a junction and moves to the next segment), do the following. Append s to the end of the vehicle's route, and add 1 to the "entered count" of s. If the end node of s is a junction, draw a random number once and let r be its value, perform select with the weights of the turn list of s, and make the chosen turn the vehicle's turn. If the end node of s is a sink, no random number is drawn.
- Heading for a bus stop: a vehicle is said to be "heading for a bus stop" when all of the following hold: the <stop> of its class is Y, the segment s it is currently on has a bus stop, it has not yet completed its stop at the bus stop of s, and the number of its current cell is less than or equal to the cell of the bus stop of s.

== 3. Simulation procedure ==
Each source has a vehicle queue (initially empty), a queue start W (initially 0) and a maximum queue length M (initially 0). Each incident has a state, and its initial state is "planned". For each step t = 1, 2, ..., N (N is the value of the steps line), do the following (1) to (10) in this order.

(1) Updating incidents: in input order of the INCIDENT lines, for each incident do only the first of the following that applies.
  - If the state is "active" and t > <end>, set the state to "cleared", make that cell empty, and record the event INCIDENT_END.
  - If the state is "planned" and t > <end>, set the state to "cancelled".
  - If the state is "planned", t >= <start>, and that cell is empty, set the state to "active", make that cell an obstacle, set the active start to t, and record the event INCIDENT_START. While a vehicle is in that cell, the activation is delayed.
  After that, if the state is "active", set the active end to t.

(2) Aggregation intervals: if (t - 1) mod I = 0, start a new aggregation interval. Aggregation intervals are numbered 1, 2, .... For each aggregation interval, the arrivals, insertions, exits, vehicle steps and speed sum are counted, and for each detector the passing count, occupancy count and occupancy speed sum (all initially 0). Below, the "current aggregation interval" is the aggregation interval started last.

(3) Arrival: in input order of the SOURCE lines, for each source draw a random number once and let r be its value; if r mod 1000 < rate(t), generate one vehicle. When generating, draw one more random number and let r' be its value, and determine the class by select(...; r') with the weights of the CLASS lines in input order. Create the vehicle with the next number, set its arrival step to t, append it to the end of that source's queue, and add 1 to the arrivals of the current aggregation interval.

(4) Insertion: in input order of the SOURCE lines, for each source do the following (a) and (b). Let s be the entry segment of that source.
  (a) While the queue is not empty, repeat the following. If no lane of s has cell 0 empty, end the repetition. Otherwise, among those lanes choose the lane l with the largest first(s, l) (if several lanes have the same value, the one with the smallest number), remove the vehicle at the head of the queue, and place it at cell 0 of lane l of s (its speed stays 0). Set the vehicle's insertion step to t, perform the processing for entering s (section 2), and add 1 to the insertions of the current aggregation interval.
  (b) If the queue is not empty and W = 0, set W = t and M = 0. Next, if W != 0, set M to the larger of M and the length of the queue (number of vehicles), and further, if the queue is empty, record the event QUEUE and set W = 0.

(5) Lane changes: go through the segments with 2 or more lanes in input order, within each segment in increasing order of lane number, and within each lane in increasing order of cell number, examining the vehicles whose remaining dwell is 0 one at a time, and determine the desired lane by the rules of section 4. The cell states used by the rules are, for every vehicle, those at the moment (5) began (the state before any lane change of this (5) has been carried out). When the desired lane differs from the current lane: if no vehicle has yet reserved, in this (5), the cell with the same number in the desired lane of the same segment, reserve that cell and record the lane change. If it has already been reserved, the vehicle does not change lanes. After all vehicles have been examined, carry out all recorded lane changes. The vehicle moves to the desired lane at the same cell number, and 1 is added to the vehicle's lane-change count and to the segment's count of that kind of lane change (section 4).

(6) Determining speeds: all vehicles on the road at the end of (5), ordered by input order of the segments, within a segment in increasing order of lane number, and within a lane in decreasing order of cell number, form the "processing order" of this step. In (6) to (9), vehicles are handled in this processing order. In processing order, determine the new speed u of each vehicle as described in section 5. The u of every vehicle is determined with the cell states at the end of (5) (vehicles do not move during (6)).

(7) Movement: in processing order, for each vehicle remember its segment s and cell x before the move, and do the following.
  - If x + u < len(s), move it to cell x + u of the same lane.
  - If x + u >= len(s) and the end node of s is a sink, remove the vehicle from the road, set its exit step to t and the sink it exited at to the end node of s, and add 1 to the exits of the current aggregation interval.
  - If x + u >= len(s) and the end node of s is a junction, the vehicle passes the junction in (8). Until then the vehicle stays in its current cell.
  Because of the way section 5 determines speeds, in (7) a vehicle never moves into a cell that holds another vehicle or an obstacle.

(8) Passing junctions: handle the vehicles that were set to pass a junction in (7) one at a time, in processing order. Let s be the vehicle's segment, l its lane, x its cell, and T the <to segment> of its turn; let l' = min(l, lanes(T)), w = x + u - len(s), and f = first(T, l'). f is computed with the cell states at that moment; that is, reflecting the moves in (7) and the moves of the vehicles handled earlier in this (8), with the vehicles not yet handled in this (8) still in their original cells. Let p = min(w, f - 1); if p < w, add 1 to the "blocked" count of s. Then do the following.
  - If p >= 0, move the vehicle to cell p of lane l' of T and set u = len(s) - x + p. Add 1 to the "passed" count of s and to the "vehicles" count of the vehicle's turn (TURN line), and perform the processing for entering T (section 2).
  - If p < 0, move the vehicle to cell len(s) - 1 of lane l of s (if it is already in that cell, it stays there), and set u = len(s) - 1 - x.

(9) Per-vehicle aggregation: for each vehicle of the processing order (including vehicles that exited in (7)), do the following in this order. v is the vehicle's speed before (6) of this step.
  - Add 1 to the steps in network (the number of steps it was on the road) and u to the distance. If u = 0, add 1 to the stopped steps, and further, if v > 0, add 1 to the stop count. Then set the vehicle's speed to u.
  - Add 1 to the vehicle steps and u to the speed sum of the current aggregation interval.
  - If the vehicle is heading for a bus stop (section 2), is in lane 1, and the number of its current cell equals the cell of that segment's bus stop, then its stop at that bus stop counts as completed, set its remaining dwell to the <dwell> of that bus stop, add 1 to the "buses" count of that bus stop, and record the event BUS_STOP.
  - If the vehicle's number is on the track line, record one trajectory line (output/tracks.txt).
  - In input order of the detector lines, for each detector: if the position before the move remembered in (7) was a cell of the detector's segment with a number less than <cell>, and the vehicle now either is not on that segment (it exited or moved to another segment) or is on a cell of that segment with a number >= <cell>, add 1 to that detector's passing count in the current aggregation interval.

(10) State aggregation:
  - In input order of the detector lines, for each detector add the number of vehicles in the cells of its measurement range to the occupancy count of the current aggregation interval, and add the sum of the speeds of those vehicles to the occupancy speed sum.
  - In input order of the SEGMENT lines, for each segment s add the number n of vehicles on s to the "vehicle sum" of s and the sum of the speeds of those vehicles to the "speed sum" of s, and if n is larger than the "maximum vehicles" of s (initially 0), set the maximum vehicles to n. If the end node of s is a junction, further do the following. If green(s, t) is true, add 1 to the "green" count of s. Let q be the number of vehicles with speed 0 on s; add q to the "queue sum" of s, and if q is larger than the "maximum queue" of s (initially 0), set the maximum queue to q. If t < N, q > 0, green(s, t) is true and green(s, t + 1) is false all hold, add 1 to the "failures" count of s.
  - In input order of the diagram lines, for each diagram line that satisfies <start> <= t <= <end>, record one space-time diagram line (output/spacetime.txt).

After all steps are done, in input order of the SOURCE lines, record the event QUEUE (with open) for each source with W != 0.

== 4. Lane-change rules ==
Let s be the segment, l the lane, x the cell, v the speed and V the maximum speed of the vehicle examined in (5) of section 3, and let n = lanes(s), here = free(s, l, x, V + 1) and want = min(v + 1, V). When here < V + 1, x + here + 1 < len(s), and cell x + here + 1 of lane l is an obstacle, we say that "there is an obstacle ahead".
Check the following rules A to D in this order and follow the first rule that determines the desired lane. If no rule determines it, the desired lane is the current lane l (the vehicle does not change lanes).
- Rule A (avoidance): if there is an obstacle ahead, check l' = l + 1, l - 1 in this order, and make the desired lane the first l' that satisfies all of 1 <= l' <= n, free(s, l', x, V + 1) > here and safe(s, l', x). The kind of this lane change is "avoidance". If no l' satisfies them, go on to rule B.
- Rule B (pulling in to a bus stop): if the vehicle is heading for a bus stop, the desired lane is l - 1 when l >= 2 and safe(s, l - 1, x) is true, and l otherwise (in either case rules C and D are not checked). The kind of a lane change to l - 1 is "return".
- Rule C (overtaking): if the <overtake> of the class is Y and l + 1 <= n, here < want, free(s, l + 1, x, V + 1) > here and safe(s, l + 1, x) all hold, the desired lane is l + 1. The kind is "overtaking".
- Rule D (return): if l >= 2, free(s, l - 1, x, V + 1) > want and safe(s, l - 1, x) all hold, the desired lane is l - 1. The kind is "return".

== 5. Determining speeds ==
Let s be the segment, l the lane, x the cell, v the speed and V the maximum speed of the vehicle handled in (6) of section 3, and determine the new speed u by doing the following 1 to 7 in this order.
1. Let a = min(v + 1, V) (right after moving to another segment v may be larger than V, but a is at most V).
2. Let g = free(s, l, x, a).
3. If g < a and x + g = len(s) - 1 (empty up to the end of the segment), change g as follows.
  - If the end node of s is a sink, set g = a.
  - If the end node of s is a junction and green(s, t) is false, g is not changed.
  - If the end node of s is a junction and green(s, t) is true, let T be the <to segment> of the vehicle's turn and l' = min(l, lanes(T)), and add free(T, l', -1, a - g) to g.
4. If the vehicle is heading for a bus stop, set g = min(g, <cell of the bus stop> - x). Even when it is not in lane 1, it does not go beyond the cell of the bus stop.
5. Let u = min(a, g).
6. Draw a random number once and let r be its value (exactly one is always drawn for every vehicle). Let P = p0 if v = 0 and P = p otherwise; if u > 0 and r mod 1000 < P, subtract 1 from u.
7. If the remaining dwell is positive, set u = 0 and subtract 1 from the remaining dwell.

== 6. Routes and travel times ==
- Travel time: the travel time of an exited vehicle is exit step - insertion step + 1 (the number of steps in which the vehicle was handled in (9); it equals the steps in network).
- Enumerating routes: the list of routes (sequences of segments) from a segment s to a sink, together with their expected shares (rational numbers), is defined by the following enum(s).
  - If the end node of s is a sink, enum(s) is the single pair of route (s) and expected share 1.
  - Otherwise, let w_s be the sum of the weights of the turns of s; take the turns of s one at a time in list order, with T its <to segment> and w its <weight>, and for each pair (route R, expected share e) of enum(T) in list order, list the pair of the route "s followed by R" and the expected share e * w / w_s.
  Expected shares are computed exactly as rational numbers. The route list of a source is enum(s) for its entry segment s. The "destination" of a route is the end node (a sink) of the last segment of the route.

== 7. Ring road experiments ==
Number the ring lines 1, 2, ... in input order. For each ring line, for each of its <count> values in the order they appear on the line, run the following experiment once. Let K be the count.
- Place K vehicles on a one-lane ring road of length L cells. The cells are numbered 0 to L - 1, and the cell after position x is (x + 1) mod L. The position of vehicle i (i = 0, 1, ..., K - 1) is (i * L) div K and its speed is 0. A new MINSTD initialized with seed R is used (it is reinitialized for each count).
- For each step 1, 2, ..., H + Q, do the following. Order the vehicles in decreasing order of position, and determine the new speed of each vehicle in that order. The vehicle one before it in the order is its "vehicle ahead"; for the first vehicle in the order, the last vehicle in the order is its vehicle ahead (if K = 1, it is the vehicle itself). Let y be the position of the vehicle ahead, x its own position and v its speed; let gap = (y - x - 1) mod L and u = min(v + 1, vmax, gap). Draw a random number once and let r be its value; let P = p0 if v = 0 and P = p otherwise, and if u > 0 and r mod 1000 < P, subtract 1 from u. After the u of all vehicles has been determined, move all vehicles simultaneously, setting each vehicle's position to (x + u) mod L and its speed to u.
- Let T be the sum of the u of all vehicles over the steps whose step number is larger than H.

[Output files]
Common format:
- Everything is ASCII, and every line ends with a newline (LF). No line has trailing spaces, and no blank lines are written unless specified.
- Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Negative numbers do not occur.
- In the formats below, a part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and all other characters are written as they are. If the "x" of "<x>" is a notation of section 0 (dec_d, frac, time notation), the string of that notation is written.
- All 9 files are always created. A file with no lines to write is empty (0 bytes).
- Lane numbers are written counted from 1.

== output/spacetime.txt ==
In input order of the diagram lines, for each diagram line write the following lines.
- "DIAGRAM <segment> lane <lane> steps <start>-<end>"
- Then, in the order t = <start>, ..., <end>, the line recorded in (10) of section 3: "<time notation of t> <cells> <signal>". <cells> is the len(s) characters giving the states of cells 0 through len(s) - 1 of that lane of that segment at that moment, "." for empty, "#" for an obstacle, and the single digit of the vehicle's speed for a vehicle. <signal> is "E" if the end node of the segment is a sink, and for a junction "G" if green(s, t) is true and "R" if it is false.

== output/detectors.csv ==
Line 1 is detector,interval,flow,density,speed. Then, in input order of the detector lines, for each detector, for aggregation interval numbers k = 1, 2, ..., N / I in order, write one line "<name>,<k>,<passing count>,<dec_4(occupancy count, I * <width> * lanes(s))>,<dec_2(occupancy speed sum, occupancy count)>". s is the detector's segment, and the passing count, occupancy count and occupancy speed sum are the values of that detector in aggregation interval k.

== output/travel_times.txt ==
Write the following lines in this order.
1. For each class in input order of the CLASS lines, the travel-time block of the exited vehicles of that class, with heading "CLASS <class name>".
2. The travel-time block of all exited vehicles, with heading "ALL".
3. For each source in input order of the SOURCE lines, the following lines. Let E be the set of vehicles generated at that source that have exited, and |E| their number.
  - "SOURCE <node> exited <|E|>"
  - In the order of that source's route list (section 6), for each route: "  ROUTE <IDs of the route's segments separated by ","> expected <expected share as a reduced fraction> vehicles <k> share <frac(k, |E|)> mean <dec_2(sum of travel times, k)>". k is the number of vehicles of E whose route (sequence of segments entered) equals this route, and the sum of travel times is the sum of those vehicles' travel times.
  - For each sink, in input order of the SINK lines, that is the destination of one or more routes: "  SINK <node> expected <sum of the expected shares of the routes whose destination is that sink, as a reduced fraction> vehicles <k> share <frac(k, |E|)> mean <dec_2(sum of travel times, k)>". k is the number of vehicles of E that exited at that sink, and the sum of travel times is the sum of those vehicles' travel times.
A travel-time block is as follows. Let n be the number of vehicles concerned.
- If n = 0, it is the single line "<heading> exited 0".
- If n >= 1, let tau_1 <= tau_2 <= ... <= tau_n be the travel times in increasing order, and let the percentile P(q) be tau_j (j = (q * n + 99) div 100). Line 1 is "<heading> exited <n> min <tau_1> p50 <P(50)> p90 <P(90)> max <tau_n> mean <dec_2(tau_1 + ... + tau_n, n)>". Then write the histogram lines. Let B be the value of the bin line, and let the bin of a travel time tau be tau div B. For each bin b = tau_1 div B, tau_1 div B + 1, ..., tau_n div B (bins with frequency 0 are also written), let the frequency c_b be the number of travel times in that bin, c_max the maximum of the c_b, and the number of stars (80 * c_b + c_max) div (2 * c_max); write "  <b * B>-<b * B + B - 1> <c_b>", and if the number of stars is 1 or more, follow it on the same line with one space and that number of "*".

== output/class_stats.txt ==
For each class in input order of the CLASS lines, write 4 lines covering the vehicles of that class (all generated vehicles), and finally 4 lines covering all vehicles.
- Line 1: for a class "CLASS <name> vmax <vmax> weight <weight> overtake <overtake> stops <stop>" (<overtake> and <stop> are the Y or N of the input); for all vehicles "ALL"
- Line 2: "  generated <G> inserted <A> exited <X> in_network <A - X> queued <G - A>". G is the number of vehicles concerned, A the number of them that were inserted, and X the number of them that exited.
- Line 3: "  vehicle_steps <S> distance <D> mean_speed <frac(D, S)> = <dec_3(D, S)>". S is the sum of the steps in network and D the sum of the distances of the vehicles concerned (vehicles that have not exited are included).
- Line 4: "  travel <dec_2(sum of travel times, X)> entry_delay <dec_2(sum of insertion step - arrival step, X)> stopped <dec_2(sum of stopped steps, X)> stops <dec_2(sum of stop counts, X)> lane_changes <dec_2(sum of lane-change counts, X)>". Every sum is taken over the vehicles concerned that have exited.

== output/network_stats.txt ==
Write the following lines in this order.
1. In input order of the SEGMENT lines, for each segment s: "SEGMENT <ID> <from node> <to node> length <len(s)> lanes <lanes(s)> limit <lim(s)> entered <entered count> mean_vehicles <dec_3(vehicle sum, N)> density <dec_4(vehicle sum, N * len(s) * lanes(s))> mean_speed <dec_2(speed sum, vehicle sum)> max_vehicles <maximum vehicles> changes <overtaking count>/<return count>/<avoidance count>". The last three are the numbers of lane changes of each kind made on that segment.
2. In input order of the JUNCTION lines, for each junction J, first write the lines of J. For a junction with a signal, "JUNCTION <node> signal cycle <C> offset <o>" and, for phases k = 1, ..., m in order, "  PHASE <k> <d_1 + ... + d_(k-1)>-<d_1 + ... + d_k - 1> <green segments>" (<green segments> is the token of the input as is; for k = 1 the first value is 0); for a junction without a signal, "JUNCTION <node> unsignalized". Then, for each segment s entering J in input order of the SEGMENT lines, write "  IN <ID> green <green count> passed <passed count> blocked <blocked count> max_queue <maximum queue> mean_queue <dec_3(queue sum, N)> failures <failures count>" and, for each turn in the order of the turn list of s, "    TURN <to segment> weight <weight> vehicles <vehicles count>".
3. In input order of the STOP lines, for each bus stop: "STOP <segment> cell <cell> dwell <dwell> buses <buses count>"
4. In input order of the INCIDENT lines, for each incident: for an incident that was ever active, "INCIDENT <segment> lane <lane> cell <cell> planned <start>-<end> active <active start>-<active end>"; otherwise "INCIDENT <segment> lane <lane> cell <cell> planned <start>-<end> active none"

== output/timeline.csv ==
Line 1 is interval,arrived,inserted,exited,in_network,queued,mean_speed. Then, for aggregation interval numbers k = 1, 2, ..., N / I in order, write one line "<k>,<arrivals>,<insertions>,<exits>,<on road>,<waiting>,<dec_2(speed sum, vehicle steps)>". The arrivals, insertions, exits, speed sum and vehicle steps are the values of aggregation interval k; <on road> is the sum of the insertions of aggregation intervals 1 to k minus the sum of their exits, and <waiting> is the sum of the arrivals of aggregation intervals 1 to k minus the sum of their insertions.

== output/tracks.txt ==
In the order of the numbers on the track line, for each number write the following lines.
- If the vehicle with that number was not generated (if the number is larger than the number of generated vehicles), only the single line "VEHICLE <number> none".
- If it was generated, first write "VEHICLE <number> class <class name> source <source> arrival <arrival step> inserted <insertion step> exited <exit step> sink <sink it exited at>". If it was not inserted, <insertion step> is "-", and if it has not exited, <exit step> and <sink it exited at> are each "-". Next write "ROUTE <IDs of the route's segments separated by single spaces>" ("ROUTE -" if the route is empty). Then write the trajectory lines recorded in (9) of section 3, in the order recorded. A trajectory line is "<time notation> <segment> <lane> <cell> <speed>" if the vehicle is on the road at the time of (9), and "<time notation> EXIT <speed>" if it exited in that step. The speed is the speed after the update in (9).

== output/fundamental.csv ==
Line 1 is ring,vehicles,density,flow,speed. Then, for each experiment of section 7, in the order they were run, write one line "<ring line number>,<K>,<dec_4(K, L)>,<dec_4(T, L * Q)>,<dec_3(T, K * Q)>".

== output/events.txt ==
Write the events recorded in section 3, one per line, in the order recorded. The line of each event is as follows (t is the step in which it was recorded).
- INCIDENT_START: "<time notation of t> INCIDENT_START <segment> lane <lane> cell <cell>"
- INCIDENT_END: "<time notation of t> INCIDENT_END <segment> lane <lane> cell <cell>"
- QUEUE: "<time notation of t> QUEUE <node> from <W> max <M>" (W and M are the values before W is reset to 0)
- BUS_STOP: "<time notation of t> BUS_STOP <vehicle number> <segment> cell <cell>"
- QUEUE (with open): "<time notation of N> QUEUE <node> from <W> max <M> open"

[Source files]
Split the source into the following 6 files (follow each language's conventions for file names).
- main: handles the overall flow (from reading the 3 input files to writing the 9 output files). It holds the dispatching of input lines by kind and the construction of the road network, demand and settings (including the setup of sources, detectors, space-time diagrams and trajectories), steps (1) to (10) of section 3 (arrival, insertion and queue events, the lane-change rules and reservations of section 4, the speed determination of section 5, movement, passing junctions, per-vehicle aggregation and state aggregation), the route enumeration and expected shares of section 6, building the lines of the 9 output files, and the ring road experiments of section 7. Most of the processing is placed in this file.
- minstd: holds the MINSTD generator and select.
- road: holds segments (the row of cells, free, first, safe, the <cells> of the space-time diagram, per-segment aggregate values) and incidents (the state update of (1) of section 3).
- vehicles: holds classes and vehicles (the information of section 2, the maximum speed V, the operation of placing a vehicle on a segment) and the random slowdown (step 6 of section 5).
- signals: holds junctions (phase plan, cycle, green, the JUNCTION and PHASE lines of network_stats.txt).
- stats: holds dec_d, frac, percentiles, and the histogram lines of travel times.

[Constraints]
- There are 1 to 30 segments. Segment IDs are distinct from each other and do not coincide with node names. The length of each segment is 20 to 200, the number of lanes 1 to 3, and the speed limit 1 to 9.
- Each node appears in exactly one SOURCE, SINK or JUNCTION line; the same node never appears in two lines. Exactly one segment starts at a source, and no segment ends at a source. One or more segments end at a sink, and no segment starts at a sink. A junction has one or more segments starting at it and one or more segments ending at it.
- A JUNCTION line has 0 to 8 phases, and each duration is 1 to 100. The IDs listed in <green segments> are segments entering that junction and are distinct from each other. The offset of a junction with a signal is >= 0 and less than the cycle.
- The <from segment> of a TURN line is a segment whose end node is a junction, and the <to segment> is a segment whose start node is that junction. There are never two or more TURN lines with the same pair of <from segment> and <to segment>. Weights are 1 to 1000. Following the turns from any segment always reaches a sink, and no route passes the same segment twice. For every source, the number of routes is at most 30.
- The lane of an INCIDENT line is 1 to the number of lanes of that segment, the cell is 1 to len - 2, and 1 <= <start> <= <end>. The cells of incidents are distinct from each other.
- There is at most one STOP line per segment; its cell is 10 to len - 2, and its dwell is 1 to 20.
- There are 1 to 9 CLASS lines. Class names are distinct from each other, vmax is 1 to 9, and the weight is 1 to 1000.
- The <rate> of a SOURCE line is 0 to 1000, and <end> is 1 or more.
- The seeds of the seed line and ring lines are 1 to 2147483646. N of the steps line is 1 to 9999 and a multiple of I. I and B are 1 or more, and p and p0 are 0 to 1000.
- The names of the detector lines are distinct from each other, <cell> >= 1, <width> >= 1, and <cell> + <width> <= len. The lane of a diagram line exists on that segment, and 1 <= <start> <= <end> <= N. The numbers of the track line are 1 or more and distinct from each other.
- In a ring line, L is 2 to 1000, vmax is 1 to 9, H is 0 or more, Q is 1 or more, H + Q is at most 2000, and each count K is 1 to L.
- At most 100000 vehicles are generated. The absolute value of every integer appearing during the computation is less than 2^47. Therefore everything can be computed exactly with 64-bit integers, and the values are also represented exactly in languages that represent integers as double-precision floating-point numbers.
- No floating-point computation is needed. All output is uniquely determined by integer computation alone. For the given input, no value written with dec_d lies exactly halfway between two rounding candidates (its exact value is at least 10^-9 away from the exact halfway value).
