[Overview]
Write a program that draws a directed graph in layers following the Sugiyama framework and produces a picture made only of characters (ASCII art).
Processing proceeds in the following order. First, cycles are removed with the Eades-Lin-Smyth greedy method (edges whose direction was reversed are drawn back in their original direction), and transitive edges are removed if requested. Next, each node is assigned to a layer with the longest-path method or the Coffman-Graham method, and long edges are reduced by promotion if requested. Dummy vertices are inserted into edges that span two or more layers. Then edge crossings are reduced by layer-by-layer sweeps with the barycenter method or the median method and by adjacent exchanges (restarts from random initial orders and sifting can also be selected). The number of crossings is counted with an accumulator tree and checked by brute force. Horizontal coordinates are determined as integers with the Brandes-Kopf method (marking of type 1 conflicts, four vertical alignments, horizontal compaction of blocks, and balancing of the four layouts). Finally, edges are routed as polylines through the dummy vertices, and node boxes, lines, corners, crossings and arrows are drawn on a character canvas.
The program reads params.txt (default settings) and graphs.txt (the sequence of graphs) from the input/ folder and writes four files to the output/ folder: layout.txt (the results of each stage and the coordinates), stats.txt (statistics), crossings.csv (the number of crossings per sweep) and drawings.txt (the drawings). It prints nothing to standard output. The only random numbers used are the pseudo-random numbers (MINSTD) defined in this statement. No real numbers are used; everything is computed with integers (barycenters are also compared by comparing fractions through integer multiplication).

[Input files]
There are two input files, input/params.txt and input/graphs.txt. Both consist of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a line feed (LF). There are no spaces at the start or end of a line, and there are no tab characters. The words in a line are separated by single spaces. In both files, empty lines (lines with 0 characters) and comment lines (lines whose first character is "#") are skipped. Line numbers are counted 1, 2, ... from the start of the file, including empty lines and comment lines.
Below, an "integer word" is a sequence of 1 to 10 digits (0-9), and its value is the value read as a decimal number (leading zeros are allowed; e.g. the value of 007 is 7).

* input/params.txt (default settings)
Each line other than empty lines and comment lines has the form "<name> <value>", where the name is one of the following 13 names. Each name appears at most once (the order of the lines is not fixed), and for a name that does not appear, the default value given in parentheses is used. All values in params.txt are valid.
- layering (longest): the layer assignment method. Either longest (longest-path method) or coffman (Coffman-Graham method).
- width (4): the upper bound W on the width of a layer in the Coffman-Graham method; an integer from 1 to 40.
- promotion (off): whether to perform promotion; on or off.
- heuristic (barycenter): the reordering method used in sweeps; barycenter (barycenter method) or median (median method).
- sweeps (12): the upper bound K on the number of sweeps in one run; an integer from 0 to 50.
- node_sep (2): the minimum number of characters left between two adjacent nodes in the same layer; an integer from 1 to 9.
- dummy_sep (1): the minimum number of characters left between two adjacent vertices in the same layer when at least one of them is a dummy vertex; an integer from 1 to 9.
- layer_gap (3): the minimum number of characters between adjacent layers; an integer from 2 to 9.
- direction (TB): the drawing direction. One of TB (layer 0 at the top, from top to bottom), BT (layer 0 at the bottom, from bottom to top), LR (layer 0 at the left, from left to right) and RL (layer 0 at the right, from right to left).
- sifting (off): whether to perform sifting; on or off.
- reduce (off): whether to remove transitive edges; on or off.
- restarts (0): the number R of restarts from random initial orders; an integer from 0 to 9.
- seed (1): the seed of the pseudo-random numbers; an integer from 1 to 2147483646.
Below, these 13 settings in this order (layering, width, promotion, heuristic, sweeps, node_sep, dummy_sep, layer_gap, direction, sifting, reduce, restarts, seed) are called the "settings order". A value expressed by a word, such as on/off or a method name, is called a "word value", and a value expressed by an integer is called an "integer value".

* input/graphs.txt (the sequence of graphs)
Each line other than empty lines and comment lines is one of the following 6 kinds.
- GRAPH <graph name>: the start of a graph block. The graph name has 1 to 20 characters and consists only of lowercase letters, digits and "_". Graph names are distinct within the file.
- OPTION <name> <value>: a setting used only for this graph. <name> and <value> are each one word (a sequence of one or more ASCII characters containing no space), but they are not necessarily a valid name or value ([Processing] section 2).
- NODE <id> <label>: declares one node. id is an integer from 1 to 999 written without extra zeros. The label has 1 to 12 characters and consists only of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, digits and "_". Different nodes may have the same label.
- EDGE <from> <to>: a directed edge from from to to. from and to are integers in the same form as id.
- PATH <from> <to>: the start and the end of a path to be drawn highlighted ([Processing] section 13). from and to are integers in the same form as id, and they are different from each other.
- END: the end of the block.
The file is a sequence of blocks, each starting with a GRAPH line and ending with an END line. There are no lines (other than empty lines and comment lines) outside blocks, and blocks are never nested. Within a block, OPTION, NODE, EDGE and PATH lines may appear in any order. A PATH line appears at most once in a block.

[Processing]
* Notation
- Positions of the elements of a sequence or list are counted from 0 at the start. |X| is the number of elements of a sequence or set X.
- floor(a / b) (b is a positive integer) is the largest integer not exceeding a / b. Even when a is negative, the result is the smaller integer, not the value with the fractional part truncated (e.g. floor(-7 / 2) = -4).
- "x mod m" (x >= 0, m is a positive integer) is the remainder of x divided by m.
- All the following computations are done exactly with integers. The absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values, is less than 2^31 (except that the product 48271 * x in section 9 (2) can be 2^31 or more; it is less than 2^47).

* 1. Overall flow
(1) Read params.txt and determine the default settings.
(2) Process the blocks of graphs.txt one at a time from the start. For each block, first read and check the block (section 2). If there is an error, output the block as an error ([Output files]) and go on to the next block.
(3) For a block without errors, perform graph analysis (section 3), cycle removal (section 4), transitive edge removal (section 5), layer assignment (section 6), dummy vertex insertion (section 7), crossing reduction (sections 8 and 9), horizontal coordinate assignment (section 10), routing geometry (section 11), drawing on the character canvas (section 12), and routes, path and highlighting (section 13) in this order, and output the results.
(4) Write the four output files.

* 2. Reading and checking a block
The settings of a block start from the default settings (the values of params.txt and the default values). Look at the lines of the block in order from the start and process them as follows. When an error is found, record only the kind of the first error and its line number, and skip the remaining lines of the block up to END (no further errors are recorded).
(1) OPTION <name> <value>: if <name> is none of the 13 setting names, or <value> is not a valid value for that name, it is the error bad_option. For a name with a word value, the value is valid if it matches one of the words listed in [Input files] exactly. For a name with an integer value, the value is valid if <value> is an integer word and its value is within the range listed in [Input files] (e.g. sweeps 75, sweeps -3, width x and direction tb are all errors). If it is valid, replace the value of that name in the block settings with the value that <value> represents (for an integer value, the value of the integer word; e.g. sweeps 04 gives 4. If there are several OPTION lines with the same name, the value of the later line is used).
(2) NODE <id> <label>: if id has already been declared in this block, it is duplicate_node; otherwise, if 40 nodes have already been declared, it is too_many_nodes. If neither, declare the node.
(3) EDGE <from> <to>: if from or to has not been declared by a NODE line earlier in this block, it is unknown_node. Otherwise, if from = to, it is a self-loop: discard this edge and add 1 to the number of self-loops. Otherwise, if an EDGE line with the same from and to has already been accepted, it is a duplicate edge: discard this edge and add 1 to the number of duplicate edges. If none of these applies, accept the edge.
(4) PATH <from> <to>: if from or to has not been declared by a NODE line earlier in this block, it is unknown_node. Otherwise, record from as the start of the path and to as its end.
(5) END: if there is no error and 0 nodes have been declared, it is no_nodes (the line number is that of the END line).
For a block without errors, the following terms are used.
- Let n be the number of nodes. The order of the nodes sorted by increasing id is called the "node order", and the nodes are given the "node numbers" 0, 1, ..., n - 1 in that order.
- Let the accepted edges be e_0, e_1, ..., e_{m-1} in the order in which they were accepted (m is the number of edges). This order is called the "edge order". In the input, EDGE u v and EDGE v u never both appear in the same block.
- The "display name" of a node is its id written in decimal.

* 3. Graph analysis
For the graph consisting of the m accepted edges (in their original direction), compute the following values.
- The out-degree out(v) and the in-degree in(v) of node v. A node with in(v) = 0 and out(v) > 0 is called a source, a node with out(v) = 0 and in(v) > 0 a sink, and a node with in(v) = out(v) = 0 isolated.
- The number of weakly connected components (the number of connected components when edge directions are ignored).
- The strongly connected components containing two or more nodes. Nodes u and v belong to the same strongly connected component when there is a directed path from u to v and also one from v to u.

* 4. Cycle removal (Eades-Lin-Smyth)
Build a sequence S of nodes as follows. Let the sequences head and tail be empty, and put all nodes in the "remaining" state. While there are remaining nodes, perform once the first applicable one of (a)-(c) below, and remove the chosen node (also remove all edges incident to that node). Out-degrees and in-degrees are counted using only the edges between remaining nodes.
(a) If there are remaining nodes with out-degree 0 (including isolated nodes), choose the one with the smallest node number among them and add it to the front of tail.
(b) Otherwise, if there are remaining nodes with in-degree 0, choose the one with the smallest node number among them and add it to the end of head.
(c) Otherwise, choose the node with the largest (out-degree - in-degree) (if tied, the one with the smallest node number) and add it to the end of head.
S is the sequence head followed by tail. An edge (u, v) is a "reversed edge" when u comes after v in S. A reversed edge (u, v) is treated as (v, u) in the layer assignment and the drawing below. The edges whose directions have been determined this way are called "DAG edges"; for a DAG edge (a, b), a is called the upper end and b the lower end (the DAG edges form no cycle). The DAG edges are also arranged in the edge order.

* 5. Transitive edge removal
When reduce is on, a non-reversed edge that satisfies the following condition is a "transitive edge": letting its DAG edge be (a, b), there is a directed path from a to b that consists of two or more edges and follows only DAG edges (that is, there is a DAG edge (a, c) with c != b such that b can be reached from c by following zero or more DAG edges). All DAG edges (including transitive edges and reversed edges) are used for this test, and all transitive edges are removed simultaneously. A reversed edge is never a transitive edge. When reduce is off, there are no transitive edges.
The edges that are not transitive edges are called "drawn edges". In sections 6 to 13 below, only (the DAG edges of) the drawn edges are used. When a DAG edge (a, b) is a drawn edge, a is called a predecessor of b, and b a successor of a.

* 6. Layer assignment
For each node v, determine a height h(v) counted from the bottom (an integer >= 0) as follows. For the DAG edge (a, b) of every drawn edge, h(a) > h(b) always holds.
(1) When layering is longest (longest-path method): a node with no successors has h(v) = 0; any other node has h(v) = 1 + (the maximum h of its successors).
(2) When layering is coffman (Coffman-Graham method): first, give each node a label pi(v). For k = 1, 2, ..., n in this order, the candidates are the nodes that do not have a label yet and all of whose predecessors have labels. For each candidate, form the sequence of the labels of its predecessors sorted in decreasing order; choose the candidate whose sequence is the smallest in lexicographic order and set its pi = k (if several candidates have the same sequence, the one with the smallest node number). In lexicographic order, two sequences are compared from the start, and the one with the smaller element at the first position where they differ is the smaller; when one sequence equals the beginning of the other, the shorter one is the smaller (the empty sequence is the smallest).
Next, determine the heights from the bottom. Let the height of the current layer be c = 0 and the number of nodes put in the current layer be cnt = 0. Repeat the following operation n times: among the nodes that do not have a height yet and all of whose successors have heights, choose the node v with the largest pi. If cnt = W, or the height of any successor of v is c, add 1 to c and set cnt to 0. Then set h(v) = c and add 1 to cnt.
(3) At this point, compute D_0 = (the sum of h(a) - h(b) - 1 over the DAG edges (a, b) of the drawn edges) (the number of dummy vertices before promotion).
(4) When promotion is on (promotion): repeat the following "round" until a round in which no node was promoted (that round is also included in the number of rounds). In one round, for v = 0, 1, ..., n - 1 in this order, for each v that has one or more predecessors, save the heights of all nodes, then perform the operation promote(v), which promotes v, and obtain the value diff. If diff < 0, the promotion is confirmed, and (round number, v, diff) is appended to the end of the promotion record. Round numbers are counted from 1. If diff >= 0, restore all heights to the saved values.
promote(v) returns a value as follows. Let diff = 0; for each predecessor u of v (in any order), if h(u) = h(v) + 1 at that moment, add the value of promote(u) to diff. Then add 1 to h(v) and return diff + (the number of successors of v) - (the number of predecessors of v).
(5) Normalizing layer numbers: let H = (the maximum of h), and compute the level t(v) = H - h(v) of each node. Level k is "used" when there is a node v with t(v) = k, or there is a DAG edge (a, b) of a drawn edge with t(a) < k < t(b). Renumber the used levels as 0, 1, 2, ... in increasing order; the result is the layer number lambda(v) of node v. Let L be the number of layers. Layer 0 is the first layer (the top one in TB), and for the DAG edge (a, b) of every drawn edge, lambda(a) < lambda(b) always holds.

* 7. Dummy vertices
Look at the drawn edges one at a time in the edge order; if the span s = lambda(b) - lambda(a) of its DAG edge (a, b) is 2 or more, create one dummy vertex in each of the layers lambda(a) + 1, lambda(a) + 2, ..., lambda(b) - 1, in this order. The dummy vertices are given the display names d1, d2, d3, ... in the order in which they are created within the graph. Nodes and dummy vertices together are called "vertices". The vertex number of a node is its node number, and the dummy vertices get the vertex numbers n, n + 1, ... in the order in which they are created.
The "chain" of a drawn edge is the sequence of vertices a, (the dummy vertices of that edge in layer order), b. Two adjacent vertices (p, q) of a chain are called a "segment", with p as its upper end (layer i) and q as its lower end (layer i + 1). Each segment belongs to the edge of the chain that contains it. The lower neighbor of p is q, and the upper neighbor of q is p. Let U(v) be the set of the upper neighbors of vertex v and D(v) the set of its lower neighbors. A dummy vertex has exactly one upper neighbor and one lower neighbor. The elements of D(v) are ordered by the edge order of the edges to which their segments belong (this is used in section 9 (1)).

* 8. Number of crossings
The sequence of the vertices of each layer is called the "order" of that layer. pos(v) is the position (counted from 0) of vertex v in the order of its layer.
The number of crossings between layer i and layer i + 1 is the number of pairs (unordered pairs) of two segments (p1, q1), (p2, q2) connecting layer i and layer i + 1 such that pos(p1) < pos(p2) and pos(q1) > pos(q2). The total number of crossings is the sum over i = 0 to L - 2 (0 if L = 1).
Count the number of crossings with the following accumulator tree method. Let q be the number of vertices of layer i + 1, and let c be the smallest power of 2 that is >= q (c = 1 if q <= 1). Initialize an integer array T of length 2c - 1 with 0. Represent each segment (p, q') connecting layer i and layer i + 1 by the pair of numbers (pos(p), pos(q')), and sort the pairs in increasing lexicographic order (by increasing first element, and by increasing second element when the first elements are equal). For each pair, in that order, let k = pos(q') + c - 1, add 1 to T[k], and while k > 0 repeat "if k is odd, add T[k + 1] to the number of crossings; set k to floor((k - 1) / 2), and add 1 to T[k]".
In stats.txt, also write the same number counted by the brute-force method that examines every pair of two segments (the two methods always give the same value).

* 9. Crossing reduction
(1) Initial order: make the orders of all layers empty and mark all vertices "unvisited". Define the visit visit(v) as "if v is unvisited, mark v visited, append v to the end of the order of layer lambda(v), and perform visit(w) for each vertex w of D(v) in the order defined in section 7". The orders obtained by performing visit(v) for the nodes v = 0, 1, ..., n - 1 in this order are the initial order (every vertex is appended exactly once).
(2) Pseudo-random numbers and restarts: for each graph, initialize the state x with seed. The "next random number" is the value of x after x has been updated to (48271 * x) mod 2147483647 (x keeps being updated during the processing of the graph, and it also carries over between restarts).
Runs are performed R + 1 times, for r = 0, 1, ..., R. The starting order of run 0 is the initial order. The starting order of run r (r >= 1) is the initial order with each layer, from layer 0 to layer L - 1 in this order, rearranged as follows: for j = (the number of vertices of that layer) - 1, ..., 2, 1 in this order, let k = (next random number) mod (j + 1), and swap the vertices at positions j and k (if k = j, do nothing; no random numbers are used for a layer with at most one vertex).
(3) One run: let O_0 be the starting order and C_0 its total number of crossings. Perform sweep s for s = 1, 2, ... in this order, and let O_s be the order after the sweep and C_s its total number of crossings. However, before performing sweep s, end the sweeps of that run if any of the following holds: C_{s-1} = 0; s - 1 = K; s - 1 >= 2 and O_{s-1} and O_{s-3} are the same order in every layer (further sweeps would only repeat the same orders).
Sweep s is downward if s is odd and upward if s is even. In a downward sweep, for the layers i = 1, 2, ..., L - 1 in this order, reorder the order of layer i by the method of (4). Here the reference vertices of each vertex v are U(v) (vertices of layer i - 1, whose positions are those after they have already been reordered in this sweep). In an upward sweep, reorder the layers i = L - 2, L - 3, ..., 0 in this order, with D(v) (vertices of layer i + 1) as the reference vertices. When all layers have been reordered, perform the adjacent exchanges of (5). The "moved count" of this sweep is the number of vertices whose position after the reordering (before the adjacent exchanges) differs from their position in O_{s-1}, and the "swap count" is the number of swaps performed in the adjacent exchanges.
(4) Reordering a layer: vertices that have no reference vertex at all stay at their current positions. The other vertices are sorted by increasing key as defined below (vertices with equal keys keep their current relative order) and placed in the remaining positions from left to right.
- For barycenter: the key is the average of the positions of the reference vertices (the sum of the positions / the number of reference vertices). Two keys a / b and c / d are compared exactly by comparing a * d and c * b.
- For median: letting the positions of the reference vertices in increasing order be p_0 <= p_1 <= ... <= p_{d-1} (d is the number of reference vertices), the key is the integer p_{floor((d-1) / 2)} + p_{floor(d / 2)} (twice the median).
(5) Adjacent exchanges: repeat the following pass until a pass in which no swap occurs. In one pass, for the layers i = 0, 1, ..., L - 1 in this order, and within each layer for j = 0, 1, ..., (the number of vertices) - 2 in this order, for the vertex v at position j and the vertex w at position j + 1 at that moment, compute c(v, w) and c(w, v), and if c(v, w) > c(w, v), swap v and w (the swapped positions are used immediately, starting with the next comparison). c(v, w) is the number of pairs (a, b) with a in U(v), b in U(w) and pos(a) > pos(b), plus the number of pairs (a, b) with a in D(v), b in D(w) and pos(a) > pos(b) (the number of crossings among the segments incident to v and w when v is placed immediately to the left of w).
(6) Result of a run: the s with the smallest C_s (if tied, the smallest s) is the best sweep of that run, and O_s is the best order of that run. Among all runs, the run whose best order has the smallest number of crossings (if tied, the run with the smaller number) is the best run, and its best order is adopted.
(7) Sifting (when sifting is on): starting from the adopted order, repeat the following "round" until a round in which no move occurred (that round is included). Rounds are numbered from 1. In one round, for each vertex v in vertex number order (0, 1, ...), do the following. Let i be the layer of v and j0 the current position of v. Remove v from layer i (keeping the order of the other vertices), and compute the cost cost(p) of inserting v at position p (counted from 0; p = 0, 1, ..., (the number of vertices after the removal)) as the sum of the number of crossings between layer i - 1 and layer i (when i >= 1) and the number of crossings between layer i and layer i + 1 (when i <= L - 2). If cost(j0) equals the minimum of cost, put v back at position j0; otherwise insert v at the smallest p among those with the minimum cost. If the position differs from j0, count it as one move. At the end of the round, record the number of moves in that round and the total number of crossings at that time.
(8) Final order: the final order is the order after sifting if sifting is on, and the order adopted in (6) if it is off. Below, pos(v) is the position in the final order. In the final order, for each pair of two crossing segments, add 1 to each of the edges to which the two segments belong; the result is the number of crossings of that edge.

* 10. Horizontal coordinates (Brandes-Kopf method)
For each vertex v, determine an integer coordinate x(v) in the direction in which the vertices of a layer are arranged (below, the horizontal direction; left-right in TB and BT, up-down in LR and RL).
(1) Sizes and required separations: the width of a node v is w(v) = (the number of characters of its label) + 2, and the width of a dummy vertex is 1. When direction is TB or BT, let l(v) = floor((w(v) - 1) / 2) and r(v) = w(v) - 1 - l(v) (the box of v occupies x(v) - l(v) through x(v) + r(v) in the horizontal direction). When direction is LR or RL, let l(v) = r(v) = 0 for every vertex.
When vertex a is immediately to the left of vertex b in the same layer (the position of a + 1 = the position of b), the required separation is delta(a, b) = r(a) + l(b) + 1 + g, where g is node_sep if a and b are both nodes, and dummy_sep if at least one of them is a dummy vertex. The coordinates satisfy x(b) - x(a) >= delta(a, b).
(2) Marking type 1 conflicts: a segment whose two ends are both dummy vertices is called an "inner segment". For i = 0, 1, ..., L - 2 in this order, with layer i as A and layer i + 1 as B, do the following. Let k0 = 0 and t = 0. For l1 = 0, 1, ..., |B| - 1 in this order, with v = (the vertex at position l1 of B), if v is the lower end of an inner segment or l1 = |B| - 1, do the following: if v is the lower end of an inner segment, let k1 = pos(the upper neighbor of v); otherwise let k1 = |A| - 1. For each position l = t, t + 1, ..., l1, for every upper neighbor u of the vertex w at position l of B such that pos(u) < k0 or pos(u) > k1, mark the segment (u, w). Then set t = l1 + 1 and k0 = k1.
(3) Four alignments: for each of the four variants ul, ur, dl, dr, compute a partition into blocks. The first letter of a variant is the vertical direction: for u, the layers are processed in the order 0, 1, ..., L - 1 with the upper neighbors as reference vertices; for d, the layers are processed in the order L - 1, L - 2, ..., 0 with the lower neighbors as reference vertices. The second letter is the horizontal direction: for l, each layer is scanned from left to right (by increasing position); for r, from right to left (by decreasing position). Let q(v) be the rank (counted from 0) of vertex v in the scan of its layer (q(v) = pos(v) for l, and q(v) = (the number of vertices of the layer) - 1 - pos(v) for r).
Set root(v) = v and align(v) = v for every vertex. For each layer from the second one onward in the processing order of the layers, let rho = -1, and look at the vertices v of that layer in scan order. If the number d of reference vertices of v is 1 or more, arrange the reference vertices as u_0, u_1, ..., u_{d-1} by increasing q, and for m = floor((d - 1) / 2), floor(d / 2) in this order (the same m twice if d is odd), do the following: if align(v) = v, the segment connecting u_m and v is not marked, and rho < q(u_m), then set align(u_m) = v, root(v) = root(u_m), align(v) = root(v) and rho = q(u_m).
Each set of vertices with equal root(v) obtained this way is one "block" (each block contains at most one vertex of each layer). root(v) is the representative of the block.
(4) Horizontal compaction: in each layer, for every two vertices a, b that are adjacent in scan order (b immediately after a), create the constraint "the block of b is placed at least delta further on than the block of a". delta is delta(a, b) for the l variants and delta(b, a) for the r variants (delta is always computed with the vertex that is on the left in the original order written first). If there are several constraints for the same pair of blocks, the maximum delta is used. Regard these constraints as edges of weight delta from block to block (this graph has no cycles).
For each block beta, let y1(beta) be 0 if no edge enters beta, and otherwise the maximum of y1(alpha) + delta over the edges entering beta (alpha -> beta, weight delta). Next, let y2(beta) be y1(beta) if no edge leaves beta, and otherwise the larger of y1(beta) and "the minimum of y2(gamma) - delta over the edges leaving beta (beta -> gamma, weight delta)". The coordinate of vertex v in a variant is y2(root(v)) for the l variants and -y2(root(v)) for the r variants.
(5) Balancing: for each variant k, compute lo_k = (the minimum of x_k(v) - l(v)), hi_k = (the maximum of x_k(v) + r(v)) and the width hi_k - lo_k + 1 (x_k is the coordinate in variant k). Choose the variant k* with the smallest width (if tied, the one that comes first in the order ul, ur, dl, dr), add lo_{k*} - lo_k to the coordinates of each l variant, and add hi_{k*} - hi_k to the coordinates of each r variant. The coordinates after the addition are called the "aligned coordinates". For each vertex v, letting its four aligned coordinates in increasing order be a1 <= a2 <= a3 <= a4, set x(v) = floor((a2 + a3) / 2). Finally, subtract (the minimum of x(v) - l(v)) from every x(v).

* 11. Routing geometry
The direction in which the layers are arranged is called the "main direction", and the horizontal direction (section 10) the "secondary direction". Below, shapes are first determined in coordinates (main coordinate m, secondary coordinate c) in which the layer 0 side is main coordinate 0, and they are mapped to the rows and columns of the character canvas in section 12.
(1) Buses and tracks: for each i = 0, 1, ..., L - 2, route the space between layer i and layer i + 1 (below, gap i) as follows. Among the segments (p, q) of gap i, those with x(p) != x(q) are called "bent segments" and those with x(p) = x(q) "straight segments". A bent segment (p, q) belongs to "the upper bus of p" if there are two or more bent segments of gap i with p as the upper end, and to "the lower bus of q" otherwise. The interval [lo, hi] of each bus consists of the minimum and the maximum of x over the end vertices of the segments belonging to that bus.
Sort the buses by increasing lo; if lo is equal, by increasing hi; if that is also equal, upper buses before lower buses; if that is also equal, by increasing x of the vertex that defines the bus (p for an upper bus, q for a lower bus). Starting with an empty list of tracks, place the buses one at a time in this order on "the track with the smallest number among the tracks whose end value e satisfies e + 1 < lo", and set the end value of that track to hi. If there is no such track, add a new track to the end of the list of tracks and place the bus on it. Tracks are numbered from 1. Let T_i be the number of tracks of gap i.
Let the width of gap i be G_i = max(layer_gap, T_i + 2), and the position of track t (1 <= t <= T_i) within the gap be o = t + floor((G_i - 2 - T_i) / 2). The position o of a segment belonging to a bus is the position of that bus's track.
(2) Bands: the thickness th_i of the band of layer i is 1 if direction is TB or BT, and the maximum width of the vertices of layer i (the width of a dummy vertex is 1) if it is LR or RL. The band starts are b_0 = 0 and b_{i+1} = b_i + th_i + G_i; the band of layer i occupies the main coordinates b_i to b_i + th_i - 1, and gap i occupies the main coordinates b_i + th_i to b_{i+1} - 1. The main coordinate of position o within gap i is b_i + th_i + o. The length in the main direction is M = b_{L-1} + th_{L-1}, and the length in the secondary direction is N = (the maximum of x(v) + r(v)) + 1.
(3) Chain ends: the size e(v) of vertex v in the main direction is 1 if direction is TB or BT, and w(v) (1 for a dummy vertex) if it is LR or RL. For the chain a = v_0, v_1, ..., v_s = b of a drawn edge, the "upper endpoint" of the edge is (main coordinate b_{lambda(a)} + e(a), secondary coordinate x(a)), and its "lower endpoint" is (main coordinate b_{lambda(b)} - 1, secondary coordinate x(b)). The upper endpoint is the position just below (in LR, just to the right of) the box of a, and the lower endpoint is the position just above (in LR, just to the left of) the box of b.

* 12. Character canvas
(1) Canvas size and coordinate mapping: if direction is TB or BT, the canvas has M rows and N columns; if it is LR or RL, N rows and M columns. Rows and columns are counted from 0; row 0 is the top and column 0 is the leftmost. The position with main coordinate m and secondary coordinate c is mapped, with m' being "M - 1 - m for BT and RL, and m for TB and LR", to the cell at row m', column c in TB and BT, and to the cell at row c, column m' in LR and RL.
(2) Line characters: each cell has a set of line characters (initially empty). The character of a "main-direction line" is "|" in TB and BT and "-" in LR and RL, and the character of a "secondary-direction line" is the opposite ("-" in TB and BT, "|" in LR and RL). In (a)-(c) below, characters are added to the sets of the cells at the specified positions.
(a) Dummy vertex v (layer i): a main-direction line at main coordinates b_i + j (j = 0, 1, ..., th_i - 1), secondary coordinate x(v).
(b) When direction is LR or RL, a node v (layer i) that has a lower neighbor: a main-direction line at main coordinates b_i + j (j = w(v), w(v) + 1, ..., th_i - 1), secondary coordinate x(v) (a short line from the box to the end of the band; if w(v) = th_i, nothing is done).
(c) Each segment (p, q) of gap i: let g0 = b_i + th_i and G = G_i. For a straight segment, a main-direction line at main coordinates g0 + j (j = 0 to G - 1), secondary coordinate x(p). For a bent segment (position o), a main-direction line at main coordinates g0 + j (j = 0 to o - 1), secondary coordinate x(p); "+" at main coordinate g0 + o, secondary coordinates x(p) and x(q); a secondary-direction line at main coordinate g0 + o at each secondary coordinate strictly between x(p) and x(q); and a main-direction line at main coordinates g0 + j (j = o + 1 to G - 1), secondary coordinate x(q).
(3) Arrows: for each drawn edge, if it is not a reversed edge, put "v" in TB, "^" in BT, ">" in LR and "<" in RL at the cell of the lower endpoint. If it is a reversed edge, put "^" in TB, "v" in BT, "<" in LR and ">" in RL at the cell of the upper endpoint. An arrow points to the to side of the original edge direction (from from to to).
(4) Highlighting: put "#" at the highlighted cells defined in section 13.
(5) Boxes: for each node v, write the string consisting of the label enclosed in "[" and "]" (for a node on the path of section 13, enclosed in "{" and "}") rightward; in TB and BT, starting at the cell to which the position with main coordinate b_{lambda(v)} and secondary coordinate x(v) - l(v) is mapped. In LR, write it rightward starting at row x(v), column b_{lambda(v)}; in RL, rightward starting at row x(v), column M - b_{lambda(v)} - w(v) (boxes are placed against the layer 0 side of the band; in RL they touch the right end of the band).
(6) Cell characters: the character of each cell is the box character if there is one; otherwise the arrow if there is one; otherwise "#" if the cell is highlighted; otherwise, if the set of line characters contains only one kind of character, that character; if it contains two or more kinds, "+"; if it is empty, a space.
A cell whose set of line characters contains both "|" and "-" is called a "crossing cell".

* 13. Routes, path and highlighting
(1) Routes: let the chain of a drawn edge be a = v_0, ..., v_s = b. Form the sequence of points consisting of the upper endpoint; then, for j = 0, 1, ..., s - 1 in this order, if the segment (v_j, v_{j+1}) is a bent segment (gap lambda(v_j), position o), the two points (main coordinate b_{lambda(v_j)} + th_{lambda(v_j)} + o, secondary coordinate x(v_j)) and (the same main coordinate, secondary coordinate x(v_{j+1})); and finally the lower endpoint. Mapping each point to a cell (row, column) by section 12 (1) gives the route point sequence of that edge. For a reversed edge, this sequence is reversed (a route always starts from the from side of the original edge). Two adjacent points are in the same row or in the same column. The length of a route is 1 + (the sum, over all pairs of adjacent points, of the absolute difference of their rows and the absolute difference of their columns). The number of bends of an edge is twice the number of bent segments of its chain.
(2) Shortest path: if the block has PATH from to, perform a breadth-first search from from in the directed graph that uses the drawn edges in their original direction (from from to to). Initially only from has been found, and the queue contains only from. Until the queue is empty, for the node taken from the front of the queue, examine the end nodes of the drawn edges that start at that node in increasing node number order, and each time a node that has not been found yet is found, append it to the end of the queue with the taken node as its parent. If to is found, the path is the reverse of the sequence obtained by following parents from to back to from. If it is not found, there is no path.
(3) Highlighting: if there is a path, for each pair of adjacent nodes (u, v) of the path, all cells on the line segments connecting adjacent points of the route point sequence of the edge u -> v (a drawn edge) (both ends included) are highlighted cells. The boxes of the nodes on the path are enclosed in "{" and "}".

[Output files]
Write the following four files to the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, every line (including the last line) ends with a line feed (LF), and there are no spaces at the end of a line. In the formats below, < > marks a part replaced by its value, and [ ] marks a part written only under a condition. The words in a line are separated by single spaces. Integers are written in decimal, with "-" for negative numbers and without extra zeros. The "edge name" is "<from id>-><to id>" (e.g. 4->2; written in the original direction).

[Source files]
Split the source code into files with the following 6 roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow, and the largest share of the processing. The processing flow for each block, graph analysis (degrees, sources, sinks and isolated nodes, weakly connected components, strongly connected components), applying edge reversal and transitive edge removal, layer number normalization, creating dummy vertices, chains, segments and neighbors, the initial order, pseudo-random numbers and the starting orders of restarts, sweeps (layer reordering by the barycenter and median methods, moved counts), adjacent exchanges, the sweep termination test and the selection of the best order, sifting, the number of crossings per edge, bus and track assignment, band and gap geometry, directing the drawing on the canvas (lines, arrows, highlighting, boxes), route point sequences and lengths, the path by breadth-first search, collecting statistics, and building and writing the lines of the four output files.
- graph_io: reading params.txt and graphs.txt, checking setting names and values, checking blocks (error kinds and line numbers, numbers of self-loops and duplicate edges, the start and end of the path).
- acyclic_layering: the sequence S by the Eades-Lin-Smyth greedy method, the longest-path method, the Coffman-Graham method (labels and heights), promotion (rounds and the promotion record).
- crossing: the number of crossings by the accumulator tree, the number of crossings by brute force, the total number of crossings, and c(v, w) used in the adjacent exchanges.
- coordinates: the Brandes-Kopf method (marking type 1 conflicts, the four alignments and blocks, horizontal compaction of blocks, balancing and aligned coordinates).
- canvas: the character canvas (mapping main and secondary coordinates to cells, sets of line characters, main-direction and secondary-direction lines and bent segments, the characters of arrows, boxes and highlighting, determining the character of each cell, the number of crossing cells, building the lines with the ruler and row numbers).

[Constraints]
- graphs.txt has at most 3000 lines, and each line has at most 80 characters. There are at most 30 blocks.
- A block contains at most 60 NODE lines and at most 150 EDGE lines.
- EDGE u v and EDGE v u never both appear in the same block.
- In a block without errors, there are at most 120 accepted edges and at most 600 vertices (nodes and dummy vertices), and the number of canvas rows and the number of canvas columns are both at most 999.
- The values in params.txt are within the ranges stated in [Input files].
- When computed by the methods of this statement, the absolute value of every integer is less than 2^31. However, the product 48271 * x in section 9 (2) can be 2^31 or more (it is less than 2^47), so compute it with, for example, 64-bit integers.
