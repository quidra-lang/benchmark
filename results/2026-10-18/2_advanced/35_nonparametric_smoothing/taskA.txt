[Overview]
Write a program that fits various nonparametric regression (scatterplot smoothing) methods to noisy one-dimensional measurement data (consisting of several series, each of which is a sequence of (x, y) pairs), selects the smoothing parameters automatically, and compares the methods.
The methods are the least-squares straight line used as the baseline for comparison, the running mean and the running median, Nadaraya-Watson estimation and local linear regression with three polynomial kernels (Epanechnikov, biweight, triweight), LOESS with robustness iterations (local degree 1 and local degree 2), the cubic smoothing spline by the Reinsch algorithm, and isotonic regression by the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm (PAVA). The smoothing parameters (window width, bandwidth, span, lambda) are selected from candidates by leave-one-out cross-validation (generalized cross-validation for the spline).
For the selected fits, the program computes diagnostics such as the K-fold cross-validation value, the residual sum of squares, the equivalent degrees of freedom, the residual standard error, an F statistic for nonlinearity, residual autocorrelations, a runs test and residual quantiles, and determines the ranking of the methods, the agreement with the best method, the slopes of the spline, and outliers and influential points. It also outputs the estimates at grid points and test points, variability bands and bootstrap intervals, the blocks of the isotonic regression, and plots drawn with characters of the data with the curves and of the residuals.
The only functions used in the computation are the four arithmetic operations and the square root. Random numbers come only from the specified MINSTD generator. All rules are defined in [Processing], so the result is uniquely determined.
The input is the 3 files in the input/ folder (measurement data, test points, settings), and the results are written to 7 files in the output/ folder. Nothing is printed to standard output.

[Input files]
The input files are the 3 files input/series.csv, input/test_points.csv and input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There are no empty lines and no spaces at the start or end of a line. Values are separated by a comma in the CSV files and by a single space in params.txt.
A "two-decimal number" is a decimal number made of, in this order, "-" (only for a negative number), a sequence of one or more digits (the integer part, with no extra leading zeros), ".", and a sequence of exactly two digits (the fractional part) (examples: 3.40, -0.25, 12.00). The integer (including its sign) obtained by removing the decimal point from a two-decimal number is called the "value in hundredths" of that two-decimal number (examples: 3.40 is 340, -0.25 is -25, 0.05 is 5).
A "plain decimal" is a positive decimal number made of a sequence of one or more digits (the integer part, with no extra leading zeros), followed, if needed, by "." and a sequence of one or more digits (the fractional part) (examples: 10, 0.001, 1.96). Exponent notation is not used.
An integer is a decimal number with no extra leading zeros.

## input/series.csv (measurement data)
The first line is a header line, the string series,x,y. Each line from the second line on represents one point and consists of the series name, the x value and the y value, in this order, separated by commas.
- A series name is a string of 1 to 16 characters that starts with a lowercase English letter and consists of lowercase English letters, digits and "_".
- The x value and the y value are two-decimal numbers.
Lines of different series may be interleaved, and the lines of one series are not necessarily in order of x. Within one series, all x values are distinct.

## input/test_points.csv (test points)
The first line is a header line, the string series,x. Each line from the second line on consists of a series name and an x value (a two-decimal number) separated by a comma, and requests the estimate of that series at that position. The series name is one that appears in series.csv. The x value may lie outside the x range of that series. The order of the lines in the file is called the "test point order".

## input/params.txt (settings)
The following 17 lines appear in this order. Each line consists of an uppercase keyword and one or more values, separated by single spaces.
- WINDOWS w_1 w_2 ...: the candidate window widths of the running mean and the running median (odd numbers >= 3, 1 to 10 values, in increasing order and distinct).
- KERNELS K_1 ...: the names of the kernels to use (1 to 3 distinct names among epanechnikov, biweight, triweight, in any order).
- BANDWIDTHS h_1 h_2 ...: the candidate bandwidths (positive two-decimal numbers, 1 to 10 values, in increasing order and distinct).
- LOESS_DEGREES p_1 ...: the degrees of the LOESS local polynomial (1 to 2 distinct values among 1 and 2, in any order).
- SPANS f_1 f_2 ...: the candidate LOESS spans (two-decimal numbers greater than 0 and at most 1.00, 1 to 10 values, in increasing order and distinct).
- ROBUST_ITERATIONS R: the number of LOESS robustness iterations (an integer from 0 to 10).
- LAMBDAS lambda_1 lambda_2 ...: the candidate lambdas of the smoothing spline (plain decimals, 1 to 10 values, in increasing order of value and distinct).
- CV_FOLDS K: the number of folds of the K-fold cross-validation (an integer from 2 to 10).
- ISOTONIC d_1 d_2 ...: the direction specification of the isotonic regression. One of increasing, decreasing, auto is given for each series, one per series, in the series order defined in 2 of [Processing]. The number of values equals the number of series.
- GRID_POINTS G: the number of grid points (an integer from 3 to 201).
- BAND_Z z: the multiplier that determines the width of the variability bands (a plain decimal).
- BAND_METHODS m_1 ...: the names of the methods for which variability bands are computed (one or more distinct names, in any order).
- OUTLIER_THRESHOLD theta: the outlier threshold (a positive two-decimal number).
- BOOTSTRAP B t seed: the number of bootstrap repetitions B (an integer from 2 to 1000), the number t of values removed at each end of the interval (an integer >= 0 satisfying 2 * t < B), and the random seed seed (an integer from 1 to 2147483646).
- PLOT_SERIES s_1 ...: the names of the series to plot (one or more distinct names that appear in series.csv, in any order).
- PLOT_METHODS m_1 ...: the names of the methods to draw in the plots (1 to 9 distinct names, in any order).
- PLOT_SIZE W H: the number of columns W (an integer from 10 to 100) and the number of rows H (an integer from 4 to 40) of the plots.
The method names in BAND_METHODS and PLOT_METHODS are contained in the list of methods determined by KERNELS and LOESS_DEGREES (3 of [Processing]). BAND_METHODS contains neither running_median nor isotonic.

[Processing]
## 1. Notation and computation rules
- All real computations are done in double precision (IEEE 754 64-bit floating point). The two-decimal numbers and plain decimals of the input are converted to double precision. The double-precision value of a two-decimal number is the same as the real obtained by dividing its value in hundredths by 100.
- sqrt is the square root, |t| is the absolute value of t, floor(t) is the largest integer <= t, and min and max are the minimum and the maximum. Only the four arithmetic operations and sqrt are used in the computation.
- A sum written with Sum starts from 0 and adds one term at a time in increasing order of the index (in the order of the sequence when an order is defined). Other sums are computed the same way unless stated otherwise. Within an expression, multiplications and divisions are computed from the left in the order written, and additions and subtractions are also computed from the left in the order written (parenthesized parts are computed first).
- "The real obtained by dividing the integer A by the integer B" is the value obtained by converting A and B to double precision and then dividing once. floor(A / B) denotes the quotient (an integer) of a non-negative integer A divided by a positive integer B.
- Comparisons of x values, comparisons of distances and range tests are all done exactly with the integers in hundredths.
- The "median" of m reals (m >= 1) is, with them sorted in increasing order, the ((m + 1) / 2)-th value if m is odd, and the sum of the (m / 2)-th value and the (m / 2 + 1)-th value divided by 2 if m is even.
- Every rule that selects the largest or smallest item always states how to choose when items have equal values.

## 2. Series and points
Order the series by the line on which each series name first appears in series.csv; this order is called the "series order". The processing of 3 to 9 is done for each series separately (only the random number generator of 8(4) is used continuously across series).
Sort the points of each series in increasing order of x and call them point 1, point 2, ..., point n (n is the number of points of that series). This order is called the "point order". The x value and y value of point i are written x_i and y_i, and their values in hundredths X_i and Y_i.
The x position represented by an integer X0 in hundredths is called "position X0", and x0 is the real obtained by dividing the integer X0 by 100.

## 3. Methods
The names and order of the methods are as follows. This order is called the "method order".
1. linear
2. running_mean
3. running_median
4. for each kernel K of KERNELS, in KERNELS order, nw_K (example: nw_biweight)
5. for each kernel K of KERNELS, in KERNELS order, ll_K (example: ll_biweight)
6. for each degree p of LOESS_DEGREES, in LOESS_DEGREES order, loessp (example: loess1)
7. spline
8. isotonic
For example, if KERNELS is biweight only and LOESS_DEGREES is 1 only, the methods are the 8 methods linear, running_mean, running_median, nw_biweight, ll_biweight, loess1, spline, isotonic.
running_mean, running_median, nw_K, ll_K, loessp and spline are called "methods with a parameter". Their parameter candidates are the values of WINDOWS, WINDOWS, BANDWIDTHS, BANDWIDTHS, SPANS and LAMBDAS respectively, and the order of those values is called the "candidate order".
Once its parameter is fixed (for a method with a parameter), each method defines the following. How they are defined concretely is described in 4.
- The fitted values yhat_i (i = 1, ..., n).
- The estimate f(X0) at any position X0. It may be undefined for nw_K and ll_K.
- The equivalent degrees of freedom edf. It is undefined for running_median.
- The diagonal elements lev_i (i = 1, ..., n) of the smoother matrix. running_median and isotonic do not have them.
- The estimate weights l_j(X0) (j = 1, ..., n). They are the coefficients satisfying f(X0) = Sum_j l_j(X0) * y_j and are used only for the variability bands (7(6) and 8(3)). running_median and isotonic do not have them.
- The subset estimate. This means fitting the same method with the same parameter to only a subset U of the points (renumbered, keeping the point order) and computing the estimate at any position. How it is defined for each method is described in 4. It may be undefined for nw_K and ll_K.
The value at position X_i of the subset estimate with U = the n - 1 points other than point i is called the "leave-one-out prediction" yhat_(-i).

## 4. Fitting each method
(1) linear (least-squares straight line)
With xbar = (Sum x_i) / n, ybar = (Sum y_i) / n, Sxx = Sum (x_i - xbar) * (x_i - xbar), Sxy = Sum (x_i - xbar) * (y_i - ybar), b = Sxy / Sxx and a = ybar - b * xbar, define:
- f(X0) = a + b * x0, yhat_i = a + b * x_i, edf = 2
- lev_i = 1 / n + (x_i - xbar) * (x_i - xbar) / Sxx, l_j(X0) = 1 / n + (x0 - xbar) * (x_j - xbar) / Sxx
- The subset estimate on U is a + b * x0 with xbar, ybar, Sxx, Sxy, b and a computed from the points of U only (with n being the number of points of U).

(2) running_mean and running_median (window width w)
Let k = (w - 1) / 2. The "half-width" of point i is r_i = min(k, i - 1, n - i), and the 2 * r_i + 1 points from point i - r_i to point i + r_i are the "window" of point i (near both ends the window shrinks symmetrically, and the windows of point 1 and point n consist of that point only).
- running_mean: yhat_i is the real obtained by dividing the sum (an integer) of Y of the points in the window of point i by the integer 100 * (2 * r_i + 1). lev_i is the real obtained by dividing the integer 1 by the integer 2 * r_i + 1, and edf = Sum lev_i.
- running_median: yhat_i is the (r_i + 1)-th value of the Y of the points in the window of point i, sorted in increasing order, divided by 100 (it equals the y of a point having that Y).
- For both methods, f(X0) is yhat_c of the point c "nearest" to position X0. The point nearest to position X0 is the point j with the smallest |X_j - X0| (if tied, the one with the smaller index).
- The weights of running_mean are, with c the point nearest to position X0, l_j(X0) = 1 / (2 * r_c + 1) (the real obtained by dividing the integer 1 by the integer 2 * r_c + 1) for the points j contained in the window of c, and 0 for the other points.
- The subset estimate on U recomputes the half-widths, windows and yhat from the points of U only (with n being the number of points of U), and is the yhat of the point of U nearest to position X0.

(3) nw_K and ll_K (kernel K, bandwidth h)
Let H be the value in hundredths of h. The points "used" for the estimate at position X0 are the points j satisfying |X_j - X0| < H (for a subset estimate on U, they are chosen from the points of U). For each used point j let
  u_j = (x_j - x0) / h, t_j = 1 - u_j * u_j, d_j = x_j - x0
and define the kernel value K_j and the constant kappa as follows.
- epanechnikov: K_j = 0.75 * t_j, kappa = 0.75
- biweight: K_j = 0.9375 * t_j * t_j, kappa = 0.9375
- triweight: K_j = 1.09375 * t_j * t_j * t_j, kappa = 1.09375
Below, Sum is the sum over the used points (in point order). The weight l_j(X0) of a point that is not used is 0.
- nw_K: if no point is used, the estimate is undefined. With S0 = Sum K_j and T0 = Sum K_j * y_j, f(X0) = T0 / S0 and l_j(X0) = K_j / S0.
- ll_K: if fewer than 2 points are used, the estimate is undefined. With S0 = Sum K_j, S1 = Sum K_j * d_j, S2 = Sum K_j * d_j * d_j, T0 = Sum K_j * y_j, T1 = Sum K_j * d_j * y_j and Q = S0 * S2 - S1 * S1, f(X0) = (S2 * T0 - S1 * T1) / Q and l_j(X0) = K_j * (S2 - S1 * d_j) / Q.
The fitted values are yhat_i = f(X_i) using all points. Using S0, S2 and Q at position X_i, lev_i is kappa / S0 for nw_K and kappa * S2 / Q for ll_K, and edf = Sum lev_i. If even one yhat_i is undefined, yhat, lev_i and edf are undefined (they are always defined for the candidate selected in 5).

(4) loessp (local polynomial degree p, span f)
Let F be the value in hundredths of f. The "local fit at position X0" using a set of points P (m points) and a "robustness weight" rho_j of each point j is defined as follows.
1. Let q = floor(F * m / 100).
2. Sort the points of P in increasing order of |X_j - X0| (if tied, in increasing order of index), and call the first q points the "neighbourhood". Let E be the maximum of |X_j - X0| over the points of the neighbourhood.
3. For each point j of the neighbourhood, let a_j be the real obtained by dividing the integer |X_j - X0| by the integer E, s_j = 1 - a_j * a_j * a_j, w_j = s_j * s_j * s_j, v_j = w_j * rho_j and d_j = x_j - x0, and let the basis vector b_j be (b_j0, b_j1) = (1, d_j) if p = 1, and (b_j0, b_j1, b_j2) = (1, d_j, d_j * d_j) if p = 2.
4. Let A be the symmetric matrix and c the vector with components numbered 0, ..., p, where A_kl = Sum v_j * b_jk * b_jl and c_k = Sum v_j * b_jk * y_j (sums over the points of the neighbourhood, added in point order).
5. Compute the Cholesky decomposition of A, and find the solution beta of A beta = c and the solution zeta of A zeta = e (e is the vector whose component 0 is 1 and whose other components are 0). The estimate of the local fit is beta_0. The weight of a point j of the neighbourhood is l_j = v_j * (Sum_k zeta_k * b_jk) (sum over k = 0, ..., p), and the weight of a point not in the neighbourhood is 0.
The Cholesky decomposition and the solution of the linear systems are computed as follows (the components of matrices and vectors are numbered 0, ..., p).
- Decomposition: for k = 0, ..., p in order, and for each k for l = 0, ..., k in order, let sigma be A_kl minus L_k0 * L_l0, L_k1 * L_l1, ..., L_k(l-1) * L_l(l-1), subtracted one at a time in this order; if l = k then L_kk = sqrt(sigma), otherwise L_kl = sigma / L_ll.
- Solution of A x = r: for k = 0, ..., p in order, eta_k is r_k minus L_k0 * eta_0, ..., L_k(k-1) * eta_(k-1), subtracted one at a time in this order, divided by L_kk. Next, for k = p, ..., 0 in order, x_k is eta_k minus L_(k+1)k * x_(k+1), ..., L_pk * x_p, subtracted one at a time in this order, divided by L_kk.
The fit of loessp is computed as follows, updating the robustness weights.
- First, set rho_j = 1 for all points, and let yhat_i^(0) (i = 1, ..., n) be the estimate of the local fit at position X_i with P being all points.
- For iterations t = 1, ..., R in order, do the following. Let the residuals be e_i = y_i - yhat_i^(t-1), and let S_t be the median of |e_1|, ..., |e_n|. For each point i let u_i = e_i / (6 * S_t); if |u_i| < 1, let nu_i = 1 - u_i * u_i and rho_i = nu_i * nu_i; if |u_i| >= 1, let rho_i = 0. Using these rho, let yhat_i^(t) be the estimate of the local fit at position X_i with P being all points. S_t is called the "scale" of iteration t, the number of points with rho_i = 0 the "number of zero-weight points", and the maximum of |yhat_i^(t) - yhat_i^(t-1)| the "maximum change".
- The fitted values are yhat_i = yhat_i^(R). The rho computed in the last iteration (all 1 if R = 0) are called the "final weights". f(X0) and l_j(X0) are the estimate and the weights of the local fit at position X0 with P being all points and the final weights as the robustness weights. Using zeta_0 of the local fit at position X_i under the same conditions, lev_i = rho_i * zeta_0 (rho_i is the final weight of point i), and edf = Sum lev_i.
- The subset estimate on U is the estimate of the local fit with P = U and all rho_j = 1 (no robustness iterations are done).

(5) spline (smoothing spline, parameter lambda)
From the x of the points (n >= 3) and lambda, compute the following.
- h_i = x_(i+1) - x_i (i = 1, ..., n - 1)
- For j = 2, ..., n - 1, a_j = 1 / h_(j-1), c_j = 1 / h_j, b_j = -(a_j + c_j)
- The symmetric matrix M whose rows and columns are numbered 2, ..., n - 1. Its elements are as follows, and all other elements are 0.
  M_jj = (h_(j-1) + h_j) / 3 + lambda * (a_j * a_j + b_j * b_j + c_j * c_j) (j = 2, ..., n - 1)
  M_j(j+1) = M_(j+1)j = h_j / 6 + lambda * c_j * (b_j + b_(j+1)) (j = 2, ..., n - 2)
  M_j(j+2) = M_(j+2)j = lambda * c_j * c_(j+1) (j = 2, ..., n - 3)
- The LDL^T decomposition of M. For j = 2, ..., n - 1 in order, compute
  L_j(j-2) = M_(j-2)j / D_(j-2) (when j >= 4)
  L_j(j-1) = (M_(j-1)j - L_j(j-2) * D_(j-2) * L_(j-1)(j-2)) / D_(j-1) (when j >= 3)
  D_j = M_jj - L_j(j-1) * L_j(j-1) * D_(j-1) - L_j(j-2) * L_j(j-2) * D_(j-2)
  L and D that are not defined (L_j(j-1) for j < 3, L_j(j-2) for j < 4, D_1, D_0, etc.) are taken to be 0.
The "fit" to data v_1, ..., v_n is defined as follows (when x and lambda are the same, the decomposition is shared).
- For j = 2, ..., n - 1, let phi_j = (v_(j+1) - v_j) / h_j - (v_j - v_(j-1)) / h_(j-1).
- Forward substitution: for j = 2, ..., n - 1 in order, let z_j = phi_j - L_j(j-1) * z_(j-1) - L_j(j-2) * z_(j-2) (L and z that are not defined are 0).
- Back substitution: let gamma_1 = gamma_n = 0, and for j = n - 1, ..., 2 in order, let gamma_j = z_j / D_j - L_(j+1)j * gamma_(j+1) - L_(j+2)j * gamma_(j+2) (L and gamma that are not defined are 0).
- The fitted values are g_i = v_i - lambda * omega_i (i = 1, ..., n), where omega_1 = (gamma_2 - gamma_1) / h_1, omega_n = 0 - (gamma_n - gamma_(n-1)) / h_(n-1), and for the other i, omega_i = (gamma_(i+1) - gamma_i) / h_i - (gamma_i - gamma_(i-1)) / h_(i-1).
For the g and gamma of a fit, the "evaluation" at position X0 is defined as follows.
- When X0 < X_1: with tau = (g_2 - g_1) / h_1 - h_1 * gamma_2 / 6, g_1 - (x_1 - x0) * tau
- When X0 > X_n: with tau = (g_n - g_(n-1)) / h_(n-1) + h_(n-1) * gamma_(n-1) / 6, g_n + (x0 - x_n) * tau
- When there is a point i with X0 = X_i: g_i
- Otherwise: for the i with X_i < X0 < X_(i+1), with pl = x0 - x_i and pr = x_(i+1) - x0,
  (pl * g_(i+1) + pr * g_i) / h_i - pl * pr * ((1 + pl / h_i) * gamma_(i+1) + (1 + pr / h_i) * gamma_i) / 6
The values of spline are as follows.
- yhat_i is g_i of the fit with v_i = y_i. f(X0) is the evaluation of that fit at position X0.
- lev_k (k = 1, ..., n) is g_k of the fit to the data with v_k = 1 and all other v_j = 0. edf = Sum lev_k.
- l_j(X0) is the evaluation at position X0 of the fit to the data with v_j = 1 and all others 0.
- The subset estimate on U builds the decomposition from the x of the points of U with the same lambda, and is the evaluation of the fit to the y of the points of U.

(6) isotonic (isotonic regression)
Once the direction (increasing or decreasing) is decided, the fit is computed by the following pool-adjacent-violators algorithm. A "block" is a range of consecutive points and holds the sum S (an integer) of Y and the number of points c. The "value" of a block is the real obtained by dividing S by the integer 100 * c.
- Start with an empty sequence of blocks, and for points 1, 2, ..., n in order, append a block consisting of that point only (S = Y_i, c = 1) at the right end of the sequence. After each append, while there are 2 or more blocks and the second block from the right (S_a, c_a) and the rightmost block (S_b, c_b) "violate", repeatedly merge these two into one block (joining the ranges, S = S_a + S_b, c = c_a + c_b). Violating means S_a * c_b > S_b * c_a for the increasing direction and S_a * c_b < S_b * c_a for the decreasing direction (compared exactly as integers).
- yhat_i is the value of the block that contains point i, and edf is the number of blocks converted to a real.
- f(X0) is yhat_j of the point with the largest index among the points j satisfying X_j <= X0 if there is such a point, and yhat_1 otherwise.
- Direction: if the ISOTONIC value of the series is increasing, the direction is increasing; if it is decreasing, the direction is decreasing. If it is auto, fit in both directions, call the respective Sum (y_i - yhat_i) * (y_i - yhat_i) the "increasing RSS" and the "decreasing RSS", and use increasing if increasing RSS <= decreasing RSS, otherwise decreasing.
- The subset estimate on U is f(X0) of the same method applied to the points of U with the direction decided from all points.

## 5. Parameter selection and loo values
For a method with a parameter, compute the "score" and the "candidate edf" of each candidate, in candidate order.
- The score of a method other than spline is the leave-one-out cross-validation value CV = (Sum_i (y_i - yhat_(-i)) * (y_i - yhat_(-i))) / n. If even one yhat_(-i) is undefined, the score is undefined.
- The score of spline is the generalized cross-validation value GCV = RSS / n / (tau * tau), where RSS = Sum (y_i - yhat_i) * (y_i - yhat_i) and tau = 1 - edf / n.
- The candidate edf is the edf with that candidate. For loessp, however, it is the edf of the fit without robustness iterations (with all rho set to 1). It is undefined for running_median and for the candidates of nw_K and ll_K whose yhat is undefined.
Among the candidates whose score is defined, the one with the smallest score (if tied, the earlier one in candidate order) is selected as the parameter of that method.
The "loo value" of each method is as follows. For running_mean, running_median, nw_K, ll_K and loessp, it is the score of the selected candidate. For spline (with the selected lambda), linear and isotonic (with the decided direction), it is the value computed by the CV formula above.
The "kfold value" of each method is the K-fold cross-validation value. Assign point i (i = 1, ..., n) to group ((i - 1) mod K) + 1, and for each group g (g = 1, ..., K), compute the value ytilde_i at position X_i of each point i of group g by the subset estimate with U = the points not in group g (the parameters are those selected in 5, and the direction of isotonic is the one decided from all points). kfold value = (Sum_i (y_i - ytilde_i) * (y_i - ytilde_i)) / n (sum in point order). If even one ytilde_i is undefined, the kfold value is undefined.

## 6. Diagnostics of the fits
For the fit of each method, compute the following values.
- Residuals e_i = y_i - yhat_i, RSS = Sum e_i * e_i, ybar = (Sum y_i) / n, TSS = Sum (y_i - ybar) * (y_i - ybar), r2 = 1 - RSS / TSS
- edf (as defined in 4), df = n - edf, rse = sqrt(RSS / df). When edf is undefined, df and rse are undefined too.
- f_nonlinear: for a method other than linear whose edf is defined and greater than 2, ((RSS_lin - RSS) / (edf - 2)) / (RSS / df), where RSS_lin is the RSS of linear. Otherwise it is undefined.
- Autocorrelation: for k = 1, ..., 5, acf_k = (Sum (e_i - ebar) * (e_(i+k) - ebar)) / (Sum (e_i - ebar) * (e_i - ebar)), where ebar = (Sum e_i) / n, the sum in the numerator is over i = 1, ..., n - k, and the sum in the denominator is over i = 1, ..., n.
- dw = (Sum (e_i - e_(i-1)) * (e_i - e_(i-1))) / RSS (sum over i = 2, ..., n)
- Runs test: list only the points with e_i != 0, in point order; let N+ be the number of points with e_i > 0, N- the number of points with e_i < 0, and the "number of runs" G the number of maximal stretches of consecutive points with the same sign. With N = N+ + N-, let mu = 1 + (the real obtained by dividing the integer 2 * N+ * N- by the integer N), V = the real obtained by dividing the integer 2 * N+ * N- * (2 * N+ * N- - N) by the integer N * N * (N - 1), and zr = (G - mu) / sqrt(V). Note that yhat_i of running_mean, running_median and isotonic is obtained from integers with a single division, so at some points yhat_i equals y_i exactly and e_i = 0.
- Largest residual: e_i and x_i of the point i with the largest |e_i| (if tied, the one with the smaller index).
- Quantiles: let s_1, ..., s_n be e_1, ..., e_n sorted in increasing order. For k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, with j = floor((n - 1) * k / 4) and r = (n - 1) * k - 4 * j, Q_k = s_(j+1) if r = 0, otherwise Q_k = s_(j+1) + r / 4 * (s_(j+2) - s_(j+1)) (r / 4 is the real obtained by dividing the integer r by the integer 4).
- Turning count: in the grid order of 8(1), let v_1, ..., v_M be the sequence of only the defined estimates of that method, and list only the nonzero values among Delta_k = v_(k+1) - v_k (k = 1, ..., M - 1). The turning count is the number of places in that list where two adjacent values have different signs.

## 7. Ranking, agreement, slope, outliers
(1) Ranking: sort all methods in increasing order of loo value (if tied, in method order). The method ranked first is called the "best method".
(2) Agreement: for each method m other than the best method, use only the grid points of 8(1) at which both the estimate f_m of m and the estimate f_best of the best method are defined; with delta = |f_m - f_best|, find the number c of grid points used, rms = sqrt((Sum delta * delta) / c) (sum in grid order), the maximum of delta, and the x of the grid point giving it (if tied, the smaller x).
(3) Slope: let g^(0), ..., g^(G-1) be the estimates of spline at the grid points, and for k = 1, ..., G - 2 compute sigma_k = (g^(k+1) - g^(k-1)) / ((X^(k+1) - X^(k-1)) / 100) ((X^(k+1) - X^(k-1)) / 100 is the real obtained by dividing the integer X^(k+1) - X^(k-1) by 100). Find the maximum and the minimum of sigma_k and, for each of them, the x of the grid point X^(k) of the k that gives it (if tied, the smaller k).
(4) Outliers: the highest-ranked method among the methods that have lev_i (all except running_median and isotonic) is the "outlier method". Using e_i, rse and lev_i of that method, compute the standardized residual t_i = e_i / (rse * sqrt(1 - lev_i)) of each point i with lev_i < 1, and the points with |t_i| > theta are outliers. Sort the outliers in decreasing order of |t_i| (if tied, in point order).
(5) Influence: using the edf of the outlier method, compute Cook's distance D_i = t_i * t_i * lev_i / ((1 - lev_i) * edf) of each point i with lev_i < 1. The first 3 (all of them if there are fewer than 3) in decreasing order of D_i (if tied, in point order) are the "influential points".
(6) Band summary: for each method of BAND_METHODS, compute the mean of the se of 8(3) (the sum over the grid points where f is defined, divided by their number). Also, at each point i let se_i = rse * sqrt(Sum_j l_j(X_i) * l_j(X_i)), and the number of points satisfying |e_i| <= z * sqrt(rse * rse + se_i * se_i) is the "number of points in the prediction interval".

## 8. Grid points, test points, variability bands, bootstrap
(1) Grid points: the G positions X^(k) = X_1 + floor(k * (X_n - X_1) / (G - 1)) for k = 0, ..., G - 1. This order is called the "grid order".
(2) The value of each method at a grid point or a test point is the estimate f(X0) at that position.
(3) Variability bands: for each method of BAND_METHODS, at each grid point X0 compute f(X0), se = rse * sqrt(Sum_j l_j(X0) * l_j(X0)), lower = f(X0) - z * se and upper = f(X0) + z * se (rse is the value of that method computed in 6). When f(X0) is undefined, none of them is defined.
(4) Bootstrap: the residuals of the spline fit are resampled with replacement and the spline is refitted. Random numbers are made by the MINSTD generator. The generator holds an integer state rho, whose initial value is seed. "Drawing a random number" means replacing rho by (48271 * rho) mod 2147483647 and returning the new rho (the product is less than 2^47, so it can be computed exactly with 64-bit integers). There is only one generator, and it is used continuously for all series in series order.
For each series, compute ebar = (Sum e_i) / n and c_i = e_i - ebar (i = 1, ..., n) from the spline residuals e_i. For b = 1, ..., B in order, do the following: for i = 1, ..., n in order, draw a random number, call it r, let k = (r mod n) + 1, and let y*_i = yhat_i + c_k (yhat_i is the fitted value of spline). Compute the fit to y* with the selected lambda, and its evaluation at each grid point is v_b of that grid point.
At each grid point, compute the mean vbar = (Sum v_b) / B of v_1, ..., v_B, the standard error sqrt((Sum (v_b - vbar) * (v_b - vbar)) / (B - 1)), and the (t + 1)-th value (lower limit) and the (B - t)-th value (upper limit) of v_1, ..., v_B sorted in increasing order.

## 9. Plots
For each series of PLOT_SERIES, make two plots of W columns and H rows. Let X_a = X_1 and X_b = X_n, let Y_a and Y_b be the minimum and the maximum of Y_1, ..., Y_n, and let ymin and ymax be the reals obtained by dividing the integers Y_a and Y_b, respectively, by 100. The columns are numbered 0, ..., W - 1 from the left and the rows 0, ..., H - 1 from the top.
The column of point i is floor(W * (X_i - X_a) / (X_b - X_a)) and the row of point i is floor(H * (Y_b - Y_i) / (Y_b - Y_a)) (computed with integers). However, a column equal to W becomes W - 1, and a row equal to H becomes H - 1.
(1) Data and curve plot: set all cells to a space, then set the cell of each point to ".". Next, represent the m-th method (m = 1, 2, ...) of PLOT_METHODS by the digit m, and for each method in PLOT_METHODS order, for columns c = 0, ..., W - 1 in order, do the following. Compute the estimate v at position X_a + floor((2 * c + 1) * (X_b - X_a) / (2 * W)) (if it is undefined, do nothing for that column). Let pi = H * (ymax - v) / (ymax - ymin), and the row is floor(pi) clamped to the range 0 to H - 1 (0 if it is less than 0, H - 1 if it is greater than H - 1). If the character of the cell at that row and column c is a space, ".", or the digit of this method, set it to the digit of this method; otherwise set it to "*".
(2) Residual plot: for the residuals e_i of the best method, let lim be the maximum of |e_1|, ..., |e_n|. Set all cells to a space, and set all cells of row floor(H / 2) to "-". Next, for each point i in point order, let pi = H * ((lim - e_i) / (2 * lim)), and set to "o" the cell whose row is floor(pi) clamped to the range 0 to H - 1 and whose column is the column of point i.
(3) Histogram of standardized residuals: count the standardized residuals t_i of 7(4) (the points with lev_i < 1) in 14 bins. Bin 0 is t_i < -3, bin k (k = 1, ..., 12) is -3 + 0.5 * (k - 1) <= t_i < -3 + 0.5 * k, and bin 13 is 3 <= t_i.

## Common rules
- The output consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). Put no extra spaces at the start or end of a line, and no blank lines unless specified.
- In the formats below, a part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and all other characters are written as they are.
- Integers are written in decimal with no extra leading zeros.
- Unless specified otherwise, reals are rounded to 4 decimal places and written in fixed-point notation with exactly 4 digits after the decimal point (examples: 12.5 is 12.5000, -0.00004 is 0.0000). Exponent notation is not used, and 0 is written when the integer part is 0. A negative number gets a leading -, but a value whose rounded result is 0 is written without -. The data contain no values close to a rounding boundary.

[Source files]
Split the program into the following 6 source files (file names follow the conventions of each language).
- main: handles the overall flow and most of the processing: building the list of methods; fitting linear, running_mean, running_median and isotonic (the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm and deciding the direction); the flow of computing the score and edf of each candidate of each method and selecting the parameter; assembling the data for subset estimates; the flow of leave-one-out cross-validation and K-fold cross-validation; the diagnostics (residual statistics, f_nonlinear, autocorrelation, dw, runs test, largest residual, quantiles, turning count); the ranking, agreement, slope, outliers, influence, variability bands and their summary, the bootstrap, building the plots and the histogram, and assembling the contents of the 7 output files.
- dataset: reads the 3 input files, converts two-decimal numbers to values in hundredths, and returns the data with the points of each series sorted, the list of test points, and the setting values.
- kernels: handles the 3 kernels, the estimates of Nadaraya-Watson estimation and local linear regression, lev_i, and the weights l_j(X0).
- loess: handles neighbourhood selection, the tricube weights, the Cholesky decomposition and the solution of the linear systems, the local fit (estimate, zeta_0, weights), the fit at all points, the median, and the robustness iterations.
- spline: handles building the band matrix M and its LDL^T decomposition, the fit to data, the fits to unit vectors (lev_k and the weights), and evaluation at any position.
- selection: handles the leave-one-out cross-validation score, the generalized cross-validation score, and selecting the candidate with the smallest score (including the handling of candidates whose score is undefined and of equal values).

[Constraints]
- There are 1 to 3 series, 10 to 200 points per series, and 1 to 100 test points. The given data are 3 series (with 161, 181 and 121 points) and 29 test points.
- The input files follow the formats above correctly. There is no need to check for input errors.
- The absolute value of every x value is less than 1000, and that of every y value is less than 100. Therefore the integer computations in this problem (such as S_a * c_b of the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm) fit within the range of 32-bit integers, except for the products of the MINSTD generator.
- In every series, X_n > X_1, and the maximum of y is greater than the minimum.
- In the local fits of loessp, in every case including subset estimates (leave-one-out predictions and K-fold cross-validation), q >= p + 3, and every sigma of the Cholesky decomposition (when l = k) is positive. Every D_j of the spline decomposition is positive (also in subset estimates). Every scale S_t of the robustness iterations is positive.
- For each method with a parameter, in every series, at least one candidate has a defined score.
- Sxx, TSS, the RSS and the autocorrelation denominator of every method, V of the runs test, lim of the residual plot, c of the agreement, and the number of grid points where f is defined in the band summary are positive. For a method whose edf is defined, edf < n.
- In the given data, every real that is output is sufficiently far from a rounding boundary. Also, the results of comparisons and selections (the minimum score, the ranking, the largest residual, the f_nonlinear condition edf > 2, the tests lev_i < 1, the maximum and minimum of the agreement and the slope, the auto direction, the outlier threshold, the influential points, the prediction interval test, the zero-weight test, the signs of the residuals and of the differences Delta_k, the plot rows, and the histogram bins) have enough margin that they do not change because of last-digit differences caused by a different order of additions or by fused multiply-add. Nevertheless, compute with the formulas and the order defined in this statement.
