[Overview]
Write a program that analyzes bond portfolios. The program reads 7 input files from the input/ folder (valuation parameters, holiday calendar, index rate fixings for floating-rate notes, yield-curve market quotes, bond definitions, bond market prices, and holdings) and writes 7 output files to the output/ folder (list of input errors, cash-flow table, analytics per bond, zero curve, portfolio report, profit and loss under interest-rate scenarios, and investment limit checks). It prints nothing to standard output.
Four kinds of bonds are handled: fixed-coupon bonds (including bonds with an early redemption (call) provision), zero-coupon bonds, bonds with scheduled redemptions (a sinking fund), and floating-rate notes. Payment dates are determined from the holiday calendar and a business-day convention, interest is computed with 5 day-count conventions, and coupon schedules are built backward from the maturity date. Cash flows are computed in integer cents.
Next, the program computes the accrued interest at the settlement date, the yield to maturity, duration, convexity, DV01, the yield to call and the yield to worst, bootstraps a zero curve from par-bond coupon rates, and computes for each bond its theoretical price off the curve and its Z-spread. Finally it aggregates the holdings per portfolio and outputs market values, weights, risk measures, key-rate durations, cash flows per year, profit and loss under interest-rate scenarios, and the results of the investment limit checks.
Real-number computations use double-precision floating point, and the only operations used are addition, subtraction, multiplication and division (+, -, *, /). Powers are computed by repeated multiplication; square root, exponential, logarithm and power functions are not used. Every equation is solved by bisection with a fixed number of steps (80).

[Input files]
== Common rules ==
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF). There are no empty lines, and no line has leading or trailing spaces.
- In a file with the extension .csv, line 1 is the header line (exactly the string shown below) and lines 2 onward are data lines. There is at least one data line; each data line has the same number of fields as the header line, separated by commas ",". Fields never contain commas, spaces or quotation marks.
- The "line number" is the number of a line, where the first line of the file (the header line included) is line 1.
- Date: 4 digits, "-", 2 digits, "-", 2 digits, in this order (e.g. 2026-10-15). In bonds.csv, in curve_quotes.csv, and in the dates inside the options of bonds.csv, a value that is not a valid date (e.g. 2027-02-30) may appear. How to handle it is defined in [Processing]. All dates in the other files are valid dates.
- Integer: a decimal number with no extra leading zeros. A negative value is preceded by "-" (negative values appear only in the shifts of params.txt).
- Price: an integer part of 1 to 3 digits, ".", and a fractional part of exactly 3 digits, in this order (e.g. 101.250, 93.875), with no extra leading zeros. The integer equal to 1000 times the price (e.g. 101250 for 101.250) is called the "thousandths price".
- Code: a string of 1 to 12 characters that starts with an uppercase letter (A-Z) and consists only of uppercase letters and digits (0-9).

== input/params.txt (valuation parameters) ==
Each line is a sequence of words separated by a single space. The first word gives the kind of the line; there are the following 4 kinds. The lines may appear in any order.
- valuation_date <date>: the valuation date. There is exactly one such line.
- settlement_days <integer>: the number of business days until settlement (0 to 5 inclusive). There is exactly one such line.
- scenario <scenario name> <shift> <shift> ...: an interest-rate scenario. The scenario name is 1 to 20 characters consisting of uppercase letters, digits and underscores "_", and is not BASE. Each shift is an integer from -500 to 500 inclusive (in bp), and there is at least one shift. There are zero or more scenario lines.
- limit <kind> <argument> ...: an investment limit. There are zero or more such lines, and several lines of the same kind may appear. The kind and its arguments are one of the following 6 forms (b, n, a and c are integers).
  limit position_weight b: upper limit on the weight of a single bond (b bp; e.g. 3000 means 30.00%). 0 <= b <= 10000.
  limit type_weight <type> b: upper limit on the weight of a type (b bp). The type is one of FIXED, ZERO, AMORT, FRN. 0 <= b <= 10000.
  limit max_maturity n: upper limit on the remaining maturity (n years). 1 <= n <= 50.
  limit min_yield b: lower limit on the yield to maturity of the portfolio (b bp). 0 <= b <= 5000.
  limit duration a b: allowed range of the modified duration of the portfolio (from a/100 years to b/100 years inclusive). 0 <= a <= b <= 5000.
  limit dv01 c: upper limit on the DV01 of the portfolio (c cents). 0 <= c <= 10000000000.
1 bp means 0.0001 (0.01%).

== input/calendar.txt (holiday calendar) ==
Each line holds the date of one holiday. The dates are in ascending order without duplicates. Dates that fall on a Saturday or Sunday may be included.

== input/fixings.csv (index rate fixings) ==
The header line is "date,rate_bp". Each data line is a date and the index rate on that day (an integer from 0 to 600 inclusive, in bp). The dates are in ascending order without duplicates.

== input/curve_quotes.csv (par-bond coupon rates) ==
The header line is "pillar_maturity,par_coupon_bp,frequency". Each data line is a maturity date (a date), a par coupon rate (an integer from 1 to 1200 inclusive, in bp), and the number of coupon payments per year (an integer from 1 to 99 inclusive). The lines may appear in any order.

== input/bonds.csv (bond definitions) ==
The header line is "id,type,issue,maturity,coupon_bp,frequency,daycount,bdc,face_cents,options". The fields are as follows.
- id: the bond code (a code).
- type: the type (a code). The valid types are the 4 types FIXED (fixed-coupon bond), ZERO (zero-coupon bond), AMORT (bond with scheduled redemptions) and FRN (floating-rate note).
- issue: the issue date (the date from which interest accrues). maturity: the maturity date. Both are dates.
- coupon_bp: an integer from 0 to 99999 inclusive. For FIXED and AMORT it is the annual coupon rate (bp); for FRN it is the spread (bp) added to the index rate.
- frequency: the number of coupon payments per year (an integer from 1 to 99 inclusive). The valid values are 1, 2, 4, 12.
- daycount: the day-count convention. 1 to 12 characters consisting of uppercase letters, digits and "/". The valid values are 30/360, 30E/360, ACT/360, ACT/365F, ACT/ACT.
- bdc: the business-day convention. 1 to 20 characters consisting of uppercase letters and underscores "_". The valid values are FOLLOWING, MODIFIED_FOLLOWING, PRECEDING, UNADJUSTED.
- face_cents: the face value of one unit (an integer from 1 to 999999999 inclusive, in cents). Cash flows are computed for this face value.
- options: the options. "-" means there are no options. Otherwise it is one or more option items joined by ";". Each item has the form "KEY" or "KEY=VALUE", where the key is 1 to 12 characters consisting only of uppercase letters. The values of the following keys always have the following formats.
  EOM, LONG: no value (and no "=").
  LAG, CAP, FLOOR: the value is an integer from 0 to 99999 inclusive.
  SINK: the value is "DATE:INTEGER", where the integer is from 0 to 999999999 inclusive (the amount redeemed by the sinking fund, in cents).
  CALL: the value is "DATE:PRICE" (the call date and the call price).
  The format of the values of any other key is not specified.

== input/prices.csv (market prices) ==
The header line is "bond,clean_price". Each data line is a bond code (a code) and the price per 100 of face value excluding accrued interest (the clean price; in the price format).

== input/holdings.csv (holdings) ==
The header line is "portfolio,bond,face_cents,book_price". Each data line is a portfolio name (1 to 20 characters consisting of uppercase letters, digits and underscores "_"), a bond code (a code), the face amount held (an integer from 1 to 10000000000 inclusive, in cents), and the book price (the clean price per 100 of face value at acquisition; in the price format).

[Processing]
== 1. Basic conventions ==
- R(a, b): for an integer a >= 0 and a positive integer b, the integer obtained by rounding a/b half up, that is, the quotient of (2a + b) divided by 2b (with the fractional part truncated). All integer computations are exact (the absolute values of all intermediate values stay below 2^53).
- Real-number computations use double-precision floating point. Integers are converted to reals before use, and "/" is real division.
- A sum (written Sum) is accumulated from the first term onward in the order the terms are listed, unless stated otherwise.
- Bisection B(F, lo, hi, T): for a function F, a lower end lo, an upper end hi and a target value T, repeat the following 80 times: let mid = (lo + hi)/2; if F(mid) > T then lo = mid, otherwise hi = mid. The result is (lo + hi)/2 after the 80 steps. (Every F used in this task is decreasing, and F(lo) > T > F(hi) holds.)
- 1 bp = 0.0001.

== 2. Dates and calendar ==
- Valid date: a date whose year is from 2001 to 2099 inclusive, whose month is from 1 to 12 inclusive, and whose day is from 1 to the number of days of that month inclusive. Leap years follow the Gregorian rule (a year divisible by 4 and either not divisible by 100 or divisible by 400).
- Date computations use a day number that is 0 for 2000-01-01 and increases by 1 per day. The "actual days" between two dates is the difference of their day numbers. Dates are compared (earlier/later, smaller/larger) by their day numbers.
- Days of the week follow the Gregorian calendar (2000-01-01 is a Saturday).
- Business day: a day from Monday to Friday that is not listed in calendar.txt.
- Business-day adjustment: a date d is adjusted according to the convention as follows.
  FOLLOWING: the first business day on or after d (d itself if d is a business day).
  PRECEDING: the last business day on or before d.
  MODIFIED_FOLLOWING: the PRECEDING date if the month of the FOLLOWING date differs from the month of d, otherwise the FOLLOWING date.
  UNADJUSTED: d unchanged.
- n business days after: starting from the day after date d and moving forward one day at a time, count business days; the result is the n-th business day counted (d itself if n = 0). n business days before is counted the same way, starting from the day before d and moving backward one day at a time.
- Settlement date S: the date settlement_days business days after the valuation date.

== 3. Day count (year fraction of a period) ==
For each day-count convention, the year fraction of the period from date d1 to date d2 (d1 <= d2) is defined as the integer fraction n/m (not reduced) as follows. Let Y1, M1, D1 be the year, month and day of d1, let Y2, M2, D2 be the year, month and day of d2, and let U = 360*(Y2 - Y1) + 30*(M2 - M1) + (D2 - D1).
- 30/360: apply the following (a) to (d) in this order, then compute U and let n = U, m = 360. (a) If d1 is the last day of February and d2 is also the last day of February, set D2 = 30. (b) If d1 is the last day of February, set D1 = 30. (c) If D2 = 31 and D1 >= 30, set D2 = 30 (D1 is the value after (b)). (d) If D1 = 31, set D1 = 30.
- 30E/360: if D1 = 31 set D1 = 30, and if D2 = 31 set D2 = 30; then compute U and let n = U, m = 360.
- ACT/360: n = actual days, m = 360.
- ACT/365F: n = actual days, m = 365.
- ACT/ACT: among the days on or after d1 and before d2, let p be the number of days that belong to a leap year and q the number of days that belong to a non-leap year; then n = 365*p + 366*q and m = 133590 (this represents p/366 + q/365).

== 4. Coupon schedule ==
The coupon schedule (the sequence of dates that delimit the periods) of a bond with issue date I, maturity date E and f coupon payments per year is built as follows. The delimiting dates are dates without business-day adjustment.
(1) If the options contain EOM and E is the last day of its month, the "end-of-month rule is in effect".
(2) For k = 0, 1, 2, ..., form the date d_k in the year and month that is (12/f)*k months before the year and month of E. Its day is the last day of that month if the end-of-month rule is in effect, otherwise the smaller of the day of E and the number of days of that month (d_0 = E).
(3) Keep increasing k as long as d_k is after I; let K be the k at which d_k <= I holds for the first time.
(4) If the options contain LONG, d_K != I, and K >= 2, remove d_(K-1) (the first period becomes a long stub period).
(5) The coupon schedule is I, d_(K-1), ..., d_1, d_0 (excluding the date removed in (4)) in ascending order of date. The interval between two adjacent dates is one period; the earlier date is called the start date of the period and the later date its end date. The periods are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... from the first.

== 5. Input validation ==
The inputs are validated in the order curve_quotes.csv, the scenario lines of params.txt, bonds.csv, prices.csv, holdings.csv. The lines of each file are examined from the first. For one line, the checks below are performed in the order written; the first one that applies is recorded as one error, and the line becomes invalid (it is not used in any later processing). A line to which none of them applies is valid. The English text in double quotes is the error message. The "key" of each line is used in the error output.

5.1 curve_quotes.csv (the key is the string in the pillar_maturity field)
(a) An earlier line has the same pillar_maturity string (regardless of whether that line is valid): "duplicate pillar"
(b) pillar_maturity is not a valid date: "invalid date"
(c) The maturity date is on or before the settlement date: "pillar not after settlement"
(d) frequency is none of 1, 2, 4, 12: "invalid frequency"
The valid lines sorted in ascending order of maturity date are called the "pillars", numbered pillar 1, pillar 2, ..., pillar P from the first.

5.2 scenario lines of params.txt (the key is the scenario name; the line number is the line number in params.txt)
(a) An earlier scenario line has the same scenario name (regardless of whether that line is valid): "duplicate scenario"
(b) The number of shifts differs from P: "wrong number of shifts"
The valid scenarios are used in the order they are written in params.txt.

5.3 bonds.csv (the key is id)
(a) An earlier line has the same id (regardless of whether that line is valid): "duplicate id"
(b) type is not a valid type: "unknown type"
(c) issue or maturity is not a valid date: "invalid date"
(d) The maturity date is on or before the issue date: "maturity not after issue"
(e) frequency is not a valid value: "invalid frequency"
(f) daycount is not a valid value: "unknown day count"
(g) bdc is not a valid value: "unknown business day rule"
(h) face_cents is not a multiple of 100000, or is greater than 10000000: "invalid face"
(i) coupon_bp is greater than 1200, or the type is ZERO and coupon_bp is not 0: "invalid coupon"
(j) Any of the following applies: "invalid option"
  - There is a key that cannot be used with the type. EOM and LONG can be used with every type; in addition, CALL can be used with FIXED, SINK with AMORT, and LAG, CAP and FLOOR with FRN.
  - A key other than SINK and CALL appears two or more times.
  - The type is FRN and LAG is absent, the value of LAG is not from 1 to 5 inclusive, the value of CAP is greater than 1200, or the value of FLOOR is greater than the value of CAP (1200 if there is no CAP).
(k) Any of the following applies: "invalid sinking fund". Build the coupon schedule (section 4) according to the options EOM and LONG, and examine the SINK items in the order written.
  - The date of a SINK item is not a valid date, or does not equal any date of the coupon schedule other than its first (the issue date) and last (the maturity date).
  - The date of a SINK item is on or before the date of an earlier SINK item.
  - The amount of a SINK item is 0.
  - The type is AMORT and the total of the amounts of the SINK items is 0 (including the case with no items), or is greater than or equal to face_cents.
(l) Any of the following applies: "invalid call schedule". Examine the CALL items in the order written.
  - The date of a CALL item meets one of the first two conditions of (k) (with SINK read as CALL).
  - The call price of a CALL item is not from 100.000 to 110.000 inclusive.
(m) The type is FRN and there is a period for which no index rate is found (see section 6): "missing fixing"
The bonds of the valid lines are called the "valid bonds".

5.4 prices.csv (the key is the bond code in the bond field)
(a) It is not a valid bond: "unknown bond"
(b) An earlier line has the same bond code (regardless of whether that line is valid): "duplicate price"
(c) issue date <= settlement date < maturity date does not hold: "not outstanding"
(d) For the function dirty and the target price T of section 8, dirty(0) > T > dirty(1) does not hold: "yield out of range"
The bonds of the valid lines are called the "priced bonds", and their thousandths price is denoted C.

5.5 holdings.csv (the key is the bond code in the bond field)
(a) It is not a valid bond: "unknown bond"
(b) It is not a priced bond: "no price"
(c) The face amount held is not a multiple of the face_cents of that bond: "invalid face"
For a valid line, the face amount held / face_cents is called the "number of units".

== 6. Cash flows ==
For each valid bond and for each period i of its coupon schedule (start date s_i, end date e_i), compute the following values. F is face_cents and N is the number of the last period.
- Year fraction n_i/m_i: the year fraction of the period from s_i to e_i (with the bond's day-count convention).
- Payment date pay_i: e_i adjusted with the bond's business-day convention.
- Rate r_i (bp): coupon_bp for FIXED and AMORT, 0 for ZERO. For FRN, the "fixing date" is the date LAG business days before s_i adjusted with the bond's business-day convention; let x be the index rate of the latest row of fixings.csv whose date is on or before the fixing date, and let r_i = x + coupon_bp (if there is no such row, it is a "missing fixing"). Further, if FLOOR is present, replace r_i with the larger of r_i and the value of FLOOR, and if CAP is present, replace r_i with the smaller of r_i and the value of CAP.
- Principal q_i: o_i (the whole remaining amount) if i = N; otherwise, if e_i is the date of a SINK item, the amount of that item; otherwise 0.
- Opening balance o_i: o_1 = F, o_(i+1) = o_i - q_i.
- Coupon c_i = R(o_i*r_i*n_i, 10000*m_i) (cents).
- Cash flow CF_i = c_i + q_i (cents).

== 7. Values at the settlement date (priced bonds) ==
- Current period: the first period whose end date is after the settlement date. Its number is c (s_c <= S < e_c holds).
- Remaining periods: periods c, c+1, ..., N. The remaining periods are renumbered k = 1, 2, ..., M (M = N - c + 1) from the first, and their cash flows are written CF_k.
- Remaining cash flows: the remaining periods whose CF_i is 1 or more (in period order). For each of them, the days t_i = pay_i - S (the actual days from the settlement date to the payment date).
- Outstanding O = o_c.
- Accrued interest AI (per 100 of face value): with n/m the year fraction from s_c to S, AI = r_c*n/(100*m).
- Accrued interest amount A = R(O*r_c*n, 10000*m) (cents).
- Clean price amount K = R(O*C, 100000) (cents).
- Unit market value V = K + A (cents). This is the market value of one unit.
- Target price T = C/1000 + AI (the price per 100 of face value including accrued interest).
- Fraction w = f*n'/m', where n'/m' is the year fraction from S to e_c (with the bond's day-count convention) and f is the number of coupon payments per year.

== 8. Valuation by yield (priced bonds) ==
For a yield y (an annual rate expressed as a decimal), let a = 1 + w*y/f, g = 1 + y/f, G_1 = 1, G_(k+1) = G_k*g, and define PV_k(y) = CF_k/(a*G_k) and dirty(y) = (PV_1(y) + ... + PV_M(y))*100/O.
- Yield to maturity ytm = B(dirty, 0, 1, T).
- Below, y = ytm, PV_k = PV_k(ytm), W = PV_1 + ... + PV_M, g = 1 + ytm/f, and tau_k = (k - 1 + w)/f.
- Macaulay duration Dmac = (Sum tau_k*PV_k)/W.
- Modified duration Dmod = Dmac/g.
- Convexity = (Sum tau_k*(tau_k + 1/f)*PV_k)/(W*g*g).
- DV01 (per 100 of face value) = (dirty(ytm - 0.0001) - dirty(ytm + 0.0001))/2.
- Yield to call: for each CALL item (call date d, thousandths call price Q) with d > S, list the coupons c_i of the periods from period c up to the period whose end date is d, in order, and add R(o*Q, 100000) (o is the opening balance of that period) to the last value only; using this sequence as CF_1, CF_2, ..., define dirty with the same w, f and O, and take B(dirty, 0, 1, T) as the yield to call of that item.
- Yield to worst ytw and workout date: initially ytw = ytm and workout date = maturity date. Then examine the CALL items in the order written (ascending order of date); if d > S and the yield to call is smaller than ytw, replace ytw with that yield to call and the workout date with that call date.
- Weighted average life WAL (years) = (Sum q_i*t_i)/(365*O). The Sum is over the remaining cash flows.
- Next payment date: the payment date of the first remaining cash flow.

== 9. Zero curve ==
A curve is represented by pillar days D_1 < D_2 < ... < D_J and a zero rate z_1, ..., z_J per pillar (annual rates expressed as decimals).
- Zero rate z(d) for days d (an integer >= 0): z_1 if d <= D_1, z_J if d >= D_J, and z_j if d equals the days D_j of some pillar. Otherwise, for the j with D_(j-1) < d < D_j, z(d) = z_(j-1) + (z_j - z_(j-1))*((d - D_(j-1))/(D_j - D_(j-1))).
- Discount factor DF(d, s): let r = z(d) + s, and let h be the quotient and rho the remainder of d divided by 365. H is the value obtained by starting from 1 and multiplying by (1 + r) h times (H = 1 if h = 0), and DF(d, s) = 1/(H*(1 + r*rho/365)). s is called the "spread"; s = 0 unless stated otherwise.
- Present value PVC(L, s): for a sequence L of (days t, amount X), PVC(L, s) = Sum X*DF(t, s) (added in the order of L).
- Forward rate FWD(d0, d1) = (DF(d0, 0)/DF(d1, 0) - 1)*365/(d1 - d0).
- Bootstrapping: process pillar 1 to pillar P in order. For pillar j (maturity date E_j, par coupon rate c_j, f_j coupon payments per year), build a coupon schedule with issue date S, maturity date E_j and f_j payments per year, with no options (neither EOM nor LONG). For each period, compute the amount c_j*n/(10000*m) from the year fraction n/m under the day-count convention 30/360, and add 1 to it for the last period only. The days are the end date of that period - S (without business-day adjustment). Call this sequence L_j; let F(x) be PVC(L_j, 0) on the curve (J = j) made of pillars 1 to (j-1) (with their zero rates already found) and pillar j (with zero rate x), and let z_j = B(F, 0, 1, 1). The days of pillar j are D_j = E_j - S.
- The curve made of all pillars 1 to P (J = P) is called the "zero curve". Below, the curve is the zero curve unless stated otherwise.

== 10. Valuation off the curve (priced bonds) ==
Let L be the sequence of (days t_i, amount CF_i) of the remaining cash flows.
- Curve price curve_clean = PVC(L, 0)*100/O - AI.
- Z-spread zs = B(G, -0.5, 0.5, V), where G(s) = PVC(L, s).
Call provisions are ignored in the curve price, the Z-spread, and the computations of sections 11 and 12.

== 11. Portfolios ==
The valid lines of holdings.csv are grouped by the pair of portfolio name and bond code into "positions". The number of units u of a position is the sum of the numbers of units of its lines, and the book value BK is the sum over its lines of R(number of units of the line*O*thousandths price of the book price, 100000) (cents). Portfolios are ordered by ascending name, and positions within a portfolio by ascending bond code (both in lexicographic order of ASCII codes). A portfolio with no valid line is not handled.
Values of a position (O, V, K, AI, F, etc. are the values of its bond):
- Market value MV = u*V (cents).
- Face = u*F, outstanding = u*O (cents).
- Unrealized gain = u*K - BK (cents).
- Position DV01 (in currency units) = DV01*u*O/10000.
- Weight (%) = MV*100/TV, where TV is the total market value of the portfolio.
Values of a portfolio:
- Totals: the number of positions, the total face, the total outstanding, TV, the total book value, the total unrealized gain, and the annual income (the sum of u*c_i over the remaining cash flows of all positions with t_i <= 365).
- Weighted averages: for each of the yield to maturity, the modified duration, the convexity and the Z-spread, (Sum MV*value)/TV (added in position order).
- DV01: the sum of the position DV01s.
- Cash-flow yield: let L be the sequence of the remaining cash flows of all positions as (t_i, u*CF_i), ordered by position and, within a position, by period. Let Y(y) be PVC(L, 0) on the flat curve with z(d) = y for every d; the cash-flow yield is B(Y, 0, 1, TV).
- Shifted curve: the curve in which the zero rate of pillar j is replaced by z_j + delta_j (j = 1, ..., P). The value of a position is PVpos(delta) = u*PVC(L, zs) (L is the sequence of remaining cash flows of its bond, zs is the Z-spread of its bond, and PVC is computed on the shifted curve), and the value of the portfolio PVport(delta) is the sum of the values of its positions. Let V0 = PVport(0) (all delta_j = 0).
- Effective duration = (PVport(-0.0001 at every pillar) - PVport(+0.0001 at every pillar))/(2*0.0001*V0).
- Key-rate duration KRD_j = (PVport(-0.0001 at pillar j only, 0 elsewhere) - PVport(+0.0001 at pillar j only, 0 elsewhere))/(2*0.0001*V0).
- For each type (in the order FIXED, ZERO, AMORT, FRN) and for each maturity bucket, compute the number of positions belonging to it, the total market value X, the weight X*100/TV, the duration contribution (Sum MV*Dmod)/TV, and the total of the position DV01s. The maturity buckets are the following 5, by the actual days d = maturity date - S: 0-1Y (d < 365), 1-3Y (365 <= d < 1095), 3-5Y (1095 <= d < 1825), 5-10Y (1825 <= d < 3650), 10Y+ (d >= 3650).
- Cash flows by year: group the remaining cash flows of all positions by the year of the payment date, and compute for each year the total coupon u*c_i and the total principal u*q_i.

== 12. Scenarios ==
For the base scenario BASE (all delta_j = 0) and for each valid scenario (delta_j = the j-th shift*0.0001; the j-th shift corresponds to pillar j), compute for each position its value PVpos(delta) and its P&L PVpos(delta) - PVpos(0), and for the portfolio its value PVport(delta) and its P&L PVport(delta) - V0.
When there is at least one valid scenario, the valid scenario with the smallest portfolio P&L (the one written first in case of a tie) is the "worst scenario", and up to 3 positions taken from the front in ascending order of their P&L under that scenario (ascending bond code in case of a tie) are the "top losers".

== 13. Limit checks ==
For each portfolio, check the limit lines of params.txt in the order written. If the following condition holds the result is a breach (BREACH), otherwise it is a pass (PASS).
- position_weight b: for the position with the largest market value MV (the smaller bond code in case of a tie), MV*10000 > b*TV (compared as integers).
- type_weight <type> b: for the total market value X of that type, X*10000 > b*TV (compared as integers).
- max_maturity n: the number of positions with maturity date - S > 365*n is 1 or more.
- min_yield b: for the weighted average yield to maturity y, y*10000 < b.
- duration a b: for the weighted average modified duration D, D < a/100 or D > b/100.
- dv01 c: with X the DV01 of the portfolio, X*100 > c.

[Output files]
== Common rules ==
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF). No line has trailing spaces, and no empty lines are output. A file with no lines to output is an empty (0-byte) file.
- Words or fields in a line are separated by a single comma in .csv files and by a single space in the other files.
- Numbers are written as follows.
  Integer: a decimal number with no extra leading zeros (preceded by "-" if negative).
  Date: in the form YYYY-MM-DD (4-digit year, 2-digit month, 2-digit day).
  Hundredths notation: if the integer x is 0 or more, the quotient of x divided by 100 (in decimal), ".", and the remainder of x divided by 100 as 2 digits (with a leading 0 if it has 1 digit), joined together. If x is negative, write "-" followed by the hundredths notation of -x (e.g. 1234567 -> 12345.67, 380 -> 3.80, -5 -> -0.05). It is used for integers such as amounts in cents and bp.
  d-decimal notation: the decimal representation of a real number rounded half up to d decimal places (d is 2, 4 or 6). The integer part has no extra leading zeros and is "0" if the absolute value is less than 1. A negative value is preceded by "-", but no "-" is written when the rounded result is 0 (e.g. the 4-decimal notation of -0.00004 is 0.0000).
  Percent notation: the 4-decimal notation of the real number multiplied by 100 (e.g. 0.0412348 -> 4.1235).
  Amount notation: the 2-decimal notation of a real number in cents divided by 100.

Output the errors recorded in section 5, in the order recorded, one per line, in the form "<file name>:<line number>: <key>: <message>" (e.g. bonds.csv:12: XB1: unknown type). The file name is one of curve_quotes.csv, params.txt, bonds.csv, prices.csv, holdings.csv.

If there are no limit lines, output only "<portfolio name> breaches 0" for each portfolio.

[Source files]
Split the source into the following 6 files (file names may follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow. Reading the input files, the 5 kinds of input validation and error recording, interpreting the options (EOM, LONG, LAG, CAP, FLOOR, SINK, CALL), building the cash flows of the 4 kinds of bonds (index rate lookup, cap and floor, sinking fund), the values at the settlement date and the clean price amount and accrued interest amount, the yield to call and the yield to worst, aggregating positions, the portfolio measures, key-rate durations, scenario P&L and the worst scenario, the limit checks, and writing the 7 output files. Most of the processing goes in this file.
- dates_calendar: conversion between dates and day numbers, date validity checks, day of the week, business-day checks with the holiday calendar, business-day conventions, and computing n business days after/before.
- daycount: year fractions of periods (integer fractions) under the 5 day-count conventions.
- schedule: building the coupon schedule backward from the maturity date (end-of-month rule, long stub period).
- pricing: bisection and price computations from a yield (dirty, yield to maturity, duration, convexity, DV01).
- curve: the zero curve (interpolation, discount factors with a spread, forward rates, shifts, present values), bootstrapping from par bonds, and the Z-spread.

[Constraints]
- bonds.csv and prices.csv each have at most 100 data lines, holdings.csv at most 300, curve_quotes.csv at most 30, fixings.csv at most 2000, calendar.txt at most 1000 lines, and params.txt at most 40 lines.
- The valuation date is a business day (so the settlement date is also a business day, and every t_i is 0 or more).
- There are at least 2 pillars. There is at least 1 valid holdings line.
- For each period of an FRN, x + coupon_bp (the value before FLOOR and CAP are applied) is at most 1200.
- The total face amount held in one portfolio is at most 200000000000.
- Every clean price in prices.csv and every book price in holdings.csv is at most 200.000.
- For every bisection used in this task, F(lo) > T > F(hi) holds (for the yield to maturity this is checked by section 5.4 (d); for the yield to call, bootstrapping, the Z-spread and the cash-flow yield it always holds for the given data).
- All dates of the coupon schedules and payment dates of the valid bonds are on or after 2000-01-01 and on or before 2099-12-31.
- With the given data, floating-point rounding errors (differences in summation order, fused multiply-add, etc.) never change the output. That is, every real number output is sufficiently far from a rounding boundary (an exact half), and comparisons such as the choice of the yield to worst, the ranking of the worst scenario and the top losers, and the limit checks are decided with a sufficient margin.
