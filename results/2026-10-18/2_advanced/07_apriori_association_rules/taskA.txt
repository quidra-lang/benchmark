[Overview]
Write a program that performs market basket analysis on supermarket purchase data. It reads three files from the input/ folder: a product master, transaction details, and parameters. First it validates the input and builds a basket for each transaction (the set of products bought in that transaction). Next it finds the frequent itemsets with the Apriori method and cross-checks them against the result of an independently implemented FP-growth method. It then computes closed, maximal and generator itemsets; association rules and their metrics; generalized rules that use product categories; the order of purchases per customer (sequential patterns) and repurchase intervals; period-split rule validation and recommendation evaluation; product and category summaries; weekly trends; and per-customer summaries, and writes them to 10 files in the output/ folder. All ratios are computed exactly as fractions of integers; floating-point numbers are not used. Nothing is printed to standard output.

[Input files]
The common format is as follows.
- Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF) (including the last line). There are no empty lines.
- In a file with the extension .csv, line 1 is a header line (its content is fixed as shown below), and each line from line 2 on is a data line holding one record. Fields are separated by a single comma (,), with no quotes and no spaces around fields.
- Line numbers in a file are counted with the header line as line 1 (the first data line is line 2).
- An integer is a non-negative decimal integer without extra leading zeros.
- A product name is a string of 1 to 16 characters consisting of lowercase letters, digits and _, beginning with a lowercase letter. A category name is a string of 1 to 12 characters consisting of uppercase letters and _, beginning with an uppercase letter (therefore a product name and a category name are never equal). A customer ID is a string of 1 to 8 characters consisting of uppercase letters and digits. A transaction ID is a positive integer of 1 to 9 digits (it may be treated as a string).
- In the descriptions below, <...> denotes a place where a value goes. The symbols < and > themselves appear in neither the input nor the output.

(1) input/items.csv (product master)
Header line: item,category,price_cents
The fields are the product name, the category name, and the unit price (in cents; an integer between 0 and 10000 inclusive).

(2) input/transactions.csv (transaction details)
Header line: tid,customer,day,item
The fields are the transaction ID, the customer ID, the day (an integer between 1 and 112 inclusive) and the product name. Each line means that in that transaction that customer bought one unit of that product on that day. The product name follows the product-name format, but it is not necessarily a product adopted from items.csv. Lines of the same transaction are supposed to be consecutive and to have the same customer ID and day (this is checked in Section 2). When the same transaction has several lines with the same product, that many units were bought.

(3) input/params.txt (parameters)
It consists of the following 15 lines, in this order. Each line is a keyword and a value separated by one space. <integer> is a positive integer, and <fraction> is two positive integers joined by / (e.g. 3/2; not necessarily reduced), representing the value of that fraction. The value of each line is referred to by the symbol shown on the right.
min_support <integer>: s (minimum support count of frequent itemsets)
min_confidence <fraction>: c_min (minimum confidence of rules)
min_lift <fraction>: l_min (minimum lift of rules)
max_size <integer>: K (maximum size of itemsets)
category_min_support <integer>: s_c (minimum support count for category rules)
sequence_min_support <integer>: s_q (minimum number of customers for sequential patterns)
split_day <integer>: D_s (last day of the training period)
train_min_support <integer>: s_tr (minimum support count in the training period)
test_min_support <integer>: s_te (minimum support count in the test period)
recommend_top <integer>: T (maximum number of products to recommend)
matrix_size <integer>: M (number of products in the lift matrix)
group_min_lift <fraction>: l_g (lower bound of lift for forming product groups)
negative_max_lift <fraction>: l_n (upper bound of lift for a substitute relation)
negative_min_expected <integer>: e_n (lower bound of expected count for examining substitute relations)
trend_threshold <fraction>: theta (threshold for judging trends)

[Processing]
Section 1. Common rules
- String order: strings are compared in lexicographic order, comparing character codes (ASCII) one character at a time from the first character (when one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one is smaller). "Ascending name order" and "ascending ID order" always mean this order. Since uppercase letters are smaller than lowercase letters, every category name is smaller than every product name.
- Items and itemsets: products and categories are collectively called items (categories appear as items only in Section 7). An itemset is a set of distinct items, always handled with its items arranged in string order. In the output, the arranged items are written joined by + (e.g. bread+butter+milk, DAIRY+bread). The number of elements is called the size of the itemset.
- Lexicographic order: two itemsets are compared by comparing their arranged items one by one from the first in string order; the first position where they differ decides. When one is a prefix of the other, the one with the smaller size is smaller. "Size order" means ascending size, and for equal sizes ascending lexicographic order.
- Support count: for a list of baskets (Section 2) and an itemset X, the number of baskets that contain all items of X is written n(X), the support count of X in that list. The support count of the empty set is the number of baskets.
- Fractions: all ratios in this task are handled exactly as fractions of integers. Perform addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and comparison of fractions exactly, with integers after clearing denominators.
- Fraction notation: a non-negative fraction v is written "<p>/<q>=<decimal>", i.e. the reduced fraction p/q (q >= 1; 0/1 if v is 0, denominator 1 if v is an integer) and the decimal obtained by truncating v to 4 decimal places, joined by =. With t = floor(10000 * p / q), the decimal is "<floor(t / 10000)>.<t mod 10000 written with 4 digits (padded with leading zeros)>". For example, 2/3 is written 2/3=0.6666, 9/4 is written 9/4=2.2500, and 5 is written 5/1=5.0000. A negative fraction -v (v > 0) is written "-<p>/<q>=-<decimal>", i.e. the notation of v with a - placed before the numerator and another before the decimal. However, when the truncated decimal is 0.0000, no - is placed before the decimal (e.g. -1/30000=0.0000). Only the improvement (Section 6) and the slope and relative slope (Section 11) can be negative.
- Percentage notation: for a non-negative integer a and a positive integer b, with t = floor(10000 * a / b), write "<floor(t / 100)>.<t mod 100 written with 2 digits (a leading 0 if it has 1 digit)>%" (e.g. for a = 1, b = 3: 33.33%).
- Money notation: all amounts of money are computed as integers in cents and written in the output as "<dollars>.<cents>". <dollars> is the quotient of the amount divided by 100, and <cents> is the remainder written with 2 digits (a leading 0 if it has 1 digit). For example, 12345 cents is 123.45 and 7 cents is 0.07.
- Median: sort a list of integers (of length at least 1) in ascending order; the median is the middle value if the length is odd, and the fraction (sum of the two middle values) / 2 if the length is even.
- Truncation: every integer division in this task divides a non-negative integer by a positive integer. floor(...) and "rounded down to an integer" mean that quotient.

Section 2. Input validation and baskets
Examine the data lines one at a time: items.csv first, then transactions.csv, and within each file in ascending order of line number. For each line, check the conditions listed below in the order listed; if a condition applies, the first one that applies rejects the line, and "<file name> line <line number>: <message>" is recorded as an error (<file name> is the name without the folder, such as items.csv). A line to which no condition applies is adopted. params.txt is not validated.

* items.csv
1. A line with the same product name has already been adopted: duplicate item <product name>
2. The unit price is 0: bad price <unit price>
The product of an adopted line is hereafter simply called a "product", and the category name of that line is that product's category. The category names that appear as categories of products (without duplicates) are simply called "categories". The unit price is also the value of that line.

* transactions.csv
While building a list of transactions (initially empty), examine the lines in order as follows. "The previous transaction" means the transaction that is last in the transaction list at that moment.
1. The transaction ID differs from the transaction ID of the previous transaction and equals the transaction ID of some transaction in the list: tid <transaction ID> not contiguous
(A line to which this condition applies is not checked any further.)
2. If the transaction ID differs from the transaction IDs of all transactions in the list, create a new transaction with this line's transaction ID, customer ID and day, and append it to the end of the list. This is not an error; continue with the next conditions.
Below, let X be the previous transaction.
3. The customer ID differs from X's customer ID: tid <transaction ID> customer <this line's customer ID> differs from <X's customer ID>
4. The day differs from X's day: tid <transaction ID> day <this line's day> differs from <X's day>
5. The product name is not a product: unknown item <product name>
A line to which none of 3 to 5 applies is adopted, and one unit of that product is added to X. An adopted line for which X already had that product at that moment is called a duplicate line.
A transaction is created by the first line with its transaction ID (even if that line is rejected by condition 5, the transaction's customer ID and day are the values of that line).
- A transaction that has no products is called an empty transaction. The non-empty transactions, arranged in transaction-list order, are called "the basket list", and each of them is called a basket. A basket has a customer ID, a day, and a count (units sold) for each product. When a basket is treated as an itemset, such as when counting support, it is the set of its products (counts are ignored). The number of products in a basket is the size of that set of products. Let N be the number of baskets.
- The customer IDs that appear as customer IDs of baskets are called customers, and their number is C. Let D_max be the maximum day of the baskets.

Section 3. The Apriori method
"Run Apriori on a basket list B with a set of items U and minimum support count m" means finding the frequent itemsets and their support counts (support counts in B) by the following procedure. When it is specified, the category exclusion of (4) is also performed.
(1) Level 1: the candidates are the itemsets consisting of exactly one item of U, and those with support count >= m form the level-1 frequent itemsets L1. The level-1 statistics are: candidate count = number of elements of U, excluded count = 0, pruned count = 0, counted count = number of elements of U, frequent count = number of itemsets in L1.
(2) For k = 2, 3, ... in order, while k <= K and L(k-1) is non-empty, compute level k by (3) to (6).
(3) Join: arrange L(k-1) in lexicographic order, and for every pair of itemsets P, Q in it (P before Q) whose first k - 2 items are equal, create the itemset obtained by adding the last item of Q to P. The number created is the candidate count.
(4) Exclusion: when category exclusion is specified and k = 2, remove the candidates consisting of a product and that product's category. The number removed is the excluded count (in all other cases the excluded count is 0).
(5) Pruning: among the remaining candidates, remove those for which any of the itemsets of size k - 1 obtained by removing one item is not in L(k-1). The number removed is the pruned count.
(6) Counting: count the support counts of the remaining candidates; the number of remaining candidates is the counted count. The candidates with support count >= m form the level-k frequent itemsets Lk, and their number is the frequent count.
(7) All of L1, L2, ... together are the result of this Apriori run (the frequent itemsets and their support counts). The number of levels computed (including the level at which Lk became empty) is the number of levels.
(The result equals the set of all itemsets of size <= K with support count >= m (when category exclusion is used, excluding those that contain both a product and that product's category). Any method of counting support counts may be used.)
As the main analysis, run Apriori on the basket list with all products and m = s. Its result is written F, and the itemsets in F are simply called frequent itemsets.

Section 4. The FP-growth method
F is also computed with the FP-growth method, independently of the Apriori method, and the two are cross-checked.
(1) Pattern base and FP-tree: a pattern base is a list of pairs of "a list of items" and "a positive integer weight". The FP-tree of a pattern base is built as follows. For each item, the sum of the weights of the pairs that contain it is its support count in the pattern base; the items with support count >= s, arranged in descending order of support count and, for equal support counts, ascending name order, form the header of the tree. Each node other than the root has an item and a count (0 when created). Starting from a tree that has only the root, process the pairs one at a time. Arrange the items of the pair that are in the header in header order, and, starting at the root, do the following for each item in that order: "if the current node has a child with that item, move to that child; otherwise create a new child with that item and move to it; add the pair's weight to the count of the node moved to". The number of nodes other than the root is the node count of the tree (the node count does not depend on the order of the pairs).
(2) The whole tree: the FP-tree of the pattern base made of one pair for each basket of the basket list, consisting of the list of that basket's products and weight 1.
(3) Search: use a stack of (FP-tree, suffix set) pairs. First push (the whole tree, the empty set); then, until the stack is empty, pop one pair (A, S) and do the following for each item a of A's header (in any order, e.g. from the end of the header):
- Add the itemset S' obtained by adding a to S to the FP-growth result, with the sum of the counts of the nodes of item a in A as its support count.
- If the size of S' is less than K, then for each node of item a in A, make a pair of "the items on the path from a child of the root to that node's parent, in order from the one nearest the root" and "that node's count". The list of these pairs is called the conditional pattern base of a; build its FP-tree (the conditional FP-tree). If the node count of the conditional FP-tree is at least 1, push (the conditional FP-tree, S').
(4) Statistics: the header count is the number of items in the header of the whole tree, the node count is the node count of the whole tree, the conditional tree count is the number of conditional FP-trees pushed, the conditional node count is the sum of their node counts, and the itemset count is the number of entries in the FP-growth result.
(5) Cross-check: if the FP-growth result (the set of pairs of itemset and support count) is exactly equal to F, the result is MATCH; otherwise it is MISMATCH.

Section 5. Closed, maximal and generator itemsets
These are defined within F as follows (since only itemsets in F are considered, an itemset of size K is always closed and maximal).
- Closed: an X such that F contains no itemset that properly contains X and has the same support count as X.
- Maximal: an X such that F contains no itemset that properly contains X.
- Generator: an X such that every proper subset of X (including the empty set) has a support count greater than the support count of X.

Section 6. Association rules
"Make rules from an Apriori result G and the number N' of baskets on which that Apriori was run" means the following.
- For each itemset Z of size 2 or more in G and each non-empty proper subset X of Z, consider the rule X -> Y with Y = Z - X. X is called the antecedent and Y the consequent. The number of rules considered is the generated count. Since X, Y, Z and their subsets are all in G, the support counts from G are used (the support count of the empty set is N').
- With n_X = n(X), n_Y = n(Y) and n_Z = n(Z), the metrics of the rule are the following fractions.
  support = n_Z / N'
  confidence = n_Z / n_X
  lift = N' * n_Z / (n_X * n_Y)
  leverage = (N' * n_Z - n_X * n_Y) / (N' * N')
  conviction = (N' - n_Y) * n_X / (N' * (n_X - n_Z)); however, when n_X = n_Z it is INF.
  improvement = confidence - (the maximum of n(W union Y) / n(W) over all proper subsets W of X (including the empty set))
- Keep only the rules with confidence >= c_min and lift >= l_min. The number of rules kept is the passed count. Since l_min >= 1, the leverage of every kept rule is >= 0. The improvement can be negative. The number of rules with improvement > 0 is the productive count.
- Sort the kept rules by confidence descending, then lift descending, then n_Z descending, then antecedent in lexicographic order, then consequent in lexicographic order, and assign ranks starting from 1 in that order.
In the main analysis, rules are made from F and N. These are called the main rules.

Section 7. Category rules (generalized rules)
- For each basket, the itemset obtained by adding all categories of its products is called its extended basket. The support count of a category in the list of extended baskets is then the number of baskets that contain at least one product of that category.
- Run Apriori on the extended basket list with all products and all categories and m = s_c, with category exclusion specified, and call its result G_c.
- Make rules from G_c and N, but only for Z that contain at least one category (the generated count also counts only those). These are called the category rules. Because of category exclusion, no rule links a product with that product's category.

Section 8. Sequential patterns and repurchase intervals
- For a customer c and a product i, let Days(c, i) be the set of days (equal days merged into one) of the baskets of c that contain i. A product for which Days(c, i) is non-empty is called a purchased product of c. The number of customers that have product i as a purchased product is the buyer count r(i).
- Sequences: for products A and B (they may be the same product), customer c satisfies "A, then B on a later day" when A and B are both purchased products of c and the minimum of Days(c, A) < the maximum of Days(c, B) (that is, there is a basket containing B on a day later than the day of a basket containing A). The number of customers that satisfy this is q(A, B). The number of pairs (A, B) with q(A, B) >= 1 is the sequence pair count.
- Pairs with q(A, B) >= s_q are frequent sequences, and the following values are computed for them.
  confidence = q(A, B) / r(A)
  lift = q(A, B) * C / (r(A) * r(B))
  gap = the median, over the satisfying customers c, of "the minimum of y - x over x in Days(c, A) and y in Days(c, B) with x < y"
- Sort the frequent sequences by q descending, then confidence descending, then name of A ascending, then name of B ascending, and assign ranks starting from 1 in that order.
- Repurchase intervals: for a customer c and a product i, if Days(c, i) has 2 or more elements, the differences between adjacent elements of Days(c, i) sorted in ascending order are the repurchase intervals of c for i. For each product, collect the repurchase intervals of all customers into one list. For each product that has at least one repurchase interval, compute the number of customers having repurchase intervals, the number of repurchase intervals, the minimum, the maximum, the mean (the fraction sum / count) and the median.

Section 9. Period-split validation and recommendation evaluation
- Of the basket list, the baskets with day <= D_s are called training baskets and those with day > D_s are called test baskets (both in the original list order). Their numbers are N_tr and N_te, respectively.
- Training rules: run Apriori on the training basket list with all products and m = s_tr, and make rules from its result G_tr and N_tr. These are called the training rules, and their ranks are the ranks among them.
- Verdict: for each training rule X -> Y, compute the support counts in the test basket list a = n(X), b = n(Y) and c = n(X union Y). If a = 0 or b = 0, the verdict is UNSEEN. Otherwise compute the test confidence c / a and the test lift N_te * c / (a * b); if the test confidence is >= c_min and the test lift is >= l_min, the verdict is HOLD, otherwise FAIL.
- Emerging rules: run Apriori on the test basket list with all products and m = s_te, and make rules from its result G_te and N_te (the test rules; their ranks are the ranks among them). A test rule whose pair of antecedent and consequent matches no training rule is called an emerging rule.
- Recommendation: given a set R of products at hand and a candidate list of products, scan the candidate list from the beginning and select each product that is not in R and has not yet been selected, continuing until T products have been selected or the end of the list has been examined. The selected products, in the order selected, are called the recommendation.
  - Rule recommendation: the recommendation whose candidate list is obtained by examining the training rules whose consequent has size 1 in rank order and listing the consequent product of each one whose antecedent is contained in R.
  - Popularity recommendation: the recommendation whose candidate list is all products arranged in descending order of support count in the training basket list and, for equal support counts, ascending name order.
- Evaluation: for each test basket with 2 or more products, and for each of its products h, compute the rule recommendation and the popularity recommendation with R = the set of products of that basket minus h. This is called one evaluation case, and h is called the held-out product. For each evaluation case: if the rule recommendation is non-empty, it is "covered"; if the first product of the rule recommendation is h, it is a "top-1 hit"; if the rule recommendation contains h, it is a "hit"; if the popularity recommendation contains h, it is a "baseline hit". Count these cases for each number of products in the basket and in total. Also, for each product, count the number of times it was the held-out product (held-out count) and, of those, the number of hits (hit count).

Section 10. Product and category summaries
Below, support counts are those in the basket list unless stated otherwise.
- The units sold units(i) of product i is the total units of i over all baskets, and its revenue is units(i) * unit price. The sum of the revenues of all products is the total revenue.
- Partner product: among the itemsets {i, j} of size 2 in F that contain i, take the one with the largest lift N * n({i, j}) / (n(i) * n(j)) (if tied, the one with the larger n({i, j}); if still tied, the one with the smaller name of j); its j is the partner product of i. If there is no such itemset, there is no partner product.
- Strongest rule: the main rule with the smallest rank among those that contain i in the antecedent or the consequent. If there is none, there is no strongest rule.
- Category summary: the product count of a category g is the number of products whose category is g; its units sold and revenue are the sums of those products' values; its basket count is the support count of g in the extended basket list.
- Product groups: each itemset {i, j} of size 2 in F whose lift N * n({i, j}) / (n(i) * n(j)) is >= l_g is an edge connecting product i and product j. Of the clusters of products connected by edges (clusters in which products that can reach each other by following some edges belong to the same cluster), those with 2 or more products are product groups. The edge count of a group is the number of edges both of whose ends are products of that group. The core product is the product of the group with the largest number of edges (edges having that product as an end) (if tied, the one with the larger support count; if still tied, the one with the smaller name). Sort the groups by number of products descending, then by the smallest product name in the group ascending, and number them starting from 1 in that order.
- Substitute relations: a pair of products a, b of L1 (the level-1 frequent itemsets of the main analysis) (name of a < name of b) that satisfies n(a) * n(b) >= e_n * N and N * n({a, b}) / (n(a) * n(b)) <= l_n is a substitute relation. n({a, b}) may be 0. The expected count is n(a) * n(b) / N, and the lift is N * n({a, b}) / (n(a) * n(b)). Sort the substitute relations by lift ascending, then expected count descending, then name of a ascending, then name of b ascending.
- Lift matrix: for a list of items u_1, ..., u_k to be put in the matrix and a basket list in which support counts are counted, the value of the cell with i != j is floor(100 * (number of baskets) * n({u_i, u_j}) / (n(u_i) * n(u_j))).
  - Product lift matrix: contains the first M products when the products are arranged in descending order of support count (for equal support counts, ascending name order), counted in the basket list.
  - Category lift matrix: contains all categories arranged in descending order of support count in the extended basket list (for equal support counts, ascending name order), counted in the extended basket list.

Section 11. Weekly trends
- The number of weeks is W = floor((D_max - 1) / 7) + 1. A basket on day d belongs to week floor((d - 1) / 7) + 1 (the quotient is the integer with the fractional part discarded).
- Series y_1, ..., y_W: in the series of each category, y_w is the total units sold of the products of that category in the baskets belonging to week w. In the series of each product, y_w is the total units sold of that product in the baskets belonging to week w.
- For each series, compute the slope beta = (W * (sum of w * y_w) - (sum of w) * (sum of y_w)) / (W * (sum of w * w) - (sum of w) * (sum of w)) (every sum is over w = 1, ..., W). Let U = sum of y_w; if U > 0, compute the relative slope r = beta * W / U, and the verdict is RISING if r >= theta, FALLING if r <= -theta, and FLAT otherwise. If U = 0, there is no relative slope, and the verdict is NONE.

Section 12. Per-customer summary
- For each customer, compute the basket count, the day count (the number of distinct days among its baskets' days), the first day, the last day, the product count (the number of purchased products), the units sold (the total units of all its baskets), the amount paid (the sum of "units sold * unit price" over all its baskets), the average payment (floor(amount paid / basket count)), and the favorite category (the category whose products have the largest total units sold; if tied, the one with the smaller name).
- Segment: check the following in order; the first one that applies is the segment.
  1. If first day > D_max - 28: NEW
  2. If basket count >= 4 and D_max - last day <= 14: LOYAL
  3. If D_max - last day > 28: AT_RISK
  4. Otherwise: REGULAR
- Offer: the recommendation with R = H, computed by the same method as in Section 9, where H is the customer's set of purchased products and the candidate list is obtained by examining the main rules whose consequent has size 1 in rank order and listing the consequent product of each one whose antecedent is contained in H.

[Output files]
Write the following 10 files in the output/ folder. In every file, the separator within a line is one space (one comma in csv files; the widths shown below for the rows of fixed-width tables), and no line ends with a space. Every line ends with a line feed (LF), and no empty lines are output. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros. Fractions, percentages and amounts of money are written in the fraction notation, percentage notation and money notation of Section 1, respectively. <itemset> is written joined by + as in Section 1.

First, output the errors recorded in Section 2, in the order recorded, in the form "ERROR <recorded error>". Then output the following lines.
ITEMS rows <number of data lines in items.csv> adopted <number of products> categories <number of categories>
TRANSACTIONS lines <number of data lines in transactions.csv> adopted <number of adopted lines> rejected <number of rejected lines> duplicates <number of duplicate lines> transactions <number of transactions> empty <number of empty transactions>

FREQUENT itemsets <number of itemsets in F> closed <number of closed itemsets> maximal <number of maximal itemsets> generators <number of generators> levels <number of levels of the main analysis>

CATEGORY_RULES itemsets <number of itemsets in G_c> generated <generated count of the category rules> passed <passed count> productive <productive count>

(3) output/closed_maximal.txt (closed, maximal and generator itemsets)
Output the closed itemsets in size order in the form "CLOSED <itemset> <support count>", then the maximal itemsets in size order in the form "MAXIMAL <itemset> <support count>", then the generators in size order in the form "GENERATOR <itemset> <support count>".

(4) output/rules.csv (main rules)
Line 1 is the header line "rank,antecedent,consequent,count,support,confidence,lift,leverage,conviction,improvement". Then output the main rules in rank order in the form "<rank>,<antecedent>,<consequent>,<n_Z>,<support>,<confidence>,<lift>,<leverage>,<conviction>,<improvement>". The antecedent and consequent are itemsets, and the metrics are in fraction notation. However, when the conviction is INF, write INF.

(5) output/category_rules.csv (category rules)

(7) output/validation.txt (period-split validation and recommendation evaluation)
Output the training rules in rank order, one per line, in the following form. <n_Z> is n(X union Y) in the training baskets, and the confidence and lift are those of the training rule.

When the verdict is UNSEEN, write - in place of the test confidence and the test lift.
Then output the emerging rules in order of their rank among the test rules, in the form "EMERGING <rank among the test rules> <antecedent> -> <consequent> test <n_Z> confidence <confidence> lift <lift>" (the values are those of the test rule). Then output the following line.

Finally, for each number k of products in a basket for which there is at least one evaluation case, in ascending order of k, output "RECOMMEND size <k> ...", followed by "RECOMMEND total ..." for the total. The ... part is "cases <number of evaluation cases> covered <number covered> hit1 <number of top-1 hits> hit <number of hits> rate <number of hits / number of evaluation cases> baseline <number of baseline hits> rate <number of baseline hits / number of evaluation cases>", and both rates are in fraction notation.

(8) output/items_report.txt (product and category summaries)
Rank the products starting from 1 by revenue descending and, for equal revenues, product name ascending, and output one line per product in that order in the following form.

<partner> is "<name of the partner product> <fraction notation of its lift>", or none if there is no partner product. <strongest> is "<rank> <antecedent> -> <consequent>" (of the strongest rule), or none if there is none. The unit price and revenue are in money notation.
Then output the categories in descending order of revenue and, for equal revenues, ascending category name, in the form "CATEGORY <category name> items <product count> units <units sold> revenue <revenue> share <percentage of revenue / total revenue> baskets <basket count> support <fraction basket count / N>". (9) output/trends.txt (weekly trends)

- Label BASKETS: the number of baskets belonging to that week

- For each product, in ascending name order: the label is the product name, and the values are that product's series
Then, for all categories and products (whose names are called series names below), output those that have a relative slope in descending order of relative slope (for equal values, ascending series name), followed by those without a relative slope in ascending series name order, in the form "TREND <series name> units <U> slope <slope> relative <relative slope> <verdict>". The slope and relative slope are in fraction notation; when there is no relative slope, write -.

The amount paid and the average payment are in money notation. <offer> is the offered products joined by + in the order selected (not re-sorted by name), or none if the offer is empty.
Finally, for the segments NEW, LOYAL, AT_RISK, REGULAR in this order, output one line each "SEGMENT <segment> customers <number of customers in that segment> revenue <total amount paid by those customers> share <percentage of that total / total revenue>" (a segment with 0 customers is also output).

[Source files]
Organize the program into the following 6 source files (name the files according to each language's conventions).
- main: holds the overall flow and the core of the processing: reading params.txt; validating items.csv and transactions.csv and assembling transactions and baskets; the per-level loop of Apriori (shared by the 4 runs) and the level statistics; the cross-check with FP-growth; determining closed, maximal and generator itemsets; enumerating rules, computing improvement and sorting; category rules; sequential patterns and repurchase intervals; period-split validation, emerging rules and recommendation evaluation; product and category summaries (partner products, strongest rules, product groups, substitute relations, lift matrices); weekly trends; per-customer summaries; and assembling the lines of all output files.
- baskets: handles the product and transaction records, splitting lines of csv files, building the set of products of a basket, building extended baskets, and testing whether an itemset is contained.
- apriori: generates the candidates of one level (join, category exclusion, pruning, and their respective counts) and counts the support counts of the candidates.
- fpgrowth: handles FP-tree nodes, building an FP-tree from a pattern base, extracting conditional pattern bases, and the stack-based search and its statistics.
- rules: handles rule records, computing the fractional metrics from support counts, threshold tests, and the comparison for sorting rules.
- report: handles fraction notation, percentage notation, money notation, itemset formatting, formatting rules.csv lines, and writing the files.

[Constraints]
- items.csv has between 1 and 80 data lines, with between 1 and 60 products and at most 12 categories.
- transactions.csv has between 1 and 10000 data lines, at most 2000 transactions and at most 500 customers. There is at least one basket.
- D_max >= 8 (therefore W >= 2). Every category appears in at least one basket.
- s, s_c, s_q, s_tr and s_te are between 1 and 2000, and K is between 2 and 5.
- Writing c_min as a/b, 1 <= a <= b <= 100. Writing l_min or l_g as a/b, 1 <= b <= a <= 1000 and b <= 100 (the value is at least 1). Writing l_n or theta as a/b, 1 <= a < b <= 100 (the value is greater than 0 and less than 1).
- T is between 1 and 5, M is between 2 and 10, and M is at most the number of products that appear in at least one basket. e_n is between 1 and 1000.
- There is at least one training basket, and at least one test basket with 2 or more products.
- The number of frequent itemsets found by each of the 4 Apriori runs is at most 5000.
- The absolute value of every integer appearing in the computation is less than 2^53. However, values such as those obtained by clearing denominators when comparing fractions, or in computing percentages of the total revenue, can exceed 2^31, so compute with 64-bit integers (or a type that represents integers below 2^53 exactly). Perform all divisions and comparisons exactly with integers.
