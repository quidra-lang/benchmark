[Overview]
Write a program that searches for and validates moves in Scrabble (a game in which tiles bearing letters are placed on a board of 15 rows and 15 columns to form English words) and plays games automatically. The program reads the tile table input/tiles.txt, which defines the point value and the count of each tile, the word list input/dictionary.txt, and input/jobs.txt, which lists the tasks (jobs) to perform. The dictionary is stored in a trie, and from a board and the tiles in hand (the rack) the program generates all legal moves with the Appel-Jacobson method, which uses anchors and cross-checks. There are four kinds of jobs. A TOPN job ranks the legal moves for a given board and rack and computes the top moves and various statistics. A VALIDATE job checks and scores each move of a game record and computes the difference from the best move. A GAME job has 2 to 4 players play a game to the end using a tile bag shuffled with random numbers. A DUPLICATE job plays a game in duplicate format, in which all players choose moves with the same rack. The results are written to output/moves.txt, output/validation.txt and output/game.txt. All computation is done with integers; floating-point numbers are not used. Nothing is written to standard output.

[Input files]
There are three input files: input/tiles.txt, input/dictionary.txt and input/jobs.txt. Every file consists only of printable ASCII characters (including the space) and line feeds (LF), and every line (including the last one) ends with a line feed. There are no spaces at the end of a line.

== input/tiles.txt (tile table) ==
Blank lines and lines starting with "#" (comment lines) are ignored. The remaining lines (data lines) are exactly 27 lines, one for each of the 26 letters A, B, ..., Z and "?", in this order, each of the form "L v c w". L is the letter of the tile ("?" denotes the blank), v is the point value of one such tile, c is the number of such tiles, and w is the leave value of the tile (used in [Processing] 7). v, c and w are each an integer written in decimal (only w may be negative, in which case it is preceded by "-"), separated by single spaces. The point value of "?" is always 0. The order of the data lines (A, B, ..., Z, ?) is called the tile table order.

== input/dictionary.txt (dictionary) ==
It contains one word candidate per line. Blank lines and lines starting with "#" are ignored; the remaining lines are called candidate lines. A candidate line that has at least 2 and at most 15 characters and consists only of uppercase letters (A to Z) is a valid word. Every other candidate line (a line containing a lowercase letter, a digit, a symbol or a space, a line of 1 character, or a line of 16 or more characters) is skipped. When the same valid word appears two or more times, the second and later occurrences are ignored as duplicates. The dictionary is the set of valid words (with duplicates removed).

== input/jobs.txt (jobs) ==
It consists of zero or more job blocks. Outside the blocks there are only blank lines and lines starting with "#", and these are ignored. Inside a block there are no blank lines and no comment lines. Each line is a sequence of strings (tokens) of 1 to 32 characters containing no spaces, joined with single spaces. The first token of the first line of a block (the header line) determines the kind of the block, and every block ends with a line "END". There are the following four kinds of blocks. Below, s is the name of the job (whether the name, numbers, racks and so on are valid is checked in [Processing]).
1. TOPN job: "TOPN s n o", "RACK r", "BOARD", the board lines (0 or more lines) and "END", in this order. n is the number of moves to show, o is the order of the moves, and r is the rack.
2. VALIDATE job: "VALIDATE s", "BOARD", the board lines (0 or more lines), the record lines (0 or more lines) and "END", in this order. A record line has one of the forms "MOVE p r X Y" (a record of placing tiles), "MOVE p r PASS" (a record of a pass) and "MOVE p r EXCHANGE t" (a record of an exchange). p is the name of the player who made the move, r is the rack at that time, X is a position, Y is a word, and t is the tiles put back in the exchange. A record line with 4 tokens (the 4th token is "PASS") is a pass record; a record line with 5 tokens is an exchange record if the 4th token is "EXCHANGE", and a record of placing tiles otherwise.
3. GAME job: "GAME s x", the player lines (0 or more lines) and "END", in this order. x is the random seed. A player line has the form "PLAYER p t h", where p is the name of the player, t is the strategy, and h is the exchange threshold.
4. DUPLICATE job: "DUPLICATE s x", the player lines (0 or more lines) and "END", in this order. A player line has the form "PLAYER p t".
The board lines are the lines from the line after the "BOARD" line up to just before the first line starting with "MOVE" or the "END" line. A board line is a line of 1 or more characters containing no spaces; it does not start with "MOVE" and is not "END". The k-th board line represents row k of the board, and its j-th character represents the square in column j.

[Processing]
== 1. Terms and common rules ==
- Board: a grid of squares, 15 rows high and 15 columns wide. Rows are numbered row 1 to row 15 from top to bottom and columns column 1 to column 15 from left to right; columns are also denoted by the letters A to O (column 1 is A, column 15 is O). The name of a square is the column letter followed by the row number (for example, the square in row 8, column 8 is H8, and the square in row 3, column 12 is L3). The square H8 is called the center square.
- Premium squares: the kind of each square of the board is given by the following diagram. Line k of the diagram is row k of the board, and each line lists the symbols of the squares from column 1 to column 15, separated by single spaces. "#" is triple word, "*" is double word, "^" is triple letter, "+" is double letter, and "." is a normal square (the center square is double word).

. * . . . ^ . . . ^ . . . * .
. . * . . . + . + . . . * . .
+ . . * . . . + . . . * . . +
. . . . * . . . . . * . . . .
. ^ . . . ^ . . . ^ . . . ^ .
. . + . . . + . + . . . + . .
# . . + . . . * . . . + . . #
. . + . . . + . + . . . + . .
. ^ . . . ^ . . . ^ . . . ^ .
. . . . * . . . . . * . . . .
+ . . * . . . + . . . * . . +
. . * . . . + . + . . . * . .
. * . . . ^ . . . ^ . . . * .
# . . + . . . # . . . + . . #
- Tiles: there are letter tiles, each bearing one uppercase letter (A to Z), and blanks, denoted by "?". When a blank is placed on the board, an uppercase letter is chosen for it and it is used as that letter. On a board, an empty square is written as ".", a letter tile as its uppercase letter, and a blank as the lowercase form of the letter it represents. The kind of a tile on the board is the letter itself for an uppercase letter, and "?" for a lowercase letter.
- Points: the point value of an uppercase letter is the point value of that letter in the tile table, and the point value of a lowercase letter (a blank) is 0. The point value of a tile is its point value in the tile table ("?" is 0).
- Tile collections: a collection of tiles such as a rack (the tiles in hand) may contain the same tile more than once, and is represented by a string of the tile letters (A to Z and "?"). The order of the characters has no meaning. When a collection is output, it is written as the string of its characters sorted in increasing ASCII code order ("?" comes before the uppercase letters), and as "-" if it contains no tiles. This is called the sorted notation of the collection. The point value of a collection is the sum of the point values of the tiles it contains.
- Valid rack: a string of 1 to 7 characters consisting only of uppercase letters and "?".
- Vowels and consonants: A, E, I, O and U are called vowels, and the other uppercase letters consonants. "?" is neither.
- Runs and words: on a board, a maximal sequence (one that cannot be extended further) of tiles that are consecutive left to right within one row (including a sequence of only one tile) is called a horizontal run, and a maximal sequence of tiles that are consecutive top to bottom within one column is called a vertical run. The length of a run is its number of tiles. A run of length 2 or more is called a word; a word that is a horizontal run is called an across word, and one that is a vertical run a down word. The notation of a word is the letters of its tiles joined in order (keeping uppercase and lowercase as they are). When the notation of a word converted to uppercase is in the dictionary, the word is said to be in the dictionary.
- Position notation: the position of something that starts at the square in row r, column c and runs across (to the right) is r followed by the letter of column c (for example 8H); the position of something that starts there and runs down is the letter of column c followed by r (for example H8). r is written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
- Order of the board words: the order in which all words on the board are listed first the across words from the top row down (within the same row from left to right), then the down words from the leftmost column rightwards (within the same column from top to bottom). The position of a word is the position notation given by the square of its first tile and the direction of the word.
- Valid as a name: a string of 1 to 20 characters consisting only of lowercase letters (a to z), digits (0 to 9) and "_".
- Valid as a number: a string such as a token is "valid as a number from a to b" when it is a string of 1 or more characters consisting only of digits, does not start with "0" if it has 2 or more characters, and its value is at least a and at most b.
- String comparison: strings are compared in lexicographic order by ASCII code (when one is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first).
- Tile count check: for a board and a rack, examine the tiles L in tile table order; for the first L such that the number of tiles of kind L on the board plus the number of L in the rack is greater than the count of L in the tile table, the reason is "too many L" (L is that letter or "?"). If there is no such L, the check does not apply.
- Unseen tiles: for a board and a rack, the collection containing each tile L as many times as the count of L in the tile table minus the number of tiles of kind L on the board minus the number of L in the rack.
- The program first reads the tile table and the dictionary as described in 2, then processes the blocks of jobs.txt one at a time from the first, and finally writes the three output files.

== 2. Reading the tile table and the dictionary ==
Read tiles.txt. Then read dictionary.txt from the beginning and add the valid words to a trie. The nodes of the trie correspond to the prefixes of words (including the empty string), and the root is the empty string. Compute the following values.
- The number of candidate lines, the number of words in the dictionary, the number of skipped candidate lines, and the number of lines ignored as duplicates.
- The number of nodes of the trie: the number of distinct prefixes (including the empty string) of the words in the dictionary.
- The number of words of each length: the words in the dictionary counted by length (number of characters).
In this input, the dictionary contains at least one word.

== 3. Checking a job ==
For each job block, perform the following checks in the order of their numbers. If one applies, the job is an error with the reason of the first check that applies (the string inside the quotes; X, i, L and so on are replaced by their values), and no further checks or processing are done for it. If none applies, the job is a valid job and the processing for its kind (9, 10, 12, 13) is performed.
(1) The name s is not valid as a name: "bad name"
(2) The name s is equal to the name of a block earlier in this file (of any kind, including blocks that were errors): "duplicate name"
For a TOPN job, the following checks continue.
(3) n is not valid as a number from 1 to 99: "bad count"
(4) o is none of "score", "tiles" and "equity": "bad order"
(5) r is not a valid rack: "bad rack"
(6) Perform the board check of 4 on the board lines with rack r; if one applies, its reason.
For a VALIDATE job, the following check continues.
(3) Perform the board check of 4 on the board lines with an empty rack; if one applies, its reason.
For a GAME job and a DUPLICATE job, the following checks continue.
(3) x is not valid as a number from 1 to 2147483646: "bad seed"
(4) The number X of player lines is not at least 2 and at most 4: "bad player count X"
(5) Examine the player lines from top to bottom; for the i-th line, if p is not valid as a name, or t is none of "score", "tiles" and "equity", or (for a GAME job) h is not valid as a number from 0 to 99, the reason is "bad player i". Otherwise, if p is equal to the p of an earlier player line, the reason is "duplicate player i". The first line to which one of these applies determines the reason.

== 4. Checking a board ==
For the board lines and a rack, perform the following checks in order. The reason of the first check that applies is the result of the check. If none applies, the board represented by the board lines is used in the subsequent processing.
(a) The number X of board lines is not 15: "board rows X"
(b) Examine the board lines from top to bottom; the length of the i-th line is not 15, or it contains a character other than ".", uppercase letters and lowercase letters: "bad board row i" (the first line to which this applies determines the reason)
(c) The board and the rack fail the tile count check: its reason ("too many L")
(d) The board has at least one tile and the center square is empty: "center empty"
(e) The board has at least one tile and, when tiles adjacent up, down, left or right are regarded as connected, the tiles do not all form a single connected group: "disconnected"
(f) Examine the board words in the order of the board words; for the first word not in the dictionary: "unknown word W at P" (W is the notation of the word and P is its position)

== 5. Legal moves ==
For a board and a rack, a move is a combination of a direction (across or down), a start square and a word w. w is a string of 2 to 15 characters consisting only of uppercase and lowercase letters. The squares lined up from the start square in the direction of the move (to the right for across, downward for down), as many as the length of w, are called the squares of the move, and the i-th square of the move is associated with the i-th character of w. The characters associated with the squares of the move that are empty are called new tiles. A new tile is a letter tile of that letter if it is uppercase, and a blank placed as that letter if it is lowercase. The board with the new tiles written into its empty squares is called the board after the move.
A move is called a legal move when it satisfies all of the following (1) to (8).
(1) All squares of the move are on the board.
(2) On each square of the move that holds a tile, the letter of the tile matches the corresponding character of w (including the distinction between uppercase and lowercase).
(3) The square adjacent to the first square of the move on the side opposite to the direction (the square before) and the square adjacent to the last square of the move on the side of the direction (the square after) are each either off the board or empty.
(4) There is at least one new tile.
(5) The new tiles can be taken from the rack. That is, for each uppercase letter L, the number of new tiles that are L is at most the number of L in the rack, and the number of new tiles that are lowercase letters is at most the number of "?" in the rack.
(6) When the board has no tiles, one of the new tiles is on the center square. When the board has tiles, one of the new tiles is adjacent up, down, left or right to a tile on the board.
(7) w converted to uppercase is in the dictionary.
(8) For each new tile, if on the board after the move the maximal sequence of consecutive tiles that contains its square and runs perpendicular to the move has length 2 or more, then the letters of that sequence converted to uppercase are in the dictionary.
From (1) to (3), w is a word (in the same direction as the move) on the board after the move. It is called the main word of the move, and the sequences of length 2 or more in (8) are called the cross words of the move. The position of a move is the position notation given by its start square and direction. The notation of a move is the position of the move and w joined with a single space (for example 8H CERTAIN, H4 ErA).
The placement of a move is the set of pairs of a square of a new tile and its letter (including the distinction between uppercase and lowercase). Moves with the same placement are regarded as the same move. Two legal moves that differ in direction, start square or w have the same placement only when there is exactly one new tile and, on the board after the move, the horizontal run and the vertical run containing its square both have length 2 or more. A legal move with one new tile is represented by the across move if, on the board after the move, the horizontal run containing its square has length 2 or more, and by the down move otherwise. The collection of all legal moves with distinct placements, represented in this way, is called the list of legal moves, and its size is called the number of legal moves. Even if the rack contains the same tile more than once, moves with the same placement are counted once. A letter tile and a blank placed as the same letter are different placements.

== 6. Scoring ==
The score of a word formed by a move (the main word of the move and each cross word) is computed as follows. Take the point value of each tile of the word; if the tile is a new tile and its square is double letter, double it, and if triple letter, triple it. Sum these, and multiply by 2 for each double-word square of the word on which a new tile was placed and by 3 for each such triple-word square; the result is the score of the word. The premiums of squares that held tiles already on the board are not used.
The score of a move is the score of the main word plus the scores of all cross words, plus 50 points (bingo) if there are 7 new tiles.
The word list of a move is the main word followed by the cross words in the order of the squares of the move of their new tiles. The string obtained by writing each word as "notation:score" and joining them with "," is called the notation of the word list of the move (for example OAF:14,OE:2,AR:2).
The number of tiles placed by a move is the number of new tiles. The leave of a move is the collection obtained by removing from the rack the tiles used as new tiles (for an uppercase letter the tile of that letter, for a lowercase letter "?").

== 7. Move orders and strategies ==
- Standard order: moves are sorted in decreasing order of the score of the move; ties are broken by increasing row number of the start square, then by increasing column number, then across before down, then by increasing w in string comparison. The moves of the list of legal moves are put in a unique order by this. The position (1, 2, ...) of a move when the list of legal moves is sorted in standard order is called the rank of the move, and the first move is called the top-scoring move.
- Leave value: the leave value of a tile collection K is the sum of the tile-table leave values w of the tiles of K, minus 4*D and minus 3*max(0, |V-C|-1). D is the sum of (its count - 1) over each uppercase letter contained in K at least once, and V and C are the numbers of vowel tiles and consonant tiles in K ("?" is not counted). The leave value of the empty collection is 0.
- Strategy order: for each strategy (score, tiles, equity), the list of legal moves is sorted in the following order.
  score: the standard order.
  tiles: decreasing number of tiles placed; ties in the standard order.
  equity: decreasing sum of the score of the move and the leave value of the leave of the move; ties in the standard order.
The move chosen by strategy t is the first move when the list of legal moves is sorted in the order of t.
- Best leave: for a rack of m tiles, among the collections that can be formed by choosing from the rack at least 0 and at most m-1 tiles (collections that are equal as collections count as one), the one with the largest leave value. Ties are broken by choosing the one with more tiles, and if still tied, the one whose string of characters sorted in ASCII code order is smaller in string comparison.

== 8. Anchors and cross-checks ==
- Anchors: the empty squares that have a tile on at least one adjacent square above, below, left or right. When the board has no tiles, only the center square is an anchor.
- Across cross-checks: an empty square with a tile on the square directly above or below it is called an across check square. For an across check square q, the set of uppercase letters X such that, when X is placed on q, the vertical run containing q (the maximal sequence of tiles consecutive top to bottom) converted to uppercase is in the dictionary, is called the across cross-check of q. A new tile of an across legal move on an across check square must be a letter contained in its across cross-check (for a lowercase letter, its uppercase form).
- Down cross-checks: similarly, an empty square with a tile on the square directly to its left or right is called a down check square, and the set of uppercase letters for which the horizontal run formed by placing it there is in the dictionary is called its down cross-check.
- Generating legal moves (Appel-Jacobson method): every across legal move places a new tile on at least one anchor. So, for each anchor, first build the part to its left (the prefix) and then extend rightwards from the anchor. When the square to the left of the anchor holds a tile, the run of tiles consecutive leftwards from there is used as the prefix and is followed in the trie. When the square to the left is empty (or off the board), prefixes are built from rack tiles along the trie (including the empty prefix), with the number of consecutive "empty squares that are not anchors" to the left of the anchor as the maximum length. When extending rightwards, tiles on the board are used as they are, rack tiles that satisfy the cross-check are placed on empty squares, and after the anchor has been filled, if the trie has reached the end of a word and the next square is empty or off the board, the move is recorded. A blank is tried as every letter that continues from that node of the trie. Down legal moves are found by applying the same procedure with the rows and columns of the board swapped. With this method each move is generated exactly once per direction. A move with one new tile may be generated both across and down, so only one of them is kept according to the representation rule of 5. Whatever method is used, the result must match the list of legal moves of 5.

== 9. TOPN job ==
For a valid TOPN job, compute the following values for the board and rack r (the output format is defined in [Output files]).
(1) The number of tiles on the board and the number of anchors.
(2) The unseen tiles (for the board and rack r) and their number.
(3) The number of vowel tiles, the number of consonant tiles and the number of "?" in rack r, and the point value of the rack.
(4) Cross-check statistics: the number of across check squares, the number of them whose across cross-check is empty, and the total number of elements of the across cross-checks of the across check squares. The same for down.
(5) The number of legal moves, the numbers of across moves and of down moves among them, the number of moves that place 7 tiles (bingos), and the number of moves whose new tiles include at least one lowercase letter.
(6) For each number of tiles placed t (t = 1, 2, ..., 7), the maximum score of the legal moves that place t tiles (none if there is no such move).
(7) The best leave of rack r and its leave value.
(8) Anagrams: the words in the dictionary all of whose letters can be supplied one each by the tiles of rack r ("?" can stand for any letter). Compute the number of anagrams, and sort the anagrams in decreasing order of length, and within the same length in increasing order by string comparison.
(9) Hooks: examine the board words in the order of the board words, and for those of length 3 or more, let W be the notation converted to uppercase. The uppercase letters X such that XW is in the dictionary are called front hooks, and those such that WX is in the dictionary are called back hooks. A word with at least one front hook or back hook is a hooked word.
(10) Shown moves: sort the list of legal moves in the order of strategy o, and the first min(n, number of legal moves) moves are the shown moves. For each of them, compute the move notation, the score, the number of tiles placed, the leave of the move, the leave value of the leave, and the word list of the move.
(11) Endgame analysis: performed only when the number of unseen tiles is at least 1 and at most 7 and there is at least one shown move. The collection of unseen tiles is taken as the opponent's rack. For each shown move, the score of the top-scoring move among the legal moves of the opponent's rack on the board after that move (0 if there is no legal move) is the opponent's reply score, and the score of the move minus the opponent's reply score is the net score. Among the shown moves with the largest net score, the earliest one (first in the shown order) is the endgame best.

== 10. VALIDATE job ==
For a valid VALIDATE job, take the board as the current board and process the record lines one at a time from top to bottom. For each record, perform the following checks in order; if one applies, the record is invalid with the reason of the first check that applies (the board does not change). If none applies, the record is a valid record.
(1) p is not valid as a name: "bad player"
(2) r is not a valid rack: "bad rack"
(3) The current board and rack r fail the tile count check: its reason ("too many L")
A pass record is a valid record if none of the checks so far applies.
For an exchange record, the following checks continue.
(4) t is not a valid rack: "bad exchange"
(5) Look at the characters of t from the first; for the first character L such that the number of that character in t is greater than the number of that character in r: "rack lacks L"
For a record of placing tiles, the following checks continue. The move is the move given by the position (direction and start square) that X denotes and w = Y.
(4) X is not valid as a position notation (it is neither a string valid as a number from 1 to 15 followed by one character from A to O (across), nor one character from A to O followed by a string valid as a number from 1 to 15 (down)): "bad position"
(5) Y is not a string of 2 to 15 characters consisting only of uppercase and lowercase letters: "bad word"
(6) The squares of the move extend off the board: "off board"
(7) Examine the squares of the move in order; for the first square that holds a tile whose letter differs from the corresponding character of Y (including the distinction between uppercase and lowercase): "mismatch at Q" (Q is the name of that square)
(8) The square before or the square after (5 (3)) is on the board and holds a tile: "not whole word"
(9) There are no new tiles: "no new tiles"
(10) Take the new tiles out of rack r one at a time in the order of the squares of the move (for an uppercase letter take the tile of that letter, for a lowercase letter take "?"). For the first new tile whose tile is no longer left in the rack: "rack lacks L" (L is the letter or "?" of the tile being taken)
(11) The current board has no tiles and none of the new tiles is on the center square: "center not covered". The current board has tiles and none of the new tiles is adjacent up, down, left or right to a tile of the current board: "not connected"
(12) Examine the word list of the move (main word, then cross words) in order; for the first word not in the dictionary: "unknown word W at P" (W is the notation of the word and P is its position. The position of the main word is the position of the move, and the position of a cross word is the position notation given by the square of its first tile and the direction perpendicular to the move)
The move of a valid record of placing tiles is a legal move. For a valid record, sort the list of legal moves of the current board and rack r in standard order, and compute the number of legal moves M and the score B of the top-scoring move (0 if there is no legal move). For a valid record of placing tiles, additionally compute the score S of the move, the number of tiles placed, the rank R, in the list of legal moves, of the move whose placement is the same as this move, and the word list of the move (starting from the main word in this move's direction), and then write the new tiles into the current board. The score S of a valid pass or exchange record is 0, and the board does not change. The loss of a valid record is B-S.
Per-player totals: for the records whose reason is not "bad player", compute for each p the number of records, the number of valid records, the sum of the scores of the valid records, and the sum of the losses of the valid records. The running total output on a record line is the sum of the scores of the valid records of p up to and including that record. Players are listed in the order in which their names first appear (as a record whose reason is not "bad player").
Job totals: compute the number of valid records, the number of invalid records, the sum of the scores of the valid records, the sum of B over the valid records, and the sum of the losses of the valid records. The board after all records have been processed is the final board.

== 11. Random numbers and the tile bag ==
- Random numbers: use the MINSTD method. The initial state is the seed x; each time a random number is taken, the state is updated to (48271 * state) mod 2147483647, and the updated state is the value taken. Within one job, the same random sequence continues to be used.
- Tile bag: a sequence of tiles. To create it, build the sequence that lists each tile L in tile table order as many times in a row as its count in the tile table (the blank as "?"), and shuffle it.
- Shuffle: let N be the number of tiles in the bag and number the positions 0, 1, ..., N-1 from the front. For i = N-1, N-2, ..., 1 in this order, take one random number, let j be its remainder when divided by (i+1), and swap the tiles at positions i and j.
- Draw: drawing one tile from the bag means removing the last tile of the bag and adding it to the rack. Drawing k tiles means repeating this k times (or until the bag becomes empty, if that happens first).
- Exchange: when an exchange is made with a chosen collection of tiles to put back from the rack, first draw from the bag as many tiles as are put back, then add the tiles to put back to the end of the bag one at a time in the order of their sorted notation, and finally shuffle the whole bag.

== 12. GAME job ==
The players are player 1, player 2, ... in the order of the player lines. Each player has a score (initially 0), a rack, a strategy t and a threshold h.
(a) Initialize the random numbers with the seed x and create the tile bag. The board is empty. Starting with player 1, each player in turn draws 7 tiles as their rack.
(b) Starting the turns with player 1, repeat the following (c) to (e) until the game ends. Turns are numbered consecutively over the whole game (1, 2, ...). Keep a consecutive scoreless count, initially 0.
(c) Compute the list of legal moves of the rack of the player to move; if there are legal moves, the move chosen by that player's strategy is the candidate.
(d) Do exactly one of the following.
- If there is a candidate and it is not the case that "the score of the candidate is less than h and the bag has 7 or more tiles": play the candidate (a play). Write the new tiles onto the board, add the score of the candidate to the player's score, set the rack to the leave of the move, and then draw tiles from the bag until the rack has 7 tiles. Set the consecutive scoreless count to 0. If, as a result, both the rack and the bag are empty, this player has gone out and the game ends (the end reason is out).
- Otherwise, if the bag has 7 or more tiles: exchange. Keep the best leave K of the rack in the rack, and perform the exchange of 11 with the other tiles as the tiles to put back. The rack after the exchange is K plus the drawn tiles. Add 1 to the consecutive scoreless count.
- If neither applies: pass (do nothing). Add 1 to the consecutive scoreless count.
(e) After an exchange or a pass, if the consecutive scoreless count has become 6, the game ends (the end reason is scoreless). If the game has not ended, the turn passes to the next player (after the last player comes player 1).
When the game has ended, perform the following settlement. If the end reason is out, the player who went out gets the sum of the point values of the racks of all other players added, and each other player has the point value of their own rack subtracted. If the end reason is scoreless, every player has the point value of their own rack subtracted. The value with added points taken as positive and subtracted points as negative is the adjustment, and the score plus the adjustment is the final score.
As per-player statistics, compute the number of plays, the number of bingos (plays that place 7 tiles), the number of exchanges, the number of passes, the maximum score of the plays (0 if there are no plays), and the loss (the sum over the plays of the score of the top-scoring move of that turn minus the score of the move played).
The standings are in decreasing order of final score, ties broken by increasing player number. The players with the largest final score are the winners (there may be more than one). Among the plays with the largest score, the one in the earliest turn is the highlight.

== 13. DUPLICATE job ==
The players are player 1, player 2, ... in the order of the player lines. Each player has a total (initially 0) and a strategy t. Only one rack, shared by all players, is used.
(a) Initialize the random numbers with the seed x and create the tile bag. The board is empty. Draw 7 tiles as the shared rack. Set the master total to 0.
(b) Compute the list of legal moves of the shared rack. If there are no legal moves, the game ends (the end reason is stuck).
(c) Advance the round number (1, 2, ...) by one. The top-scoring move is the master move. Each player adds the score of the move chosen by their own strategy to their total (the chosen moves are not placed on the board). Add the score of the master move to the master total.
(d) Play the master move on the board (write the new tiles), set the shared rack to the leave of the master move, and then draw tiles from the bag until the rack has 7 tiles. If, as a result, the rack is empty, the game ends (the end reason is out). Otherwise, go back to (b).
The percentage of each player is floor(100 * total / master total) (the integer obtained by truncating the fractional part) if the master total is 1 or more, and 0 if the master total is 0. The players with the largest total are the winners (there may be more than one).

== 14. Board display and position summary ==
- Board display: the board is shown in 16 lines. Line 1 is 3 spaces followed by the 15 letters A to O separated by single spaces. Of the following 15 lines, the k-th line (k = 1, 2, ..., 15) is the number of row k right-aligned in a width of 2 (padded on the left with one space if it has 1 digit), one space, and the characters of the squares of row k from column 1 to column 15 separated by single spaces. The character of a square is the letter of its tile if it holds one (keeping uppercase and lowercase as they are), and the symbol of the premium square diagram if it is empty.
- Position summary: compute the number of tiles on the board, the number of board words, the longest word (the notation of the first word, in the order of the board words, among those of maximum length; "-" if there are no words), the number of lowercase tiles, and, for each kind of premium square (triple word, double word, triple letter, double letter), the number of such squares that hold a tile.

[Output files]
The program writes the following three files into the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, every line ends with a line feed (LF), and no line has trailing spaces. Integers are written in decimal, negative numbers are preceded by "-", and there are no extra leading zeros. Each file is a sequence of paragraphs, with exactly one blank line between consecutive paragraphs and no blank line at the beginning or end of the file. There are no blank lines inside a paragraph. In the formats, only the symbols that are explained (s, N, P and so on) are replaced by values; all other characters are output as they are. The name s is output exactly as the token written in the header line (also for a job that is an error).
The paragraph of a job that is an error consists of two lines: "K s", and "error " followed by the reason (K is the kind of the job, one of TOPN, VALIDATE, GAME and DUPLICATE).
The position summary line is "position tiles=T words=W longest=L blanks=B premiums=TW:a,DW:b,TL:c,DL:d". T, W, L and B are the number of tiles, the number of words, the longest word and the number of lowercase tiles of the position summary, and a, b, c and d are the numbers of triple-word, double-word, triple-letter and double-letter squares that hold a tile.
The board display is the 16 lines of the board display of 14.

== output/moves.txt ==
Output the dictionary paragraph, one paragraph per TOPN job block (in file order), and the summary paragraph, in this order.
The dictionary paragraph consists of the following 2 lines.
1. "DICTIONARY lines=A words=B skipped=C duplicates=D nodes=E": A is the number of candidate lines, B is the number of words in the dictionary, C is the number of skipped candidate lines, D is the number of lines ignored as duplicates, and E is the number of nodes of the trie.
2. "lengths" followed, for each length k for which the dictionary has words, in increasing order of k, by a single space and "k=N" (N is the number of words of length k).
The paragraph of a valid TOPN job consists of the following lines in this order. A line with a condition is output only when the condition holds. All values are those computed in 9.
1. "TOPN s n=N order=O rack=R": N is the value of n, O is o, and R is the sorted notation of rack r.
2. "board tiles=T anchors=A": T is the number of tiles on the board and A is the number of anchors.
3. "unseen count=U tiles=K": U is the number of unseen tiles and K is the sorted notation of the unseen tiles.
4. "rack vowels=V consonants=C blanks=B value=X": the number of vowels, the number of consonants and the number of "?" in rack r, and the point value of the rack.
5. "checks across=C1,D1,L1 down=C2,D2,L2": C1, D1 and L1 are the number of across check squares, the number of them whose across cross-check is empty, and the total number of elements of the across cross-checks; C2, D2 and L2 are the same values for down.
6. "moves total=M across=X down=Y bingos=B blanks=Z": the number of legal moves, the number of across moves, the number of down moves, the number of bingo moves, and the number of moves whose new tiles include a lowercase letter.
7. "best_by_tiles 1=S1 2=S2 3=S3 4=S4 5=S5 6=S6 7=S7": St is the maximum score of the legal moves that place t tiles, or "-" if there is no such move.
8. "exchange keep=K value=V": K is the sorted notation of the best leave and V is its leave value.
9. "anagrams total=N top=W": N is the number of anagrams, and W is the first at most 10 of the sorted anagrams joined with "," ("-" if there are no anagrams).
10. For each hooked word, in the order of the board words, "hook P W front=F back=B": P is the position of the word, W is the notation of the word (keeping uppercase and lowercase as they are), F is the letters of the front hooks joined in order from A to Z, and B is the letters of the back hooks joined in the same way (each "-" if there are none).
11. (When the endgame analysis was performed) "endgame opponent=K best=k net=N": K is the sorted notation of the opponent's rack, k is the number of the endgame best in the shown order, and N is its net score.
12. For each shown move, in the shown order, "k P W score=S tiles=T leave=L value=V words=Z": k is the number in the shown order (1, 2, ...), P W is the move notation, S is the score, T is the number of tiles placed, L is the sorted notation of the leave of the move, V is its leave value, and Z is the notation of the word list of the move. When the endgame analysis was performed, "net=N" (N is the net score of that move) is inserted between "value=V" and "words=Z", separated by single spaces, giving "k P W score=S tiles=T leave=L value=V net=N words=Z".
The summary paragraph is the single line "SUMMARY jobs=J errors=E moves=M bingos=B". J is the number of TOPN job blocks, E is the number of them that are errors, and M and B are the sums, over the valid TOPN jobs, of the number of legal moves and of the number of bingo moves, respectively.

== output/validation.txt ==
Output one paragraph per VALIDATE job block (in file order) and the summary paragraph, in this order.
The paragraph of a valid VALIDATE job consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "VALIDATE s"
2. One line per record, in record order. With k as the number of the record (1, 2, ...), the line starts with "move k p A". p is the token written in the record line itself, and A is the tokens of the record line after r joined with single spaces (one of "X Y", "PASS" and "EXCHANGE t"). Then append " invalid " followed by the reason for an invalid record, " score=0 total=T moves=M best=B" for a valid pass or exchange record, and " score=S total=T tiles=N rank=R/M best=B words=Z" for a valid record of placing tiles. S is the score of the move, T is the running total, N is the number of tiles placed, R is the rank, M is the number of legal moves, B is the score of the top-scoring move (0 if there is no legal move), and Z is the notation of the word list of the move.
3. For each player, in player order, "player p moves=A valid=B score=C loss=D": A, B, C and D are the number of records, the number of valid records, the sum of the scores and the sum of the losses.
4. "total valid=V invalid=I score=S best=B loss=L": the values of the job totals, written in the order in which they were described.
5. The position summary line of the final board.
6. The 16 lines of the board display of the final board.
The summary paragraph is the single line "SUMMARY jobs=J errors=E moves=M valid=V invalid=I loss=L". J is the number of VALIDATE job blocks, E is the number of them that are errors, M is the sum of the numbers of records of the valid VALIDATE jobs, and V, I and L are the sums, over the valid VALIDATE jobs, of the number of valid records, the number of invalid records and the losses, respectively.

== output/game.txt ==
Output one paragraph per GAME job block and DUPLICATE job block (in file order, with both kinds taken together) and the summary paragraph, in this order.
The paragraph of a valid GAME job consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "GAME s seed=X players=P": X is the value of the seed and P is the number of players.
2. For each player, in player order, "player i p strategy=t threshold=H": i is the player number, p is the name, t is the strategy, and H is the value of the threshold.
3. One line per turn, in turn order. The line starts with "turn k p rack=R moves=M". k is the turn number, p is the name of the player to move, R is the sorted notation of the rack at the start of the turn, and M is the number of legal moves of that rack. Then append " best=B play P W score=S total=T draw=D bag=N" for a play, " exchange keep=K draw=D bag=N" for an exchange, and " pass bag=N" for a pass. B is the score of the top-scoring move, P W is the notation of the move played, S is its score, T is the player's score after the play, K is the sorted notation of the best leave, D is the sorted notation of the tiles drawn from the bag in that turn, and N is the number of tiles in the bag at the end of that turn.
4. "end reason=out player=p turns=K" if the end reason is out, "end reason=scoreless turns=K" if it is scoreless: p is the name of the player who went out and K is the number of the last turn.
5. For each player, in player order, "final p score=S rack=R adjust=A total=T": S is the score before the settlement, R is the sorted notation of the rack when the game ended, A is the adjustment (preceded by "+" if positive, "0" if 0, preceded by "-" if negative), and T is the final score.
6. In standings order, "standing k p total=T plays=A bingos=B exchanges=C passes=D top=E loss=F": k is the standing number (1, 2, ...), T is the final score, and A to F are that player's number of plays, number of bingos, number of exchanges, number of passes, maximum play score and loss.
7. "winner W": W is the names of the winners joined with "," in player order.
8. (When there was at least one play) "highlight turn=k player=p play P W score=S": the turn number, the player name, the move notation and the score of the highlight.
9. The position summary line of the board when the game ended.
10. The 16 lines of the board display of the board when the game ended.
The paragraph of a valid DUPLICATE job consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "DUPLICATE s seed=X players=P"
2. For each player, in player order, "player i p strategy=t"
3. For each round, in round order, "round k rack=R moves=M master P W score=S picks=Q": k is the round number, R is the sorted notation of the shared rack at the start of that round, M is its number of legal moves, P W is the notation of the master move, S is its score, and Q is the scores of the moves chosen by the players joined with "," in player order.
4. "end reason=stuck rounds=K rack=R" if the end reason is stuck, "end reason=out rounds=K" if it is out: K is the number of rounds played and R is the sorted notation of the shared rack when the game ended.
5. "master total=T": T is the master total.
6. For each player, in player order, "final p total=T percent=Q": T is the total and Q is the percentage.
7. "winner W": the same format as for a GAME job.
8. The position summary line of the board when the game ended.
9. The 16 lines of the board display of the board when the game ended.
The summary paragraph is the single line "SUMMARY games=G duplicates=D errors=E turns=T rounds=R plays=P exchanges=X passes=Q bingos=B". G and D are the numbers of GAME job blocks and DUPLICATE job blocks, E is the total number of them that are errors, T is the sum of the numbers of the last turns of the valid GAME jobs, R is the sum of the numbers of rounds of the valid DUPLICATE jobs, and P, X, Q and B are the sums of the numbers of plays, exchanges, passes and bingos of all players of the valid GAME jobs, respectively.

[Source files]
Split the source into files with the following six roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow, and the largest share of the processing. Reading the three input files (the tile table, classifying the candidate lines of the dictionary and their statistics, splitting into job blocks), checking jobs and boards and determining the error reasons, the tile count check and the unseen tiles, attaching scores, leaves and leave values to legal moves and sorting them in the standard order and the strategy orders, leave values and the best leave, the TOPN job statistics (the rack breakdown, the cross-check statistics, the top score per number of tiles placed, sorting the anagrams, hooks, the endgame analysis), checking, scoring and totaling the records of VALIDATE jobs, running the turns of GAME jobs (move selection by strategy and threshold, exchanges and passes, detecting the end, the settlement, statistics, standings, the highlight), running DUPLICATE jobs and the percentages, the position summary, and building all output lines and writing the three output files.
- board: the board size, the center square, the layout of the premium squares, square names and position notation (writing and parsing), transposing the board and the list of tiles, extracting words (in the order of the board words), checking the connectivity of the tiles, and building the lines of the board display.
- dictionary_trie: the trie (nodes, adding words and detecting duplicates, the number of nodes and the number of words, finding the node of a prefix, checking whether a word is in the dictionary) and enumerating the words that can be formed from the rack tiles (anagrams).
- move_generator: computing anchors and cross-checks, and generating legal moves with the Appel-Jacobson method (building the left part and extending to the right, generating down moves by transposition, unifying the representation of moves with one new tile).
- scoring: enumerating the squares of a move, the score of a word (applying premium squares), extracting cross words, the score of a move (including the 50-point bingo), the word list of a move and the number of tiles placed, and the constants for the rack size (7) and the bingo bonus (50).
- game: MINSTD random numbers, creating and shuffling the tile bag, drawing tiles, exchanging (draw, then put back and shuffle), and the point value of a tile collection.

[Constraints]
- In the data lines of tiles.txt, v is from 0 to 10, c is from 0 to 12, and w is from -20 to 30. The total number of tiles is at most 200, and is at least 7 times the number of players of each valid GAME job, and at least 7.
- dictionary.txt has at most 3000 lines, and each line has at most 40 characters.
- jobs.txt has at most 800 lines and at most 60 blocks, and one VALIDATE job has at most 50 record lines.
- In this input, the number of legal moves for any board and rack is at most 5000, and the number of turns of a GAME job and the number of rounds of a DUPLICATE job are each at most 200.
- All intermediate values of the computation (including the products of the MINSTD method) are integers whose absolute value is less than 2^53. All values other than the products of the MINSTD method fit in the range of a signed 32-bit integer.
