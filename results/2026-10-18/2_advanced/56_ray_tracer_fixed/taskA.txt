[Overview]
Write a ray tracer that renders a scene using fixed-point arithmetic with integers only. The scene contains three kinds of objects (spheres, planes, and axis-aligned rectangular boxes, called boxes below) and four kinds of lights (point lights, spotlights, directional lights and area lights). For each pixel, a ray is cast from the camera to find the nearest intersection, and the color is determined by computing shadows (including attenuation of light by semi-transparent objects), diffuse and specular reflection (the Phong model), ambient light, reflected and refracted rays (including the Fresnel term and total internal reflection), and fog. Objects are searched using a bounding volume hierarchy (called BVH below) built based on surface area. Pixels on object boundaries and pixels whose colors differ greatly from neighboring pixels are re-rendered with 2 x 2 subsamples (adaptive supersampling).
As the result, the program writes 10 files: the rendered image (PPM format), character maps showing the object, the illumination and the ray depth of each pixel, images of depth, normals, base color and ray count, statistics such as the number of rays of each kind and the number of hits per object, and the answers to queries (including ray tracing records). The program reads files from the input/ folder and writes files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output.
All computation is done with integers; no floating-point numbers are used. A real number is represented by the integer equal to 4096 times its value (a fixed-point number), and every division rounds toward -infinity.

[Input files]
There are three input files: input/scene.txt, input/render.txt and input/queries.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and each line ends with a line feed (LF). Words within a line are separated by single spaces, and there is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line. Integers are written in decimal, and negative numbers have a leading "-".

(1) input/scene.txt: the scene
Blank lines and lines starting with "#" (comments) are ignored. Every other line has one of the following forms, determined by its first word. Parts enclosed in < > are values; coordinates, vectors, lengths, distances and coefficients are all fixed-point numbers (4096 represents 1).
- eye <x> <y> <z>: the position of the viewpoint e.
- forward <x> <y> <z>: the forward vector F of the camera.
- right <x> <y> <z>: the right vector R of the camera.
- up <x> <y> <z>: the up vector U of the camera.
- view <hw> <hh>: the half-width hw and half-height hh of the view.
- background <r> <g> <b>: the background color.
- ambient <ka>: the ambient light intensity ka.
- fog <r> <g> <b> <start> <end>: the fog color, the distance start at which the fog begins, and the distance end at which the fog is densest. This line may be absent; in that case there is no fog.
- material <name> <r> <g> <b> <kd> <ks> <n> <kr> <kt> <ior>: a material, with color (r, g, b), diffuse coefficient kd, specular coefficient ks, specular exponent n (an integer, not a fixed-point number), reflectivity kr, transmissivity kt and refractive index ior (for example, ior = 6144 represents a refractive index of 1.5).
- checker <name> <r> <g> <b> <size>: gives the material with that name a checker pattern with the second color (r, g, b) and square size size.
- point <x> <y> <z> <I>: a point light at position (x, y, z) with intensity I.
- spot <x> <y> <z> <dx> <dy> <dz> <co> <ci> <I>: a spotlight at position (x, y, z) with direction (dx, dy, dz) and intensity I. co and ci are the cosines of the angles of the outer boundary and the inner boundary of the light cone, respectively.
- sun <dx> <dy> <dz> <I>: a directional light whose light travels in the direction (dx, dy, dz), with intensity I.
- area <x> <y> <z> <ux> <uy> <uz> <vx> <vy> <vz> <k> <I>: a parallelogram area light with one vertex at (x, y, z), whose two edges leaving that vertex are the vectors (ux, uy, uz) and (vx, vy, vz). k (an integer, not a fixed-point number) is the number of divisions; the area light is approximated by k x k points. Its intensity is I.
- sphere <label> <name> <cx> <cy> <cz> <r>: a sphere with center c = (cx, cy, cz) and radius r.
- plane <label> <name> <px> <py> <pz> <nx> <ny> <nz>: the plane that passes through the point (px, py, pz) and is perpendicular to the vector (nx, ny, nz).
- box <label> <name> <x0> <y0> <z0> <x1> <y1> <z1>: the box consisting of all points (x, y, z) with x0 <= x <= x1, y0 <= y <= y1, z0 <= z <= z1.
A name is the name of a material and consists of one or more lowercase English letters; a label is one uppercase English letter denoting an object. The <name> in a sphere, plane or box line denotes the material of that object.
The things described by sphere, plane and box lines are called objects; they are numbered 1, 2, ... in the order they are written in the file, regardless of kind. This order is called the object order. The things described by point, spot, sun and area lines are called lights; they are likewise numbered 1, 2, ... in the order they are written, regardless of kind. The kind names of objects are sphere, plane and box, and the kind names of lights are point, spot, sun and area.
There is exactly one line each of eye, forward, right, up, view, background and ambient, and at most one fog line. Material names are all distinct, and labels are all distinct. The materials named in checker lines and object lines are defined by an earlier material line. There is at most one checker line per material.

(2) input/render.txt: the render settings
The following six lines appear in this order.
  size <W> <H>: the width W and height H of the image (in pixels).
  contrast <C>: the threshold C of adaptive supersampling.
  depth <D>: the upper limit D of the depth of reflected and refracted rays.
  far <T>: the upper limit T of the distance at which a ray is regarded as hitting an object (a fixed-point number).
  epsilon <eps>: the lower limit eps of the distance at which a ray is regarded as hitting an object (a fixed-point number).
  pad <m>: the margin m added to the bounding boxes of the BVH (a fixed-point number).

(3) input/queries.txt: the queries
It consists of zero or more lines, and each line is a query of one of the following forms. Their meanings are explained in [Processing] Section 13.
  pixel <x> <y>
  trace <x> <y>
  ray <ox> <oy> <oz> <dx> <dy> <dz>
  object <label>
  visible <x> <y> <z>
  shadow <x> <y> <z>
  region <x0> <y0> <x1> <y1>

[Processing]
Process the data according to the rules of Section 1 to Section 13 below.

## Notation
- Fixed-point numbers: an integer a represents the real number a / 4096. Below, S = 4096.
- floor(a / b) (a and b integers, b != 0) is the largest integer less than or equal to a / b. The same definition applies when b is negative; for example, floor(-7 / 2) = -4, floor(7 / (-2)) = -4 and floor(-7 / (-2)) = 3. Every division in this task is computed in this form.
- mul(a, b) = floor(a * b / S) (the product of two fixed-point numbers).
- isqrt(n) (n an integer >= 0) is the largest integer s with s * s <= n.
- A vector is a triple of integers u = (ux, uy, uz). x, y and z are called the 1st axis, the 2nd axis and the 3rd axis, in this order, and the component on axis a is written u_a. u + v, u - v and -u are the componentwise sum, difference and sign negation.
- dot(u, v) = floor((ux * vx + uy * vy + uz * vz) / S) (dot product).
- sc(u, k) = (floor(ux * k / S), floor(uy * k / S), floor(uz * k / S)) (k is an integer).
- len(u) = isqrt(ux * ux + uy * uy + uz * uz) (length).
- norm(u) = (floor(ux * S / L), floor(uy * S / L), floor(uz * S / L)), where L = len(u) (normalization). In the given input, the length L of a vector to be normalized is never 0.
- A color is a triple (r, g, b) of three integers between 0 and 255 inclusive. The color mix(c_1 : w_1, c_2 : w_2, ..., c_j : w_j) obtained by mixing the colors c_1, c_2, ..., c_j with the weights w_1, w_2, ..., w_j is the color obtained by computing floor(c_1 * w_1 / S) + floor(c_2 * w_2 / S) + ... + floor(c_j * w_j / S) for each component. In this task the weights are >= 0 and their sum is S.
- Raster order: the order of the pixels (x, y) by increasing y, and for equal y by increasing x. The x of pixel (x, y) runs from 0 to W - 1, counted from left to right, and y runs from 0 to H - 1, counted from top to bottom.
- Integers appearing during the computation may have an absolute value of 2^31 or more, but for the given input all of them have an absolute value less than 2^41. Compute with a type that can represent integers below 2^53 exactly (such as 64-bit integers).

## 1. Preparing the scene
- Plane normal: n = norm((nx, ny, nz)), the normalized vector (nx, ny, nz) of the plane line, is the normal of that plane.
- Spotlight direction: let sd = norm((dx, dy, dz)).
- Light direction of a directional light: let sl = norm((-dx, -dy, -dz)) (a vector pointing toward the light).
- Bounding boxes: spheres and boxes are called bounded objects. The bounding box of a bounded object is a pair (lo, hi) of two vectors: for a sphere, lo = (cx - r - m, cy - r - m, cz - r - m) and hi = (cx + r + m, cy + r + m, cz + r + m); for a box, lo = (x0 - m, y0 - m, z0 - m) and hi = (x1 + m, y1 + m, z1 + m).

## 2. Building the BVH
The BVH is a binary tree, and each node has a bounding box (lo, hi). A leaf holds one or two bounded objects, and an internal node has a left child and a right child. A node is built from a set G of bounded objects (one or more) by the following (1) to (3).
(1) The smallest box that contains all bounding boxes of the objects of G, that is, the box whose lo components are the minima of the lo components of the bounding boxes of the objects of G and whose hi components are the maxima of the hi components, is called the union box of G. The bounding box of the node is the union box of G.
(2) If G has two or fewer objects, the node is a leaf holding the objects of G.
(3) Otherwise, for each axis a among the 1st axis, the 2nd axis and the 3rd axis, sort the objects of G by increasing center coordinate floor((lo_a + hi_a) / 2) of their bounding box (lo, hi), and for equal values by increasing object number. Let A be the set of the first i objects of the sorted sequence (i = 1, 2, ..., |G| - 1) and B the set of the remaining objects, and compute the cost area(A) * |A| + area(B) * |B|. Here |X| is the number of objects in the set X, and area(X) is dx * dy + dy * dz + dz * dx, where dx = hi_x - lo_x, dy = hi_y - lo_y and dz = hi_z - lo_z for the union box (lo, hi) of X. Among all pairs of an axis a and i, choose the one with the smallest cost (if equal, the one with the smaller axis number; if that is also equal, the one with the smaller i), and make the node built from its A the left child and the node built from its B the right child.
The node built from the set of all bounded objects is the root. If there are no bounded objects, there is no BVH. The depth of the root is 0, and the depth of a child is the depth of its parent plus 1.

## 3. Intersecting rays and objects
A ray is a pair (o, d) of an origin o and a direction d, where d is a vector normalized with norm. The points on the ray are o + (t / S) * d (t is a fixed-point number representing the distance from the origin). For each object, either a candidate distance t is computed as follows, or it is decided that the ray "does not intersect" it. A candidate distance is always a value greater than eps.
(1) Slab test: for a box represented by lo and hi, such as a bounding box or a box object, and a ray (o, d), do the following for each axis a in order from the 1st axis to the 3rd axis.
  - When d_a = 0: if o_a < lo_a or o_a > hi_a, the result is "miss" and the test ends. Otherwise, nothing is done for this axis.
  - When d_a != 0: the smaller of floor((lo_a - o_a) * S / d_a) and floor((hi_a - o_a) * S / d_a) is the entry of this axis, and the larger is the exit of this axis.
  Let tn be the maximum of the entries of the axes with d_a != 0, and an the axis with the smallest number among the axes whose entry equals tn. Likewise, let tf be the minimum of the exits, and af the axis with the smallest number among the axes whose exit equals tf (there is always an axis with d_a != 0). If tn > tf, the result is "miss"; otherwise the result is (tn, an, tf, af).
(2) Sphere (center c, radius r): let oc = o - c, b = dot(oc, d), tc = floor((-b) * S / dot(d, d)), h = oc + sc(d, tc) and q = floor(r * r / S) - dot(h, h). If q < 0, there is no intersection. Otherwise, let w = floor(isqrt(q * S) * S / len(d)), t1 = tc - w and t2 = tc + w; if t1 > eps, t1 is the candidate distance; otherwise, if t2 > eps, t2 is the candidate distance; if neither holds, there is no intersection.
(3) Plane (the normal n of Section 1 and the point p = (px, py, pz) of the plane line): if den = dot(d, n) is 0, there is no intersection. Otherwise, let t = floor(dot(p - o, n) * S / den); if t > eps, t is the candidate distance, and otherwise there is no intersection.
(4) Box (lo = (x0, y0, z0) and hi = (x1, y1, z1), with no margin added): if the result of the slab test is "miss", there is no intersection. When the result is (tn, an, tf, af): if tn > eps, the candidate distance is tn, the face hit is the "entering face", and the face axis is an. Otherwise, if tf > eps, the candidate distance is tf, the face hit is the "exiting face", and the face axis is af. If neither holds, there is no intersection.

## 4. Searching for objects
Computing the candidate distance of an object by Section 3 (2) to (4) is called "testing the object", and performing the slab test of Section 3 (1) on the bounding box of a BVH node is called "testing the node". When the result of testing a node is not "miss" and tf > eps, the ray is said to "enter" the node, and tn is called the entry distance of the node.
(1) Nearest intersection: the nearest intersection of a ray (o, d) is found by the following procedure. Initially there is no tentative intersection.
  - First, test all planes in object order.
  - Next, if there is a BVH, visit the root. Visiting a node first tests the node. If the ray does not enter the node, if the entry distance is greater than T, or if there is a tentative intersection and the entry distance is greater than its distance, the visit of the node ends. Otherwise, for a leaf, test its objects in increasing order of number; for an internal node, visit the left child and then the right child.
  - When testing an object gives a candidate distance t, t <= T, and moreover there is no tentative intersection, or t is less than the distance of the tentative intersection, or t equals the distance of the tentative intersection and the number of the object is smaller than the number of the object of the tentative intersection, the object and the distance t become the new tentative intersection (for a box, also record whether the face hit is the entering face or the exiting face, and the face axis).
  The tentative intersection at the end of the procedure is the nearest intersection. If there is no tentative intersection, the ray hits nothing.
(2) Occluders: the set of occluders for an origin o, a direction d and a distance L is found by the following procedure. Test all planes in object order, and then, if there is a BVH, visit the root. Visiting a node first tests the node; if the ray does not enter the node, or if the entry distance is L or more, the visit ends. Otherwise, for a leaf, test its objects; for an internal node, visit the left child and then the right child. When testing an object gives a candidate distance t and t < L, the object is an occluder. The occluders are used sorted by increasing object number.
(3) Test counts: count the node tests and object tests performed in the rendering of Section 12, per ray kind. There are four ray kinds: primary for camera rays, reflection for reflected rays, refraction for refracted rays, and shadow for shadow rays. The tests of procedure (1) are counted under the kind of that ray, and the tests of procedure (2) are counted as shadow. Also, for each node, count the number of times the node was tested (the visit count). Tests performed in the queries of Section 13 are not counted.

## 5. Camera rays
A position inside pixel (x, y) is expressed by coordinates (qx, qy) in units of a quarter pixel. The camera ray of position (qx, qy) is the ray whose origin is e and whose direction is given by the following expression.
  norm(F + sc(R, u) + sc(U, v)), where u = floor((qx - 2 * W) * hw / (2 * W)) and v = floor((2 * H - qy) * hh / (2 * H))
The center ray of pixel (x, y) is the camera ray of (qx, qy) = (4x + 2, 4y + 2). The four subsample rays of pixel (x, y) are the camera rays of (4x + 1, 4y + 1), (4x + 3, 4y + 1), (4x + 1, 4y + 3) and (4x + 3, 4y + 3), used in this order.

## 6. Intersection information
When a ray (o, d) hits an object at distance t as its nearest intersection, compute the following values.
- Intersection point: P = (ox + floor(t * dx / S), oy + floor(t * dy / S), oz + floor(t * dz / S)).
- Outward normal N0: for a sphere, norm(P - c); for a plane, the normal n of Section 1. For a box, the vector whose component on the face axis an or af (called a below) is s * S and whose other components are 0. Here s is, for the entering face, -1 when d_a > 0 and 1 when d_a < 0; for the exiting face, 1 when d_a > 0 and -1 when d_a < 0.
- Front and back: if dot(N0, d) <= 0, the ray hit the object from the front (called "front" below), and the normal is N = N0. Otherwise, it hit the object from the back (called "back" below), and N = -N0.
- Base color: if the material has no checker pattern, the color of the material. If it has a checker pattern, let k = floor(Px / size) + floor(Py / size) + floor(Pz / size); the base color is the second color if k is odd and the color of the material if k is even (the same applies when k is negative).

## 7. Light samples
At an intersection point P, the sequence of samples of each light is built as follows. Each sample is a triple of a direction l pointing toward the light, a distance L to the light, and an intensity Is.
- Point lights and spotlights: one sample, with Q the position of the light.
- Area lights: k x k samples, one for each pair of integers (i, j) with 0 <= i <= k - 1 and 0 <= j <= k - 1, ordered by increasing j, and for equal j by increasing i. The position Q of the sample of pair (i, j) is, for each axis a, Q_a = (component a of the vertex of the area light) + floor((2i + 1) * u_a / (2k)) + floor((2j + 1) * v_a / (2k)) (u = (ux, uy, uz), v = (vx, vy, vz)).
- A sample at position Q has l = norm(Q - P), L = len(Q - P) and Is = floor(I / (the number of samples of that light)).
- Directional lights: one sample with l = sl, L = T and Is = I.

## 8. Local color
At an intersection point P (normal N, base color (c_r, c_g, c_b)), let kd, ks, n, kr, kt and ior be the values of the material of the object, and V = -d. Take the lights in order of number, and the samples of each light in the order of the sequence of Section 7, and do the following (1) to (4) for each sample (l, L, Is).
(1) Let ndl = dot(N, l). If ndl <= 0, the sample is "backfacing", and (2) and later are not done.
(2) The cone factor g is determined as follows for a spotlight; for any other light, g = S. For a spotlight, let cs = -dot(l, sd); if cs <= co, g = 0; if cs >= ci, g = S; if neither holds, g = floor((cs - co) * S / (ci - co)). If g = 0, the sample is "outside", and (3) and later are not done.
(3) Shadow ray: find the occluders (Section 4 (2)) for the origin P, the direction l and the distance L. This counts as one shadow ray. Starting with f = g, take the occluders one at a time in increasing order of number and replace f with mul(f, kt of the material of that occluder). If the final f is 0, the sample is a "blocked" sample, and (4) is not done. If f > 0 and there are one or more occluders, the sample is an "attenuated" sample.
(4) The sample is a "lit" sample; compute Ie = mul(Is, f) and the diffuse component dif = mul(mul(kd, ndl), Ie). Also let r = sc(N, 2 * ndl) - l and rv = dot(r, V); if rv > 0, start with p = S, repeat replacing p with mul(p, rv) n times, and let the specular component be spe = mul(mul(ks, p), Ie). If rv <= 0, spe = 0.
Let lit_j be the number of lit samples of light j, D_j the sum of their dif, and Sp_j the sum of their spe; let Dsum be the sum of D_j over all lights and Spsum the sum of Sp_j. The number of lights with one or more lit samples is called the illumination count of the intersection.
Each component of the local color is min(raw, 255), where raw = floor(c * (ka + Dsum) / S) + floor(255 * Spsum / S) is computed for the corresponding component c of the base color. The number of components with raw > 255 is counted as "saturated" components.

## 9. Reflection and refraction
When the depth (Section 11) of the ray whose intersection was found equals D, or when kr = 0 and kt = 0, the color of the intersection is the local color, and no reflected or refracted ray is cast. Otherwise, the color of the intersection is found by the following (1) to (5).
(1) Let wr = kr and wt = kt, and there is no refraction direction tdir yet.
(2) If kt > 0, do the following. Let eta be floor(S * S / ior) for "front" and ior for "back". Let cn = -dot(d, N) and disc = S * S - floor(mul(eta, eta) * (S * S - cn * cn) / S).
  - If disc < 0, this is "total internal reflection": let wr = kr + kt and wt = 0.
  - If disc >= 0, let tdir = norm(sc(d, eta) + sc(N, mul(eta, cn) - isqrt(disc))). Further, let r0 = floor((ior - S) * S / (ior + S)), R0 = mul(r0, r0) and x = S - min(cn, S); start with p = S and repeat replacing p with mul(p, x) 5 times; compute Fr = R0 + mul(S - R0, p) and fr = mul(kt, Fr), and let wr = kr + fr and wt = kt - fr.
(3) If wr > 0, trace (Section 11) the reflected ray (kind reflection) with origin P and direction norm(d - sc(N, 2 * dot(d, N))), and let Cr be its color. If wr = 0, no reflected ray is cast and Cr = (0, 0, 0).
(4) If wt > 0, trace the refracted ray (kind refraction) with origin P and direction tdir, and let Ct be its color. If wt = 0, no refracted ray is cast and Ct = (0, 0, 0). The tracing of the reflected ray of (3) is done before (4).
(5) The color of the intersection is mix(local color : S - kr - kt, Cr : wr, Ct : wt).

## 10. Fog
When there is fog, the color of a ray that hit an object at distance t is mix(c : S - fg, fog color : fg), that is, the color c of the intersection with fog applied. Here fg is 0 if t <= start, S if t >= end, and floor((t - start) * S / (end - start)) otherwise. Fog is not applied to the color of a ray that hit nothing (the background color). When there is no fog, the color of the ray is the color of the intersection itself.

## 11. Tracing rays
Tracing a ray (o, d) of kind primary, reflection or refraction with depth delta computes the color of the ray as follows.
(1) Find the nearest intersection (Section 4 (1)).
(2) If the ray hits nothing, the color of the ray is the background color.
(3) If it hits, find the color of the intersection by Section 6 to Section 9, apply the fog of Section 10, and use the result as the color of the ray. The reflected and refracted rays of Section 9 are traced with depth delta + 1.
Camera rays (center rays and subsample rays) are traced with kind primary and depth 0.
In the tracing performed in the rendering of Section 12, count the following values: the number of rays per kind (four kinds, including shadow rays); among the primary, reflection and refraction rays, the number that hit nothing (per kind); the number of rays per depth, excluding shadow rays; per object, the number of primary hits (the number of primary rays whose nearest intersection is that object); per object, the number of secondary hits (the total number of hits of reflection and refraction rays); per object, the occlusion count (the number of times the object was among the occluders of a shadow ray); the number of total internal reflections; the number of saturated components; per light, the number of shadow rays and the numbers of blocked, attenuated, backfacing and outside samples; and the test counts of Section 4 (3).

## 12. Rendering
(1) Pass 1: process the pixels in raster order; trace the center ray of each pixel, and let the color of that ray be the center color of the pixel. When the center ray hits an object, the label of that object becomes the label of the pixel, and the distance of the intersection, the intersection point P, the normal N and the base color of Section 6, and the illumination count of Section 8 are recorded for the pixel. When it hits nothing, the label of the pixel is ".".
(2) Pass 2: process the pixels in raster order. If, among the pixels adjacent above, below, left and right (positions outside the image excluded), there is a pixel whose label differs, or a pixel for which the maximum of the absolute differences of the three components of the center color is greater than C, the pixel is a pixel to be subsampled. For a pixel to be subsampled, trace the four subsample rays in order; the color of the pixel is the color obtained by dividing the sum of each component of the colors of the four rays by 4, rounded down. The color of a pixel that is not subsampled is its center color.
(3) The ray count of a pixel is the total number of the rays traced for that pixel in pass 1 and pass 2, together with all the reflected, refracted and shadow rays cast during that tracing. The ray depth of a pixel is the maximum depth of the rays (excluding shadow rays) cast for that pixel.

## 13. Queries
Process the queries of input/queries.txt in order from the first one, and write the results to output/queries.txt (see [Output files] (10) for the format). Processing the queries does not change the values counted in Section 4 (3) and Section 11.
- pixel <x> <y>: report the rendering result of pixel (x, y).
- trace <x> <y>: trace the center ray of pixel (x, y) and output its record.
- ray <ox> <oy> <oz> <dx> <dy> <dz>: trace the ray with origin (ox, oy, oz) and direction norm((dx, dy, dz)) with kind primary and depth 0, and output its record.
- object <label>: report information about the object with that label.
- visible <x> <y> <z>: report whether the point X = (x, y, z) is visible from the viewpoint. Let rel = X - e and the direction dv = norm(rel); if there is no occluder for the origin e, the direction dv and the distance len(rel) - eps, the point is "visible", and if there are one or more, it is "not visible". Also report the nearest intersection of the ray (e, dv).
- shadow <x> <y> <z>: regard the point X = (x, y, z) as the intersection point P, build the samples of Section 7, and for each light find, for each sample, (the occluders for the origin X, the direction l and the distance L). For each sample, starting from the cone factor g of Section 8 (2), compute f by multiplying by the kt of the occluders in the same way as Section 8 (3) (the check of Section 8 (1) is not done, and the occluders are found even for samples with g = 0); the sum of f over all samples of the light is the transmittance of the light.
- region <x0> <y0> <x1> <y1>: report a summary of the range of pixels with x0 <= x <= x1 and y0 <= y <= y1.

[Output files]
Write the following 10 files into the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and each line ends with a line feed (LF). Words within a line are separated by single spaces, there is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and no blank lines are output. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, and negative numbers have a leading "-". In the formats below, the parts enclosed in < > are replaced by values, and everything else is output as is. A vector or color value is written as its components in order, separated by spaces (for example, <P> is the three words <Px> <Py> <Pz>).

## Image files
A PPM (P3) file has P3 on line 1, <W> <H> on line 2 and 255 on line 3; of the H lines that follow, line y + 1 (y = 0, 1, ..., H - 1) lists r, g, b of the colors of the pixels (0, y), (1, y), ..., (W - 1, y), in this order (3 * W integers per line). A PGM (P2) file has P2 on line 1, <W> <H> on line 2 and 255 on line 3; of the H lines that follow, line y + 1 lists the values of the pixels (0, y), ..., (W - 1, y) (W integers per line).
(1) output/image.ppm: the image of the pixel colors found in Section 12.
(2) output/depth.pgm: the depth image. Let tmin be the minimum and tmax the maximum of the intersection distances over all pixels whose center ray hit. The value of a pixel is 0 for a pixel whose center ray did not hit; for a pixel whose center ray hit, it is 255 if tmax = tmin, and otherwise 255 - floor(235 * (intersection distance - tmin) / (tmax - tmin)).
(3) output/normal.ppm: the normal image. A pixel whose center ray did not hit is (0, 0, 0); for a pixel whose center ray hit, compute floor((N_a + S) * 255 / (2 * S)) for each component N_a of the recorded normal N, replace it with 0 if it is less than 0 and with 255 if it is greater than 255, and use these values as r, g, b.
(4) output/albedo.ppm: the base color image. A pixel whose center ray did not hit is (0, 0, 0), and a pixel whose center ray hit has the recorded base color.
(5) output/cost.pgm: the ray count image. Let cmax be the maximum of the ray counts of all pixels; the value of a pixel is floor(255 * (ray count of the pixel) / cmax).

## Character maps
Each consists of H lines, and line y + 1 consists of W characters representing the pixels (0, y), (1, y), ..., (W - 1, y), with no spaces between them.
(6) output/object_map.txt: the label of each pixel ("." for a pixel that hit nothing).
(7) output/light_map.txt: for a pixel whose center ray hit, one digit representing the recorded illumination count; for a pixel whose center ray did not hit, ".".
(8) output/ray_depth.txt: one digit representing the ray depth of each pixel.

## Text files
(9) output/stats.txt: the statistics. Output the following lines in this order.
  image <W> <H>
  supersampled <number of subsampled pixels>
  rays primary <count> reflection <count> refraction <count> shadow <count> total <sum of the counts of the four kinds>
  misses primary <count> reflection <count> refraction <count>
  depth <count at depth 0> <count at depth 1> ... <count at depth D>
  tests primary nodes <number of node tests> objects <number of object tests>
  tests reflection nodes <count> objects <count>
  tests refraction nodes <count> objects <count>
  tests shadow nodes <count> objects <count>
  total_internal_reflection <number of total internal reflections>
  clamped <number of saturated components>
  distance <tmin> <tmax>
  max_pixel_rays <cmax>
  mean <r> <g> <b>
  histogram <h0> <h1> <h2> <h3> <h4> <h5> <h6> <h7>
  - The depth line lists the D + 1 counts for depths 0 to D (shadow rays are not included).
  - tmin and tmax of the distance line are those of (2). If there is no pixel whose center ray hit, this line is distance none.
  - The mean line is the sum of each component of the colors of all pixels of image.ppm divided by W * H, rounded down.
  - h_i of the histogram line is the number of pixels of image.ppm whose brightness Y = floor((299 * r + 587 * g + 114 * b) / 1000) satisfies floor(Y / 32) = i (i = 0, 1, ..., 7).
  Next, if there is a BVH, output all nodes of the BVH one per line, in the order node, subtree of its left child, subtree of its right child (preorder), in the following form.
  node <depth> leaf <label sequence> box <lo> <hi> visits <visit count> (leaf)
  node <depth> inner box <lo> <hi> visits <visit count> (internal node)
  <label sequence> is the labels of the objects of the leaf in object order, with no spaces between them (for example CR).
  Next, for each object in object order, output the following line.
  object <label> <kind> primary <number of primary hits> secondary <number of secondary hits> shadow <occlusion count> pixels <pixel count> box <minimum x> <minimum y> <maximum x> <maximum y> color <r> <g> <b>
  <pixel count> is the number of pixels with the label of that object in object_map.txt, box is the minimum and maximum of x and y of those pixels, and color is the sum of each component of the colors of those pixels in image.ppm divided by the pixel count, rounded down. If the pixel count is 0, the values of box and color are each the single word none (box none color none).
  Finally, for each light in order of number, output the following line.
  light <number> <kind> samples <number of samples> pixels <number of lit pixels> shadow_rays <number of shadow rays> blocked <number of blocked samples> attenuated <number of attenuated samples> backfacing <number of backfacing samples> outside <number of outside samples>
  <number of samples> is the number of samples of that light at one intersection (k x k for an area light, 1 otherwise), and <number of lit pixels> is the number of pixels, among the pixels whose center ray hit, for which that light had one or more lit samples at the intersection.
(10) output/queries.txt: the results of the queries. For each query, first output
  query <query number> <query line>
(the query number is a serial number starting from 1, and <query line> is the line of input/queries.txt itself), and then output the following lines depending on the kind of query.
  - pixel: one line label <label> distance <distance> color <r> <g> <b> samples <sample count> rays <ray count of the pixel>. <distance> is the distance of the intersection of the center ray (none for a pixel that hit nothing), color is the color of the pixel in image.ppm, and <sample count> is 4 for a subsampled pixel and 1 otherwise.
  - trace, ray: the ray tracing record (described below).
  - object: one line <kind> material <material name> bounds <lo> <hi> leaf <depth>. bounds is the bounding box of Section 1 (including the margin), and <depth> is the depth of the BVH leaf that holds the object. For a plane, output bounds none leaf none.
  - visible: one line <yes or no> first <label> t <distance>. It is yes if the point is visible and no if it is not visible, and after first come the label of the object of the nearest intersection and the distance. If there is no nearest intersection, output <yes or no> first none.
  - shadow: for each light in order of number, one line light <number> factor <transmittance> occluders <label sequence>. <label sequence> is the labels of the objects that were occluders for any sample of that light, without duplicates, in object order, with no spaces between them; if there is no such object, it is none.
  - region: first output one line pixels <number of pixels in the range> mean <r> <g> <b> (mean is the sum of each component of the colors in image.ppm of the pixels in the range divided by the number of pixels, rounded down); then, for the label of each object in object order and after that for ".", if the number of pixels in the range with that label is 1 or more, output one line label <label> <count>.
  Ray tracing record: each time one ray is traced, output the following lines.
  - First, one line ray <kind> <depth> origin <o> direction <d>.
  - If the ray hits nothing, one line miss.
  - If it hits: one line hit <label> t <distance> point <P> normal <N> base <base color>, one line per light in order of number light <number> lit <lit_j> of <number of samples> diffuse <D_j> specular <Sp_j>, and one line local <local color>. Then, if (1) to (5) of Section 9 are performed, output one line total_internal_reflection only when total internal reflection occurred, next one line weights <S - kr - kt> <wr> <wt>, and after that the tracing record of the reflected ray if it is cast, and the tracing record of the refracted ray if it is cast, in this order.
  - Last, one line color <color of the ray> (the color after fog is applied).
  The records of reflected and refracted rays appear nested inside the record of the ray that cast them, at the position described above.
  If there are no queries, output/queries.txt is an empty file (0 bytes).

[Source files]
There are the following six source files. The names are language-neutral role names; extensions and the use of upper and lower case follow the conventions of each language.
- main: handles the overall flow and contains most of the processing: reading the render settings and the queries, building the BVH (Section 2), searching for objects and tallying the test counts (Section 4), camera rays (Section 5), the front/back decision of the intersection information (Section 6), light samples (Section 7), the local color and shadow rays (Section 8), assembling reflection and refraction (Section 9), fog (Section 10), ray tracing and tallying (Section 11), rendering and adaptive supersampling (Section 12), queries (Section 13), and writing the 10 output files.
- fixed_point: fixed-point arithmetic (the division floor(a / b), mul, isqrt).
- vector3: vector operations (sum, difference, sign negation, dot, sc, len, norm).
- scene: reading input/scene.txt; the representation of materials, checker patterns, lights, objects, the camera and fog; the normalization of plane normals and of the directions of spotlights and directional lights (part of Section 1).
- intersect: the slab test and the intersection of spheres, planes and boxes (Section 3), outward normals (Section 6), bounding boxes (Section 1).
- shading: base colors and checker patterns (Section 6), diffuse and specular components (Section 8 (4)), the reflection direction, the refraction direction and the Fresnel term (Section 9), the color mixing mix.

[Constraints]
- 1 <= W <= 128, 1 <= H <= 128, 0 <= C <= 255, 0 <= D <= 3, 1 <= T <= 2^20, 1 <= eps <= 4096, 0 <= m <= 4096.
- The absolute value of each component of the viewpoint, the light positions, the vertices of area lights, the centers of spheres, the points of planes, the vertices of boxes, the points of visible and shadow queries, and the origins of ray queries is at most 2^18.
- The absolute value of each component of F, R, U, the directions of spot and sun, the vectors of plane lines, and the directions of ray queries is at most 2^13, and none of them is (0, 0, 0). 1 <= hw <= 2^14 and 1 <= hh <= 2^14.
- The absolute value of each component of u and v of area lights is at most 2^16, and 1 <= k <= 4.
- 1 <= r <= 2^17. Boxes satisfy x0 < x1, y0 < y1 and z0 < z1.
- All color components are between 0 and 255 inclusive. 0 <= kd <= 4096, 0 <= ks <= 4096, 1 <= n <= 128, 0 <= kr, 0 <= kt, kr + kt <= 4096, 4096 <= ior <= 12288, 1 <= size <= 2^18.
- 0 <= ka <= 4096, 0 <= I <= 8192, -4096 <= co < ci <= 4096, 0 <= start < end <= 2^21.
- There are at least 1 and at most 26 objects, at least 1 and at most 9 lights, and at least 1 and at most 26 materials.
- The (x, y) of pixel and trace queries is a pixel inside the image, and region queries satisfy 0 <= x0 <= x1 <= W - 1 and 0 <= y0 <= y1 <= H - 1. The object with the label of an object query exists. There are at most 100 queries.
