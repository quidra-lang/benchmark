[Overview]
Write a program that analyzes and repairs a three-dimensional triangle mesh (a shape of a solid or a surface represented by connecting polygonal faces). The mesh is read from a file written in a subset of the OBJ format (vertex lines, polygon face lines and group lines). All vertex coordinates are integers.
First, the polygonal faces are split into triangles as fans, and then snapping (rounding the vertex coordinates to a grid), merging of vertices with equal coordinates, and removal of degenerate triangles and duplicate triangles are performed. Next, from the connections between edges and faces, the boundary edges, the non-manifold edges and vertices, the connected components and the boundary loops are found, the orientations of the faces (the winding direction of their vertex sequences) are made consistent by breadth-first search, and short boundary loops (holes) are closed with triangles. For each connected component of the finished mesh, the Euler characteristic and genus, the volume and centroid (exact fractions), the surface area, the convexity of edges and so on are computed. Further, the nesting relations between solids are determined by winding numbers (how many times the faces surround a point), and the faces of outer solids are oriented outward and the faces of cavities inward. Finally, queries about vertices, faces, edges, points and so on are answered.
The program reads model.obj (the mesh) and params.txt (the settings) in the input/ folder and writes four files in the output/ folder: report.txt (the analysis report), components.csv (the list of connected components), boundaries.txt (the list of boundary loops and defects) and repaired.obj (the repaired mesh). It prints nothing to standard output. Only the areas and the winding numbers are computed with real numbers (double-precision floating point); every other computation is done exactly with integers. No random numbers are used.

[Input files]
There are two input files: input/model.obj and input/params.txt. Both consist only of ASCII characters, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no tab characters. The words in a line are separated by single spaces. In both files, empty lines (lines of 0 characters) and comment lines (lines whose first character is "#") are skipped.
Hereafter, an "integer word" is a sequence of one or more digits (0 to 9), or such a sequence preceded by a single "-" (e.g. 17, -4, 007, -0). Its value is the value read as a decimal number (the value of -0 is 0).

* input/model.obj (mesh)
Line numbers are counted 1, 2, ... from the beginning of the file, including empty lines and comment lines. In a line that is neither empty nor a comment line, the first word is called the keyword and the remaining words are called the arguments. The line is handled according to its keyword as follows.
- v: a vertex line. If it has exactly 3 arguments and all of them are integer words, it is a correct vertex line, and it defines one vertex whose coordinates are the argument values x, y, z. Otherwise it is an error line with error kind bad_vertex (no vertex is defined). Counting the correct vertex lines in order from the beginning, the vertex defined by the k-th such line is called "original vertex k", and k is called the "original number" of that vertex.
- f: a polygon line. Each argument is called a reference. If a reference contains "/", the part before the first "/" is called the vertex part of the reference; otherwise the whole reference is its vertex part (the part after "/" is not used; for example, the vertex part of each of 12/5/3, 12//3, 12/5 and 12 is 12). Let N be the number of correct vertex lines before that line. Check (1) and (2) below in this order; the line is an error line whose kind is that of the first error found.
  (1) If there are fewer than 3 references, short_face.
  (2) Check the references from left to right. If the vertex part is not an integer word, or its value is 0, bad_index. Otherwise, compute a number m from the value i of the vertex part as m = i if i > 0 and m = N + 1 + i if i < 0; if m is not between 1 and N inclusive, out_of_range. Once either error is found, the references to its right are not checked.
  A line with no error is a correct polygon line, and it defines one polygon whose vertex sequence is the sequence of computed numbers (m_1, m_2, ..., m_j) (j is the number of references). The same number may appear two or more times. Counting the correct polygon lines in order from the beginning, the polygon defined by the k-th such line is called "polygon k", and the line number of that line is called the line number of polygon k.
- g: a group line. The current group name becomes the first argument if there are arguments, and default otherwise. At the beginning of the file, the current group name is default. A polygon belongs to the current group at the time of its line.
- vt, vn, s, o, usemtl, mtllib: ignored lines. Nothing is done; only the number of such lines is counted.
- Any other keyword: an error line with error kind unknown_keyword.
A group name is a word consisting of letters, digits and "_". There is no group named fill (fill is the group name of the faces added by hole filling).

* input/params.txt (settings)
Each line that is neither empty nor a comment line consists of a name followed by one or more values, and is one of the following. Each name other than query appears at most once (the order of the lines is not fixed), and for a name that does not appear, the default value given in parentheses is used.
- snap s (1): the grid spacing s for snapping (an integer from 1 to 10).
- merge on or off (on): whether to merge vertices with equal coordinates.
- degenerate on or off (on): whether to remove triangles with zero area.
- duplicates on or off (on): whether to remove duplicate triangles.
- orient on or off (on): whether to make the face orientations consistent by breadth-first search.
- outward on or off (on): whether to orient the faces of solids outward.
- fill_holes h (0): the upper limit h on the length of the boundary loops to fill (an integer from 0 to 50). If it is 0, no hole filling is done.
- keep_unused on or off (on): whether to output also the vertices that are not used by any face.
- sections name1 name2 ... (summary components): the names of the sections to write to report.txt, in the order in which they are written. Each name is one of summary, warnings, cleanup, components, groups, contacts, edges, valence, queries, and no name appears two or more times.
- query: a query. Any number of query lines may appear, and they are answered in the order in which they appear (section 12). There are the following 6 forms, where v, f, u, x, y, z are all integer words.
  query vertex v
  query face f
  query edge u v
  query point x y z
  query path u v
  query view x y z

[Processing]
* 1. Symbols and notation
- Points and vectors are represented as triples of integers. For p = (p_x, p_y, p_z) and q = (q_x, q_y, q_z), the sum p + q and the difference p - q are the componentwise sum and difference, the dot product is dot(p, q) = p_x q_x + p_y q_y + p_z q_z, and the cross product is cross(p, q) = (p_y q_z - p_z q_y, p_z q_x - p_x q_z, p_x q_y - p_y q_x).
- The coordinates of vertex v are written P_v.
- A triangle is represented as a triple (a, b, c) of vertex numbers, and the winding direction of this sequence is called the orientation of the triangle. Triangle (a, b, c) is said to contain the three directed edges a->b, b->c and c->a. To "flip" a triangle means to replace (a, b, c) with (a, c, b).
- For a triangle t = (a, b, c), the coordinates of its vertices are written T_1 = P_a, T_2 = P_b, T_3 = P_c, and the normal is n_t = cross(T_2 - T_1, T_3 - T_1), q_t = dot(n_t, n_t), and the determinant is d_t = dot(T_1, cross(T_2, T_3)). All of these are integers.
- Fraction notation: the ratio p/q of an integer p and a nonzero integer q is written in the form "numerator/denominator", reduced to lowest terms with a positive denominator. A negative number has "-" on the numerator. If the denominator is 1, only the numerator is written (e.g. 12/8 is 3/2, -6/3 is -2, 0/5 is 0, 5/-10 is -1/2).
- Real number notation: fixed-point notation rounded to 3 decimal places, with exactly 3 digits after the decimal point (e.g. 85.17691 is 85.177, 16 is 16.000). Every real number that is output is 0 or more.
- Integer notation: a decimal number without extra zeros or "+", with "-" for a negative number.
- List notation: a list of integers or names is its elements separated by single spaces. An empty list is written "-".
- A value written as yes/no is yes if the condition holds and no otherwise.
- Group order: the groups to which one or more correct polygons belong, in increasing order of the number of the first polygon of each group, followed at the end by fill when one or more faces were added by hole filling.

* 2. Splitting into triangles
A polygon with vertex sequence (m_1, m_2, ..., m_j) is split into the j - 2 triangles (m_1, m_2, m_3), (m_1, m_3, m_4), ..., (m_1, m_{j-1}, m_j), in this order. All the triangles split from the polygons, starting from polygon 1 in order, are listed and numbered triangle 1, triangle 2, ... from the beginning. Each triangle remembers its original polygon (its number, line number and group). At this point, the vertex numbers of the triangles are original numbers.

* 3. Cleanup
(1) Snapping: for every original vertex, replace each coordinate x with s * floor((2x + s) / (2s)). floor(...) rounds toward negative infinity (the same holds when x is negative). This is the multiple of s nearest to x (the larger one when x is exactly halfway). If s = 1, the coordinates do not change. A vertex for which one or more coordinates changed is called a "snapped vertex". Hereafter, all vertex coordinates are the coordinates after snapping.
(2) Merging: when merge is on, the "representative" of original vertex k is, among the original vertices whose coordinates are equal to those of k, the one with the smallest original number. When merge is off, the representative of each original vertex is the vertex itself. A vertex whose representative is not itself is called a "merged vertex". Every vertex of every triangle is replaced with its representative.
(3) Triangle removal: for each triangle in order from triangle 1, check the following three conditions from top to bottom, and remove the triangle with the reason of the first condition that applies.
  - repeated: two or more of the three vertex numbers are equal (this condition is always checked).
  - zero_area: degenerate is on and n_t = (0, 0, 0).
  - duplicate: duplicates is on and, among the earlier triangles that have not been removed, there is one with an equal set of vertex numbers (regardless of the order of the sequence).
  The triangles that are not removed are called "faces". The group of a face is the group of its original polygon.

* 4. Vertex and face numbers
- Output vertices: if keep_unused is on, all original vertices whose representative is themselves. If it is off, all original vertices used by some face. The output vertices are sorted in increasing order of original number and given new numbers 1, 2, .... Hereafter, a plain "vertex number" means this new number, and this number is also what is written to the output files (where an original number is written, this is stated explicitly). The order of the new numbers is the same as the order of the original numbers.
- The faces are listed in the order in which they remain (the order of triangle numbers) and numbered face 1, face 2, ..., face F. The faces added by hole filling are numbered face F + 1, face F + 2, ... following them (section 8). "Face order" means increasing order of face number.

* 5. Topological definitions
For a collection of faces (a mesh), the following are defined. These definitions are used both for the mesh before hole filling (sections 6 to 8) and for the mesh after hole filling (the final mesh; section 9 onward).
- Edge: a pair of two vertices obtained from a directed edge of a face by removing its direction. An edge is represented as the pair (a, b) of the smaller number a and the larger number b. "Edge order" is the lexicographic order of (a, b) (increasing a, and for equal a, increasing b). The faces that contain the directed edge a->b or b->a are called the faces of that edge, and they are listed in face order. An edge with 1 face is a boundary edge, an edge with 2 faces is a manifold edge, and an edge with 3 or more faces is a non-manifold edge.
- Connected component: a collection of faces connected by repeatedly joining two faces that share an edge. The connected components are numbered component 1, component 2, ... in increasing order of the smallest number of the faces they contain. The edges of a component are the edges of the component's faces, and the vertices of a component are the vertices of the component's faces.
- Fan: when the faces containing vertex v are divided into groups that are connected by repeatedly "joining two faces that both contain an edge (v, w) (w is a vertex other than v)", each such group is called a fan of v. A vertex with 2 or more fans is called a non-manifold vertex. All faces of one fan belong to the same component. When two or more of the fans of vertex v belong to component c, v is called a "non-manifold vertex of component c".
- Consistency: a manifold edge (a, b) is said to be consistent when one of its two faces contains the directed edge a->b and the other contains the directed edge b->a. When the two faces contain directed edges in the same direction, the edge is said to be inconsistent.

* 6. Orientation repair
Perform the analysis of section 5 on the mesh before hole filling (faces 1 to F), and count the number of inconsistent edges at this point (inconsistent_before).
When orient is on, make the face orientations consistent by the following breadth-first search. When it is off, do nothing.
  Mark all faces as unvisited. Look at faces 1 to F in order, and each time there is an unvisited face s, mark s as visited, create a queue (a sequence from which the elements are taken out in the order in which they were put in) containing only s, and repeat the following until the queue is empty.
  Take out the face g at the front of the queue, let (a, b, c) be the current vertex sequence of g, and examine the directed edges u->v in the order a->b, b->c, c->a. If the edge (u, v) is a manifold edge and the other face h of that edge is unvisited, flip h only if h contains the directed edge u->v, then mark h as visited and put it at the end of the queue. If the edge is not a manifold edge, or if h is visited, do nothing.
Flipping does not change the set of vertices of a face, so the edges, the faces of the edges, the components and the fans do not change.

* 7. Boundary loops
For the mesh after orientation repair and before hole filling, build boundary loops (hereafter loops) component by component, starting from component 1, by tracing the boundary edges. Mark all boundary edges of component c (the boundary edges whose single face belongs to component c) as unused, look at them in edge order, and each time there is an unused edge (a, b), do the following.
  Mark (a, b) as used, and let the vertex sequence be (a, b). Letting x be the last vertex of the sequence, repeat the following until x = a. If no unused boundary edge of component c has x as an endpoint, end the repetition. Otherwise, mark as used the one among them whose other endpoint y has the smallest number, and append y to the end of the sequence.
  If the repetition ended with x = a, the sequence with the last a removed, (l_1, l_2, ..., l_L), is a closed loop, and L is the length of the loop. Otherwise, the sequence (l_1, l_2, ..., l_{L+1}) is an open loop, and L (the number of vertices in the sequence - 1) is the length of the loop.
For each edge (l_i, l_{i+1}) of a closed loop (i = 1, 2, ..., L, where l_{L+1} = l_1), check whether the single face of that edge contains the directed edge l_i->l_{i+1}. If it does for all edges, the loop is coherent, and the sequence stays as it is. If it does for none of the edges, the loop is also coherent, and the sequence is replaced with (l_1, l_L, l_{L-1}, ..., l_2). In this way, the sequence of a coherent loop runs in the same direction as the directed edges of the faces. Any other closed loop is mixed. An open loop is open.
The loops are numbered loop 1, loop 2, ... in the order in which they are built.

* 8. Hole filling
A loop that satisfies all of the following conditions is a loop to fill.
- It is a coherent closed loop, and its length L satisfies 3 <= L <= h (h is the value of fill_holes).
- The vertices of the loop's sequence are all distinct.
- The component in which the loop was built has neither non-manifold edges nor inconsistent edges.
For each loop to fill, in order of loop number, create from the loop's sequence (l_1, l_2, ..., l_L) the L - 2 triangles (l_1, l_3, l_2), (l_1, l_4, l_3), ..., (l_1, l_L, l_{L-1}) in this order, and append them as new faces to the end of the list of faces. The group of the new faces is fill, and they have no original polygon. The mesh obtained in this way is called the final mesh.

* 9. Properties of the final mesh
Perform the analysis of section 5 on the final mesh, and build loops by the same method as in section 7 (the loops built here are used only to count them per component). For each component c, define the following.
- V, E, F_c: the numbers of vertices, edges and faces of the component.
- The number of each of the following: the component's boundary edges, its non-manifold edges, the non-manifold vertices of component c, and its loops (including open loops). Let b be the number of loops.
- closed: the component has no boundary edge.
- manifold: the component has no non-manifold edge and no non-manifold vertex of component c.
- consistent: all manifold edges of the component are consistent.
- solid: closed, manifold and consistent all hold. A component that satisfies solid is called a "solid".
These do not change even if all faces of the component are flipped.

* 10. Winding numbers, nesting and outward orientation
- Solid angle: for a point p (with integer coordinates) and a triangle t, let A = T_1 - p, B = T_2 - p, C = T_3 - p, |A| = sqrt(dot(A, A)), |B| = sqrt(dot(B, B)), |C| = sqrt(dot(C, C)), and
  Omega = 2 * atan2(dot(A, cross(B, C)), |A|*|B|*|C| + dot(A, B)*|C| + dot(B, C)*|A| + dot(C, A)*|B|)
  where atan2(y, x) is the argument of the point (x, y) (a value greater than -pi and at most pi).
- Winding number: the winding number w_c(p) of component c at the point p is the integer nearest to the value obtained by dividing by 4 * pi the sum of Omega over the faces of component c, added in face order.
- Nesting: for two different solids c and d, let z be the vertex with the smallest number among the vertices of c that are not vertices of d. If such a z exists and w_d(P_z) != 0, c is said to be inside d. For a solid c, the list of the numbers of the solids that c is inside, in increasing order, is called the inside of c, and its length is called the depth of c. inside is computed for all solids before the following outward orientation (whether a winding number is 0 does not depend on the orientation of the faces).
- Outward orientation: when outward is on, for each solid c, compute 6V_c (the formula in section 11) with the face orientations at that time, and if 6V_c != 0 and either "the depth is even and 6V_c < 0" or "the depth is odd and 6V_c > 0", flip all faces of component c. In this way, the volume of an outer solid becomes positive, and the volume of a cavity inside it becomes negative. The number of flipped solids is called outward_components. When outward is off, do nothing.
- flipped_faces: the number of faces among faces 1 to F whose vertex sequence after outward orientation differs from their sequence right after section 3 (the faces added by hole filling are not counted).
- orientable: on a copy of the face orientations of the final mesh after outward orientation, perform the breadth-first search of section 6 (regardless of the value of orient, over all faces of the final mesh). If, as a result, all manifold edges of a component are consistent, that component is orientable. This search is done only for this test and does not change the actual face orientations.
- Euler characteristic: chi = V - E + F_c.
- Genus: if the component is manifold and orientable, the genus is g = (2 - chi - b) / 2. If it is manifold but not orientable, the number of crosscaps is k = 2 - chi - b. If it is not manifold, neither is defined.

* 11. Geometric quantities
The following values are computed on the final mesh after outward orientation.
- 6V_c: the sum of d_t over the faces t of component c (an integer). The volume of component c is 6V_c / 6.
- Centroid: for a solid c with 6V_c != 0, for each of the x, y and z coordinates, compute the integer S that is the sum over the component's faces t of "d_t * (that coordinate of T_1 + T_2 + T_3)", and let S / (4 * 6V_c) be the value of the centroid for that coordinate.
- Area: the area of face t is sqrt(q_t) / 2. The area of a collection of faces is the value obtained by starting from 0, adding sqrt(q_t) in face order, and finally dividing by 2.
- Normal sum: the sum of n_t over the faces of a component (a sum per component).
- Bounding box: the minimum and the maximum of each of the x, y and z coordinates of the component's vertices.
- Edge shape: let f and g be the two faces of a manifold edge (a, b), in face order. If the edge is inconsistent, its shape is inconsistent. If it is consistent, let d be the vertex of g that is neither a nor b, compute s = dot(P_d - T_1, n_f) using T_1 and n_f of face f, and the shape is convex if s < 0, concave if s > 0, and flat if s = 0.
- Triangle angle: for a face t, if the minimum of dot(T_2 - T_1, T_3 - T_1), dot(T_3 - T_2, T_1 - T_2) and dot(T_1 - T_3, T_2 - T_3) is negative, it is obtuse; if it is 0, right; and if it is positive, acute.
- Squared edge length: len2 of an edge (a, b) is dot(P_a - P_b, P_a - P_b).
- Degree: the number of edges that have vertex v as an endpoint. The "degree within a component" counts only the component's edges.
- Non-planar polygon: for the vertex sequence (m_1, m_2, ..., m_j) of a polygon (using the vertices with these original numbers and the coordinates after snapping), let N = cross(P_{m_2} - P_{m_1}, P_{m_3} - P_{m_1}) + cross(P_{m_3} - P_{m_1}, P_{m_4} - P_{m_1}) + ... + cross(P_{m_{j-1}} - P_{m_1}, P_{m_j} - P_{m_1}). If there is an i with dot(P_{m_i} - P_{m_1}, N) != 0, the polygon is non-planar.

* 12. Queries
The queries are answered on the final mesh after outward orientation. The answer formats are defined in the queries section under [Output files].

[Output files]
Every output file consists only of ASCII characters, and every line ends with a newline (LF). There is no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and there are no empty lines. The words in a line are separated by single spaces (in components.csv, by single commas). In the formats below, each part enclosed in < > is replaced with the value described.

[queries]
Then, for each query, in the order in which they appear, write the following one line. Let N_v be the number of output vertices and N_f the number of faces of the final mesh.
- query vertex v: if v is not between 1 and N_v inclusive, "vertex <v> invalid". Otherwise, "vertex <v> at <x y z of P_v> faces <number of faces containing v> valence <degree of v> fans <number of fans of v> boundary <whether there is a boundary edge with v as an endpoint (yes/no)> components <list of the numbers of the components that have v as a vertex (in increasing order)>". For a vertex not used by any face, faces, valence and fans are 0, boundary is no, and components is "-".
- query face f: if f is not between 1 and N_f inclusive, "face <f> invalid". Otherwise, letting (a, b, c) be the vertex sequence of face f, "face <f> vertices <a> <b> <c> group <group name> polygon <number of the original polygon> line <line number of that polygon> component <number of the component containing face f> normal <x y z of n_f> area <area of face f (real)> neighbors <r_1> <r_2> <r_3>". For a face added by hole filling, the values of polygon and line are "-". r_1, r_2 and r_3 correspond to the edges joining vertices a and b, b and c, and c and a, respectively: each is the number of the other face if the edge is a manifold edge, "-" if it is a boundary edge, and "*" if it is a non-manifold edge.
- query edge u v: let a be the smaller of u and v, and b the larger. If (a, b) is not an edge, "edge <a> <b> none". If it is an edge, "edge <a> <b> faces <list of the numbers of the faces of the edge> kind <boundary if a boundary edge, manifold if a manifold edge, nonmanifold if a non-manifold edge> shape <the edge shape if a manifold edge, otherwise "-"> len2 <len2>".
- query point x y z: let p = (x, y, z). Examine the solids c in order of number, let I be the list of the numbers of the solids with w_c(p) != 0, and W the sum of their w_c(p), and write "point <x> <y> <z> inside <I> winding <W>".
- query path u v: if at least one of u and v is not between 1 and N_v inclusive, "path <u> <v> invalid". Otherwise, perform a breadth-first search that follows vertices joined by edges. Mark u as discovered and create a queue containing only u; until the queue is empty, take out the vertex w at the front, examine the vertices joined to w by an edge in increasing order of number, and for each undiscovered vertex y, mark y as discovered, set the parent of y to w, and put y at the end of the queue. If v was not discovered, "path <u> <v> none". If it was discovered, take the sequence of vertices obtained by following the parents from v to u and reverse it (the path from u to v), and write "path <u> <v> hops <number of edges of the path> vertices <list of the vertices of the path>". If u = v, it is "path <u> <u> hops 0 vertices <u>".
- query view x y z: let d = (x, y, z), compute s_t = dot(n_t, d) for each face t, and write "view <x> <y> <z> front <number of faces with positive s_t> back <number of faces with negative s_t> side <number of faces with s_t = 0> silhouette <number of manifold edges such that s_t of one of the two faces is positive and s_t of the other is negative>".

* output/components.csv (list of connected components)
The first line is the header line, which is exactly the following string.
component,faces,vertices,closed,euler,genus,volume6,area
From the second line on, write one line for each component of the final mesh in order of component number, containing the component number, F_c, V, closed (yes/no), chi, the genus (g if manifold and orientable, otherwise "-"), 6V_c (an integer if a solid, otherwise "-") and the area of the component's faces (real), in this order.

* output/boundaries.txt (list of boundary loops and defects)
For the mesh after orientation repair and before hole filling, write the following lines in this order. The face and vertex numbers are the same as in the final mesh, and the component numbers are the component numbers of the mesh before hole filling.
loops <number of loops built in section 7>
For each loop, in order of number, "loop <loop number> component <number of the component in which the loop was built> length <L> <one of coherent, mixed, open> <filled if the loop was filled, otherwise kept> vertices <sequence of the loop>". The sequence of the loop is the sequence after replacement when the loop is coherent and its sequence was replaced; it lists L vertex numbers for a closed loop and L + 1 vertex numbers for an open loop.
nonmanifold_edges <number of non-manifold edges>
For each non-manifold edge, in edge order, "edge <a> <b> faces <list of the numbers of the faces of the edge>".
nonmanifold_vertices <number of non-manifold vertices>
For each non-manifold vertex, in order of number, "vertex <v> fans <number of fans of v> components <list of the numbers of the components to which the fans of v belong (in increasing order, each number only once)>".
inconsistent_edges <number of inconsistent edges>
For each inconsistent edge, in edge order, "edge <a> <b> faces <numbers of the two faces of the edge>".

* output/repaired.obj (repaired mesh)
The first line is "# vertices <number of output vertices> faces <number of faces of the final mesh>". Next, for each output vertex in order of number, write "v <x> <y> <z>" (the coordinates after snapping). Next, for each face of the final mesh in face order, write "f <a> <b> <c>" with the vertex sequence (a, b, c) after outward orientation. However, before the first face, and before each face whose group differs from the group of the preceding face, write "g <group name>".

[Source files]
Split the source code into files with the following 6 roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow and the largest share of the processing: reading params.txt and the default values, the cleanup flow (calling snapping, merging and removal, and recording their results), renumbering the vertices and faces, the analysis of the mesh before hole filling (the coherent test of loops and the replacement of their sequences, the choice of the loops to fill, non-manifold edges and vertices, inconsistent edges) and writing boundaries.txt, the per-component properties of the final mesh (closed, manifold, consistent, solid, orientable, Euler characteristic, genus), nesting and outward orientation, the non-planar polygon test, the aggregation of components, groups, contacts, edges and degrees, the 6 kinds of queries, and writing each section of report.txt and components.csv.
- obj_io: reading model.obj (keyword detection, integer words, the vertex parts of references, error kinds, groups) and writing repaired.obj.
- topology: the table mapping edges to their faces, connected components, fans, and the tracing of boundary loops.
- orientation: testing whether a face contains a directed edge, flipping, the breadth-first search that makes orientations consistent, and listing the inconsistent edges.
- measure: integer vector operations (difference, dot product, cross product), normals, determinants, areas, centroids, fraction and real number notation, solid angles and winding numbers, and the classification of triangle angles.
- repair: splitting into triangles, snapping, choosing the merge representatives, triangle removal and its reasons, and creating the hole-filling triangles.

[Constraints]
- model.obj has at most 2000 lines, and each line has at most 200 characters. There are at most 1000 correct vertex lines and at most 1000 correct polygon lines, and at most 2000 triangles. The absolute values of the coordinates in correct vertex lines are at most 100.
- The values in params.txt are within the ranges stated in [Input files], and there are at most 50 query lines. The absolute values of the integers in queries are at most 10000; among them, the absolute values of the coordinates of query point are at most 100, and the absolute values of x, y, z of query view are at most 10 (they are never all three 0).
- The final mesh has at least one face.
- Within these ranges, the absolute values of all integers, including intermediate values of the computations, are less than 2^53 (they can be computed exactly with 64-bit integers). In the given data, the absolute values of all integers are less than 2^31.
- In the given data, the exact value of every area that is output (the value with the square roots computed exactly) is at least 10^-6 away from a rounding boundary at 3 decimal places. Also, no point at which a winding number is computed (the vertex z of the nesting test and the points of query point) lies on a face of a solid, and the difference between the value divided by 4 * pi and the nearest integer is less than 10^-6. Therefore, the output is not changed by differences in the order of additions, differences in the last digit of the results of sqrt or atan2, fused multiply-add operations and the like. Nevertheless, compute with the formulas and in the orders defined in this statement.
