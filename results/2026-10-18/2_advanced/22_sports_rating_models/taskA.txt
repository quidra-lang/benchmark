[Overview]
Write a program that, from the match results of one season of a league in a ball game such as handball, estimates several ratings that represent the strength of the teams, compares them, and evaluates their predictive performance. There are five kinds of ratings: Elo, Colley, Massey, Bradley-Terry and offense-defense (OD); the program also computes indicators based on winning percentage (strength of schedule, RPI) and the Pythagorean winning percentage. It summarizes the standings (with tie-breaking by head-to-head results), the progression round by round, upsets, and the agreement between the rankings of the models (Kendall's rank correlation coefficient and Spearman's rank correlation coefficient).
Some of the later rounds are used for validation. For each validation round, the program refits every model using only the matches up to just before that round, predicts the probability that the home team wins each match of that round, and evaluates the predictions by log loss, Brier score, accuracy and so on. In addition, it performs a grid search over the Elo parameters and predicts the final standings by a random simulation of the remaining matches.
The input is three files in the input/ folder (teams, match results, settings), and the results are written to seven files in the output/ folder. Nothing is written to standard output. Random numbers come only from the generator defined in [Processing], so the result is uniquely determined.

[Input files]
There are three input files: input/teams.csv, input/matches.csv and input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There is no whitespace at the start or end of a line. Integers are base-10 numbers without extra leading zeros, and there are no negative numbers.

* input/teams.csv (teams)
The first line is a header line, the string id,name. Each line from the second line on represents one team and lists the team ID and the team name in this order, separated by a comma. A team ID consists of 3 uppercase English letters and is different for each team. A team name is a string of 1 to 30 characters consisting of English letters and spaces, whose first and last characters are letters. The lines are sorted by team ID in ascending order (character code order).
Let n be the number of teams. The order of the lines in the file is called the "team order".

* input/matches.csv (match results)
The first line is a header line, the string round,home,away,home_score,away_score. Each line from the second line on represents one match and lists the round number, the ID of the home team, the ID of the away team, the score of the home team and the score of the away team in this order, separated by commas. A round number is an integer >= 1, and a score is an integer from 0 to 99. The home team and the away team are different, and both appear in teams.csv.
Every integer from 1 to R appears as a round number (R is called the final round). In each round, every team plays exactly one match (either at home or away). Therefore each round has n / 2 matches.
The lines are mostly in round order, but a postponed match may be placed after the lines of rounds later than its own round.

* input/params.txt (settings)
It consists of the following 20 lines, in this order. Each line is a keyword followed by one or more values, separated by single spaces.
- WIN_POINTS a: the points for a win (a positive integer)
- DRAW_POINTS b: the points for a draw (an integer >= 0 and less than a)
- FORM_LENGTH f: the number of matches shown as the recent form (an integer from 1 to R)
- CLOSE_MARGIN c: the upper limit of the score difference for a match to be regarded as close (an integer >= 0)
- PYTHAGOREAN_EXPONENT x: the exponent of the Pythagorean winning percentage (a positive decimal number. A decimal number is a base-10 number consisting of, in this order, a sequence of one or more digits, ".", and a sequence of one or more digits)
- ELO_K K: the coefficient K of the Elo rating (a positive integer)
- ELO_HOME H: the home advantage of the Elo rating (an integer >= 0)
- ELO_INITIAL E0: the initial value of the Elo rating (a positive integer)
- ELO_GRID_K K_1 K_2 ...: the values of K for the grid search (one or more distinct positive integers, in increasing order)
- ELO_GRID_HOME H_1 H_2 ...: the values of H for the grid search (one or more distinct integers >= 0, in increasing order)
- BT_ITERATIONS N_bt: the number of iterations of the Bradley-Terry model (a positive integer)
- OD_ITERATIONS N_od: the number of iterations of the offense-defense ratings (a positive integer)
- LINK_ITERATIONS N_link: the number of iterations of Newton's method in fitting the link functions (a positive integer)
- HOLDOUT_ROUND h: the round at which validation starts (an integer from 2 to R)
- UPSETS U: the maximum number of matches shown as upsets (a positive integer)
- CALIBRATION_BINS B: the number of bins of the calibration table (an integer from 2 to 10)
- SIMULATIONS S: the number of simulations (a positive integer that is a divisor of 10000)
- SIM_SEED s0: the random seed (an integer from 1 to 2147483646)
- TOP_POSITIONS T: the number of positions regarded as the top (an integer from 1 to n)
- BOTTOM_POSITIONS Q: the number of positions regarded as the bottom (an integer from 1 to n)

[Processing]
* 1. Notation and computation rules
- ln is the natural logarithm, exp is the exponential function, sqrt is the square root, |v| is the absolute value of v, and floor(v) is the largest integer not greater than v. 10^v is 10 to the power v, and v^w is v to the power w (v > 0).
- The "match order" is the order obtained by sorting the matches by round number in increasing order, with the matches of the same round in the order of the lines of matches.csv. From here on, a collection of matches is always handled in match order. A postponed match is also processed at the position of its round number, not at its position in the file.
- Unless stated otherwise, a sum SUM (written in place of the summation sign) is computed by adding the terms one at a time from the first: matches in match order, teams in team order, and anything else in increasing order of the index.
- A value defined as a "fraction" is computed exactly with integer arithmetic and handled as a reduced fraction (with a positive denominator). When the value of a fraction is written as a real number, its exact value is rounded.
- The "d-decimal value" of a real number v is v rounded to d decimal places (the same value as written to the output files). Some comparisons for ranking or selection are made on d-decimal values; whenever that is the case, it is always stated explicitly.
- Every rule that selects a maximum or a minimum always states how to choose among equal values.

* 2. Match results and team records
- The result y of a match from the home side is 1 (home win) if the home score is greater than the away score, 1/2 (draw) if they are equal, and 0 (away win) if it is smaller.
- The matches of team t are the matches in which t played as the home team or as the away team. In such a match, t's score is called "scored" and the opponent's score "conceded"; if scored is greater than conceded the match is a win (letter W), if they are equal a draw (letter D), and if it is smaller a loss (letter L). The points are a for a win, b for a draw and 0 for a loss.
- The record of team t in a collection of matches G consists of the following, over the matches of t contained in G: the number of matches, the numbers of wins, draws and losses, the total scored, the total conceded, the goal difference (total scored - total conceded), and the total points.
- The winning percentage wp_t of team t in G is the fraction (2 * number of wins + number of draws) / (2 * number of matches).
- "W-D-L" is the string that joins the numbers of wins, draws and losses over some matches, in this order, with - (example: 9-1-5).

* 3. Standings
The standings of a collection of matches G are all teams sorted by the following priorities.
1. total points in G, descending
2. head-to-head points, descending
3. head-to-head goal difference, descending
4. goal difference in G, descending
5. total scored in G, descending
6. team ID, ascending (character code order)
The head-to-head values are computed as follows. Teams with the same total points in G form one group (a group may consist of a single team). For each team of a group, its total points and its goal difference in the collection consisting only of the matches of G between teams of the group (matches in which both the home team and the away team belong to the group) are its "head-to-head points" and its "head-to-head goal difference". The head-to-head values are computed only once per group; they are not recomputed for a part of a group.
The position of the k-th team in the standings is k. The standings of the collection of all matches are called the "final standings".

* 4. Winning-percentage-based indicators and Pythagorean winning percentage
From the record of each team t in the collection of all matches, compute the following values.
- sos_t (strength of schedule): the fraction obtained by dividing the sum of the winning percentages wp of the opponents in t's matches by the number of t's matches. If t played the same opponent twice, that opponent is counted twice.
- sos2_t: the fraction obtained by dividing the sum of the sos of the opponents in t's matches by the number of t's matches.
- rpi_t = (wp_t + 2 * sos_t + sos2_t) / 4 (fraction)
- pythag_t = 1 / (1 + (total conceded / total scored)^x) (real number)
- luck_t = (real value of wp_t) - pythag_t

* 5. Elo rating
From a collection of matches G, a coefficient kappa, a home advantage eta and an initial value epsilon, the Elo ratings are computed as follows. First set the rating of every team to epsilon, then process the matches of G one at a time in match order. For a match with home team i and away team j, let Ri and Rj be the ratings just before processing it:
- expected value: e = 1 / (1 + 10^((Rj - Ri - eta) / 400))
- margin: g = |home score - away score|
- multiplier: if g = 0, M = 1. If g > 0, let D = Ri + eta - Rj when the home team won and D = Rj - Ri - eta when the away team won, and M = ln(g + 1) * 2.2 / (0.001 * D + 2.2).
- change: Delta = kappa * M * (y - e)
- new ratings: replace Ri by Ri + Delta and Rj by Rj - Delta.
This e is called the "pre-match expected value" of the match, and Delta the "change" of the match. The ratings just after all matches of a round have been processed are called the "post-round ratings" of that round.
Unless stated otherwise, the Elo rating is the rating after processing all matches of G with kappa = K, eta = H, epsilon = E0.

* 6. Colley rating
From a collection of matches G, build an n x n matrix C and a vector psi with n components (the teams are numbered 1 to n in team order). C[i][i] = 2 + (number of matches of team i in G), C[i][j] = -(number of matches between team i and team j in G) for i != j, and psi[i] = 1 + (number of wins of i in G - number of losses of i in G) / 2 (draws are counted as neither). Solve the linear system C r = psi by Cholesky decomposition, and let r[i] be the Colley rating of team i.
- Decomposition: compute the lower triangular matrix L for i = 1, ..., n in order, and for each i for j = 1, ..., i in order. For j < i, L[i][j] = (C[i][j] - SUM_{k<j} L[i][k] * L[j][k]) / L[j][j]; for j = i, L[i][i] = sqrt(C[i][i] - SUM_{k<i} L[i][k] * L[i][k]). SUM is the sum added in increasing order of k, and that sum is subtracted from C[i][j].
- Forward substitution: for i = 1, ..., n in order, z[i] = (psi[i] - SUM_{k<i} L[i][k] * z[k]) / L[i][i]
- Back substitution: for i = n, ..., 1 in order, r[i] = (z[i] - SUM_{k>i} L[k][i] * r[k]) / L[i][i] (SUM in increasing order of k)

* 7. Massey rating
The ratings r[1], ..., r[n] of the teams and the home advantage mu are computed by least squares so that, in each match, "home score - away score" is close to r[home] - r[away] + mu. Let N = n + 1. Start with an N x N matrix A and a vector phi with N components, all zero, and for each match of G (home i, away j, margin g = home score - away score) add the following.
- 1 to A[i][i] and A[j][j], -1 to A[i][j] and A[j][i]
- 1 to A[i][N] and A[N][i], -1 to A[j][N] and A[N][j], 1 to A[N][N]
- g to phi[i], -g to phi[j], g to phi[N]
Next, replace row n of A by A[n][k] = 1 (k = 1, ..., n), A[n][N] = 0, and replace phi[n] by 0 (this is the condition that the ratings sum to 0). Solve the linear system A v = phi by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting as follows, and let v[1], ..., v[n] be the Massey ratings and v[N] the Massey home advantage mu.
- Elimination: for k = 1, ..., N in order, (1) among rows k to N, choose the row p with the largest |A[p][k]| (if equal, the smaller row number), and swap rows k and p of A, and swap phi[k] and phi[p]. (2) For i = k + 1, ..., N in order, let lambda = A[i][k] / A[k][k], replace A[i][j] by A[i][j] - lambda * A[k][j] for j = k, ..., N in order, and replace phi[i] by phi[i] - lambda * phi[k].
- Back substitution: for i = N, ..., 1 in order, v[i] = (phi[i] - SUM_{j>i} A[i][j] * v[j]) / A[i][i] (SUM in increasing order of j)

* 8. Bradley-Terry model
In the model where the probability that home team i beats away team j is theta * s_i / (theta * s_i + s_j), the strength s_i of each team and the home factor theta are computed by the MM algorithm. Let W_i be the number of wins of team i in G plus 1/2 of its number of draws in G, and let Wh be the sum of y over all matches of G. Initially set s_i = 1 for every i and theta = 1, then repeat the following update N_bt times.
1. Set zeta_i = 0 for every i. For each match of G (home i, away j), let q = theta * s_i + s_j, and add theta / q to zeta_i and 1 / q to zeta_j.
2. For every i, replace s_i by W_i / zeta_i.
3. Let Sm be SUM_i s_i, and for every i replace s_i by s_i * n / Sm (computed from left to right).
4. Replace theta by Wh / SUM_g (s_i / (theta * s_i + s_j)). SUM_g is the sum over the matches of G (home i, away j), s are the values updated in steps 2 and 3, and theta on the right-hand side is the value before the update.
The final s_i is the Bradley-Terry rating of team i, and the final theta is the home factor.

* 9. Offense-defense (OD) rating
Let P be the n x n matrix with P[i][j] = (total of the scores of j in the matches of G between team i and team j) (P[i][i] = 0). Start with the defense value d_i = 1 for every i, and repeat the following update N_od times.
1. For j = 1, ..., n, o_j = SUM_i P[i][j] / d_i (divide each term first, then add in increasing order of i)
2. For i = 1, ..., n, d_i = SUM_j P[i][j] / o_j (using the o computed in step 1; divide each term first, then add in increasing order of j)
The final o_i is the offense value, d_i the defense value, and od_i = o_i / d_i the OD rating.

* 10. Fitting the models and ranks
To "fit the models" to a collection of matches G means to compute, for G, the Elo ratings (kappa = K, eta = H, epsilon = E0), the Colley ratings, the Massey ratings and mu, the Bradley-Terry ratings and theta, and the offense values, defense values and OD ratings of sections 5 to 9, and the winning percentage wp of each team.
From the model values fitted to the collection of all matches and the values of section 4, build the following "comparison values" of each team.
- elo: the 2-decimal value of the Elo rating, colley: the 4-decimal value of the Colley rating, massey: the 4-decimal value of the Massey rating, bt: the 4-decimal value of the Bradley-Terry rating, od: the 2-decimal value of the OD rating, winpct: wp (fraction), rpi: rpi (fraction), pythag: the 4-decimal value of pythag, standings: the position in the final standings multiplied by -1
For every comparison value, a larger value means a higher place. The rank of team t in a model is the number of teams whose comparison value in that model is greater than that of t, plus 1 (teams with equal comparison values get the same rank).
The six models elo, colley, massey, bt, od, winpct are called the "rating models", and this order is called the "rating model order". The "rank total" of a team is the sum of its ranks in the six rating models.

* 11. Round-by-round progression
For each round r = 1, ..., R, compute the following values.
- The Elo ratings after round r (the "post-round ratings" of section 5), and their change (the value minus the rating after round r - 1; for r = 1, the value minus E0)
- The Elo rank after round r: the number of teams whose 2-decimal value of the Elo rating after round r is greater than that of the team, plus 1
- Each team's total points in the collection of matches with round number <= r, and its position in the standings of that collection (called the "position after round r")
Leader changes: for r = 1, ..., R in order, look at the team in position 1 after round r, and record round r if r = 1 or if that team differs from the team in position 1 after round r - 1.
Trajectory extremes: for each team, find the round with the largest and the round with the smallest 2-decimal value of the post-round Elo rating, and the round with the smallest (best) and the round with the largest post-round position. In every case, if several rounds have the same value, take the earliest one.

* 12. Upsets, longest streaks, head-to-head table
- Upsets: for each match that is not a draw, compute the pre-match expected value of the winning team (e if the home team won, 1 - e if the away team won). Here e is the value obtained when computing the Elo ratings on the collection of all matches (kappa = K, eta = H, epsilon = E0). Sort these matches by the 4-decimal value of the winning team's expected value in increasing order (if equal, in match order); the first U matches (all of them if there are fewer than U matches that are not draws) are the upsets. The rating gained by the winning team is the absolute value |Delta| of the change of that match.
- Longest win streak: arrange the matches of team t in match order; among the runs of consecutive wins, take the longest one (if several have the same length, the one that starts earlier); the streak is given by its number of matches, the round number of its first match and the round number of its last match. If there is no win at all, the length is 0. In the same way, the run of consecutive wins or draws is the "longest unbeaten streak".
- Head-to-head table: for each pair (u, v) (u != v) of the top T teams of the final standings, the W-D-L from u's point of view over all matches of team u whose opponent is v.

* 13. Rank correlation
The nine models standings, elo, colley, massey, bt, od, winpct, rpi, pythag are called the "comparison models", and this order is called the "comparison order". For two comparison models A and B, let alpha_t and beta_t be the comparison values of team t in A and in B, respectively.
- Kendall's tau: for every pair (t, u) in which t comes before u in team order, the pair is "concordant" if alpha_t - alpha_u and beta_t - beta_u are both positive or both negative, and "discordant" if one is positive and the other is negative; pairs in which either difference is 0 are not counted. tau is the fraction (number of concordant pairs - number of discordant pairs) / (n * (n - 1) / 2).
- Spearman's rho: the average rank of team t in A is (number of teams with a comparison value greater than alpha_t) + (number of teams with a comparison value equal to alpha_t (including t itself) + 1) / 2, and the average rank in B is computed in the same way. With d_t the difference between the average ranks of t in A and in B, rho is the fraction 1 - 6 * SUM_t d_t * d_t / (n * (n * n - 1)).
Even when A and B are the same model, the same formulas are used (if some teams have equal comparison values, tau is not 1).

* 14. Validation (refitting per round and prediction)
Matches with round number >= h are called "validation matches", and each round from h to R is called a "validation round". For each validation round r, fit the models to the collection of matches with round number less than r (the "training matches" of round r), and use those values to predict the probability that the home team wins each match of round r. Below, for a match with home i and away j, all ratings are the values fitted to the training matches.
(1) Features: for the four models colley, massey, od, winpct (the "link models", handled in this order), the feature z of a match is defined as follows.
- colley: (Colley rating of i) - (Colley rating of j)
- massey: (Massey rating of i) - (Massey rating of j)
- od: ln(od_i) - ln(od_j)
- winpct: (real value of wp_i) - (real value of wp_j)
(2) Fitting the link functions: let sigma(v) = 1 / (1 + exp(-v)). For each link model, with z_k the feature and y_k the result of training match k (k = 1, 2, ...), find alpha and beta of the home win probability sigma(alpha + beta * z) by Newton's method. Starting from alpha = 0, beta = 0, repeat the following update N_link times.
- For each training match, let p_k = sigma(alpha + beta * z_k) and w_k = p_k * (1 - p_k).
- Let g0 = SUM_k (y_k - p_k), g1 = SUM_k (y_k - p_k) * z_k, h00 = SUM_k w_k, h01 = SUM_k w_k * z_k, h11 = SUM_k w_k * z_k * z_k.
- With det = h00 * h11 - h01 * h01, replace alpha by alpha + (h11 * g0 - h01 * g1) / det and beta by beta + (h00 * g1 - h01 * g0) / det (both use the alpha and beta before the update and the same g0, g1, h00, h01, h11).
(3) Prediction: for each match of round r, compute the home win probability p of each of the following eight "prediction models". This order is called the "prediction model order".
- base: the sum of y over the training matches divided by the number of training matches
- elo: 1 / (1 + 10^((Rj - Ri - H) / 400)) (Ri and Rj are the Elo ratings on the training matches)
- colley, massey, od, winpct: sigma(alpha + beta * z) for the feature z of the match (alpha and beta are that model's values from (2))
- bt: theta * s_i / (theta * s_i + s_j)
- ensemble: the p of elo, colley, massey, bt, od, winpct added in this order and divided by 6
(4) Massey margin error: for each validation match, compute the predicted margin (Massey rating of i - Massey rating of j + mu), and let error = (home score - away score) - predicted margin.

* 15. Evaluation
Let m be the number of validation matches. For each prediction model, compute the following values for each validation match.
- Log loss: -ln(p) if y = 1, -ln(1 - p) if y = 0, and -(ln(p) / 2 + ln(1 - p) / 2) if y = 1/2
- Brier score: (p - y) * (p - y)
- Correctness score: p > 1/2 means that a home win was predicted, and p < 1/2 means that an away win was predicted. The score is 1 if the prediction is right, 1/2 if the match is a draw, and 0 if the prediction is wrong.
The "mean" is the values added in match order and divided by their count. The log loss and the Brier score of a model are their means over the validation matches, the skill is 1 - (Brier score of the model) / (Brier score of base), and the accuracy is the fraction obtained by dividing the sum of the correctness scores by m.
- Model rankings: sort the prediction models in three ways: by the 4-decimal value of the log loss in increasing order, by the 4-decimal value of the Brier score in increasing order, and by the accuracy in decreasing order (in every ordering, equal values are kept in prediction model order).
- Paired comparison: the model ranked first in the log loss ranking is the "reference model". For each prediction model other than the reference model, compute for each validation match the difference delta = (log loss of that model) - (log loss of the reference model); let ma be the mean of these differences, sd = sqrt((SUM (delta - ma) * (delta - ma)) / (m - 1)), and t value = ma / (sd / sqrt(m)).
- Massey margin error: the mean absolute error (the mean of the absolute values of the errors), the root mean squared error (the square root of the mean of the squared errors), and the bias (the mean of the errors)
- Log loss per round: for each validation round, the mean of the log loss over only the validation matches of that round
- Calibration table: for each prediction model and each bin k = 1, ..., B, the number of validation matches for which floor(B * p) + 1 is k (a match for which floor(B * p) + 1 is greater than B is put into bin B), the mean of their p, and the mean of their y (fraction)

* 16. Elo grid search
For each pair of a value K' in ELO_GRID_K and a value H' in ELO_GRID_HOME, compute the Elo ratings on the collection of all matches with kappa = K', eta = H', epsilon = E0, take the pre-match expected values of the validation matches as the home win probabilities, and compute the mean log loss in the same way as in section 15 (since each team plays only one match in each round, this expected value depends only on the matches before that round). The pair with the smallest 4-decimal value of the log loss (if equal, the one with the smaller K', and if that is also equal, the one with the smaller H') is the "best pair".

* 17. Simulation of the remaining matches
The validation matches are called the "remaining matches". Each team's total points in the collection of matches with round number less than h are its "current points", and its position in the standings of that collection is its "current position". The home win probability p of each remaining match (home i, away j) is 1 / (1 + 10^((Rj - Ri - H) / 400)) with the Elo ratings Ri, Rj computed on the collection of matches with round number less than h, and it does not change during the simulation.
- Random numbers: generate the integer sequence X_0 = s0, X_(k+1) = (48271 * X_k) mod 2147483647, and every time a random number is needed, use X_1, X_2, ... one at a time in order (a new simulation does not go back to X_1 but continues with the following values). The value used is u = X_k / 2147483647 (real number).
- One simulation: make a points table that is a copy of the current points, and process the remaining matches in match order. For the next random number u, the home team wins if u < p, and otherwise the away team wins; add a to the points of the winning team (there are no draws). After all remaining matches, sort the teams by points in decreasing order (if equal, by current position in increasing order); the final place of the k-th team is k.
- Perform S simulations, and for each team count the total of its points (over the S simulations), the number of times its final place is 1, the number of times its final place is <= T, and the number of times its final place is >= n - Q + 1.
- Projected rank: sort the teams by their total points over the S simulations in decreasing order (if equal, by current position in increasing order); the projected rank of the k-th team is k.
- Remaining schedule strength: the mean of the above Elo ratings (the values computed on the matches with round number less than h) of the opponents in the team's remaining matches.
- Projection agreement: Kendall's tau of section 13 with the projected rank multiplied by -1 and the position in the final standings multiplied by -1 as the comparison values.

[Output files]
Write the seven files output/standings.csv, output/ratings.csv, output/history.csv, output/season.txt, output/predictions.csv, output/evaluation.txt and output/projection.csv.

* Common rules
- The output consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). Do not put extra whitespace at the start or end of a line, and do not insert blank lines that are not specified.
- In the formats below, each part enclosed in < > is replaced by a value, and all other characters are written as they are. In text files values are separated by a single space, and in CSV files by a comma. When values of the same kind are listed, as in "<...> ...", they are listed with that separator.
- Integers are written in base 10 without extra leading zeros, with a leading - for negative numbers.
- Unless specified otherwise, a real number is rounded to 4 decimal places and written in fixed-point notation with exactly 4 digits after the decimal point (example: 12.5 is written as 12.5000). A value specified with 2 decimal places is likewise written with exactly 2 digits after the decimal point. Exponent notation is not used, and 0 is written when the integer part is 0. Negative numbers have a leading -, but a value whose rounded result is 0 is written without - (example: -0.00004 is written as 0.0000).
- A fraction is written in the form <numerator>/<denominator> as a reduced fraction (with a positive denominator). A negative fraction has - on its numerator; 0 is written as 0/1 and 1 as 1/1.
- Teams are written as their team IDs.

* output/standings.csv

[Source files]
Split the program into the following seven source files (file names follow each language's conventions).
- main: handles the overall flow and most of the processing: calling each module, the team records and the standings (head-to-head tie-breaking), recent form, streaks, close matches and longest streaks, the winning-percentage-based indicators and the Pythagorean winning percentage, the comparison values, the ranks in each model and the rank totals, the round-by-round progression, leader changes and trajectory extremes, upsets, the head-to-head table, the rank correlation matrices and the most similar pairs, refitting per validation round, features, calling the link function fitting, prediction and the Massey margin error, evaluation (log loss, Brier score, skill, accuracy, rankings, paired comparison, per round, calibration table), the Elo grid search, the simulation of the remaining matches, and assembling the contents of the seven output files.
- season: reads the three input files and returns the sequence of team IDs and names, the sequence of matches in match order, and the settings. It also handles determining the result y of a match and extracting the matches before a given round.
- elo: handles the expected value, the multiplier and the Elo rating processing (recording each match's pre-match expected value and change, and the ratings after processing).
- linear_models: handles the Colley rating (building the matrix and solving it by Cholesky decomposition) and the Massey rating (building the matrix and Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting).
- iterative_models: handles the MM algorithm of the Bradley-Terry model and the iteration of the offense-defense ratings.
- evaluation: handles sigma, link function fitting by Newton's method, log loss, Brier score, the correctness score, average ranks, Kendall's tau and Spearman's rho.
- report: handles real number formatting (number of decimals, rounding, treatment of -0), fraction formatting, d-decimal values, and writing a sequence of lines to a file.

[Constraints]
- 4 <= n <= 30 (n is even), 2 <= R <= 40, and the number of matches is at most 600. The given data has n = 22, R = 30, 330 matches and h = 25 (66 validation matches), and the number of simulations is S = 2000.
- The input files correctly follow the formats above. There is no need to check for input errors.
- For the collection of all matches and for the training matches of every validation round, the following hold. Every team has at least one win and at least one loss, and there is at least one home win. The graph with the teams as vertices and the matches as edges is connected. The argument of sqrt in the Cholesky decomposition is positive, the pivot A[k][k] in Gaussian elimination is nonzero, and det in Newton's method is positive. Newton's method converges sufficiently within N_link updates. Every team's total scored and total conceded are positive.
- The sd of the paired comparison is never 0. Every p is greater than 0 and less than 1.
- The numerators and denominators of fractions, and the integers that appear in their computation, are less than 2^53 (in the given data, the denominators of the winning-percentage-based indicators are at most 216000; if fractions are reduced after every addition, the intermediate values also stay within this range). The random number computation 48271 * X_k is also less than 2^53.
- In the given data, every real number written to the output is sufficiently far from a rounding boundary, and so are the comparisons on d-decimal values. In addition, decisions such as the comparison of p with 1/2, floor(B * p), the comparison u < p and the pivot selection have enough margin that they do not change because of differences in the order of additions, fused multiply-add, or differences in the last digit (about 1 ulp) of the results of exp, ln and powers. Nevertheless, compute with the formulas and in the order defined in this statement.
- Bradley-Terry, the offense-defense ratings and Newton's method do not stop early on convergence; they update exactly the specified number of times.
