[Overview]
Write a program that reads time zone definitions written in the format of the time zone database (tz database) and computes conversions between local time and Coordinated Universal Time (UT) and the switches of daylight saving time (DST). It reads the 3 files in the input/ folder (definitions, a sequence of queries, settings) and checks each line of the definitions, reporting errors. For each correctly defined time zone, it derives, from the rules (Rule lines) and the zone lines (Zone lines and continuation lines), the sequence of "transitions" at which the difference from UT (the offset) changes. Using them, it answers the queries (conversion from local time to UT, conversion from UT to local time, conversion between two time zones, offset lookup, listing of transitions, elapsed time, and the time range where working hours overlap) and builds a summary of each time zone. The results are written to 4 files in the output/ folder; nothing is written to standard output.
All computations are done with integers (in seconds); floating-point numbers are not used.

[Input files]
Every input file consists only of printable ASCII characters (including the space), and every line ends with a newline (LF). There are no tab characters.

* input/tzdata.txt (time zone definitions)
Each line is given a line number, starting from 1 at the beginning of the file. Line numbers also count blank lines and comment-only lines.
For each line, remove as a comment everything from the first "#" to the end of the line, and then remove trailing spaces. Lines for which nothing remains are ignored. Below, the string after this processing is called the "line content", and only lines that are not ignored are considered.
Each part obtained by splitting the line content at runs of one or more spaces is called a "field" (leading spaces do not create a field). The kind of a line is determined as follows.
- A line whose line content begins with a space is a "continuation line".
- Otherwise, a line whose first field is Rule, Zone or Link is a "Rule line", "Zone line" or "Link line", respectively.
- Any other line is an "unknown line".
The fields of each kind of line are as follows (<...> is the name of a field).
- Rule line: the 10 fields "Rule <NAME> <FROM> <TO> <TYPE> <IN> <ON> <AT> <SAVE> <LETTER>". It is one rule belonging to the collection of rules named NAME (below, a "rule set"), and means that every year from year FROM to year TO, on day ON of month IN at time AT, the DST save amount becomes SAVE and the letter used in abbreviations becomes LETTER. TYPE is always written as "-".
- Zone line: 5 to 9 fields "Zone <NAME> <STDOFF> <RULES> <FORMAT> <UNTIL>" (UNTIL is 0 to 4 fields). It is the first zone line of the time zone (below, "zone") named NAME.
- Continuation line: 3 to 7 fields "<STDOFF> <RULES> <FORMAT> <UNTIL>". It is the next zone line of the same zone, following the preceding zone line.
- Link line: the 3 fields "Link <TARGET> <ALIAS>". It defines ALIAS as an alias of zone TARGET.
Zone lines and continuation lines are together called "zone lines". A zone line means that, until the date-time UNTIL, the zone uses the standard offset (the difference of standard time from UT) STDOFF, applies daylight saving time according to RULES, and builds abbreviations from FORMAT. A line without UNTIL is the last line of that zone and is in effect from then on forever. The correct syntax of each field is defined in [Processing].
Example:
Rule    US    2007  max   -  Mar  Sun>=8   2:00   1:00  D
Zone America/New_York  -4:56:02 -   LMT   1883 Nov 18 12:03:58
                       -5:00    US  E%sT

* input/queries.txt (sequence of queries)
One query is written per line. Each line consists of words separated by single spaces, with no leading or trailing spaces. The first word is the command, and each line has one of the following forms.
- TOUTC <zone> <local> (may be followed by the word EARLIER or LATER)
- TOLOCAL <zone> <UT>
- CONVERT <zone> <zone> <local> (may be followed by the word EARLIER or LATER)
- OFFSET <zone> <UT>
- TRANSITIONS <zone> <year> <year>
- DURATION <zone> <local> <local>
- MEETING <date> <zone> ... (one or more <zone>)
<zone> is where a zone name or an alias is written; it is a string without spaces. <local> has the form "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS", <UT> is that form followed by "Z", <date> has the form "YYYY-MM-DD", and <year> is 4 digits. YYYY is 4 digits, and each of the others, MM, DD, HH, MM, SS, is 2 digits; "-", ":", "T" and "Z" are those characters themselves. These forms (the numbers of digits and the positions of the separators) are always respected, but the values are not necessarily valid.
Example:
TOUTC America/New_York 2025-11-02T01:30:00 EARLIER
MEETING 2025-03-12 Europe/London America/New_York

* input/params.txt (settings)
It consists of the following 2 lines.
- Line 1: REFERENCE and the reference instant (in the <UT> form), separated by a single space.
- Line 2: WORKDAY and the start time and end time of work (both in the form "HH:MM"), separated by single spaces.
The value of the reference instant is valid (the year is at least 1800 and at most 2099), and in the working times HH is 00 to 23 and MM is 00 to 59, with start < end.
Example:
REFERENCE 2025-07-01T12:00:00Z
WORKDAY 09:00 17:00

[Processing]
* Common notation
- floor(x) is the largest integer not greater than x (floor(-4.5) = -5). a mod b (b > 0) is a - b * floor(a / b), which is always at least 0 and less than b.
- The calendar is the Gregorian calendar, applied with the same rules also to years before 1582. A year y is a leap year when y is divisible by 4 and not divisible by 100, or divisible by 400. The number of days in each month is 31 for months 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12; 30 for months 4, 6, 9, 11; and for month 2, 29 in a leap year and 28 otherwise. "The number of days in a common year" means the number of days of each month with February taken as 28 days.
- The month names are Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec (months 1 to 12 in order), and the weekday names are Sun, Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat (Sunday to Saturday in order).
- The day number D(y, m, d) is the integer that is 0 for January 1, 1970 and increases by 1 for each day forward (days before that are negative). The weekday of the day with day number n is (n + 4) mod 7, where 0 is Sunday, 1 is Monday, ..., 6 is Saturday.
- All lengths of time (offsets, save amounts, etc.) and times are handled as integers in seconds. The "time value" of year y, month m, day d, h hours, mi minutes, s seconds is D(y, m, d) * 86400 + h * 3600 + mi * 60 + s. Conversely, the date of time value t is the date of the day with day number floor(t / 86400), and its hours, minutes and seconds are obtained by splitting t mod 86400 seconds into hours, minutes and seconds. The time value of a UT date-time is called a "UT time value", and the time value of a local date-time is called a "local time value". When the offset is f, local time value = UT time value + f.
- -infinity and +infinity denote a value smaller than every time value and a value larger than every time value. The half-open interval [x, y) is the set of time values that are at least x and less than y.
- In the forms and result templates of this statement, a part enclosed in < and > (such as <word> or <STATE>) stands for the value it describes; the enclosing < and > themselves are not written.

* Syntax and values of fields
- HMS: one of the forms "H", "H:MM", "H:MM:SS". H is 1 or 2 digits, and MM and SS are exactly 2 digits, both at most 59. The value is H * 3600 + MM * 60 + SS seconds (a missing part is 0).
- Year (FROM, and the year of UNTIL): 4 digits whose value is at least 1800 and at most 2099.
- TO: only (the same year as FROM), max (the year 2099), or 4 digits whose value is at least FROM and at most 2099.
- TYPE: "-".
- Month (IN, and the month of UNTIL): one of the month names.
- Day specification (ON, and the day of UNTIL): one of the following 4 forms. N is 1 or 2 digits, must not begin with 0, and its value must be at least 1 and at most the number of days of that month in a common year. Xxx is a weekday name.
  - N: day N of the month.
  - lastXxx: the last Xxx of the month.
  - Xxx>=N: the first Xxx on or after day N of the month (if day N is an Xxx, that day; it may fall in the next month).
  - Xxx<=N: the last Xxx on or before day N of the month (if day N is an Xxx, that day; it may fall in the previous month).
  The value of a day specification for year y, month m is the day number of that day. Weekdays are computed with the day-number formula above.
- Time (AT, and the time of UNTIL): an HMS followed by one character w, s or u (the time type), or an HMS alone (the type is w). The value must be at most 86400 seconds (24:00:00). Type w denotes wall clock time (local time including the standard offset and the save amount in effect at that time), s denotes standard time (local time including only the standard offset), and u denotes UT.
- SAVE (save amount): an HMS of the form "H" or "H:MM" whose value is at most 7200 seconds (2:00).
- LETTER: "-", or 1 to 5 uppercase letters (A to Z). "-" denotes the empty string.
- STDOFF (standard offset): an HMS, or "-" followed by an HMS (the value is then negative), whose absolute value is less than 57600 seconds (16:00:00).
- RULES: if "-", no daylight saving time (save amount 0); if it begins with a digit, a fixed save amount written in the same syntax as SAVE; otherwise, the name of a rule set. A fixed save amount that is not in the SAVE syntax is incorrect.
- FORMAT (abbreviation format): consists only of letters, digits, "+", "-", "/" and "%"; every "%" is immediately followed by "s" (that is, it appears as "%s"); there is at most one "%s" and at most one "/"; it does not contain both "%s" and "/"; and if it contains "/", the parts before and after it are both non-empty. Furthermore, on a line whose RULES is not the name of a rule set, it must not contain "%s".
- UNTIL: 1 to 4 fields, which are in order "year", "month", "day specification", "time". Trailing fields may be omitted; an omitted month is taken as Jan, an omitted day as 1, and an omitted time as 0:00 (type w).
- The syntax of NAME, TARGET and ALIAS is not checked (see [Constraints]).

* Blocks
To group zone lines by zone, look at the non-ignored lines in file order and build "blocks" as follows.
- A Zone line starts a new block.
- A continuation line is appended to the end of a block when the immediately preceding line is the last line of some block and that line is either "a Zone line with 6 or more fields" or "a continuation line with 4 or more fields" (it does not matter whether that line has an error). Any other continuation line belongs to no block.
- Rule lines, Link lines and unknown lines are not added to blocks.
Therefore the first line of a block is always a Zone line.

* Error checks
The errors of each line are checked in the order (1) to (3) below, and only the first one found is the error of that line (a line has at most 1 error). Errors found in (1) and (2) are called "field errors", and errors found in (3) are called "structural errors".
(1) If any of the following applies, the error is syntax error.
  - An unknown line.
  - A continuation line that belongs to no block.
  - A line whose number of fields is not 10 for a Rule line, 5 to 9 for a Zone line, 3 to 7 for a continuation line, or 3 for a Link line.
(2) Check the fields from front to back; the first field that is not in the correct syntax determines the error as follows.
  - Rule line: FROM and TO give bad year, TYPE gives syntax error, IN gives bad month, ON gives bad day spec (judged for the month IN), AT gives bad time, SAVE gives bad save, LETTER gives bad letter.
  - Zone line (from the 3rd field on) and continuation line: STDOFF gives bad offset, RULES (when it is a fixed save amount) gives bad save, FORMAT gives bad format, the year of UNTIL gives bad year, the month gives bad month, the day specification gives bad day spec (judged for the month of UNTIL), and the time gives bad time.
  - Link lines have no errors of kind (2).
(3) For Zone lines and continuation lines without a field error, check the following in order.
  - For a Zone line, if there is an earlier Zone line with the same NAME and without a field error, the error is duplicate zone NAME.
  - If RULES is the name of a rule set and no Rule line without a field error has that name as its NAME, the error is unknown rule <name> (<name> is the RULES field).
  - For a continuation line that has UNTIL, if the immediately preceding line in the block has no field error and the UNTIL value of this line is less than or equal to the UNTIL value of the immediately preceding line, the error is until not increasing. Here the UNTIL value is, ignoring the time type, (the value of the day specification for the year and month of UNTIL) * 86400 + the value of the time.
  - If the line has UNTIL and is the last line of the block, the error is missing continuation.
Link lines are checked in file order after the checks of all other lines are finished and the "defined zones" below have been determined. For a Link line without a field error: if TARGET is not the name of a defined zone, the error is unknown link target TARGET; otherwise, if ALIAS is the name of a defined zone or is equal to the ALIAS of an earlier Link line without an error, the error is duplicate link ALIAS. If neither applies, the Link line has no error and ALIAS becomes an "alias" of TARGET.

* Defined zones
If no line of a block has an error (neither a field error nor a structural error), the block is a "defined zone", and its name is the NAME of its first Zone line. Two defined zones with the same name never arise. In queries, a zone is specified by the name or an alias of a defined zone. An alias denotes the zone of its TARGET.
Below, the "rules" of rule set X are the Rule lines without an error whose NAME is X. Only a NAME that has at least one rule is a rule set: a NAME that appears only on Rule lines with an error is not a rule set (it is listed neither in "unused rules" nor in the "rule set" lines of zone_summary.txt).

* Rule instances
A pair of a rule of a rule set and a year y from its FROM year to its TO year is called a "rule instance". The nominal time of a rule instance is (the value of ON for year y, month IN) * 86400 + the value of AT; its type is the type of AT, its save amount is the value of SAVE, and its letter is LETTER. All rule instances of a rule set, sorted by nominal time in ascending order (ties broken by the line number of the Rule line, smaller first), are called the "instance sequence" of that rule set.

* Periods and transitions of a zone
For a time type c, a standard offset o and a save amount v, the correction adj(c, o, v) is 0 if c is u, o if c is s, and o + v if c is w. The value of a time value V written in type c, converted to a UT time value, is V - adj(c, o, v).
Let the zone lines of a defined zone, in block order, be L1, L2, ..., Ln. The valid range of each line Li is the half-open interval [Si, Ei), where S1 = -infinity, En = +infinity, and S(i+1) = Ei for i < n. Letting o be the standard offset of Li, U the UNTIL value when UNTIL exists, and c the time type of UNTIL, compute Ei and the "switch sequence" for each Li as follows. A switch is a triple of "start time, save amount, letter".
(A) A line whose RULES is "-" or a fixed save amount: let the save amount v be 0 for "-", or the fixed save amount's value. If UNTIL exists, Ei = U - adj(c, o, v). The switch sequence consists of the single switch (Si, v, empty string).
(B) A line whose RULES is the name of a rule set: process the instance sequence of that rule set from the beginning. Starting the "current save amount" r at 0, do the following steps 1 to 4 for each instance (nominal time N, type c', save amount v').
  1. Let T = N - adj(c', o, r) be the UT time value of that instance.
  2. If UNTIL exists and T >= U - adj(c, o, r), stop processing here (this instance and all later instances are not used).
  3. If T <= Si, this instance is a "pre-start instance"; otherwise it is an "applied instance".
  4. Set r to v'.
  When the last instance has been processed or processing has stopped, if UNTIL exists, Ei = U - adj(c, o, r) (r is its value at that point).
  If there are pre-start instances, the save amount and letter of the last one are the "starting save amount and letter". If there are no pre-start instances, the starting save amount is 0, and the starting letter is the letter of the first instance whose save amount is 0 in the whole instance sequence (regardless of stopping) (the empty string if there is no such instance).
  The switch sequence is (Si, starting save amount, starting letter) followed by (T, save amount, letter) of each applied instance, in processing order.
A switch (t, v, l) of Li means that from time t on, the state has offset o + v, is DST if v != 0 and standard time if v = 0, and has the following abbreviation. If FORMAT contains "/", the abbreviation is the part before the "/" when v = 0, and the part after the "/" otherwise. If it does not contain "/", it is FORMAT with "%s" replaced by l (FORMAT itself if there is no "%s").
Concatenate the switch sequences of L1 to Ln in this order, look at them from front to back, and remove every switch whose represented state (all 3 of offset, whether DST, abbreviation) is identical to that of the switch kept immediately before it. Call the remaining switches, in order, "periods" P0, P1, ..., Pk; the start time of each is the start time of the period, and the state it represents is the "state" of the period (offset, whether DST, abbreviation) (the start time of P0 is -infinity). The valid range of period Pj is [start time of Pj, start time of P(j+1)) (for Pk, +infinity is used instead of the start time of P(j+1)). The period of a UT time value t is the period whose valid range contains t.
For j >= 1, the change from P(j-1) to Pj is called a "transition", and the start time of Pj is the time of the transition. The "period before" a transition is P(j-1), and the "period after" is Pj.
The "number of times applied" of each rule is the number of times an instance of that rule became an applied instance in processing (B) of zone lines (used in the zone summary).

* Resolving local times
To "resolve" a local time value L of a zone, find all periods Pj whose valid range contains L - (offset of Pj) (the candidates). The UT time value of a candidate is L - (offset of that period).
- If there is 1 candidate, L is unique and is determined as the UT time value of that candidate.
- If there are 2 candidates, L is ambiguous (the same local time occurs twice). The one with the earlier UT time value is called the "earlier candidate", and the one with the later UT time value the "later candidate".
- If there are no candidates, L does not exist (the local time is skipped). In this case there is exactly one transition satisfying (time of the transition + offset of the period before) <= L < (time of the transition + offset of the period after); it is called "the transition that skipped L".
By [Constraints], there are never 3 or more candidates.

* Checking queries
The arguments of each query (the words after the command, excluding a final EARLIER or LATER of TOUTC and CONVERT) are checked from front to back, and the first error found determines the result.
- If <zone> is neither the name nor an alias of a defined zone, the result is "ERROR unknown zone <word>".
- If the value of <local>, <UT>, <date> or <year> is not valid, the result is "ERROR invalid value <word>". A valid value means the year is at least 1800 and at most 2099, the month is 1 to 12, the day is at least 1 and at most the number of days in that month, the hour is 0 to 23, and the minute and second are 0 to 59 (for <date> only the year, month and day are checked, and for <year> only the year).
- If the arguments have no error and, for TRANSITIONS, the first year > the second year, the result is "ERROR invalid range".
Here <word> is the string exactly as written (for <UT> it includes the trailing Z).

* Query results
The results of queries without errors are as follows. How date-times, offsets, durations, STATE and so on are written is defined in "Formatting rules" in [Output files]. Times within a result are written in the local date-time format if they are local time values, and in the UT format if they are UT time values.
- TOUTC: resolve the local time value of <local>. If unique, the result is "<UT time> (<STATE>)", where <UT time> is the UT time value of the candidate and <STATE> is the state of the candidate's period. If ambiguous and EARLIER is given, write the earlier candidate, and if LATER is given, the later candidate, in the same form as in the unique case. If ambiguous and neither is given, the result is "AMBIGUOUS <UT time of the earlier candidate> (<STATE of the earlier candidate>) or <UT time of the later candidate> (<STATE of the later candidate>)". If it does not exist (even if EARLIER or LATER is given), the result is "NONEXISTENT gap A to B at C", where A and B are (time of the skipping transition + offset of the period before) and (time of that transition + offset of the period after), each written as a local time value, and C is the time of that transition. In the unique case, EARLIER and LATER have no effect.
- TOLOCAL: letting P be the period of the UT time value t of <UT>, the result is "<local date-time> (<STATE>)". The local time value is t + offset of P, and <STATE> is the state of P.
- CONVERT: resolve <local> in the first zone in the same way as TOUTC. If it becomes AMBIGUOUS or NONEXISTENT, the result is the same string as for TOUTC. If a UT time value t is determined, letting P be the period of t in the second zone, the result is "<local date-time> (<STATE>) at <UT time>, day shift K". The local time value is t + offset of P, <UT time> is t, and K is floor((local time value in the second zone) / 86400) - floor((local time value of <local>) / 86400); if positive, it is prefixed with "+", and 0 is written as "0".
- OFFSET: letting Pj be the period of the UT time value of <UT>, write the STATE of Pj, and append, in this order, " since X" where X is the start time of Pj if j >= 1, and " until Y" where Y is the start time of P(j+1) if P(j+1) exists.
- TRANSITIONS: with the years y1 and y2, select in time order all transitions whose time is at least D(y1, 1, 1) * 86400 and less than D(y2 + 1, 1, 1) * 86400; the result is "transitions: <number of selected transitions>". The selected transitions are also written to transitions.txt.
- DURATION: resolve the local time values of the first and second <local>, in this order (EARLIER and LATER are not used). If any is not unique, then for the first such one the result is "AMBIGUOUS <word>" if ambiguous, or "NONEXISTENT <word>" if it does not exist (<word> is the <local> as written). If both are unique, letting t1, t2 be the UT time values, f1, f2 the offsets of the candidates' periods, and L1, L2 the local time values, the result is "E s (P), wall clock W, offset change C". E is the value of t2 - t1 (an integer), P is t2 - t1 written in the duration format, W is L2 - L1 written in the duration format, and C is f2 - f1 written in the offset format.
- MEETING: the string of "Working hours overlap" in the next section.

* Working hours overlap
Let n be the day number of <date>, and a, b the start and end working times converted to seconds (HH * 3600 + MM * 60). The "working hours" of a zone is the set of UT time values whose local time value is at least n * 86400 + a and less than n * 86400 + b. That is, for each period Pj, take the intersection of [n * 86400 + a - (offset of Pj), n * 86400 + b - (offset of Pj)) and the valid range of Pj, and the set is the union of these over all periods.
Divide the intersection of the working hours of all zones written in MEETING into a sequence of half-open intervals [x, y) that neither overlap nor touch each other, and sort them by x in ascending order. Write each interval in the form "X to Y (<duration>) [<zone list>]", and the result is these joined with "; ". X and Y are x and y in the UT format, and <duration> is y - x in the duration format. <zone list> is, for each <zone> in the order written in MEETING (as many times as it is written, if the same one is written more than once), "<word> <local date-time> <abbreviation>", joined with ", ". Here, letting P be the period of the UT time value x in that zone, the local time value is x + offset of P, and <abbreviation> is the abbreviation of P. If the intersection is empty, the result is NONE.

[Output files]
Every line of every file ends with a newline (LF), with no trailing spaces. In a file that lists "blocks", exactly one blank line is placed between consecutive blocks (none before the first block or after the last block). A file with no lines to write is empty (0 bytes). Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, and negative numbers are prefixed with "-" (except where a number of digits is specified).

* Formatting rules
- Local date-time format: write the date-time of time value t in the form "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS". The year is 4 digits and the month, day, hour, minute and second are 2 digits, padded with leading zeros. Example: 2021-03-28T02:00:00
- UT format: write the time value in the local date-time format followed by "Z". Example: 2021-03-28T01:00:00Z
- Offset format: write "+" if the value f is 0 or more and "-" if it is negative, followed by the hours of |f| (2 digits), ":", and the minutes (2 digits). If the seconds of |f| are not 0, further append ":" and the seconds (2 digits). If the hours have fewer than 2 digits, pad with a leading zero. Example: +00:00, -04:56:02, +05:30
- Duration format: write "-" if the value d is negative, followed by the hours of |d| (no specified number of digits), ":", the minutes (2 digits), ":", and the seconds (2 digits). Example: 23:00:00, -49:00:00, 0:30:00, 8784:00:00
- Clock format: write a value v of 0 or more in the form hours (no specified number of digits), ":", minutes (2 digits), and if the seconds are not 0, further append ":" and the seconds (2 digits). Example: 2:00, 24:00, 0:00, 12:03:58
- STATE: write the state of a period in the form "<offset> <kind> <abbreviation>". <offset> is in the offset format, and <kind> is DST for DST and STD for standard time. Example: +02:00 DST CEST
- CHANGE: write a transition in the form "<time of the transition> <STATE of the period before> -> <STATE of the period after>" (the time of the transition is in the UT format). Example: 2021-03-28T01:00:00Z +01:00 STD CET -> +02:00 DST CEST
- SHIFTS: for a collection of transitions, letting G be the number of those for which (offset of the period after) - (offset of the period before) is positive, O the number for which it is negative, and N the number for which it is 0, write "gaps G, overlaps O, no shift N".

* output/answers.txt
Write one line per query, in query order. Each line is the query line itself, " => ", and the result, concatenated in this order.
Example: TOUTC Example/Alias 2021-10-31T02:30:00 LATER => 2021-10-31T01:30:00Z (+01:00 STD CET)

* output/transitions.txt
Write one block per TRANSITIONS query whose result is not ERROR, in query order. The first line of the block is the query line itself, followed by one line per selected transition in time order, and the last line is the SHIFTS of the selected transitions. A transition line is "CHANGE (local A -> B, <type>)", where A and B are the time of the transition plus the offset of the period before and plus the offset of the period after, respectively, written in the local date-time format. With d = (offset of the period after) - (offset of the period before), <type> is "gap <d in the duration format>" if d > 0, "overlap <-d in the duration format>" if d < 0, and "no shift" if d = 0.
Example: 2021-06-30T16:30:00Z +09:30 STD +0930 -> +10:00 STD AEST (local 2021-07-01T02:00:00 -> 2021-07-01T02:30:00, gap 0:30:00)

* output/zone_summary.txt
First write the database summary block, then one block per defined zone, in character code order of the zone names (lexicographic order comparing ASCII values one character at a time). In both kinds of blocks, the second and later lines are prefixed with 2 spaces.
The database summary block consists of the following lines in this order.
- DATABASE
- rule lines: V valid, I invalid (V is the number of Rule lines without an error, I the number of Rule lines with an error)
- zones: D defined, I invalid (D is the number of defined zones, I is the number of blocks minus D)
- zone lines: N in defined zones (N is the total number of zone lines of the defined zones)
- links: D defined, I invalid (D is the number of Link lines without an error, I the number of Link lines with an error)
- errors: E (E is the number of lines of errors.txt)
- unused rules: <name list> (the names of the rule sets that appear in the RULES of no zone line of any defined zone, in order of the line number of the first rule of the rule set, separated by single spaces; none if there are none)
- invalid zones: <list> (for each block that did not become a defined zone, "<NAME of the first Zone line> (line <its line number>)", joined with ", " in block order; none if there are none)
- rule set <name>: lines L, years A-B, zones Z (one line per rule set, in order of the line number of the first rule. L is the number of rules; A is the minimum of FROM; B is max if there is a rule whose TO is max, otherwise the maximum of the TO years (only counts as the year of FROM); Z is the number of defined zones that have a zone line whose RULES is that name)
A zone block consists of the following lines in this order.
- ZONE <zone name>
- aliases: <alias list> (the ALIASes of the Link lines without an error whose TARGET is this zone, in Link line order, separated by single spaces; none if there are none)
- One line for each zone line Li, in order of i: "line i: STDOFF RULES FORMAT", followed by " from X" where X is Si in the UT format if i >= 2, and " until <UNTIL notation> = Y" where Y is Ei in the UT format if UNTIL exists, and finally ", transitions M". STDOFF is in the offset format; RULES is "-", the fixed save amount in the clock format, or the name of the rule set; FORMAT is as written. The UNTIL notation is the year, the month (Jan if omitted), the day specification (1 if omitted) and the time (the clock format immediately followed by the type character; 0:00w if omitted), separated by single spaces; the year, month and day specification are as written. M is the number of transitions whose time is at least Si and less than Ei.
- For each rule set appearing in the RULES of this zone's zone lines (in order of first appearance), one line for each of its rules in line-number order: "rule <name> FROM TO IN ON AT SAVE LETTER, applied K". FROM, TO, IN, ON and LETTER are as written; AT is the clock format immediately followed by the type character; SAVE is in the clock format. K is the total number of times that rule was applied, over all zone lines of this zone.
- offset range: <minimum> to <maximum> (the minimum and maximum of the offsets of all periods, in the offset format)
- For each distinct STATE of the periods, in order of first appearance of a period with that STATE, one line: "state STATE, periods N" (N is the number of periods with that STATE)
- reference: <local date-time> (<STATE>), line K (letting P be the period of the reference instant, the local time value is the reference instant + offset of P, <STATE> is the state of P, and K is the i such that Si <= reference instant < Ei)
- previous transition: the CHANGE of the last transition whose time is less than or equal to the reference instant (none if there is none)
- next transition: the CHANGE of the first transition whose time is greater than the reference instant (none if there is none)
- transitions: N, SHIFTS (N is the number of all transitions, and SHIFTS is over all transitions)
- dst years: A-B (the minimum A and maximum B of the UT years of the start times of the DST periods. If there are no DST periods, "dst years: none")
- Letting Y0 be the UT year of the reference instant, take the union of the intersections of [D(Y0, 1, 1) * 86400, D(Y0 + 1, 1, 1) * 86400) with the valid ranges of the DST periods, divide it into a sequence of half-open intervals [x, y) that neither overlap nor touch each other, and write one line per interval in ascending order of x: "dst period: X to Y (<duration>)" (X and Y are x and y in the UT format, and <duration> is y - x in the duration format). If there is no such interval, there is no such line.
- dst total <year>: <duration> (<year> is Y0, and <duration> is the sum of y - x over the intervals above, in the duration format; 0:00:00 if there are no intervals)

* output/errors.txt
Write one line per line with an error, in ascending order of line number. Each line is "line <line number>: <error>", where <error> is one of the following strings. In the parts NAME, <name>, TARGET and ALIAS, write the corresponding field as is.
syntax error, bad year, bad month, bad day spec, bad time, bad save, bad offset, bad letter, bad format, duplicate zone NAME, unknown rule <name>, until not increasing, missing continuation, unknown link target TARGET, duplicate link ALIAS

[Source files]
Use the following 6 source files (the extension follows the language).
- main: holds the overall flow: reading the 3 input files, determining line kinds and assembling blocks, the order of the field checks for each line kind and the structural error checks, registering defined zones and aliases, assembling the period sequence of each zone (the starting save amount and letter per zone line, switch sequences, merging periods, recording valid ranges and numbers of times applied), checking queries and processing the 7 commands (the result strings of local time resolution, day shift, since and until of OFFSET, transition lines and SHIFTS, DURATION values, working hour sets and their intersection), assembling the database summary and the zone summaries (including the aggregation of DST periods), and writing the 4 output files. Most of the processing is placed in this file.
- tz_parser: extracting the line content (removing comments and trailing spaces, detecting continuation lines, splitting into fields), and judging the syntax of and reading the values of each field (HMS, year, TO, month, day specification, time, SAVE, LETTER, STDOFF, FORMAT, UNTIL).
- day_rules: month names and weekday names, leap years and days per month, conversion between day numbers and dates, weekdays, computing the value of a day specification, conversion between time values and year/month/day/hour/minute/second.
- transitions: expanding rule instances and sorting the instance sequence, the correction adj, processing the instance sequence for one zone line (computing UT time values, distinguishing pre-start and applied instances, stopping and computing Ei), building abbreviations.
- resolver: finding the period of a UT time value (binary search), valid ranges of periods, enumerating candidates for a local time value, finding the transition that skipped a local time value.
- report: building the strings of the local date-time, UT, offset, duration and clock formats and of STATE and CHANGE, and writing sequences of lines and sequences of blocks to files.

[Constraints]
- tzdata.txt has at most 400 lines and queries.txt at most 500 lines, and each line has at most 200 characters.
- NAME, TARGET and ALIAS begin with a letter and consist only of letters, digits, "/", "_", "-" and "+". If a RULES field is neither "-" nor a string beginning with a digit, it has the same form as NAME. A Zone line has at least the NAME field.
- For defined zones the following is guaranteed. On each zone line, Si < Ei. The start times of the periods are strictly increasing, the difference between the times of adjacent transitions is greater than 2 days (172800 seconds), and the absolute values of the period offsets are less than 16 hours. The first period P0 is not DST. Therefore a local time value has at most 2 candidates, and when there are 2, they are two adjacent periods. The abbreviation of every period is non-empty.
- Time values range from about -5.4 * 10^9 to 4.2 * 10^9, so 32-bit integers are not sufficient. The absolute values of all intermediate integers are less than 2^53.
