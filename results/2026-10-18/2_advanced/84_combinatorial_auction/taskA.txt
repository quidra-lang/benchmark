[Overview]
Write an "auction laboratory" program that runs several auction formats, centered on combinatorial auctions, on the same bid data and compares them. All money amounts are integers, and ratios and similar quantities are handled exactly as reduced fractions.
The program reads the list of items and reserve prices input/items.txt, the combinatorial auction bids input/bids.txt, the data for single-item auctions and ad slot auctions input/single_item.txt, and the parameters input/params.txt.
In a combinatorial auction, bidders bid on sets of items (bundles) with XOR bids or OR bids. After validating the bids, the program exactly computes, by depth-first branch and bound, the allocation that maximizes social welfare subject to each item being sold at most once (the winner determination problem). It also computes an approximate allocation by a greedy method together with critical payments, VCG payments, a check of whether an outcome belongs to the core (whether no group of bidders can overturn the outcome by teaming up with the seller), and a simulation of a simultaneous ascending auction with straightforward bidders.
For single-item auctions, it runs, on the same valuations, a first-price sealed-bid auction, a second-price sealed-bid auction, an English auction (ascending clock), a Dutch auction (descending clock), and an all-pay auction. For ad slot auctions, it runs generalized second price (GSP) and VCG, and checks whether the submitted GSP bids are locally envy-free, whether they are a Nash equilibrium, and whether they are a locally envy-free equilibrium, which satisfies both.
The results are written to 6 files in the output/ folder. Nothing is written to standard output. Random numbers come only from the generator defined in [Processing], and no real (floating-point) numbers are used, so the results are uniquely determined.

[Input files]
There are 4 input files: input/items.txt, input/bids.txt, input/single_item.txt, input/params.txt. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line (including the last line) ends with a newline (LF). There are no blank lines, the tokens on a line are separated by single spaces, and there are no spaces at the beginning or end of a line.
Below, a "name" is a string of one or more characters consisting only of lowercase English letters (a to z), and an "integer" is a non-negative integer written in decimal without extra leading zeros.
In the formats below, <...> denotes a part where a value goes.

== input/items.txt (items) ==
Each line represents one item and has the following format.
  <item name> <reserve price>
The item name is a name, and it differs from line to line. The reserve price is an integer of at least 1. The order of the lines of this file is called the "item order".

== input/bids.txt (combinatorial auction bids) ==
It consists of the following 2 kinds of lines, and the first line is an auction line.
(1) auction line
  auction <auction name> <item name> <item name> ...
  It marks the start of one auction, and the bidder lines that follow it (up to the next auction line) are the bidder lines of that auction. The list of item names gives the items sold in that auction (below, the offered items); there is at least one, all of them are in items.txt, there are no duplicates, and they are listed in item order. The auction name is a name, and it differs from line to line.
(2) bidder line
  bidder <bidder name> <bidding language> <bid> <bid> ...
  The bidder name is a name. The bidding language should be XOR or OR, but it may be wrong (it is a string of one or more characters without spaces). There is at least one bid; each is a string of one or more characters without spaces, and its correct form is item names joined by "+", followed by ":" and an amount. For example, "bos+nyc:330" is a bid that puts the amount 330 on the bundle of items bos and nyc. The item names inside a bid are not necessarily in item order. A bid string may be wrong (it is validated in [Processing] section 2).
Bid number: within each auction, all bids are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in order of appearance (in line order, and from left to right within a line). Bids with errors are numbered too.

== input/single_item.txt (single items and ad slots) ==
It consists of the following 4 kinds of lines, and the first line is a lot line or a slots line. A lot line and a slots line each mark the start of one "group", and the bidder lines or ad lines that follow it (up to the start of the next group) are the participant lines of that group.
(1) lot line (a group for a single-item auction)
  lot <group name> <reserve price>
  The reserve price is an integer of at least 1.
(2) bidder line (a participant of a lot group)
  bidder <participant name> <valuation> <sealed bid>
  The valuation and the sealed bid are integers of at least 1.
(3) slots line (a group for an ad slot auction)
  slots <group name> <click-through rate> <click-through rate> ...
  There is at least one click-through rate; from left to right they are the click-through rates of slot 1, slot 2, .... Each is a string of one or more characters without spaces; its correct form is a fraction of the form "<numerator>/<denominator>", but it may be wrong (it is validated in [Processing] section 10).
(4) ad line (a participant of a slots group)
  ad <participant name> <valuation> <bid>
  The valuation and the bid are amounts per click and are integers of at least 1.
Group names and participant names are names. Group names are all distinct from each other and also distinct from the auction names in bids.txt. Bidder lines appear only in lot groups, and ad lines appear only in slots groups. There may be groups with no participants and groups with several participants of the same name (they are validated in [Processing] sections 9 and 10).
The order of the groups in this file is called the "group order", and the order of the participant lines within a group is called the "participant order".

== input/params.txt (parameters) ==
The following 10 lines appear in this order.
  run <auction name> <auction name> ...
  max_bundle_size <integer>
  max_bids_per_bidder <integer>
  core_max_bidders <integer>
  saa_increment <integer>
  round_cap <integer>
  seed <integer>
  english_increment <integer>
  dutch_start <integer>
  dutch_decrement <integer>
- run: the names of the auctions to run, one or more, listed in the order in which they are run. All of them are in bids.txt and there are no duplicates. An auction that is in bids.txt but not in run is not processed. The order of run is called the "run order".
- max_bundle_size: the maximum number of items one bid may contain (section 2).
- max_bids_per_bidder: the maximum number of valid bids one bidder line may have (section 2).
- core_max_bidders: the maximum number of bidders for which the core check is performed (section 7).
- saa_increment, round_cap, seed: the price increment, the maximum number of rounds, and the random seed of the simultaneous ascending auction (section 8).
- english_increment: the clock increment of the English auction (section 9).
- dutch_start, dutch_decrement: the starting price and the decrement of the Dutch auction clock (section 9).

[Processing]
Process according to sections 1 to 10 below. Sections 2 to 8 are the processing of each auction in run order, and sections 9 and 10 are the processing of the groups of single_item.txt.

== 1. Terms and notation ==
(1) Fraction: a rational number written as a reduced fraction. If the denominator is 1, only the numerator is written (examples: "12", "-3", "0"); otherwise it is written "<numerator>/<denominator>" (examples: "1360/3", "-1/4"). The denominator is a positive integer, and a negative number has "-" on the numerator. Values described as "fraction" in [Output files] are written in this form. Integer values are written as they are in decimal (with "-" if negative).
(2) Bundle notation: the names of the items contained in a set of items (a bundle), in item order, joined by "+" (example: "nyc+bos+chi"). The empty bundle is "-". The output always uses this notation (it never uses the order inside a bid string).
(3) Number list notation: a set of bid numbers in increasing order joined by "+" (example: "1+6+9"). The empty set is "-".
(4) Lexicographic order: for integer sequences A and B, compare from the beginning; the one with the smaller value at the first differing position comes first (is smaller). If one is a prefix of the other (for example, A is 1, 6 and B is 1, 6, 9), the shorter one comes first.
(5) Bundle size: the number of items contained in the bundle. Bundle reserve: the sum of the reserve prices of the items contained in the bundle.

== 2. Bid validation ==
Examine the bidder lines of the auction in order.
(1) Line errors: for each bidder line, check the following in this order; the first one that applies is the error of that line.
  - The bidder name equals the bidder name of an earlier bidder line of the same auction (including lines with errors): duplicate bidder
  - The bidding language is neither XOR nor OR: bad language <bidding language>
  A line with a line error does not create a bidder, and every bid on that line gets this message as its error. Each line without a line error becomes one bidder. The order of the bidders is called the "bidder order"; a bidder whose bidding language is XOR is called an XOR bidder, and a bidder whose bidding language is OR is called an OR bidder.
(2) Bid errors: examine the bids of a line without a line error from left to right; the first of (a) to (f) below that applies is the error of that bid.
  (a) The bid is not in the correct form: malformed bid
    The correct form is: it contains exactly one ":", every part obtained by splitting the part before ":" at "+" consists of one or more lowercase English letters only (if there is an empty part, the form is not correct), and the part after ":" consists of 1 to 5 digits (0 to 9) only and, if it has 2 or more characters, does not start with 0. Each part obtained by splitting at "+" is called an item name, and the integer represented by the part after ":" is called the amount.
  (b) Examine the item names from left to right; for the first item name to which one of the following applies, that message: not in items.txt: unknown item <item name>; in items.txt but not an offered item of this auction: item not offered <item name>; the same item name appears to its left in the same bid: duplicate item <item name>
  (c) The number of item names is greater than max_bundle_size: bundle too large
  (d) The amount is 0: zero value
  (e) Its bundle is the same as the bundle of an earlier valid bid on the same line (regardless of the order of the item names): duplicate bundle <bid number of that valid bid>
  (f) The number of earlier valid bids on the same line is at least max_bids_per_bidder: too many bids
  A bid to which none of these applies is called a valid bid. A valid bid has a bid number, a bidder, a bundle, and an amount (the value the bidder puts on that bundle).
(3) Eligible bids: a valid bid whose amount is at least its bundle reserve is called an eligible bid, and a valid bid whose amount is less than its bundle reserve is called a below-reserve bid. Sections 4 to 7 use only the eligible bids, and section 8 uses all valid bids.

== 3. Allocation and social welfare ==
(1) Allocation: a set of eligible bids (possibly empty) that satisfies the following 2 conditions. The bids contained in an allocation are called winning bids.
  - No two winning bids share an item.
  - An XOR bidder has at most 1 winning bid (there is no limit on the number of winning bids of an OR bidder).
(2) Unsold: an offered item that is not contained in the bundle of any winning bid. The seller keeps the unsold items and regards the reserve price of such an item as the value of that item to the seller.
(3) Values of an allocation: accepted amount A (the sum of the amounts of the winning bids), retained amount R (the sum of the reserve prices of the unsold items), and social welfare W = A + R.
(4) Optimal allocation: among the allocations with maximum social welfare, the one whose list of winning bid numbers in increasing order is lexicographically smallest. Its social welfare is called the optimal welfare. The optimal allocation is computed by the branch and bound of section 4 (the procedure of section 4 always returns the optimal allocation).
(5) Winner: a bidder who has 1 or more winning bids in the optimal allocation.

== 4. Winner determination (branch and bound) ==
Given a set B of eligible bids, the following search computes the optimal allocation that uses only bids of B. Usually B is all eligible bids of the auction, but sections 6 and 7 use only some of the bids.
(1) State: a tuple of the set D of decided items, the set U of XOR bidders that have already won, the current social welfare w, and the set C of chosen bids. In the initial state D, U and C are empty and w = 0.
(2) Usable bids: for a state, the bids of B whose bundle contains no item of D and, if it is a bid of an XOR bidder, whose bidder is not in U.
(3) Shares and upper bound: for a state, for each offered item i not in D, determine the share s(i) and its source as follows. First set s(i) to the reserve price of i and the source to reserve. Then examine the usable bids whose bundle contains i in increasing order of bid number; if the fraction obtained by dividing the bid's amount by its bundle size is greater than s(i) (if equal, do not change it), set s(i) to that fraction and the source to the bid number of that bid. The upper bound of the state is the fraction obtained by adding to w the s(i) of all offered items not in D. Shares and the upper bound are computed exactly as fractions, and the upper bound and the incumbent's social welfare are also compared exactly (they can be exactly equal).
(4) Node processing: count the number of processed nodes, the number of pruned nodes, the number of leaves, and the number of improvements (all 0 initially). Initially there is no incumbent (a social welfare and a list of bid numbers). For a state, the following steps (i) to (v) are called "node processing".
  (i) Add 1 to the number of processed nodes.
  (ii) If D consists of all offered items, this node is a leaf. Add 1 to the number of leaves, and let L be the list of the bid numbers of C in increasing order. If there is no incumbent, or w is greater than the incumbent's social welfare, or w equals the incumbent's social welfare and L is lexicographically smaller than the incumbent's list, set the incumbent to w and L, add 1 to the number of improvements, and keep an improvement record (the number of processed nodes at that moment, w, L). In every case, finish the processing of this node.
  (iii) If there is an incumbent and the upper bound is less than the incumbent's social welfare (if equal, continue), add 1 to the number of pruned nodes and finish the processing of this node.
  (iv) Let j be the first offered item in item order that is not in D. Take the usable bids whose bundle contains j one at a time in increasing order of bid number, and for each, perform node processing on the state obtained by adding the items of that bid's bundle to D, adding the bidder to U if it is a bid of an XOR bidder, adding the bid's amount to w, and adding the bid to C.
  (v) Then perform node processing on the state obtained by adding j to D and adding the reserve price of j to w (the state in which j is left unsold).
  The node processing of (iv) and (v) is each performed on a new state made from the current state, and the current state is not changed (this is a depth-first search).
(5) Perform node processing on the initial state; when it finishes, the incumbent's social welfare is the optimal welfare, and its list gives the bid numbers of the winning bids of the optimal allocation. The shares and sources of (3) in the initial state are called the root shares, and the upper bound in the initial state is called the root bound.

== 5. Greedy method and critical payments ==
(1) Greedy order: the eligible bids listed in decreasing order of the value obtained by dividing the square of the amount by the bundle size. Bids with equal values are in increasing order of bid number. The values are compared exactly (for bids a and b, compare the square of a's amount * b's bundle size with the square of b's amount * a's bundle size).
(2) Greedy selection: examine a list of bids from the beginning, and select a bid if it shares no item with the bids selected so far and, if it is a bid of an XOR bidder, no bid of that bidder has been selected yet. The set of bids selected by applying this selection to the greedy order is called the greedy allocation (the greedy allocation is an allocation in the sense of section 3).
(3) Critical payment: for each winning bid b of the greedy allocation, apply the selection of (2) to the greedy order with b removed, and let c be the first selected bid (first in order of selection) that conflicts with b. b and c conflict if their bundles share an item, or if b is a bid of an XOR bidder and c is a bid of the same bidder. c is called the rival bid of b.
  - If there is a rival bid c: let t be the smallest positive integer that satisfies the following condition. "The square of t * the bundle size of c is greater than the square of c's amount * the bundle size of b. Or the two are equal and the bid number of b is less than the bid number of c." (This is the condition that, if the amount of b were changed to t, b would come before c in the greedy order.) The critical payment of b is the larger of t and the bundle reserve of b.
  - If there is no rival bid: the critical payment of b is the bundle reserve of b.

== 6. VCG payments ==
Let W be the optimal welfare. For each winner k, compute the following values.
  - Value of k, v_k: the sum of the amounts of k's winning bids
  - Welfare without k, W_k: the optimal welfare when the search of section 4 is performed with B being the set of eligible bids with all bids of k removed. The optimal allocation of that search is called k's alternative allocation.
  - VCG payment of k: p_k = W_k - (W - v_k)
  - VCG utility of k: u_k = v_k - p_k
The VCG payment and VCG utility of a bidder who is not a winner are 0. The VCG revenue is the sum of the VCG payments, and the VCG seller payoff is the sum of the VCG revenue and the retained amount of the optimal allocation.

== 7. Core check ==
Let n be the number of bidders. For an auction where n is greater than core_max_bidders, the check is not performed. If n is at most core_max_bidders, perform the following check.
(1) Coalition: a subset of the bidders (there are 2^n of them, including the empty set and the set of all bidders). The coalition order is increasing order of the number of bidders contained, and among coalitions with the same number, lexicographic order of the list of the positions of the contained bidders (counted 1, 2, ... in bidder order) in increasing order. For example, for n = 3 the order is the empty set, {1}, {2}, {3}, {1, 2}, {1, 3}, {2, 3}, {1, 2, 3}.
(2) Coalition welfare: for each coalition S, perform the search of section 4 with B being only the eligible bids of the bidders of S; its optimal welfare is V(S), and its optimal allocation is called the allocation of S.
(3) Outcomes: check the following 4 outcomes in this order. Each outcome consists of a seller payoff pi and a utility for each bidder.
  - vcg: the outcome of the optimal allocation with VCG payments. pi is the VCG seller payoff, and the utilities are the VCG utilities.
  - greedy: the outcome in which, with the greedy allocation, the bidders of the winning bids pay their amounts. pi is the social welfare of the greedy allocation, and all utilities are 0.
  - critical: the outcome in which, with the greedy allocation, the bidder pays the critical payment for each winning bid. pi is the sum of the critical payments and the retained amount of the greedy allocation, and a bidder's utility is the sum of (amount - critical payment) over that bidder's winning bids in the greedy allocation (0 if there are no winning bids).
  - saa: the outcome of the simultaneous ascending auction (section 8). pi is the sum of the revenue of section 8 and the sum of the reserve prices of the unsold items, and the utilities are the utilities of section 8.
(4) Blocking: for each outcome, the payoff of a coalition S is the sum of pi and the sum of the utilities of the bidders of S. If V(S) is greater than the payoff of S, S is said to block that outcome, and the difference V(S) - (payoff of S) is called the deficit. A blocking coalition none of whose proper subsets blocks is called a minimal blocking coalition. An outcome with no blocking coalition belongs to the core.

== 8. Simultaneous ascending auction ==
For each auction, run the following simulation using all valid bids.
(1) Candidates: for each bidder, the candidates of that bidder are the non-empty sets of that bidder's valid bids that, for an XOR bidder, consist of exactly 1 bid, and, for an OR bidder, have no two bids sharing an item. The bundle of a candidate is the union of the bundles of the bids it contains, and the value of a candidate is the sum of the amounts of the bids it contains. The number of a candidate is the sum, over the bids contained in the candidate, of 2^(t-1), where the bid is the bidder's t-th valid bid (counted from 1 in increasing order of bid number). The candidate order is increasing order of number.
(2) Value function: the value to a bidder of obtaining a set of items X is the maximum value of the candidates whose bundle is contained in X (including the case of being equal to X), or 0 if there is no such candidate.
(3) State: each offered item has a current price (initially its reserve price) and a holder (initially none), and each bidder has an eligibility (initially the maximum bundle size of that bidder's candidates, or 0 if there are no candidates). Set the random state x to seed (each auction starts from seed). Drawing one random number means replacing x by (48271 * x) mod 2147483647 and taking the replaced x as the value.
(4) Rounds: run rounds r = 1, 2, ... as in steps (i) to (v) below.
  (i) For each bidder k, determine the following based on the state at the beginning of the round. Let H be the set of items whose holder is k. The asking price of item j for k is the current price of j if j has no holder or its holder is k, and the current price + saa_increment if its holder is another bidder. For the candidates for which the number of items in the union of the bundle and H is at most k's eligibility, compute surplus = (value of the candidate) - (sum of the asking prices of the items of the candidate's bundle), and choose the candidate with the maximum surplus (if equal, the one earlier in candidate order). However, if the maximum surplus is 0 or less, or there is no such candidate, no candidate is chosen.
     The activity of k is the number of items in the union of the bundle and H if a candidate was chosen, and the number of items of H if none was chosen.
     On each item of the chosen candidate's bundle whose holder is not k, k bids at the asking price. These are called k's new bids.
  (ii) A round with no new bids at all is called a quiet round.
  (iii) If it is not a quiet round, examine the offered items in item order; for each item j, list the bidders who made a new bid on j in bidder order, and let m be their number. If m = 0, do nothing. If m = 1, choose that bidder; if m >= 2, draw one random number, let q (0 <= q < m) be the remainder of its value divided by m, and choose the (q + 1)-th bidder of the list. Make the chosen bidder the holder of j, and set the current price of j to the asking price of the new bids on j (the asking prices of all bidders who made a new bid on j are equal).
  (iv) For each bidder whose activity is less than its eligibility, set the eligibility to that activity (this is done in a quiet round too).
  (v) If it is a quiet round, end the auction with end reason quiet. Otherwise, if r equals round_cap, end the auction with end reason cap. If neither, proceed to the next round.
(5) Results: at the end, each item that has a holder is bought by its holder at its current price, and each item without a holder is unsold. For each bidder, compute the set X of items bought, the payment (the sum of the current prices of the items of X), the value (the value of X by the value function of (2)), and the utility (value - payment). A bidder with negative utility is called an exposed bidder, and its (payment - value) is called its exposure loss. The revenue is the sum of the current prices of the items sold, the social welfare is the sum of the values of all bidders and the sum of the reserve prices of the unsold items, and the efficiency is social welfare / optimal welfare.
(6) Item statistics: for each item, compute the number of new bids it received (summed over all rounds), the number of rounds in which it received new bids, and the number of the last round in which it received a new bid.

== 9. Single-item auctions ==
For each lot group, do the following.
(1) Validation: check the following in this order; the first one that applies is the error of that group. For a group with an error, the only things done are writing the error to single_item.txt and counting it in the SUMMARY line.
  - There are no participants: no bidders
  - Examining the participants in participant order, there is a participant with the same name as an earlier participant: duplicate bidder <that name> (the first one found)
  - Examining the participants in participant order, there is a participant whose sealed bid is greater than its valuation: bid above value <that name> (the first one found)
(2) Symbols and common values: let r be the reserve price. The optimum is the larger of r and the maximum valuation. If the valuation of the participant with the maximum valuation (if equal, the participant earlier in participant order) is at least r, that participant is the efficient participant; if it is less than r, there is no efficient participant.
  The result of each format is the winner (or unsold if none), the winner's price, the revenue, the social welfare (the winner's valuation, or r if unsold), the efficiency (social welfare / optimum), and the utility of each participant. In every format the winner's utility is (valuation - price). In (v), the utility of a payer who is not the winner is (- sealed bid). The utility of every other participant (participants other than the winner in (i) to (iv), and participants who are not payers in (v)) is 0. The revenue, in every format except (v), is the winner's price (0 if unsold).
  (i) First price (first_price): among the participants whose sealed bid is at least r, the participant with the maximum sealed bid (if equal, the one earlier in participant order) wins, and the price is its own sealed bid. If there is no such participant, it is unsold.
  (ii) Second price (second_price): participants are assumed to bid their valuations. Among the participants whose valuation is at least r, the participant with the maximum valuation (if equal, the one earlier in participant order) wins, and the price is the larger of r and the maximum valuation among the participants other than the winner whose valuation is at least r (r if there is no such participant). If there is no participant whose valuation is at least r, it is unsold.
  (iii) English (english): the clock price starts at r and is raised by english_increment at a time. The participants remaining at price p are the participants whose valuation is at least p. If no participant remains at price r, it is unsold; if one remains, that participant wins at price r. If two or more remain, raise the price repeatedly; when exactly one participant remains at the raised price, that participant wins at that price, and when none remains (when all participants remaining at the previous price drop out at the same time), the first participant in participant order among those remaining at the previous price wins at the previous price. The number of times the price was raised is the steps. Also, the drop price of each participant is the first price shown by the clock (r and each price after raising) that is greater than its valuation. If there is no such price, there is no drop price.
  (iv) Dutch (dutch): the clock price starts at dutch_start and is lowered by dutch_decrement at a time. While the price p is at least r, if there are participants whose sealed bid is at least p, the one among them with the maximum sealed bid (if equal, the one earlier in participant order) wins at price p; if there are none, the price is lowered. When the price becomes less than r (including the case where it is less from the start), it is unsold. The number of times the price was lowered is the steps (if unsold, the last lowering, which made the price less than r, is counted too).
  (v) All-pay (all_pay): the participants whose sealed bid is at least r are the payers. The winner and the price are the same as in (i). Each payer pays its own sealed bid whether or not it wins, and the revenue is the sum of the payers' sealed bids.
(3) Revenue ranking: the 5 formats listed in decreasing order of revenue. Formats with equal revenue are in the order (i) to (v).

== 10. Ad slot auctions ==
For each slots group, do the following.
(1) Validation: check the following in this order; the first one that applies is the error of that group. For a group with an error, the only things done are writing the error to single_item.txt and counting it in the SUMMARY line.
  - Examining the click-through rates from left to right, the first one to which one of the following applies: bad ctr <that click-through rate string>
    - It is not of the form: contains exactly one "/", the parts before and after it each consist of one or more digits only, and neither starts with 0 (this means the numerator and the denominator are integers of at least 1).
    - Its value (numerator / denominator) is greater than 1.
    - It is the second or a later click-through rate, and its value is at least the value of the previous click-through rate.
  - There are no participants: no advertisers
  - Examining the participants in participant order, there is a participant with the same name as an earlier participant: duplicate advertiser <that name> (the first one found)
(2) Symbols: let K be the number of slots, c_s the click-through rate of slot s (s = 1, ..., K), and n the number of participants. For s > K, c_s = 0. All the values below are fractions.
(3) GSP: the participants listed in decreasing order of bid (if equal, the one earlier in participant order) form the GSP ranking. Let b_s be the bid of the participant at rank s, with b_s = 0 for s > n. The participant at rank s = 1, ..., min(n, K) gets slot s and pays b_(s+1) per click. With v being that participant's valuation, the payment (per impression) is c_s * b_(s+1), and the utility is c_s * (v - b_(s+1)). The GSP revenue is the sum of the payments, and the social welfare is the sum of c_s * v over the participants who got a slot.
(4) VCG: the participants listed in decreasing order of valuation (if equal, the one earlier in participant order) form the VCG ranking, and let w_t be the valuation of the participant at rank t. The participant at rank s = 1, ..., min(n, K) gets slot s and pays, per impression, P_s = Sum w_t * (c_(t-1) - c_t) (summed over t = s + 1, ..., min(n, K + 1); 0 if there are no terms). The price per click is P_s / c_s, and the utility is c_s * w_s - P_s. The VCG revenue is the sum of P_s, and the optimum is the sum of c_s * w_s over ranks s = 1, ..., min(n, K). The GSP efficiency is GSP social welfare / optimum, and the VCG efficiency is 1.
(5) Local envy-freeness: for each participant at GSP rank s = 2, ..., min(n, K + 1), with v being its valuation, compute the stay payoff c_s * (v - b_(s+1)) and the swap payoff c_(s-1) * (v - b_s). If the stay payoff is at least the swap payoff, it is ok; if less, it is violated. If there is no violated at all, the submitted bids are said to be locally envy-free.
(6) Nash equilibrium: for each participant at GSP rank s = 1, ..., n, let v be its valuation and its current payoff be c_s * (v - b_(s+1)). For each slot t = 1, ..., K (t != s), the payoff of moving to slot t is c_t * (v - b_t) if t < s, and c_t * (v - b_(t+1)) if t > s; if the payoff of moving is greater than the current payoff, it is called a profitable deviation, and the difference (payoff of moving - current payoff) is its gain. If there is no profitable deviation at all, the submitted bids are a Nash equilibrium.
(7) Locally envy-free equilibrium: the submitted bids are locally envy-free and also a Nash equilibrium.

[Source files]
Split the source into the following 6 files (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: handles the overall flow and the core of the processing, and holds the most logic. It does bid validation (the line errors of section 2, the messages of (a) to (f), and eligible bids), the computation of the accepted amount, retained amount and unsold items of an allocation, the assembly of each line of wdp.txt from the results of winner determination and the greedy method, the computation of the winners' values, the VCG utilities and the seller payoff, the assembly of the 4 outcomes of the core check and the BLOCK and RESULT lines, the simulation of the simultaneous ascending auction (asking prices, choice of candidates under the eligibility limit, activity and eligibility, processing of new bids and breaking ties with random numbers, item statistics, the end test, and the per-bidder results and exposure losses), the validation of lot groups and slots groups, the first-price, second-price and all-pay formats, the utility table and the revenue ranking, each GSP and VCG line, the tests of local envy-freeness and Nash equilibrium, the total lines, and the assembly of each line of the 6 output files and the calls that write them.
- bids_io: handles reading the 4 input files. It returns the list of items and reserve prices, the offered items and bidder lines (bidder name, bidding language, list of bid strings) of each auction, the kind, group name, arguments and participant list of each group, and the parameter values. It also defines the record representing a valid bid (bid number, bidder, bundle, amount, whether XOR).
- wdp_solver: handles the branch and bound of section 4 (usable bids, shares and sources, upper bound, node processing, the incumbent and improvement records, search statistics) and the greedy order and greedy selection of section 5.
- payments: handles the VCG payments of section 6 (re-search for each winner and the alternative allocation), the computation of coalition welfare in coalition order and the determination of blocking coalitions and minimal blocking coalitions for each outcome in section 7, the critical payments of section 5, the GSP price per click, and the ad slot VCG payment P_s.
- auctions: handles the clock processing of the English auction (including the steps and drop prices) and the Dutch auction of section 9, and the enumeration of candidates and the value function of section 8.
- report: handles the fraction format, the bundle notation, and writing lists of lines to files.

[Constraints]
- input/items.txt has at least 1 and at most 12 lines. Reserve prices are at most 1000.
- input/bids.txt has at most 10 auctions, each auction has at least 1 and at most 15 bidder lines, one bidder line has at least 1 and at most 10 bids, and each bid string is at most 60 characters long. bids.txt is written exactly in the format of [Input files], but the bidding language and the bid strings may be wrong (they are validated in section 2). Each auction that is run has at least 1 bidder line without a line error.
- input/single_item.txt has at least 1 and at most 20 groups, each group has at most 20 participants, and valuations, sealed bids and bids are at most 100000. A slots line has at least 1 and at most 10 click-through rates, each a string of at most 20 characters. For a click-through rate that satisfies the form of section 10 (1), the numerator and the denominator are at most 1000, and the denominator is a divisor of 1000.
- The values of input/params.txt satisfy 1 <= max_bundle_size <= 12, 1 <= max_bids_per_bidder <= 10, 0 <= core_max_bidders <= 10, 1 <= saa_increment <= 1000, 1 <= round_cap <= 1000, 1 <= seed <= 2147483646, 1 <= english_increment <= 1000, 1 <= dutch_start <= 100000, 1 <= dutch_decrement <= 1000.
- If fractions are kept reduced during the computation, the absolute value of every integer, including intermediate values, is less than 2^53. The random number computation 48271 * x is also less than 2^53.
- In the given data there are 12 items, 7 auctions (6 of which are run), and about 145 bids, and the auction with the most bidders has 11 bidders.
