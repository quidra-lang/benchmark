[Overview]
Write a program that solves the propositional satisfiability problem (SAT) with the DPLL method. The program reads the job file input/jobs.txt and executes the jobs one line at a time, in order. There are seven kinds of jobs: solving a formula written in a DIMACS CNF file (a .cnf file in the input/ folder); enumerating models of a formula (assignments that satisfy the formula) up to a limit count; extracting a minimal unsatisfiable part (an unsatisfiable core) from an unsatisfiable formula; encoding each of the N-queens, pigeonhole and graph coloring problems into a formula with a fixed variable numbering and solving it; and finding the chromatic number of a graph. Graphs are read from input/graphs.txt. A formula is first simplified (preprocessing) by removing tautologies and duplicate literals, applying unit clauses, removing subsumed clauses and eliminating pure literals, and is then solved by a DPLL search that performs unit propagation with two watched literals, decisions by the DLIS rule, and chronological backtracking using an explicit trail. Every model found is verified against the clauses of the original formula, and models are enumerated by repeating the search while adding blocking clauses that negate the models. The program writes the job results, the verification and analysis, and the statistics (numbers of decisions, propagations and conflicts, maximum depth, number of clauses removed by preprocessing) to output/results.txt, the models to output/models.txt, the puzzle boards and colorings to output/boards.txt, and the per-job statistics to output/stats.csv. All computation is done with integers and booleans only; no floating-point numbers are used. Nothing is printed to standard output.

[Input files]
The input files are input/jobs.txt, input/graphs.txt, and the DIMACS files (files with the extension .cnf) in the input/ folder that jobs.txt names. Every file consists only of printable ASCII characters (including the space) and line feeds (LF), and every line (including the last one) ends with a line feed. No file contains tab characters.

* input/jobs.txt (job file)
Each line is a job line, an empty line, or a comment line starting with "#". Empty lines and comment lines are ignored. Job lines are given job numbers 1, 2, 3, ... in order from the top of the file. A job line consists of one or more tokens separated by single spaces; there is no space at the start or end of the line, and there are never two or more consecutive spaces. The first token is called the command and the remaining tokens are called the arguments. A correct job line has one of the following seven forms (the arguments are checked in section 2 of [Processing]).
- "SOLVE f": solve the formula in DIMACS file f and find one model.
- "ENUM f L": enumerate up to L models of the formula in DIMACS file f.
- "CORE f": solve in the same way as SOLVE, and if the formula is unsatisfiable, find an unsatisfiable core.
- "QUEENS n L": encode the N-queens problem on an n x n board and enumerate up to L solutions.
- "PIGEON p h L": encode the pigeonhole problem of putting p pigeons into h holes and enumerate up to L solutions.
- "COLOR g k L": encode the coloring with k colors of the graph named g and enumerate up to L colorings.
- "CHROMATIC g K": for k = 1, 2, ..., K in this order, check whether the graph named g can be colored with k colors, and find its chromatic number.

* input/graphs.txt (graph file)
A sequence of zero or more graph blocks. Outside the blocks there are only empty lines and comment lines starting with "#", which are ignored. A block consists of a line "GRAPH s v", edge lines (zero or more lines), and a line "END", in this order. s is the name of the graph, a string of 1 to 20 characters consisting only of lowercase letters (a-z), digits (0-9) and "_". No two blocks have the same name. v is the number of vertices, an integer from 1 to 30 written in decimal without extra leading zeros. The vertices are numbered 1, 2, ..., v. An edge line is a line that is not "END", and a correct edge line has the form "a b" (a and b are the numbers of the two end vertices of the edge). Edge lines are checked in section 3 of [Processing]. Edges are undirected. A block contains no empty lines and no comment lines. No line has a space at its start or end, and there are never two or more consecutive spaces.

* input/<name>.cnf (DIMACS file)
A file that writes a formula in DIMACS CNF format. The condition on file names is given in section 2 of [Processing]. Each line consists of runs of non-space characters (tokens) separated by one or more spaces; a line may have spaces at its start or end. The file consists of the following lines (errors are checked in section 4 of [Processing]).
- Comment line: a line whose first character is "c".
- A line with no tokens (an empty line or a line of only spaces).
- Header line: a line of the form "p cnf V C", where V is the number of variables and C is the number of clauses.
- Clause line: a line of integer tokens. A nonzero integer denotes a literal, and 0 denotes the end of one clause. Several clauses may be written on one line, and one clause may be written across several lines.

[Processing]
* 1. Terms and common rules
- Variable: denoted by an integer number of 1 or more. The variables of a formula with V variables are 1, 2, ..., V.
- Literal: a variable x itself (a positive literal) or its negation (a negative literal). A positive literal is represented by the integer x and a negative literal by the integer -x. The negation of a literal l is written -l, and the absolute value of l is called the variable of l. When a literal is written as a string, a positive literal is written as x, and a negative literal as "-" followed by x, in decimal (e.g. 3, -12).
- Clause: a sequence of literals. The position within the sequence (1st, 2nd, ... from the front) can matter. The length of a clause is the number of literals it contains (a literal that appears several times is counted that many times). A clause of length 0 is called an empty clause, and a clause of length 1 is called a unit clause.
- Formula: a pair of a number of variables V and a sequence of clauses. The clauses of the original formula (the one read from a DIMACS file or built by the encoding of section 5) are given clause numbers 1, 2, 3, ... from the front of the sequence. M denotes the number of clauses of the original formula.
- Assignment: a choice of true, false or unassigned for each variable. A positive literal x is true when the variable x is true and false when it is false; a negative literal -x is the opposite. If the variable is unassigned, its literal is also unassigned.
- A clause is satisfied when at least one of the literals it contains is true. An empty clause is never satisfied. A formula is satisfied when all of its clauses are satisfied.
- Model: an assignment that gives true or false to every one of the variables 1, 2, ..., V and satisfies the formula.
- Well-formed number: a string that is "0", or one digit 1-9 followed by zero or more digits 0-9.
- Number list: the integers written in decimal in the specified order and joined with ",". If there are no integers, it is written as "-".
- Frequency list: each pair of an integer value k and its count c is written as "k:c", and the pairs are joined with "," in increasing order of k. If there are no pairs, it is written as "-".
- Name characters: lowercase letters (a-z), digits (0-9) and "_".
- Job limit: 1 for SOLVE and CORE jobs, and the value of the argument L for ENUM, QUEENS, PIGEON and COLOR jobs.

* 2. Job validation
For each job line, check (1) to (3) below in order. If one of them applies, the job is an error: record the reason of the first one that applies (the string in double quotes, with X, T and R replaced by the actual strings), and perform no further checks or processing for that job. A job that is not an error is called a valid job.
(1) The command is none of "SOLVE", "ENUM", "CORE", "QUEENS", "PIGEON", "COLOR", "CHROMATIC": "unknown command X" (X is the command token).
(2) The number of arguments differs from the number of arguments of the command's form (1 for SOLVE and CORE; 2 for ENUM, QUEENS and CHROMATIC; 3 for PIGEON and COLOR): "wrong argument count".
(3) Check the arguments from left to right, each according to its kind, as follows. T is the argument token.
  - File name (f): T does not satisfy the condition that it ends with ".cnf" and the part before that has 1 to 20 characters and consists only of name characters: "bad file name T".
  - Graph name (g): graphs.txt has no graph named T: "unknown graph T". That graph is an invalid graph (defined in section 3): "invalid graph T: R" (R is that graph's invalidity reason).
  - Number (n, p, h, k, K, L): T is not a well-formed number of at most 6 characters: "bad number T". The value of T is outside its range: "out of range T". The ranges are 1 to 8 for n, p and h, 1 to 6 for k and K, and 1 to 200 for L.
For a SOLVE, ENUM or CORE job to which none of (1) to (3) applies, next read the file input/f (f is the file name argument) by the rules of section 4. If there is an error, the job is an error with that reason. A file named by a file name that satisfies the file name condition always exists in the input/ folder.

* 3. Reading and validating graphs
For each block of graphs.txt, examine its edge lines from top to bottom. An edge line is split into tokens at each single space. For an edge line, check (1) to (4) below in order; if one of them applies, the graph is an invalid graph, and its invalidity reason is "R at line N" for the first one that applies, where R is the string in double quotes below and N is the line number of that edge line within graphs.txt (the first line of the file is 1, and empty lines and comment lines are also counted). For an invalid graph, the remaining edge lines of the block are not examined.
(1) The number of tokens is not 2, or either token is not a well-formed number of at most 6 characters: "bad edge line".
(2) Either of the values a, b of the two tokens is not between 1 and v inclusive: "vertex out of range".
(3) a and b are equal: "self loop".
(4) An earlier edge line of the same block has an edge connecting the same two vertices (swapping a and b gives the same edge): "duplicate edge".
For a graph that is not invalid, each edge is represented by the pair (u, w), where u is the smaller and w the larger of the numbers of its two end vertices, and the sequence of edges is sorted in increasing order of u, and in increasing order of w when u is equal (regardless of the order of the edge lines and of the order in which the two ends are written). The number of edges of the graph is the number of edge lines. The degree of a vertex is the number of edges that have that vertex as an end.

* 4. Reading a DIMACS file
Process the lines of the file in order from the top. The line number N counts the first line of the file as 1. Stop reading where the first error is found by the following rules, and use its reason (the string in double quotes, with N, T, C and F replaced by values) as the error reason.
(1) Comment lines and lines with no tokens are ignored.
(2) A line whose first token is "p" is a header line. If there is an earlier header line, the error is "duplicate header at line N". If the line does not satisfy the condition that it has exactly 4 tokens, the 2nd token is "cnf", and the 3rd and 4th tokens are well-formed numbers of at most 6 characters, the error is "bad header at line N". Otherwise, the value of the 3rd token is the number of variables V, and the value of the 4th token is the declared number of clauses C.
(3) Any other line is a clause line. If there is no earlier header line, the error is "missing header". Examine the tokens of the clause line from left to right. If a token T is not an integer token, the error is "bad token T at line N". An integer token is a string that is "0", or that consists of an optional single "-" followed by one digit 1-9 and zero or more digits 0-9, where the part excluding the "-" has at most 6 characters. If the absolute value of the value of T is greater than V, the error is "literal out of range T at line N". If the value of T is not 0, append it to the end of the clause under construction. If the value of T is 0, append the clause under construction (which may be empty) to the end of the formula's sequence of clauses, and make the clause under construction empty again. The clause under construction is empty at the start of reading the file.
(4) After the last line has been processed, check in this order: if there is no header line at all, "missing header"; if the clause under construction has one or more literals, "unterminated clause"; if the number F of clauses of the formula differs from C, "clause count mismatch header=C found=F".
If there is no error, the number of variables V and the sequence of clauses read form the original formula. The order of the literals in a clause is the order in which they are written in the file.

* 5. Encoding
For QUEENS, PIGEON, COLOR and CHROMATIC jobs, the original formula is built as follows. Clauses are appended to the end of the sequence of clauses in the order written here. An order such as "pairs (a, b) in increasing order of a, and in increasing order of b when a is equal" is called lexicographic order.
(1) N-queens (argument n): the number of variables is n*n. Variable (r-1)*n+c means that a queen is placed on the square in the r-th row from the top and the c-th column from the left (written square (r, c)) (r, c = 1, 2, ..., n).
  (i) For r = 1, 2, ..., n in this order, add the clause [(r-1)*n+1, (r-1)*n+2, ..., (r-1)*n+n].
  (ii) For all pairs (a, b) of variables a, b (a < b) of two squares such that the two squares are in the same row, are in the same column, or have an absolute difference of row numbers equal to the absolute difference of column numbers, in lexicographic order, add the clause [-a, -b].
(2) Pigeonhole (arguments p, h): the number of variables is p*h. Variable (i-1)*h+j means that pigeon i goes into hole j (i = 1, ..., p, j = 1, ..., h).
  (i) For i = 1, 2, ..., p in this order, add the clause [(i-1)*h+1, ..., (i-1)*h+h].
  (ii) For all tuples (i, j1, j2) of a pigeon i and two holes j1 < j2, in lexicographic order, add the clause [-((i-1)*h+j1), -((i-1)*h+j2)].
  (iii) For all tuples (j, i1, i2) of a hole j and two pigeons i1 < i2, in lexicographic order, add the clause [-((i1-1)*h+j), -((i2-1)*h+j)].
(3) Graph coloring (number of vertices of the graph v, number of colors k): the number of variables is v*k. Variable (x-1)*k+c means that the color of vertex x is c (x = 1, ..., v, c = 1, ..., k).
  (i) For x = 1, 2, ..., v in this order, add the clause [(x-1)*k+1, ..., (x-1)*k+k].
  (ii) For all tuples (x, c1, c2) of a vertex x and two colors c1 < c2, in lexicographic order, add the clause [-((x-1)*k+c1), -((x-1)*k+c2)].
  (iii) In the order of the sequence of edges sorted in section 3, for each edge (u, w), for c = 1, 2, ..., k in this order, add the clause [-((u-1)*k+c), -((w-1)*k+c)].

* 6. Preprocessing
Preprocessing receives the original formula and whether to perform pure literal elimination. Pure literal elimination is performed only when the limit used for solving (defined in section 8) is 1. Preprocessing works with a sequence of clauses (the working list) and a fixed assignment (true or false decided for some of the variables). Each clause of the working list remembers its clause number in the original formula. The following statistics and records are all initially 0 or empty.
- The tautology count, the duplicate literal count, the unit fixed count, the unit removed count, the subsumed removed count, the pure fixed count and the pure removed count.
- Records of the clause numbers of the removed clauses (four kinds: tautology, unit, subsumed, pure).
Preprocessing performs (1) to (4) below in this order.
(1) Normalization: examine the clauses of the original formula in clause number order. A clause that contains both some literal and its negation is a tautology. A tautology is not put into the working list; add 1 to the tautology count, and record its clause number as tautology. For any other clause, if the same literal appears two or more times, keep only the first occurrence of each literal and remove the second and later occurrences (add the number of removed occurrences to the duplicate literal count); then append the clause to the end of the working list, keeping the order of the remaining literals.
(2) Applying unit clauses: repeat (a) to (c) below.
  (a) If the working list contains an empty clause, preprocessing ends with a conflict. In this case, the clause number of the empty clause closest to the front of the working list is the empty clause number.
  (b) If the working list contains no unit clause, end (2).
  (c) Let l be the literal of the unit clause closest to the front of the working list. Add to the fixed assignment the value of the variable of l that makes l true, and add 1 to the unit fixed count. Remove from the working list all clauses that contain l (including that unit clause itself), add their number to the unit removed count, and record their clause numbers as unit. From each of the remaining clauses, remove all occurrences of -l. The order of the clauses and the order of the remaining literals do not change.
(3) Subsumption removal: for each clause B of the working list, remove B when the working list has a clause A at a position different from that of B such that the set of literals appearing in A is contained in the set of literals appearing in B, and either "the length of A is less than the length of B" or "A comes before B in the working list". Which clauses are removed is decided by judging all clauses against the working list before any removal, and they are then removed at once. The number of removed clauses is the subsumed removed count, and their clause numbers are recorded as subsumed. The order of the remaining clauses does not change.
(4) Pure literal elimination (only when it is performed): repeat the following. Among the literals appearing in the clauses of the working list, those whose negation appears in no clause of the working list are called pure literals. If there are no pure literals, end (4). Otherwise, for every pure literal, add to the fixed assignment the value of its variable that makes it true, and add the number of pure literals to the pure fixed count. Then remove from the working list all clauses that contain one or more pure literals, add their number to the pure removed count, and record their clause numbers as pure. The order of the remaining clauses does not change.
If preprocessing did not end with a conflict, the clauses of the working list after (4), in their order and with their literal order, are called the simplified formula. The variables in the fixed assignment do not appear in the simplified formula. Among the variables 1, 2, ..., V, those not in the fixed assignment are called active variables. The number of clauses removed by preprocessing is the sum of the tautology count, the unit removed count, the subsumed removed count and the pure removed count (if preprocessing ended with a conflict, the sum of these values up to when the conflict was found).

* 7. DPLL search
One search is called a round. A round receives the number of variables V and a sequence of clauses (the search clause list), and determines whether the result is satisfiable (a model was found) or unsatisfiable. The search swaps the positions of literals within clauses, but at the start of a round the positions of the literals of each clause are always as in the received clause list (swaps made in previous rounds are not carried over). A round uses the following.
- The value of each variable: true, false or unassigned. Initially all are unassigned.
- Trail: a sequence of pairs of an assigned literal and its kind (decision, flip or propagation). Initially empty.
- Propagation position: the position in the trail of the element that unit propagation processes next. Initially the start of the trail.
- Watch lists: sequences of clauses, one per literal. Initially all empty.
- Depth: the number of elements in the trail whose kind is decision or flip.
- Statistics: the decision count, the propagation count, the conflict count and the maximum depth. Initially all 0.
- Event log: a sequence of strings. Initially empty.
Assigning a literal l with kind t means: set the value of the variable of l so that l is true, append (l, t) to the end of the trail, and record as an event the string made of "D" if t is decision, "F" if t is flip, or "P" if t is propagation, followed by l (e.g. "D-3", "P12").
A round proceeds by steps (1) to (6) below.
(1) Watch initialization: take the clauses of length 2 or more of the search clause list one at a time in list order, and append each to the end of the watch list of its 1st literal and to the end of the watch list of its 2nd literal. The literals at the 1st and 2nd positions of a clause are called the watched literals of that clause.
(2) Root processing: examine the clauses of the search clause list in order from the front. If a clause is an empty clause, or is a unit clause whose literal is false, add 1 to the conflict count, record the event "C", and end the round as unsatisfiable. If it is a unit clause whose literal is unassigned, assign that literal with kind propagation and add 1 to the propagation count. Do nothing for any other clause. After all clauses have been examined, go to (3).
(3) Unit propagation: repeat the following until the propagation position goes past the end of the trail. Let p be the literal of the element at the propagation position, and advance the propagation position by one. Let f = -p (the literal that has just become false), and process the clauses of the watch list of f one at a time, in order from the front of the list, by (i) to (iv) below.
  (i) If the 1st literal of the clause is f, swap the 1st and 2nd literals. Now the 2nd literal is f.
  (ii) If the 1st literal is true, leave this clause in the watch list of f and go on to the next clause.
  (iii) Examine the 3rd and later literals from front to back; if there is a literal that is not false (that is, true or unassigned), swap the first such literal with the 2nd literal, remove this clause from the watch list of f, append it to the end of the watch list of the new 2nd literal, and go on to the next clause.
  (iv) If there is no such literal, leave this clause in the watch list of f. If the 1st literal is false, there is a conflict: add 1 to the conflict count, record the event "C", end unit propagation with a conflict, and go to (5). The not yet processed clauses of the watch list of f remain as they are. If the 1st literal is unassigned, assign it with kind propagation, add 1 to the propagation count, and go on to the next clause.
  Removing a clause from a watch list does not change the order of the clauses remaining in the list. Newly assigned literals are also processed later as p, in trail order. When the propagation position goes past the end of the trail, unit propagation ends without a conflict; go to (4).
(4) Decision: for each clause of the search clause list that contains no true literal (an unsatisfied clause), count the unassigned literals of that clause, once each (the DLIS rule). If no literal was counted, end the round as satisfiable. Otherwise, choose the literal with the largest count. If several literals have the largest count, choose the one with the smallest variable number, and if there are still two, choose the positive literal. Assign the chosen literal with kind decision, add 1 to the decision count, set the maximum depth to the depth if the depth is greater than the maximum depth, and go to (3).
(5) Backtracking: repeatedly remove the last element of the trail and make its variable unassigned, until the kind of the last element of the trail is decision. If the trail becomes empty, end the round as unsatisfiable. Otherwise, remove the last element (d, decision), make the variable of d unassigned, assign -d with kind flip, set the propagation position to the position of this flip element, and go to (3). Watch lists are not changed by backtracking.
(6) When the round ends as satisfiable, the value of each variable at that time (an unassigned variable is taken as false) is the search result.

* 8. Solving a formula
Solving a formula (number of variables V, clauses of the original formula) with limit L means the following.
(1) Perform the preprocessing of section 6 (with pure literal elimination if L is 1, and without it if L is 2 or more). If preprocessing ends with a conflict, there are 0 models, the number of rounds is 0, and the stop reason is "preprocess".
(2) Otherwise, make the sequence of models and the sequence of blocking clauses empty, and repeat the following while the number of models is less than L. Add 1 to the number of rounds, and perform one round of section 7 with, as the search clause list, the clauses of the simplified formula followed by the clauses of the sequence of blocking clauses in their order. If the round is unsatisfiable, set the stop reason to "all" and end the repetition. If it is satisfiable, build the model in which the value of each variable x (x = 1, ..., V) is its value in the fixed assignment if x is in the fixed assignment, and its value in the search result otherwise, and append it to the end of the sequence of models. Then append to the end of the sequence of blocking clauses the clause that lists, for the active variables x in increasing order, -x if x is true in that model and x if it is false (an empty clause if there are no active variables).
(3) When the repetition ends because the number of models has reached L, the stop reason is "limit" (no further rounds are performed).
The status of the result is "SAT" if there are one or more models and "UNSAT" if there are 0. The search statistics are the sums over all rounds of the decision count, the propagation count and the conflict count, and the maximum over all rounds of the maximum depth (all 0 if there are no rounds). The trace is the event log of the first round.
For SOLVE, ENUM, CORE, QUEENS, PIGEON and COLOR jobs, the original formula is solved with the job limit.

* 9. Model verification and analysis
When there are one or more models, compute (1) to (3) below for the original formula and the sequence of models; also compute (4) when there are two or more models, and (5) when the stop reason is all and there are one or more models. The models are numbered 1, 2, ... from the front of the sequence of models.
(1) Verification: examine the models in number order, and for each model the clauses of the original formula in clause number order; if there is a clause that is not satisfied, verification fails with the model number i and clause number j of the first one found. If there is no unsatisfied clause, verification succeeds.
(2) True literal distribution: for the 1st model, compute for each clause of the original formula the number of true literals (a literal that appears several times is counted that many times), and make a frequency list of the number of clauses for each value.
(3) Safe flips: the number list, in increasing order, of the variables x (1 <= x <= V) such that the assignment obtained from the 1st model by inverting only the value of x satisfies the original formula.
(4) Diversity: for every pair of model numbers i < j, compute the number of variables whose values differ between the two models (the distance), and find the number of pairs and the minimum and maximum of the distances.
(5) Backbone: for each variable x that has the same value in all models, x if that value is true and -x if it is false, listed in increasing order of x.

* 10. Unsatisfiable core (CORE jobs)
When the status of a CORE job is UNSAT, find an unsatisfiable core. Start with the set of clause numbers S = {1, 2, ..., M}, and perform the following test for i = 1, 2, ..., M in this order. Solve, as in section 8 with limit 1, the formula (with V variables) made of the clauses whose clause numbers are in S without i, arranged in the clause number order of the original formula; if the status is UNSAT, remove i from S. The S that remains at the end is the unsatisfiable core, and its clause numbers are output in increasing order. Also compute, over the search statistics of the M tests, the sum of the decision counts and the sum of the conflict counts.

* 11. Chromatic number (CHROMATIC jobs)
For k = 1, 2, ..., K in this order, solve, as in section 8 with limit 1, the formula that encodes the coloring of graph g with k colors by section 5 (3). This is called an attempt. When an attempt has status SAT, stop the attempts there. If the status of the last attempt is SAT, that k is the chromatic number. The job's status, models, number of variables, number of clauses of the original formula, the verification and analysis of section 9, and the board decoding use those of the last attempt. The job's search statistics are the sums over all attempts of the decision, propagation and conflict counts, and the maximum over all attempts of the maximum depth. The job's number of rounds and number of clauses removed by preprocessing are the sums over all attempts.

* 12. Board decoding and analysis
(1) Position decoding: let R = n, W = n for QUEENS; R = p, W = h for PIGEON; and R = v (the number of vertices), W = k (the number of colors of that formula) for COLOR and CHROMATIC. For a model, for each of r = 1, 2, ..., R, find the c (1 <= c <= W) such that variable (r-1)*W+c is true (since the model satisfies all clauses of the encoding of section 5, there is exactly one such c for each r). This is called the position sequence. In QUEENS r is the row and c is the column of the queen; in PIGEON r is the pigeon and c is the hole; in COLOR and CHROMATIC r is the vertex and c is the color.
(2) N-queens classes: for the board represented by the position sequence (q1, ..., qn) (qr is the column of the queen in row r), build the 8 boards (rotations and reflections) obtained by moving each square (r, c) to square (r, c), (c, n+1-r), (n+1-r, n+1-c), (n+1-c, r), (r, n+1-c), (n+1-r, c), (c, r), (n+1-c, n+1-r), respectively, and for each board find the column sequence that lists the column of the queen of each row in row order. Among the 8 column sequences, the smallest in lexicographic order (compare from the first element; the sequence whose first differing element is smaller is the smaller one) is called the canonical form, and the number of distinct column sequences among the 8 is called the orbit size. Solutions with equal canonical forms belong to the same class. Classes are given class numbers 1, 2, 3, ... in the order in which they first appear in the sequence of models.
(3) Coloring classes: for a color sequence (c1, ..., cv), the sequence obtained by looking at the vertices in order from vertex 1 and renumbering the colors as 1, 2, 3, ... in the order in which they first appear is called the canonical form. Colorings with equal canonical forms belong to the same class. Class numbers are assigned in the same way as in (2). The number of colors used is the number of distinct colors appearing in the color sequence. The vertex counts per color are, for each of the colors 1, 2, ..., k, the number of vertices of that color (which may be 0).
(4) Greedy coloring: take the vertices of the graph one at a time, in decreasing order of degree, and in increasing order of vertex number when the degrees are equal, and give each vertex as its color the smallest positive integer that differs from the colors of all adjacent vertices that are already colored. The number of colors of the greedy coloring is the largest color given.
(5) Pigeonhole: let h_i be the position of pigeon i, and call a hole that contains no pigeon an empty hole. The expected number of solutions E is h*(h-1)*...*(h-p+1) if p <= h, and 0 if p > h.

* 13. Aggregation
For each valid job, compute the following 12 values. These 12 values are called the job's statistics values and are written to output/stats.csv.
job (job number), command (command), status (status), variables (number of variables V), clauses (number of clauses of the original formula M), removed_clauses (number of clauses removed by preprocessing), decisions (decision count), propagations (propagation count), conflicts (conflict count), max_depth (maximum depth), models (number of models), rounds (number of rounds).
For jobs other than CHROMATIC, the values are those obtained by solving as in section 8; for CHROMATIC, they are those defined in section 11. The search statistics of the CORE tests are not included.

[Output files]
The program writes the following 4 files into the output/ folder. Every file consists only of ASCII characters, every line ends with a line feed (LF), and no line has trailing spaces. No empty lines are output other than those specified as paragraph separators. Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, and negative numbers are written with a "-". In a format, only the explained symbols (J, V, M, K, etc.) are replaced by values; all other characters are output as they are.

* output/results.txt
Output one paragraph per job line in job number order, and finally the summary paragraph. Put one empty line between consecutive paragraphs. Put no empty line at the start or end of the file. The 1st line of every job paragraph is "JOB J X", where J is the job number and X is the job line written as it is.

The paragraph of an error job is the 2 lines "JOB J X" and "error R" (R is the error reason).

The paragraph of a valid job other than CHROMATIC consists of the following lines in this order. A line with a condition is output only when the condition holds.
1. "JOB J X"
2. (for COLOR) "graph vertices=V edges=E max_degree=D greedy=G": V is the number of vertices, E is the number of edges, D is the maximum degree of a vertex, and G is the number of colors of the greedy coloring.
3. "formula variables=V clauses=M literals=T lengths=H": V is the number of variables, M is the number of clauses of the original formula, T is the sum of the clause lengths of the original formula, and H is the frequency list of the clause lengths of the original formula.
4. "preprocess pure=P tautologies=A duplicate_literals=B unit_fixed=C unit_removed=D subsumed=E pure_fixed=F pure_removed=G removed=R": P is "on" if pure literal elimination was performed and "off" if it was not. A to G are the tautology count, the duplicate literal count, the unit fixed count, the unit removed count, the subsumed removed count, the pure fixed count and the pure removed count of section 6. R is the number of clauses removed by preprocessing.
5. "removed_clauses tautology=W unit=X subsumed=Y pure=Z": W, X, Y and Z are the number lists of the clause numbers recorded as tautology, unit, subsumed and pure, respectively, each in increasing order.
6. If preprocessing ended with a conflict, "simplified empty_clause=N" (N is the empty clause number). Otherwise, the 2 lines "simplified clauses=M2 literals=T2 lengths=H2 fixed=F active=A" and "fixed literals=Y". M2, T2 and H2 are the number of clauses, the sum of the clause lengths and the frequency list of the clause lengths of the simplified formula; F is the number of variables in the fixed assignment, and A is the number of active variables. Y is the number list that writes the variables x of the fixed assignment in increasing order, as x if the value is true and as -x if it is false.
7. "result S models=K rounds=N stop=T": S is the status, K is the number of models, N is the number of rounds, and T is the stop reason.
8. "search decisions=D propagations=P conflicts=C max_depth=X": the search statistics.
9. (when the number of rounds is 1 or more) "trace E": E is the first 12 events of the trace (all of them if there are fewer than 12), joined with single spaces. If there are more than 12 events, it is followed by a space and "+N" (N is the number of remaining events). If there are no events, E is "-".
10. (when there are one or more models) "verify ok models=K clauses=M true_literals=H" if verification succeeded, or "verify failed model=I clause=N" if it failed: K is the number of models, M is the number of clauses of the original formula, H is the true literal distribution, and I and N are the model number and clause number at which verification failed.
11. (when there are one or more models) "flips safe=N variables=Y": Y is the number list of the safe flips and N is its count.
12. (when there are two or more models) "diversity pairs=P min_distance=A max_distance=B": the number of pairs, the minimum distance and the maximum distance of the diversity.
13. (when the stop reason is all and there are one or more models) "backbone size=N literals=Y": Y is the number list of the backbone and N is its count.
14. (for CORE when the status is UNSAT) "core size=N decisions=D conflicts=C clauses=Y": Y is the number list of the unsatisfiable core, N is its count, and D and C are the sum of the decision counts and the sum of the conflict counts of the tests.

The paragraph of a CHROMATIC job consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "JOB J X"
2. "graph vertices=V edges=E max_degree=D greedy=G": the same as for COLOR.
3. For each attempt, in order of k, "attempt k=K clauses=M removed=R result=S stop=T decisions=D propagations=P conflicts=C max_depth=X": K is the number of colors, M is the number of clauses of that formula, R is the number of clauses removed by preprocessing, S is the status, T is the stop reason, and D, P, C and X are the search statistics of that attempt.
4. "result S chromatic=N attempts=A": S is the status of the job, N is the chromatic number ("-" if the status is UNSAT), and A is the number of attempts.
5. "search decisions=D propagations=P conflicts=C max_depth=X": the search statistics of the job.
6. (when the status is SAT) lines 10 and 11 above (verification and safe flips), for the formula and model of the last attempt.

The summary paragraph consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "SUMMARY jobs=N sat=S unsat=U error=E": N is the number of job lines, S and U are the numbers of valid jobs whose status is SAT and UNSAT, respectively, and E is the number of error jobs.
2. "totals models=A rounds=B decisions=C propagations=D conflicts=F removed_clauses=G": the sums, over all valid jobs, of the statistics values models, rounds, decisions, propagations, conflicts and removed_clauses, respectively.
3. For the commands SOLVE, ENUM, CORE, QUEENS, PIGEON, COLOR, CHROMATIC in this order, for each command that has one or more valid jobs, "command X jobs=N sat=S unsat=U decisions=D propagations=P conflicts=C": X is the command, N is the number of valid jobs of that command, S and U are the numbers of them whose status is SAT and UNSAT, respectively, and D, P and C are the sums of the statistics values decisions, propagations and conflicts of those jobs.
4. (when there are one or more valid jobs) the 3 lines "hardest job=J conflicts=C", "deepest job=J max_depth=D" and "busiest job=J propagations=P": for each line, among the valid jobs, the job number J of the job with the largest value of conflicts, max_depth and propagations, respectively (if several jobs have the same value, the one with the smallest job number), and that value.

* output/models.txt
For the valid jobs that have one or more models, output one paragraph per job in job number order. Put one empty line between consecutive paragraphs, and no empty line at the start or end of the file. If there is no such job, the file is empty (0 bytes). The 1st line of a paragraph is "JOB J X" (the same as in results.txt), followed, for each model in number order, by a line "model I" (I is the model number) and the v lines of that model. The v lines are built as follows. For the variables x = 1, 2, ..., V in order, list the tokens that write as a literal x if x is true in the model and -x if it is false, and finally add the token "0". Split this sequence of tokens from the front into groups of 10 (the last group may have fewer than 10), and for each group output a line made of "v" followed by alternating single spaces and tokens (e.g. "v 1 -2 3 0").

* output/boards.txt
For the valid jobs that are QUEENS, PIGEON, COLOR or CHROMATIC jobs, output one paragraph per job in job number order. Put one empty line between consecutive paragraphs, and no empty line at the start or end of the file. If there is no such job, the file is empty (0 bytes). The model numbers I are the same as in section 9.

The QUEENS paragraph consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "QUEENS job=J n=N"
2. For each model in number order, "solution I columns=Q class=C": Q is the number list of the position sequence (in row order), and C is the class number of that solution. If that solution is the first solution of its class to appear, output the N lines of the board immediately after this line. The r-th line of the board is a string of N characters whose qr-th character is "Q" and whose other characters are ".".
3. For each class in class number order, "class C orbit=O found=F first=I canonical=Q": O is the orbit size, F is the number of models that belong to that class, I is the model number of the first solution of that class to appear, and Q is the number list of the canonical form.
4. "summary solutions=K stop=T classes=N": K is the number of models, T is the stop reason, and N is the number of classes.

The PIGEON paragraph consists of the following lines in this order.
1. "PIGEON job=J pigeons=P holes=H"
2. For each model in number order, "assignment I holes=X empty=Y": X is the number list of h_1, h_2, ..., h_p, and Y is the number list of the numbers of the empty holes in increasing order.
3. "summary assignments=K stop=T expected=E": K is the number of models, T is the stop reason, and E is the expected number of solutions.

The COLOR and CHROMATIC paragraphs consist of the following lines in this order.
1. "COLOR job=J graph=G vertices=V edges=E colors=K": for a CHROMATIC job, "CHROMATIC" is written instead of the leading "COLOR". G is the name of the graph, V is the number of vertices, E is the number of edges, and K is the value of the argument k for COLOR, and the value of k of the last attempt for CHROMATIC.
2. "greedy used=U colors=Y": U is the number of colors of the greedy coloring, and Y is the number list of the colors of the greedy coloring in vertex number order.
3. For each model in number order, "coloring I colors=Y used=U sizes=Z class=C": Y is the number list of the position sequence (the colors in vertex order), U is the number of colors used, Z is the number list of the vertex counts per color, and C is the class number of that coloring.
4. For each class in class number order, "class C found=F first=I canonical=Y": F is the number of models that belong to that class, I is the number of the first model of that class to appear, and Y is the number list of the canonical form.
5. "summary colorings=K stop=T classes=N": K is the number of models, T is the stop reason (for CHROMATIC, the stop reason of the last attempt), and N is the number of classes.

* output/stats.csv
The 1st line is the header line "job,command,status,variables,clauses,removed_clauses,decisions,propagations,conflicts,max_depth,models,rounds". Then, for each valid job in job number order, output one line that joins the job's statistics values with "," in the order of the header. No line is output for an error job.

[Source files]
Split the source files into files with the following 6 roles (file names and extensions follow the conventions of each language).
- main: the overall flow and the largest share of the processing. Reading the job file and validating the jobs, reading the graph file and validating the edges, preparing the original formula of each job, solving formulas (the repetition of rounds, building the models, blocking clauses), verification and analysis (true literal distribution, safe flips, diversity, backbone), the unsatisfiable core tests, the chromatic number attempts, board decoding and analysis (N-queens classes, coloring classes, greedy coloring, the expected number of pigeonhole solutions), aggregation, building all output lines, and writing the 4 output files.
- cnf_io: checking well-formed numbers, reading DIMACS files and checking them for errors, building the v lines of a model.
- encoders: the N-queens, pigeonhole and graph coloring encodings, and decoding positions from a model.
- preprocess: normalization, applying unit clauses, subsumption removal and pure literal elimination, with their statistics and records.
- dpll: the round state, watch initialization, root processing, unit propagation, decisions by the DLIS rule, backtracking, the search statistics and the event log.
- report: building lines of "name=value" items, number lists, frequency lists and board lines, and writing text files of joined paragraphs and CSV files.

[Constraints]
- jobs.txt has at most 100 lines, of which at most 60 are job lines. graphs.txt has at most 300 lines and at most 20 blocks. Each DIMACS file has at most 600 lines, and V is at most 100 and C is at most 600 when there is a header line. In every file, each line has at most 200 characters.
- Every original formula (including those of the CORE tests and the CHROMATIC attempts) has at most 200 variables, at most 3000 clauses, and clauses of length at most 100.
- In the input of this task, over all jobs together (including the CORE tests and the CHROMATIC attempts), there are at most 1000 rounds, at most 20000 decisions in total, and at most 200000 propagations in total.
- All intermediate values of the computation fit in the range of 32-bit signed integers.
