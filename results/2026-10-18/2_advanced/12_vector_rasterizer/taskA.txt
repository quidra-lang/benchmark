[Overview]
Write a program that interprets a scene written in a simple vector drawing language and draws it onto a grid of pixels (a raster image). A scene consists of several layers, and on each layer the scene draws lines, rectangles, circles, ellipses, polygons, paths that include Bezier curves, text, and so on. Drawing can use coordinate transformations (translation, scaling, rotation in multiples of 90 degrees), a clip rectangle (the area where drawing is allowed), repetition, and reuse of symbols (named drawing parts). Colors, pens and brushes are given in a style file, and the shapes of characters are given in a bitmap font file.
The program composites the finished layers by alpha blending, writes the final image as an ASCII PPM (P3), and also writes a reduced text preview, a log of the executed commands, per-layer statistics, overall statistics, and the results of probing the given points and areas. Some commands in the scene contain errors; a command with an error draws nothing and only leaves a log entry.
All computations use integers only; no floating-point numbers are used. The program reads files from the input/ folder and writes files to the output/ folder. It prints nothing to standard output.

[Input files]
Every input file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF). No file contains tab characters.

(1) input/styles.txt: styles
Each line is an empty line, a comment line or a definition line. Empty lines (lines with nothing written on them) and comment lines (lines whose first character is "#") are skipped. A definition line is a sequence of words separated by single space characters (there is no whitespace at the beginning or end of the line), and there are the following four kinds:
- color <name> <r> <g> <b> <a>: defines a color. r, g and b are the red, green and blue values, and a is the opacity (alpha value; 0 is transparent and 255 is opaque); each is an integer from 0 to 255 inclusive. Below, a color is written as the tuple of four integers (r, g, b, a).
- pen <name> <width> <color name> <on> <off>: defines a pen. The width w is the side length, in pixels, of the square to which each drawn point is expanded, and on and off are integers that describe the dash pattern (Section 6). A pen with off = 0 draws solid lines.
- brush <name> <color name> <rule>: defines a solid brush. The rule is evenodd or nonzero and is used when filling polygons and paths (Section 7 (7)).
- gradient <name> <color name 1> <color name 2> <axis> <rule>: defines a gradient brush. The axis is x or y, and the rule is the same as for brush.
Every color name that appears in a pen or brush definition is the name of a color defined on an earlier line. The things defined by brush and by gradient are together called brushes. Names are unique within colors, within pens and within brushes (the same name may be used by things of different kinds).

(2) input/font.txt: bitmap font
The first line is font <gw> <gh>, where gw and gh are the width and height of a character (in dots). Then come the definitions of the characters, each consisting of 1 + gh lines. The first line of a definition is glyph <c>, where c is the character being defined (a single printable ASCII character other than the space and "_"). The following gh lines are the shape of that character: line j + 1 of them (j = 0, 1, ..., gh - 1) represents row j counted from the top, and if character k + 1 of that line (k = 0, 1, ..., gw - 1) is "#", the dot at column k, row j is a drawn dot; if it is ".", it is not drawn. Each of these lines has exactly gw characters. There are no empty lines or comment lines, and no character is defined twice.

(3) input/scene.txt: scene
Each line is an empty line, a comment line or an instruction line. Empty lines (lines with nothing written on them, or lines consisting only of space characters) and comment lines (lines whose first non-space character is "#") are skipped. An instruction line is a sequence of one or more words separated by one or more space characters, and it may have indenting space characters at the beginning of the line. There is no whitespace at the end of a line. The first word of an instruction line is called the keyword, and the second and later words are called the arguments. Line numbers are counted 1, 2, ... from the start of the file, including empty lines and comment lines.
The following six kinds of lines are called structure lines. Structure lines are always written correctly.
- CANVAS <W> <H> <color name>: the first instruction line; it appears exactly once. W and H are the width and height of the image, and the color name is the background color.
- LAYER <name> <opacity> <mode>: the start of a layer block. The opacity is an integer from 0 to 255 inclusive, and the mode is one of normal, multiply and screen.
- SYMBOL <name>: the start of a symbol block.
- END: the end of a LAYER or SYMBOL block.
- REPEAT <n> <dx> <dy>: the start of a repetition. n is an integer >= 1, and dx and dy are integers.
- ENDREPEAT: the end of a repetition.
After the CANVAS line, LAYER blocks and SYMBOL blocks follow in any order. Inside a block there are the structure lines REPEAT and ENDREPEAT, and instruction lines other than structure lines (called command lines below); CANVAS, LAYER and SYMBOL lines do not appear inside a block. REPEAT and ENDREPEAT appear within the same block and are correctly nested and matched, and the lines between a matching REPEAT and ENDREPEAT are called the body of that repetition. Layer names are unique among layers, and symbol names are unique among symbols. Some command lines contain errors (Section 4).

(4) input/probes.txt: probes
The first line is the number of probes P, and each of the following P lines is one of the following. Words are separated by single space characters. There are no empty lines or comment lines.
- point <x> <y>: a probe of pixel (x, y).
- area <x0> <y0> <x1> <y1>: a probe of the area of pixels satisfying x0 <= x <= x1 and y0 <= y <= y1.

[Processing]
Process the input as described in Section 1 to Section 10 below.

Notation
- A pixel is denoted by its coordinates (x, y). x increases from left to right, and y increases from top to bottom. The pixels of the image (the canvas) are the W * H pixels satisfying 0 <= x <= W - 1 and 0 <= y <= H - 1.
- floor(a / b) (b is a positive integer) is the largest integer not greater than a / b (for negative numbers it rounds toward -infinity; for example floor(-7 / 2) = -4). |a| is the absolute value of a.
- round(n / d) (n and d are integers and d != 0): if d < 0, first negate both n and d; then the value is floor((2 * n + d) / (2 * d)) (this is n / d rounded to the nearest integer, with an exact half rounded to the larger integer; for example round(5 / 2) = 3, round(-5 / 2) = -2, round(7 / -2) = -3).
- Integer word: a word that may begin with a single "-", followed by one or more digits (0 to 9) (for example 12, -3, 0). Any other word (for example 2.5, +4, -, x1) is not an integer word.
- The rectangle (x0, y0, x1, y1) denotes the set of all pixels satisfying x0 <= x <= x1 and y0 <= y <= y1; it is empty if x0 > x1 or y0 > y1. The intersection of two rectangles (x0, y0, x1, y1) and (x0', y0', x1', y1') is the rectangle (max(x0, x0'), max(y0, y0'), min(x1, x1'), min(y1, y1')). The rectangle (0, 0, W - 1, H - 1) is called the full screen.
- The bounding box of a non-empty set of pixels is (minimum x, minimum y, maximum x, maximum y).
- ASCII order: the lexicographic order that compares strings character by character by character code (if one string is a prefix of the other, the shorter one comes first).
- The point sequence connecting two points (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) (called the Bresenham point sequence below) is the sequence of points obtained by the following procedure. Let dx = |x1 - x0| and dy = -|y1 - y0|; let sx be 1 if x0 < x1 and -1 otherwise, and sy be 1 if y0 < y1 and -1 otherwise. Starting with e = dx + dy and (x, y) = (x0, y0), repeat the following (i) to (v).
  (i) Append the point (x, y) to the end of the sequence.
  (ii) If x = x1 and y = y1, stop.
  (iii) Let e2 = 2 * e.
  (iv) If e2 >= dy, add dy to e and add sx to x.
  (v) If e2 <= dx, add dx to e and add sy to y (e2 here is the value computed in (iii)).
  The sequence starts at (x0, y0), ends at (x1, y1), and never contains the same point twice.

Section 1. Overall flow
(1) Read the four input files.
(2) Create one layer for each LAYER block, in the order in which the blocks appear in the file, and number the layers 1, 2, .... For each pixel a layer holds the state "empty" or "painted", and a painted pixel holds a pair of a color (r, g, b, a) and a line number. Initially every pixel is empty.
(3) Execute the layers one at a time in order of their numbers (Section 2 to Section 8). Executing a layer means executing its block (Section 3). A SYMBOL block is executed only when it is called by a USE command.
(4) Composite all the layers to make the final image (Section 9), put together the log and statistics (Section 10), and write the output files.

Section 2. Drawing state
While a layer is being executed, the program keeps a drawing state consisting of the following three things. When the execution of each layer starts, the state is set to the initial values below.
- Transformation matrix M = (a, b, c, d, e, f): a tuple of six integers. The initial value is (1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0).
- Clip rectangle C: the rectangle in which drawing is allowed. The initial value is the full screen. C is always non-empty and contained in the full screen.
- Stack: a stack onto which (M, C) pairs are pushed. The initial value is empty.
Coordinates in command arguments are user coordinates, and they are converted into pixel coordinates (called device coordinates below) as follows.
- Point transformation: the point (x, y) in user coordinates is transformed into the point T(x, y) = (a * x + b * y + e, c * x + d * y + f) in device coordinates.
- Device rectangle: for two points p and q in user coordinates, with T(p) = (X0, Y0) and T(q) = (X1, Y1), the rectangle (min(X0, X1), min(Y0, Y1), max(X0, X1), max(Y0, Y1)) is called the device rectangle of p and q.
- Device radii: for a pair of radii (rx, ry) in user coordinates, (|a| * rx + |b| * ry, |c| * rx + |d| * ry) is called the device radii (in M, either b = c = 0 or a = d = 0 always holds, so these are the horizontal and vertical radii after the transformation).
- Translation translate(dx, dy): replace e with e + a * dx + b * dy, and f with f + c * dx + d * dy.

Section 3. Executing a block
When a block (or the body of a repetition) is executed, its lines are processed from top to bottom as follows.
- Command line: execute that command (Section 4 to Section 8). This counts as one "command execution".
- Repetition (from REPEAT n dx dy to the matching ENDREPEAT): save a copy of the current (M, C, stack) as S. For k = 0, 1, ..., n - 1 in this order, restore (M, C, stack) to the same contents as S, perform translate(k * dx, k * dy), and then execute the body. After the last iteration, restore (M, C, stack) to the same contents as S. The stack is restored including the pairs on it (for example, a pair pushed by SAVE inside the body is no longer there in the next iteration). The REPEAT and ENDREPEAT lines do not count as command executions.
Command executions are given sequence numbers 1, 2, ... in the order in which they are executed, continuing across all layers. The line number of a command execution is the line number of that command line (for a command inside a symbol block, it is the line number of that line inside the block). Command executions inside a symbol called by USE are also command executions of the layer being executed.

Section 4. Checking commands
There are the following 23 commands.
- Drawing commands (15): LINE, RECT, FILLRECT, CIRCLE, FILLCIRCLE, ELLIPSE, FILLELLIPSE, POLYLINE, POLYGON, FILLPOLYGON, PATH, FILLPATH, FLOOD, TEXT, ERASE
- State commands (8): TRANSLATE, SCALE, ROTATE, SAVE, RESTORE, CLIP, NOCLIP, USE
Of these, LINE, RECT, CIRCLE, ELLIPSE, POLYLINE, POLYGON and PATH are called pen commands, and FILLRECT, FILLCIRCLE, FILLELLIPSE, FILLPOLYGON, FILLPATH, FLOOD and TEXT are called brush commands.
When a command is executed, first check the following (i) to (v) in order, and the first one that applies is the error of that command. A command with an error does nothing except being logged (Section 10) (it changes neither the drawing state nor the layer). If there is no error, execute the command (Section 5 to Section 8).
(i) unknown_command: the keyword is not one of the 23 commands.
(ii) bad_arguments: the arguments do not match the form in the table below.
(iii) unknown_style: for a pen command, the first argument is not the name of a pen; or for a brush command, the first argument is not the name of a brush.
    unknown_symbol: for USE, the first argument is not the name of a symbol, or the END line of that symbol's block comes after this USE line (a symbol cannot be used before its block has ended).
(iv) unknown_glyph: for TEXT, the string contains a character that is neither "_" nor a character defined in the font.
(v) bad_value: the value condition in the table below is not satisfied.
    stack_empty: for RESTORE, the stack is empty.
In the table, "integer" means that the argument is an integer word. p is the position where a pen name is written and b is the position where a brush name is written, but for the form in (ii) any word is accepted there.
- LINE p x0 y0 x1 y1, RECT p x0 y0 x1 y1, FILLRECT b x0 y0 x1 y1
  Form: 5 arguments, the 2nd to 5th are integers.
- CIRCLE p cx cy r, FILLCIRCLE b cx cy r
  Form: 4 arguments, the 2nd to 4th are integers. Value: r >= 1.
- ELLIPSE p cx cy rx ry, FILLELLIPSE b cx cy rx ry
  Form: 5 arguments, the 2nd to 5th are integers. Value: rx >= 1 and ry >= 1.
- POLYLINE p n x1 y1 ... xn yn, POLYGON p n x1 y1 ... xn yn, FILLPOLYGON b n x1 y1 ... xn yn
  Form: 2 or more arguments, the 2nd and all later ones are integers, and the number of arguments equals 2 + 2 * n (n is the value of the 2nd argument). Value: for POLYLINE n >= 2; for POLYGON and FILLPOLYGON n >= 3.
- PATH p s <path data>, FILLPATH b s <path data>
  Form: 2 or more arguments, the 2nd is an integer, and the sequence of the 3rd and later words is valid path data (Section 8 (6)). Value: 1 <= s <= 32.
- FLOOD b x y
  Form: 3 arguments, the 2nd and 3rd are integers. Value: T(x, y) is contained in C.
- TEXT b x y <alignment> <string>
  Form: 5 arguments, the 2nd and 3rd are integers. Value: the alignment is one of left, center and right.
- ERASE x0 y0 x1 y1
  Form: 4 arguments, all integers.
- TRANSLATE dx dy, SCALE sx sy
  Form: 2 arguments, both integers. Value: for SCALE, sx != 0 and sy != 0.
- ROTATE k
  Form: 1 argument, an integer.
- SAVE, RESTORE, NOCLIP
  Form: 0 arguments.
- CLIP x0 y0 x1 y1
  Form: 4 arguments, all integers. Value: the intersection of the device rectangle of (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) with C is not empty.
- USE <symbol name> dx dy
  Form: 3 arguments, the 2nd and 3rd are integers.

Section 5. Executing state commands
- TRANSLATE dx dy: perform translate(dx, dy).
- SCALE sx sy: replace a with a * sx, c with c * sx, b with b * sy, and d with d * sy.
- ROTATE k: compute the remainder q of k divided by 4 (the integer from 0 to 3 inclusive such that k - q is a multiple of 4; for example q = 3 for k = -1), and repeat q times the replacement of (a, b, c, d) with (b, -a, d, -c). With one replacement, shapes drawn afterwards are rotated 90 degrees clockwise on the screen around the origin of the user coordinates.
- SAVE: push the pair (M, C) onto the stack.
- RESTORE: remove the top pair from the stack, and set M and C to the values of that pair.
- CLIP x0 y0 x1 y1: set C to the intersection of the device rectangle of (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) with C.
- NOCLIP: set C to the full screen.
- USE name dx dy: first, log this command execution (Section 10). Next, save a copy of the current (M, C, stack) as S, perform translate(dx, dy), and then execute the block of the symbol name (Section 3; repetitions and USE inside it are executed in the same way). Finally, restore (M, C, stack) to the same contents as S.

Section 6. Common drawing rules
Every drawing command other than ERASE computes a set of pixels K (called the coverage set below) and paints all pixels of K. K is always contained in C. Painting a pixel means setting that pixel of the layer being executed to the "painted" state and giving it the pair of the pixel color and the line number of the command execution. A pixel that is already painted is also replaced by the new pair regardless of its color or opacity (colors are not mixed within the same layer).
- Pixel color: for a pen command, the color of every pixel of K is the pen's color. For a brush command with a solid brush, the color of every pixel is the brush's color. For a gradient brush, if K is not empty, let (X0, Y0, X1, Y1) be the bounding box of K; if the axis is x, for pixel (x, y) let t = x - X0 and L = X1 - X0; if the axis is y, let t = y - Y0 and L = Y1 - Y0. From the color of color name 1, (r1, g1, b1, a1), and the color of color name 2, (r2, g2, b2, a2), each of the four components of the pixel color is computed by the following formula (v stands for each of r, g, b and a).
  if L > 0, v = v1 + round((v2 - v1) * t / L); if L = 0, v = v1
- Drawing a sequence of segments with a pen: given a pen (width w, on, off) and a sequence of segments in device coordinates (P_1 -> Q_1), (P_2 -> Q_2), ..., the coverage set K is computed as follows.
  (1) Clip the segments one at a time, in sequence order, against C with the Cohen-Sutherland method (Section 7 (2)). For each segment that is not discarded, compute the Bresenham point sequence from the start point to the end point after clipping, and append it to the end of the point list. The point list may contain the same point several times (for example, the joint point of two consecutively drawn segments appears twice).
  (2) If off > 0, number the points of the list t = 0, 1, 2, ... from the start, and keep only the points for which the remainder of t divided by on + off is less than on. If off = 0, keep all points.
  (3) For each remaining point (x, y), let lo = -floor((w - 1) / 2), and collect all pixels (x + i, y + j) satisfying lo <= i <= lo + w - 1 and lo <= j <= lo + w - 1 (for w = 1 this is the point itself, for w = 2 the point and the pixels to its right, below it and to its lower right, and for w = 3 the 3 * 3 square centered on the point). This is called expanding a point into a square.
  (4) K is the set of the collected pixels that are contained in C (the same pixel counts once).

Section 7. Basic procedures
(1) Bresenham point sequence: as described in the Notation section.
(2) Cohen-Sutherland method: for the rectangle C = (x0, y0, x1, y1), the region code of a point (x, y) is the set of those of the four conditions x < x0 (left), x > x1 (right), y < y0 (top) and y > y1 (bottom) that hold. A segment P -> Q is clipped by repeating the following (a) to (c).
  (a) If the region codes of P and Q are both empty, stop with the current P -> Q as the clipped segment.
  (b) If the region codes of P and Q have a condition in common, discard the segment and stop.
  (c) If the region code of P is not empty, let A = P and B = Q; if it is empty, let A = Q and B = P. With A = (ax, ay) and B = (bx, by): if the region code of A contains "top", let N = (ax + round((bx - ax) * (y0 - ay) / (by - ay)), y0); otherwise, if it contains "bottom", let N = (ax + round((bx - ax) * (y1 - ay) / (by - ay)), y1); otherwise, if it contains "right", let N = (x1, ay + round((by - ay) * (x1 - ax) / (bx - ax))); otherwise (it contains "left"), let N = (x0, ay + round((by - ay) * (x0 - ax) / (bx - ax))). Replace A with N (if A is P, set P to N; if A is Q, set Q to N), and go back to (a).
  A denominator is never 0. As with the original segment, the clipped segment has its start point on the P side and its end point on the Q side.
(3) Sutherland-Hodgman method: the sequence of vertices V_1, V_2, ..., V_m in device coordinates is clipped against C = (x0, y0, x1, y1). Because polygon filling ((7)) regards vertex coordinates as pixel corners, the boundaries use x0, y0 and x1 + 1, y1 + 1. Apply the following four boundaries one at a time, in this order.
  Left: a point satisfying x >= x0 is inside, and intersection points are taken on the line x = x0.
  Right: a point satisfying x <= x1 + 1 is inside, and intersection points are taken on the line x = x1 + 1.
  Top: a point satisfying y >= y0 is inside, and intersection points are taken on the line y = y0.
  Bottom: a point satisfying y <= y1 + 1 is inside, and intersection points are taken on the line y = y1 + 1.
  In applying one boundary, let the input sequence be V_1, V_2, ..., V_m (m is the length of that sequence), prepare an empty output sequence, and for each i = 1, 2, ..., m in order, let S = V_(i-1) (V_0 is V_m) and E = V_i, and do the following. If E is inside: if S is not inside, append the intersection point I(S, E) to the end of the output sequence; then append E. If E is not inside and S is inside, append I(S, E). If neither is inside, append nothing. The resulting output sequence is the input sequence for the next boundary, and the sequence after applying all four boundaries is the clipped vertex sequence (once the sequence becomes empty partway, it stays empty from then on).
  The intersection point I(S, E) (S = (sx, sy), E = (ex, ey)) is (c, sy + round((c - sx) * (ey - sy) / (ex - sx))) for a boundary whose intersection points are taken on the line x = c, and (sx + round((c - sy) * (ex - sx) / (ey - sy)), c) for a boundary whose intersection points are taken on the line y = c (exactly one of S and E is inside, so the denominator is never 0).
(4) Circle point set: for a positive integer r, the circle point set O_r of radius r is the set of points (dx, dy) obtained by the following procedure (the same point counts once). Starting with a = r, b = 0 and e = 1 - r, repeat the following (i) to (iii) while a >= b.
  (i) Add the eight points (a, b), (b, a), (-b, a), (-a, b), (-a, -b), (-b, -a), (b, -a), (a, -b) to the set.
  (ii) Add 1 to b.
  (iii) If e < 0, add 2 * b + 1 to e. Otherwise, subtract 1 from a, and then add 2 * (b - a) + 1 to e (a and b here are the updated values).
(5) Ellipse point set: for positive integers rx and ry, the ellipse point set E(rx, ry) is the set of points (dx, dy) obtained by the following procedure (the same point counts once). Let x = 0 and y = ry, add the two points (0, ry) and (0, -ry) to the set, and then perform (i) and (ii) in order.
  (i) While ry^2 * x < rx^2 * y, repeat the following: compute D = 4 * ry^2 * (x + 1)^2 + rx^2 * (2 * y - 1)^2 - 4 * rx^2 * ry^2, add 1 to x, subtract 1 from y if D >= 0, and add the four points (x, y), (-x, y), (x, -y), (-x, -y) to the set.
  (ii) While y > 0, repeat the following: compute D = ry^2 * (2 * x + 1)^2 + 4 * rx^2 * (y - 1)^2 - 4 * rx^2 * ry^2, subtract 1 from y, add 1 to x if D <= 0, and add the four points (x, y), (-x, y), (x, -y), (-x, -y) to the set.
  In both cases, D is computed with the values of x and y before they are updated.
(6) Bezier curve point sequence: from control points P_0, P_1, ..., P_n in device coordinates (n is 2 or 3, and P_k = (x_k, y_k)) and the number of segments s, compute s + 1 points B_0, B_1, ..., B_s as follows. For each i = 0, 1, ..., s, let the weights w_0, ..., w_n be w_0 = (s - i)^2, w_1 = 2 * i * (s - i), w_2 = i^2 if n = 2, and w_0 = (s - i)^3, w_1 = 3 * i * (s - i)^2, w_2 = 3 * i^2 * (s - i), w_3 = i^3 if n = 3, and let
  B_i = (round((w_0 * x_0 + w_1 * x_1 + ... + w_n * x_n) / s^n), round((w_0 * y_0 + w_1 * y_1 + ... + w_n * y_n) / s^n))
  (then B_0 = P_0 and B_s = P_n).
(7) Polygon filling: from one or more vertex sequences and a rule (evenodd or nonzero), the set of pixels to fill is computed as follows. Here a vertex (X, Y) is regarded as a point in the plane, and the pixel (x, y) is regarded as the square of side 1 whose center is at (x + 1/2, y + 1/2).
  For each vertex sequence V_1, ..., V_m, consider the m edges V_1 -> V_2, ..., V_(m-1) -> V_m, V_m -> V_1 (if m = 1, the single edge V_1 -> V_1), and remove the edges whose two endpoints have equal y coordinates. The remaining edges of all sequences are used together. An edge (xa, ya) -> (xb, yb) is said to cross row y for each integer y satisfying min(ya, yb) <= y < max(ya, yb) (it crosses the line Y = y + 1/2 that passes through the pixel centers). The x coordinate of that intersection point is X = xa + (y + 1/2 - ya) * (xb - xa) / (yb - ya), and the direction of the edge is +1 if yb > ya and -1 if yb < ya.
  For pixel (x, y), let cnt be the number of edges crossing row y that satisfy x + 1/2 > X (edges whose intersection point is to the left of the pixel center), and let wind be the sum of the directions of those edges. If the rule is evenodd, the pixels to fill are those with odd cnt; if it is nonzero, those with wind != 0.
  (The test x + 1/2 > X can be done exactly with integers only: let D = yb - ya and N = 2 * xa * D + (2 * y + 1 - 2 * ya) * (xb - xa); if D < 0, negate both N and D; then test x >= floor((N - D) / (2 * D)) + 1.)

Section 8. Executing drawing commands
(1) LINE, RECT, FILLRECT
- LINE p x0 y0 x1 y1: draw with pen p (Section 6) the sequence consisting of the single segment T(x0, y0) -> T(x1, y1).
- RECT p x0 y0 x1 y1: let (X0, Y0, X1, Y1) be the device rectangle of (x0, y0) and (x1, y1), and draw with pen p the sequence of the four segments (X0, Y0) -> (X1, Y0), (X1, Y0) -> (X1, Y1), (X1, Y1) -> (X0, Y1), (X0, Y1) -> (X0, Y0) in this order.
- FILLRECT b x0 y0 x1 y1: let K be the set of pixels in the intersection of the device rectangle of (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) with C, and paint it with brush b.
(2) POLYLINE, POLYGON, FILLPOLYGON
Let V_i = T(x_i, y_i) (i = 1, 2, ..., n).
- POLYLINE: draw with the pen the sequence of segments V_1 -> V_2, V_2 -> V_3, ..., V_(n-1) -> V_n.
- POLYGON: draw with the pen the sequence of segments V_1 -> V_2, ..., V_(n-1) -> V_n, V_n -> V_1.
- FILLPOLYGON: clip the vertex sequence V_1, ..., V_n against C with the Sutherland-Hodgman method (Section 7 (3)), perform polygon filling (Section 7 (7)) with the single clipped vertex sequence and the brush's rule, and let K be the set of pixels to fill (K is then contained in C; if the clipped sequence is empty, K is also empty).
(3) CIRCLE, FILLCIRCLE, ELLIPSE, FILLELLIPSE
Let the center be (X, Y) = T(cx, cy). For CIRCLE and FILLCIRCLE, let (Rx, Ry) be the device radii of (r, r); the shape point set is O_Rx (Section 7 (4)) if Rx = Ry, and E(Rx, Ry) (Section 7 (5)) if Rx != Ry. For ELLIPSE and FILLELLIPSE, let (Rx, Ry) be the device radii of (rx, ry); the shape point set is always E(Rx, Ry).
- CIRCLE, ELLIPSE: for each point (dx, dy) of the shape point set, expand the pixel (X + dx, Y + dy) into a square with the pen width w (Section 6 (3)), and let K be the set of the collected pixels that are contained in C. Dashes and the Cohen-Sutherland method are not used (the pen's on and off are ignored).
- FILLCIRCLE, FILLELLIPSE: for each dy that appears in the shape point set, let m(dy) be the maximum of |dx| over the points with that dy, and collect all pixels (X + dx, Y + dy) satisfying -m(dy) <= dx <= m(dy). Let K be the set of the collected pixels that are contained in C.
(4) FLOOD b x y
Let the seed be s = T(x, y). The value of a pixel of the layer being executed is its color (r, g, b, a) if it is painted, and "empty" if it is empty (line numbers are not compared). K is the set of pixels (including s) that can be reached from s by repeatedly moving from a pixel (X, Y) to an adjacent pixel above, below, left or right, (X + 1, Y), (X - 1, Y), (X, Y + 1), (X, Y - 1). However, only pixels that are contained in C and whose value equals the value of s are traversed. Paint K with brush b (the brush's rule is not used).
(5) TEXT b x y <alignment> <string>
Let the string be c_0 c_1 ... c_(m-1) (m characters), and let its width be ext = m * (gw + 1) - 1. The left edge xl is x if the alignment is left, x - floor(ext / 2) if center, and x - ext + 1 if right. The origin of character c_i (i = 0, 1, ..., m - 1) is (ox, y) (ox = xl + i * (gw + 1)); if c_i is "_", do nothing (it is a blank). Otherwise, for each drawn dot (column k, row j) of the shape of c_i in the font, let (X0, Y0) and (X1, Y1) be the results of transforming with T the two user-coordinate points (ox + k, y + j) and (ox + k + 1, y + j + 1) respectively, and collect all pixels (X, Y) satisfying min(X0, X1) <= X < max(X0, X1) and min(Y0, Y1) <= Y < max(Y0, Y1) (1 pixel if the transformation does not enlarge, 2 * 2 pixels if it enlarges by a factor of 2). Let K be the set of the collected pixels that are contained in C, and paint it with brush b.
(6) PATH, FILLPATH
- Path data: path data is a sequence of words made of path commands. A path command consists of a word that is one of the following 14 single characters, followed by a fixed number of integer words.
  M x y and m x y (2): start a new subpath.
  L x y and l x y (2): advance along a straight line.
  H x and h x (1): advance along a horizontal straight line.
  V y and v y (1): advance along a vertical straight line.
  Q x1 y1 x y and q x1 y1 x y (4): advance along a quadratic Bezier curve. (x1, y1) is the control point and (x, y) is the end point.
  C x1 y1 x2 y2 x y and c x1 y1 x2 y2 x y (6): advance along a cubic Bezier curve. (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are the control points and (x, y) is the end point.
  Z and z (0): close the subpath.
  When the word sequence is divided into path commands from the beginning, it is valid path data if all of the following conditions hold: the word sequence is not empty; the first word of each path command is one of the 14 above, and the fixed number of words remain after it and are all integer words; the first path command is M or m, and any path command immediately after Z or z is also M or m.
- Computing points: process the path commands in order, and compute the points of each path command in user coordinates using the current point (cx, cy) and the start point. The initial values of the current point and the start point are both (0, 0). The coordinates of an uppercase path command are the point coordinates as they are. The coordinates of a lowercase path command are relative to the current point just before that path command is processed: cx is added to x coordinates and cy to y coordinates to give the point coordinates (for q and c, the same current point is added to all control points and the end point). However, H x denotes the point (x, cy), h x the point (cx + x, cy), V y the point (cx, y), and v y the point (cx, cy + y), and below they are handled the same as L. After any path command other than Z and z, the last point of that path command (the given point for M and L, the end point for Q and C) becomes the new current point. The point of M and m becomes the new start point, and after Z and z the start point becomes the new current point.
- Subpaths: process the path commands in order to build a list of subpaths, each holding a point sequence in device coordinates.
  M, m: for point p, append to the end of the subpath list a new subpath whose point sequence is the single point T(p).
  L, l, H, h, V, v: for point p, append T(p) to the end of the point sequence of the last subpath.
  Q, q: let P_0 be the last point of the point sequence of the last subpath, P_1 = T(p1) for the control point p1, and P_2 = T(p) for the end point p; of the Bezier curve point sequence (Section 7 (6)) B_0, B_1, ..., B_s (s is the 2nd argument of PATH or FILLPATH), append B_1, ..., B_s in this order to the end of the point sequence.
  C, c: in the same way, with P_0 the last point, P_1 and P_2 the transformations by T of the two control points, and P_3 the transformation by T of the end point, append B_1, ..., B_s.
  Z, z: mark the last subpath as "closed".
- PATH p s <path data>: for each subpath, let its point sequence be V_1, ..., V_m; consider the segments V_1 -> V_2, ..., V_(m-1) -> V_m, plus V_m -> V_1 if the subpath is closed, and draw with pen p the sequence of these segments arranged in subpath order (for a closed subpath with m = 1, it is the single segment V_1 -> V_1).
- FILLPATH b s <path data>: take the point sequence of each subpath, whether closed or not, as a vertex sequence, and clip each of them against C with the Sutherland-Hodgman method. Perform polygon filling (Section 7 (7)) with all the clipped vertex sequences and the brush's rule, and let K be the set of pixels to fill (a sequence that became empty has no edges).
(7) ERASE x0 y0 x1 y1
Let R be the intersection of the device rectangle of (x0, y0) and (x1, y1) with C. Return to empty every painted pixel of the layer being executed that is contained in R. The set of pixels returned to empty is called the target set of this ERASE (if R is empty, the target set is also empty).

Section 9. Compositing
The color (R, G, B) of each pixel (x, y) of the final image is computed as follows. Initially it is the r, g, b of the background color (the color of the CANVAS color name). Go through the layers in order of their numbers; if pixel (x, y) of a layer is painted, let its color be (r, g, b, a) and the layer's opacity be op, compute the effective alpha A = round(a * op / 255), and for each of R, G and B, with s the corresponding component of the layer's color and v the current value, let
  s' = s (when the mode is normal), s' = round(s * v / 255) (when multiply), s' = 255 - round((255 - s) * (255 - v) / 255) (when screen)
  new v = round((A * s' + (255 - A) * v) / 255)
A layer whose pixel is empty changes nothing. Below, the effective alpha of a layer's pixel means the A above if it is painted, and 0 if it is empty.

Section 10. Log and statistics
Each command execution is classified as exactly one of the following.
- Error: an execution that had an error in Section 4.
- Drawing: an execution of a drawing command without an error. Its pixel count is the number of pixels of K for commands other than ERASE, and the number of pixels of the target set for ERASE.
- State: an execution of a state command without an error.
For each command execution, one line is written to output/commands.txt according to its classification ([Output files] (3)). The log line of a USE comes before the log lines of the command executions inside the called symbol. Whether a pen, brush, color or symbol was "used" is determined as follows.
- Pens and brushes: used if there is at least one execution classified as drawing whose first argument is it.
- Colors: used if it is the background color, the color of a used pen, or a color of a used brush (one color for a solid brush; two colors, color name 1 and color name 2, for a gradient brush).
- Symbols: used if called at least once by a USE without an error.

[Output files]
Write the following six files to the output/ folder. Every file consists of ASCII characters only, and every line ends with a line feed (LF). Words in a line are separated by single space characters, with no whitespace at the beginning or end of a line, and no empty lines are written (preview.txt is a sequence of characters without word separators). Integers are written in decimal without extra leading zeros, and negative numbers have a leading "-". In the formats below, parts enclosed in < > are replaced by values, and everything else is written as it is. <bounding box> is the four values of the bounding box in the order <minimum x> <minimum y> <maximum x> <maximum y> if the set of target pixels is not empty, and none if it is empty.

(1) output/image.ppm: final image
A PPM in P3 format. The first line is P3, the second line is <W> <H>, and the third line is 255. Of the following H lines, line y + 1 (y = 0, 1, ..., H - 1) contains the 3 * W integers R, G, B of the pixels (0, y), (1, y), ..., (W - 1, y), in this order. No comments are written.

(2) output/preview.txt: reduced text preview
Divide the final image into cells 2 pixels wide and 4 pixels high, and write H / 4 lines. Each line has W / 2 characters. Character u + 1 of line v + 1 (u = 0, 1, ..., W / 2 - 1, v = 0, 1, ..., H / 4 - 1) is computed from the 8 pixels satisfying 2 * u <= x <= 2 * u + 1 and 4 * v <= y <= 4 * v + 3: compute the brightness lum = floor((sum over the 8 pixels of 299 * R + 587 * G + 114 * B) / 8000) (from 0 to 255 inclusive); the character is character number floor(lum * 10 / 256) (counting from 0 at the start) of the 10-character string "@%#*+=-:,." (the darker, the closer to "@"; the brighter, the closer to ".").

(3) output/commands.txt: log of command executions
For each command execution, in order of sequence numbers, write the following one line according to its classification.
  Error: cmd <sequence number> line <line number> <keyword> error <error name>
  Drawing: cmd <sequence number> line <line number> <keyword> pixels <pixel count> box <bounding box>
  State: cmd <sequence number> line <line number> <keyword> ok
<keyword> is the first word of the command line, written as it is. <error name> is the name from Section 4 (unknown_command and so on). The <bounding box> of a drawing is that of K for commands other than ERASE, and that of the target set for ERASE.

(4) output/layers.txt: per-layer statistics
The first line is layers <number of layers>. Then, for each layer in order of its number, write the following one line followed by color lines (at most 5 lines).
  layer <number> <name> opacity <opacity> mode <mode> commands <number of executions> errors <number of errors> pixels <pixel count> box <bounding box> visible <visible pixel count> colors <number of colors>
  color <r> <g> <b> <a> pixels <pixel count of that color>
- The number of executions and the number of errors are the number of command executions of that layer (including those inside symbols called by USE) and the number of those classified as errors.
- The pixel count and the bounding box are the number of painted pixels of that layer after all layers have been executed, and the bounding box of that set.
- The visible pixel count is the number of pixels where the effective alpha of that layer is greater than 0 and the effective alpha of every layer with a larger number is less than 255.
- The number of colors is the number of distinct colors (r, g, b, a) among the painted pixels of that layer. The color lines list the colors in decreasing order of pixel count, ties broken by increasing lexicographic order of (r, g, b, a), and at most the first 5 are written.

(5) output/summary.txt: overall statistics
Write the following lines in this order.
  canvas <W> <H>
  background <r of the background color> <g> <b>
  layers <number of layers>
  symbols <number of symbols>
  commands <number of command executions>
  drawing <number of executions classified as drawing>
  state <number of executions classified as state>
  errors <number of executions classified as error>
  error <error name> <count>: 7 lines, for unknown_command, bad_arguments, unknown_style, unknown_symbol, unknown_glyph, bad_value, stack_empty in this order.
  painted <total pixel count of executions classified as drawing other than ERASE>
  erased <total pixel count of ERASE executions classified as drawing>
  colors <number of distinct colors (R, G, B) in the final image>
  top <rank> <R> <G> <B> pixels <pixel count>: sort the colors of the final image in decreasing order of pixel count, ties broken by increasing lexicographic order of (R, G, B), and write at most the first 8, one per line, with ranks 1, 2, ... in order.
  symbol <symbol name> uses <count>: one line for every symbol, in ASCII order of names. The count is the number of times that symbol was called by a USE without an error.
  keyword <keyword> executed <count> errors <number of errors> pixels <total pixel count>: one line for each keyword that appeared in a command execution, in ASCII order of keywords. The count is the number of command executions with that keyword, the number of errors is how many of them were classified as errors, and the total pixel count is the sum of the pixel counts of those classified as drawing.
  unused <number of unused things>
  unused <kind> <name>: the colors (kind color), pens (pen), brushes (brush) and symbols (symbol) that were not used (Section 10), one per line, in this order of kinds, and within the same kind in ASCII order of names.

(6) output/probes.txt: probe results
For each probe, in input order, write the following one line. <number> is the position in the input (1, 2, ...).
  point <number> <x> <y> rgb <R> <G> <B> layers <k> stack <overlap>
  area <number> <x0> <y0> <x1> <y1> pixels <n> mean <mean of R> <mean of G> <mean of B> colors <number of colors> cover <c_1> <c_2> ... <c_L>
- point: R, G and B are the color of pixel (x, y) of the final image, and k is the number of layers whose effective alpha at pixel (x, y) is greater than 0. <overlap> lists those layers in increasing order of number in the form <layer number>:<line number of that pixel in that layer> (for example 1:28 5:70), and is none if k = 0.
- area: n is the number of pixels in the area; each mean is the sum of the final image values over the pixels of the area divided by n and rounded down; the number of colors is the number of distinct final image colors (R, G, B) in the area; and c_i (i = 1, 2, ..., L, where L is the number of layers) is the number of pixels of the area where the effective alpha of layer i is greater than 0.

[Source files]
There are the following six source files. The names are language-neutral role names; extensions and the use of uppercase and lowercase follow each language's conventions.
- main: handles the overall flow and contains most of the processing: reading the input files (styles, font, the block and repetition structure of the scene, probes), the drawing state (transformation matrix, clip rectangle, stack) and its operations (Section 2, Section 5), executing blocks, repetitions and USE (Section 3), checking commands (Section 4), parsing path data and building subpaths, building the coverage set of each drawing command (including the procedure for drawing a sequence of segments with a pen and dashes, text, and erasing; Section 6, Section 8), gradient coloring, the log and statistics (Section 10), and writing commands.txt, layers.txt, summary.txt and probes.txt.
- canvas: layers (color and line number per pixel), computing round, effective alpha, compositing (Section 9).
- primitives: the Bresenham point sequence, expanding a point into a square, the circle and ellipse point sets, the Bezier curve point sequence (Section 7 (1), (4) to (6)).
- fill: polygon filling (Section 7 (7)), filling circles and ellipses (Section 8 (3)), flood fill (Section 8 (4)).
- clipping: the Cohen-Sutherland method and the Sutherland-Hodgman method (Section 7 (2), (3)).
- ppm_writer: writing image.ppm and preview.txt.

[Constraints]
- 16 <= W <= 160 and 8 <= H <= 120; W is a multiple of 2 and H is a multiple of 4.
- The names of colors, pens, brushes, symbols and layers are words of 1 to 24 characters consisting of lowercase English letters, digits and "_". There are at most 100 colors, at most 100 pens and at most 100 brushes. The CANVAS color name is the name of a defined color, and that color's a is 255.
- The pen width w is an integer from 1 to 5, on is an integer >= 1 and off is an integer >= 0, with on + off <= 32.
- The font's gw and gh are from 1 to 8, and at most 100 characters are defined.
- scene.txt has at most 1000 lines, and there are from 1 to 20 layers and from 1 to 20 symbols. The number of command executions is at most 5000. The n of REPEAT is at most 50, and the combined nesting depth of repetitions and USE is at most 4.
- Every integer word in scene.txt has an absolute value of at most 10000.
- At the time of every command execution, the absolute values of a, b, c and d of the transformation matrix are at most 64, and those of e and f are at most 1000. In every execution of a drawing command without an error, every point transformed by T (including path control points and the corners of TEXT dots) has coordinates with absolute values of at most 1000, and the device radii are at most 100.
- P is from 1 to 50, the probed pixels and areas are inside the image, and for area x0 <= x1 and y0 <= y1.
- Within these ranges, every value, including intermediate values of computations, has an absolute value less than 2^31.
